# Fundamental Theology — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 1 · ~22 lessons · Prereqs: none · Roadmap id: `fundamental-theology`

## Goal

This is the gateway to the Catholic Theology field, taught from within the tradition: how the Church holds that God has spoken, how that word reaches us, and how to tell what the Church teaches, at what weight, and on whose authority. You will finish able to read a doctrinal claim and say where it comes from (Scripture, Tradition, a solemn definition, the ordinary magisterium, the theologians), what kind of assent it asks for, and what it would mean to deny it — the skill every later theology course assumes when it marks a claim *de fide* or freely disputed. Along the way it teaches revelation, faith and reason, inspiration and the canon as doctrines, the development of doctrine, the sense of the faithful, and theology's own method. It deliberately skips the arguments for God's existence (owned by `philosophy-of-religion`), the historical formation of the canons and the exegesis of particular books (`old-testament`, `new-testament`), the papacy as an institution and the history of Vatican I's definition (`ecclesiology-and-mariology`), and the events of the councils and the modernist crisis (`church-history`).

**Scope discipline.**

| Topic | Owner | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Arguments for God, evidentialism, reformed epistemology | `philosophy-of-religion` | Only Vatican I's doctrinal claim that God *can* be known by reason, and what that claim does and does not assert (1.2, 2.2) |
| Formation of the Old and New Testament canons; exegesis of books; historical-critical practice | `old-testament`, `new-testament` | The canon as defined (Trent), inspiration and inerrancy as doctrines, and Dei Verbum's principles of interpretation — the practice happens there (3.1–3.3) |
| Papacy as an institution; history of *Pastor Aeternus*; content of the Marian dogmas | `ecclesiology-and-mariology` | The conditions for infallibility and the logic of assent; the Marian dogmas appear only as examples of solemn definitions (4.2) |
| Councils, the Galileo affair, the modernist crisis as events | `church-history` | Cited as cases; the doctrinal texts (Trent Session IV, *Dei Filius*) are read here |
| Aquinas's metaphysics, analogy, the structure of the *Summa* | `thomistic-synthesis` | *Summa Theologiae* I q.1 and the treatise on faith (II-II qq.1–2) only (2.1, 5.4) |
| Argument reconstruction; the disputed-question form | [`philosophical-method`](../philosophical-method/syllabus.md) (1.3, 4.4) | Used, not taught |
| Sola scriptura and Protestant and Orthodox objections, argued out | `apologetics-catholic-claims` | Stated once as a foil where the Catholic doctrine is defined against them (3.3–3.4) |
| Church teaching on religious liberty, the death penalty, usury | `catholic-social-teaching`, `moral-theology`, `theology-of-debt` | Test cases for levels of authority and for development — the substance stays with the owners (4.5, Module 5) |
| **Levels of doctrinal authority (the ladder)** | **this course** | Every Catholic Theology course cites 4.4–4.5 and the table in Notes |

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md). Most practice is **exegetical** and graded strictly: what does this text actually teach, at what level, in which document — and overstating a level is an error. **Evaluative** problems are reserved for questions genuinely disputed among Catholic theologians (the analysis of the act of faith, the material sufficiency of Scripture, the scope of inerrancy, whether a given change is development or contradiction), where either school's answer passes if the required moves are made. A few **apologetic** parts ask for the Catholic position against a named alternative stated at its best. Close reading uses public-domain texts: Aquinas (English Dominican Province), Trent (Waterworth's 1848 translation), Vatican I (the translation in Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*), Vincent of Lérins (*Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*), Newman, and scripture in the Douay–Rheims. Vatican II and later documents are paraphrased and cited by name and section.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Say what the Church claims about natural knowledge of God and supernatural revelation, and separate what Vatican I defined from what it did not (that anyone has a sound proof, for instance)
- [ ] Distinguish public from private revelation and state exactly what an approval of an apparition does and does not bind
- [ ] Analyse the act of faith — its object, its motive, its certainty, its freedom — and explain how credibility and the preambles of faith keep it from being either blind or merely a conclusion of reason
- [ ] Diagnose fideism and rationalism in a piece of prose and state the Catholic middle position with its sources
- [ ] State what inspiration, inerrancy and canonicity mean as doctrines, and name the live dispute over how Dei Verbum limits inerrancy
- [ ] Explain the relation of Scripture, Tradition and Magisterium, and read Trent Session IV closely enough to say what it does and does not settle about "two sources"
- [ ] Say whether a given teaching act is extraordinary or ordinary, definitive or not, and check a claimed infallible teaching against the conditions
- [ ] **Assign a level of doctrinal authority to any claim, cite the act that fixes it, and say what assent is owed and what denial would amount to**
- [ ] Read a document's weight from its genre (dogmatic constitution, encyclical, catechism, instruction, interview), and not confuse the hierarchy of truths with levels of authority
- [ ] Apply Vincent's canon and Newman's notes to a proposed development and argue whether it is growth or corruption
- [ ] Explain what the sensus fidei is and is not, and why it is not majority opinion
- [ ] Build a theological argument from Cano's loci, marking which premises rest on proper and which on extrinsic authority

## Modules

### Module 1: Revelation

What theology is, and the claim everything else rests on: that God can be known, and that he has in fact spoken.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What fundamental theology asks | Say what the discipline is for and read the tradition's citations | faith seeking understanding (Anselm), theology vs religious studies, citing DH / CCC / ST / councils, a first look at the ladder |
| 1.2 | Natural knowledge of God | State what *Dei Filius* defined about reason's reach, and what it left open | Romans 1:20 and Wisdom 13, *Dei Filius* ch. 2 and its canon, "can be known" vs "is proved", the moral necessity of revelation |
| 1.3 | Supernatural revelation | Explain what revelation is, in word and deed, and why some truths exceed reason | revelation as divine self-communication, propositional vs personal models (Dulles), mysteries strictly so called, Christ as the fullness of revelation (Dei Verbum ch. 1) |
| 1.4 | Public and private revelation | Tell public from private revelation and weigh an approved apparition correctly | closure of public revelation with the apostles, private revelations as aids not additions, what ecclesiastical approval means, the Dicastery's 2024 norms on alleged supernatural phenomena |

**Boss problem 1:** Read the chapter "Of Revelation" in *Dei Filius* together with its first canon (Schaff's public-domain translation). (a) State precisely what is defined about natural knowledge of God, word by word — its object, its certainty, its instrument, its starting point — and list two claims a careless reader thinks are defined that are not. (b) Reconstruct the chapter's argument for why revelation was needed even for truths reason can reach, and say which premise it shares with *Summa Theologiae* I q.1 a.1. (c) A friend says a recognized apparition "added" a doctrine he is now bound to believe. Correct him, citing what approval does and does not mean. (d) A fideist objects that if revelation is morally necessary even for truths reason can reach, the canon's "can be known" is idle. In 150 words, assess the objection, stating what the chapter's distinction between capacity and actual condition does for the reply. Parts (a)–(c) are exegetical; (d) is evaluative.

### Module 2: Faith and reason

What believing God is, why it is reasonable without being a proof, and the two errors the Church defined against.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The act of faith | Analyse faith's object, motive and certainty | *Dei Filius* ch. 3's definition, belief on the authority of God revealing, *credere Deo / Deum / in Deum* (ST II-II q.2 a.2), assent with thought, faith as free and graced, *fides qua* vs *fides quae* |
| 2.2 | Credibility and the preambles of faith | Explain how faith can be reasonable without being demonstrated | motives of credibility (miracles, prophecy, the Church herself), preambles of faith (ST I q.2 a.2 ad 1), credibility vs evidence of the thing believed, the "eyes of faith" (Rousselot), the analysis of faith as a disputed question |
| 2.3 | Fideism and rationalism | Diagnose both errors and state the middle position | the double order of knowledge (*Dei Filius* ch. 4), no real conflict between faith and reason, the nineteenth-century condemnations of fideism, traditionalism and semi-rationalism, reason's role in defending but not proving the articles (ST I q.1 a.8) |
| 2.4 | *Fides et Ratio* | Give John Paul II's account of the partnership and what it asks of philosophy | "two wings", philosophy's legitimate autonomy, no official philosophy yet Aquinas's standing, the circle between theology and philosophy, the crisis of meaning |

**Boss problem 2:** (a) Using *Dei Filius* chs. 3–4 and ST II-II q.2, reconstruct in numbered premises the Catholic account of why an act of faith is both certain and reasonable, marking which premise carries the motive (God's authority) and which the credibility. (b) Read an invented op-ed paragraph on "faith and science" and diagnose it: fideist, rationalist, or neither, and which sentence decides it. (c) State W. K. Clifford's evidentialist principle from "The Ethics of Belief" (1877, public domain) at its strongest, then reply on the Catholic account in 150 words, conceding what is true in it. (d) The analysis of faith — how the believer can believe *that God reveals* on God's own authority without circularity — is freely disputed among Catholic schools; in 150 words, present one resolution and its strongest objection. Parts (a)–(b) are exegetical; (c) is apologetic; (d) is evaluative.

### Module 3: Scripture and Tradition

How the revealed word is handed on: a book the Church holds to be God's, and a living transmission that is not a second book.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Inspiration | Explain how God and the human writers are both true authors | God as author, the human writer as true author and instrument, dictation theories rejected, Aquinas on prophecy (ST II-II), Dei Verbum 11 |
| 3.2 | Inerrancy | State what the Church teaches about Scripture's truth and where Catholics disagree about its scope | *Providentissimus Deus* (1893), literary genres in *Divino Afflante Spiritu* (1943), Dei Verbum 11's truth "for the sake of our salvation" and its restrictive and non-restrictive readings, Dei Verbum 12's criteria of interpretation |
| 3.3 | The canon as defined | Say what Trent defined about the canon and what canonicity adds to inspiration | Trent Session IV (1546), "entire with all their parts", the deuterocanon as defined (its history ceded), the Church recognizes rather than confers inspiration (*Dei Filius* ch. 2), the Vulgate as "authentic" in a juridical sense |
| 3.4 | Tradition | Distinguish Tradition from traditions and state how it relates to Scripture | Trent's unwritten traditions and "equal affection of piety", the "two sources" debate and material sufficiency, Dei Verbum 9–10 (one wellspring; Magisterium as servant of the word), Tradition vs ecclesiastical traditions |

**Boss problem 3:** Read the decree on the canonical scriptures from Trent Session IV in Waterworth's public-domain translation. (a) Close reading: list each thing the decree binds the faithful to, and for each say whether it concerns the canon, tradition, or the edition of the text. (b) Say what the decree does *not* settle about whether some revealed truths are found in Tradition alone, and quote the words that leave it open. (c) Combine with Dei Verbum 9 (paraphrased): is Scripture materially sufficient — does it contain, at least implicitly, every revealed truth? This is freely disputed; argue either side in 300–400 words, meeting the strongest text against you. (d) A student claims inerrancy means every scientific and historical statement in the Bible is accurate as a modern report would be. Say at what level that reading is *taught*, at what level it is *disputed*, and why. Parts (a), (b), (d) are exegetical; (c) is evaluative.

### Module 4: The Magisterium and the ladder of authority

Who teaches with authority, when that teaching is infallible, what assent the rest of it asks for — and the course's centerpiece: the ladder every later course cites.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | The Magisterium | Say what the teaching office is and how its acts are classified | the pope and bishops as authentic teachers, servant not master of the word (Dei Verbum 10), extraordinary vs ordinary, universal vs particular, Magisterium vs theologians |
| 4.2 | Infallibility and its conditions | Check whether a teaching meets the conditions for infallibility | ex cathedra conditions (*Pastor Aeternus*, 1870 — history ceded), ecumenical councils, the ordinary universal magisterium (Lumen Gentium 25), nothing counts as infallibly defined unless that is clearly established (Code of Canon Law can. 749 §3), primary and secondary objects |
| 4.3 | Religious submission | Explain what is owed to non-definitive teaching and what responsible disagreement looks like | *obsequium religiosum* of intellect and will (Lumen Gentium 25), signs of the mind of the Magisterium (character of documents, frequency, manner of speaking), *Humani Generis* (1950) on encyclicals, *Donum Veritatis* (1990) on the theologian's difficulties and on dissent |
| 4.4 | The ladder of doctrinal authority | Assign a level to a claim and say what assent it asks and what denial would be | the Profession of Faith (1989) and its three paragraphs, *Ad Tuendam Fidem* and the CDF's doctrinal commentary (1998), heresy (can. 751) vs rejecting definitive doctrine (can. 750 §2), where theological notes sit below the magisterial rungs |
| 4.5 | Reading a document's weight | Read the weight of a claim from its source and genre, and avoid the classic overstatements | dogmatic vs pastoral constitutions, encyclicals, apostolic exhortations, instructions, the Catechism as a compendium whose claims keep their sources' weight, the manuals' notes and censures (*de fide*, *theologice certa*, *sententia communis*, *probabilis*), the hierarchy of truths (Unitatis Redintegratio 11) as a different axis |

**Boss problem 4:** Assign each claim its level on the ladder (see Notes), cite the act or source that fixes it, say what assent is owed and what a Catholic who denied it would be doing (heresy, rejecting definitive doctrine, withholding religious submission, departing from common teaching, or taking a side in a free dispute): (i) the bodily Assumption of Mary; (ii) God can be known with certainty by the natural light of reason; (iii) the Church has no authority to ordain women to the priesthood; (iv) the principle of subsidiarity; (v) Mary appeared at Lourdes; (vi) unbaptized infants who die go to limbo; (vii) efficacious grace works by physical premotion rather than by God's middle knowledge; (viii) the books of Tobit and Wisdom are inspired Scripture; (ix) a remark a pope made in an in-flight press conference. (a) For each claim, level, source and assent — exegetical, graded strictly; overstating any level is an error. (b) Some of these levels are themselves argued about among Catholic theologians (for example, whether (iii) is taught as divinely revealed or as definitive only, given the CDF's 1995 response and 1998 commentary). Pick one and, in 150 words, argue for a level while stating the case for the neighbouring rung — evaluative. (c) Explain why "the Catechism says so" does not by itself answer (a) for any item — exegetical.

### Module 5: Development and method

How teaching grows without changing, whose sense of the faith counts, and how theology argues as a science from its own sources.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Vincent of Lérins | Read Vincent's canon and his account of progress, and use both as tests | "everywhere, always, by all", progress vs alteration, growth "in the same sense and the same meaning", *Dei Filius* ch. 4's use of Vincent |
| 5.2 | Newman's theory of development | Apply Newman's notes to tell a development from a corruption | *Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine* (1845), the seven notes, ideas living in minds over time, Dei Verbum 8 on growth in understanding, *Mysterium Ecclesiae* (1973) on historically conditioned formulas |
| 5.3 | The sensus fidei | Say what the sense of the faithful is, how it is discerned, and what it is not | Lumen Gentium 12, Newman's *On Consulting the Faithful in Matters of Doctrine* (1859), the International Theological Commission's *Sensus Fidei* (2014) and its criteria, reception, sensus fidei vs public opinion |
| 5.4 | Sacred doctrine as a science | Explain in what sense theology is a science and how it argues | ST I q.1 a.2 (subalternation to God's knowledge), a.8 (arguing from authority, defending against objections), the ranking of authorities in a.8 ad 2, *fides quaerens intellectum* |
| 5.5 | The loci theologici | Build and grade a theological argument from its sources | Melchior Cano's *De locis theologicis* (published 1563), the ten loci, proper vs extrinsic loci, matching a conclusion's note to its weakest locus, the ITC's *Theology Today* (2012) |

**Boss problem 5:** The question: is the Second Vatican Council's teaching on religious liberty (*Dignitatis Humanae*, 1965) a legitimate development of, or a departure from, nineteenth-century papal teaching such as *Quanta Cura* (1864)? Catholic theologians genuinely disagree; the substance of the teaching belongs to `catholic-social-teaching`. (a) Close reading: from Vincent's passage on progress in the *Commonitorium* (public domain, *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*), state his test for true growth and the words that carry it. (b) Name the three of Newman's seven notes most relevant to this case and say what each would require the continuity reading to show. (c) Before arguing, fix the levels: what level did the nineteenth-century statements carry, and what level does *Dignitatis Humanae*'s core teaching carry? Say what follows for the question if neither is a solemn definition. (d) In 400–500 words, argue for continuity or rupture, laying out the argument as numbered premises with each premise tagged by its locus (Cano) and its theological note, and conceding the strongest point for the other side. Parts (a)–(c) are exegetical; (d) is evaluative.

## Sources of truth

- Heinrich Denzinger and Peter Hünermann, *Enchiridion Symbolorum* (43rd ed.) — the DH numbers; and the documents themselves: Trent Session IV, *Dei Filius*, *Pastor Aeternus*, Dei Verbum, Lumen Gentium, the 1989 Profession of Faith with the CDF's 1998 commentary, *Donum Veritatis*
- Francis A. Sullivan, *Magisterium: Teaching Authority in the Catholic Church* and *Creative Fidelity: Weighing and Interpreting Documents of the Magisterium* — the course's conventions for levels and for reading a document's weight
- Avery Dulles, *Models of Revelation* and *The Assurance of Things Hoped For* — revelation and the act of faith
- Ludwig Ott, *Fundamentals of Catholic Dogma* — the manual system of theological notes, used as a reference for how the tradition labelled claims, not as a magisterial source
- Public-domain primary texts: Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* (English Dominican Province); Waterworth's Trent; Schaff, *Creeds of Christendom* (Vatican I); Vincent of Lérins in *NPNF*, second series; Newman's *Essay on Development* and *On Consulting the Faithful*

## Notes

- **The ladder, refined.** This course owns the levels table in [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md). The syllabus keeps its five rungs and examples and adds the assent each asks for and what denial amounts to. Rungs 1–3 are acts of the Magisterium, following the three paragraphs of the 1989 Profession of Faith. Rungs 4–5 are theological qualifications, not magisterial acts, so they are not strictly ordered against rung 3.

  | Level | Example | Assent owed | Denial is |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Divinely revealed and proposed as such (*de fide divina et catholica*): solemnly defined **or** taught infallibly by the ordinary universal magisterium | the hypostatic union (Chalcedon); the Immaculate Conception (1854) | theological faith | heresy, if obstinate (can. 751) |
  | Definitive teaching, to be held: truths connected with revelation, including dogmatic facts | reserved priestly ordination (*Ordinatio Sacerdotalis*, 1994) | firm acceptance and holding | rejecting the doctrine of the Church (can. 750 §2), not heresy as such |
  | Authentic ordinary magisterium, non-definitive | the principle of subsidiarity (*Quadragesimo Anno*, 1931) | religious submission of intellect and will, graded by the document's character, frequency and wording | not heresy; responsible non-assent is possible but has conditions (*Donum Veritatis*) |
  | Theologically certain / common teaching | many conclusions of the manuals | the respect due to a strong theological consensus | theological rashness or error, in the manuals' censures |
  | Freely disputed among Catholics | Thomist vs Molinist accounts of grace; the fate of unbaptized infants | none beyond the evidence | taking a side |

  Two refinements to the brief's first row: dogma does not require a solemn definition (the ordinary universal magisterium can propose a truth as revealed), and the first two rungs can both be infallible — they differ in whether the truth is held as *revealed*. The level of *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis* is itself a teaching case (4.4): the CDF's 1995 response says the teaching is infallibly taught by the ordinary universal magisterium, and the 1998 commentary lists it under the second paragraph; lessons should present that exactly and not collapse it into either rung.
- **Accuracy discipline.** Lessons name documents by title, year and chapter or section only where the section is certain; otherwise by title alone. DH numbers are given only when checked against Denzinger–Hünermann. Which papal acts count as ex cathedra beyond the two uncontested ones (1854, 1950) is disputed, and lessons say so rather than list.
- **Where this goes next.** The ladder (4.4–4.5) is cited by every Catholic Theology course; Scripture and Tradition (Module 3) are the starting point of `old-testament`, `new-testament` and `patristics`; faith and reason (Module 2) opens onto `philosophy-of-religion` and `apologetics-foundations`; infallibility (4.2) is taken up historically and institutionally by `ecclesiology-and-mariology` and argued against its critics in `apologetics-catholic-claims`; development (5.1–5.2) is the lens `theology-of-debt` uses for usury and `moral-theology` for disputed moral teaching; ST I q.1 (5.4) is the doorway to `thomistic-synthesis`.
- **Helpful but not required.** [`philosophical-method`](../philosophical-method/syllabus.md) 1.3 (reconstruction) and 4.4 (the disputed question) are used here without being retaught.
- **Lesson count.** The roadmap estimated ~20; the syllabus runs 22 (within 25%). The Magisterium module needs five lessons because the ladder and the reading of a document's weight are separate skills, and the final module needs five because development, the sense of the faithful, and theological method each carry distinct primary texts.
