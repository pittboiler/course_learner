# Fundamental Theology · Reference Card

> Open book. This card is meant to be open while you work — including during
> quizzes. Nothing here is something you're expected to recall cold.

This course's machinery is a set of readings: read a doctrinal claim and find the act behind it, read that act's genre and language, read off the level of authority and the assent owed. The card holds the ladder, the tests, the document genres and the citation conventions, so that problems can spend your time on judgement rather than recall. Every rung and every test cites the lesson that teaches it.

## The ladder of doctrinal authority

The course's centerpiece, owned by [4.4](lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md). **It grades acts, not topics.**

| Level | Example | Assent owed | Denial is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Divinely revealed, proposed as such** (*de fide divina et catholica*) — solemnly defined **or** taught infallibly by the ordinary universal magisterium | the hypostatic union (Chalcedon); the Immaculate Conception (1854) | theological faith | heresy, if obstinate (can. 751) |
| **Definitive, to be held** — truths necessarily connected with revelation, including dogmatic facts | the legitimacy of an ecumenical council | firm acceptance and holding | rejecting a doctrine of the Church (can. 750 §2) |
| **Authentic ordinary magisterium, non-definitive** | subsidiarity (*Quadragesimo Anno*, 1931) | religious submission of intellect and will, graded by the three signs below | not heresy; responsible non-assent is possible, under conditions |
| **Theologically certain / common teaching** | many conclusions of the manuals | the respect due a strong consensus | theological rashness or error, in the manuals' censures |
| **Freely disputed** | Thomist vs Molinist accounts of grace; the fate of unbaptized infants | none beyond the evidence | taking a side |

Two things the table does not say on its face: **a dogma need not be solemnly defined** (the ordinary universal magisterium can propose a truth as revealed), and **the first two rungs can both be infallible** — they differ in whether the truth is held as *revealed*, which changes the motive of assent, not its firmness.

**Rungs 4–5 are theologians' qualifications, not acts of the Magisterium,** so they do not rank strictly against rung 3.

### The procedure

1. Find the **act** that proposes the claim — not the book you read it in.
2. Ask what the act proposes it **as**: revealed / definitively to be held / authentically taught / a theological conclusion.
3. Read off the **assent owed** and what denial would be.
4. **If step 2 is not manifest, default down.** Nothing is held to be infallibly defined unless that is clearly established (can. 749 §3).

*From* [4.4](lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md); the anti-overstatement rule in step 4 is the one the whole field leans on.

### The three signs of the mind of the Magisterium

For weighing an authentic non-definitive act: **the character of the document**, **the frequency with which the teaching is repeated**, and **the manner of speaking** (Lumen Gentium 25). These read the *weight* of a teaching; they are not a test for infallibility.

*From* [4.3](lessons/04-03-religious-submission.md)

### Document genres and what they carry

| Genre | Typically |
|---|---|
| Dogmatic constitution of a council | The heaviest genre; may define, but not every sentence defines |
| Pastoral constitution; declaration | Conciliar teaching; weight read from the language |
| Encyclical | Authentic ordinary magisterium; normally defines nothing |
| Apostolic letter; exhortation | Ordinary magisterium; read the operative verb |
| Instruction (can. 34); dicastery response or note | Authentic, non-definitive; weight partly from papal approval *in forma specifica* or *communi* |
| Motu proprio | A **mode of issuance**, not a rung |
| Address, homily, interview, press remark | Not a teaching act of the kind the ladder grades |
| Catechism | A compendium; each claim keeps its source's weight |
| ITC and (since 1971) PBC studies | **Advisory**; the weight of their argument, not of a teaching act |

*From* [4.5](lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md); the advisory point is set up in [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-fundamental-theology-asks.md).

### The hierarchy of truths is a different axis

*Unitatis Redintegratio* 11 concerns how a truth **relates to the foundation** of the Christian faith, not how firmly it is taught. The Assumption and the Incarnation can sit on the same rung while standing at different distances from the foundation. **Moving right never moves a claim down.**

*From* [4.5](lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md)

## Positions and distinctions

### Natural knowledge of God

*Dei Filius* (Vatican I, 1870) defines that God, the beginning and end of all things, **can be known with certainty** by the natural light of human reason from created things. What it does **not** define: that any particular proof succeeds, that any individual has one, or that anyone comes to faith that way.

*Introduced:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-natural-knowledge-of-god.md)

### Moral necessity of revelation

Even truths reason can in principle reach would be known by few, after long time, and with error mixed in — so revelation is fitting for them too. A claim about our actual condition, not about reason's capacity, which is why it does not contradict the canon above.

*Introduced:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-fundamental-theology-asks.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-natural-knowledge-of-god.md)

### Revelation as self-communication

God reveals himself in deeds and words together, reaching its fullness in Christ — not merely a list of propositions delivered, though it has propositional content that can be stated and defined.

*Introduced:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-supernatural-revelation.md)

### Public and private revelation

Public revelation closed with the apostolic age. Private revelation is an aid for living the faith in a given time and **never an addition to the deposit**. Church approval of an apparition judges that nothing in it is contrary to faith and morals and permits devotion; it does not guarantee authenticity in a way that binds faith, and it defines no doctrine. Assent to an approved private revelation is human faith at most, and is never required.

*Introduced:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-public-and-private-revelation.md)

### The act of faith

Its **object** is God and all he has revealed; its **motive** is the authority of God revealing, not the evidence of the thing believed; its **certainty** exceeds that of reasoned conclusions because of that motive; and it is **free and graced** — assent commanded by the will under grace. *Credere Deo* (believing God), *credere Deum* (believing God to be), *credere in Deum* (believing unto God).

*Introduced:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-the-act-of-faith.md)

### Credibility vs evidence of the thing believed

The motives of credibility show that believing is reasonable; they do not make the mystery evident. Faith that waited for evidence of its object would no longer be faith. **How the two fit together without circularity is a genuinely disputed question** among Catholic schools.

*Introduced:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-credibility-and-the-preambles-of-faith.md)

### Fideism and rationalism

Rationalism: reason alone suffices, or revealed mysteries can be demonstrated once revealed. Fideism and traditionalism: reason is incapable, so faith rests on authority alone. The middle is the **double order of knowledge** (*Dei Filius* ch. 4): the orders are distinct in principle and in object, and there can be no real conflict between them, since the same God is author of both. Reason may defend a mystery, illustrate it by analogy and answer objections against it — it may not prove it.

*Introduced:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-fideism-and-rationalism.md)

### Faith and reason as two wings

The modern restatement (*Fides et Ratio*, 1998): philosophy keeps a legitimate autonomy, the Church canonizes no philosophical system — while still holding Aquinas up in a particular way — and theology needs philosophy for its own concepts. An encyclical, so authentic ordinary magisterium: its philosophical judgements are taught, not defined.

*Introduced:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-fides-et-ratio.md)

### Inspiration

God is the author of the sacred books **and** the human writers are true authors — both fully. The human writer is a free instrument whose style, sources and purposes are genuinely his. Dictation theories are rejected. Inspiration is distinct from revelation (not everything inspired was revealed) and from canonicity.

*Introduced:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-inspiration.md)

### Inerrancy, and the dispute about its scope

Scripture teaches without error the truth God willed put there for the sake of our salvation. **Restrictive reading:** the clause limits inerrancy to matters of salvation. **Non-restrictive reading:** the clause states the purpose of all Scripture's teaching rather than a limit on it. The doctrine is taught at a high level; **the scope question is freely disputed**, and both readings have serious defenders.

*Introduced:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-inerrancy.md)

### Canonicity

The Church **recognizes** inspiration rather than conferring it — a book is not made inspired by ecclesiastical approval (*Dei Filius* ch. 2). Trent (Session IV, 1546) defined the list, received entire with all their parts.

*Introduced:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-canon-as-defined.md)

### The Vulgate as "authentic"

A **juridical** claim about which Latin edition was to be used publicly — not a claim that it surpasses the Greek and Hebrew or is free of translation faults. A classic overstatement to correct.

*Introduced:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-canon-as-defined.md)

### Tradition and traditions

Tradition is the living transmission of what was handed on; traditions are customs and disciplines, which can change. Scripture and Tradition flow from one divine wellspring and form one deposit, and the Magisterium is the **servant** of the word, teaching only what has been handed on.

*Introduced:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-tradition.md)

### Material sufficiency and the "two sources"

Whether Scripture contains all revealed truth at least implicitly, with Tradition as its authentic interpreter, or whether some truths are found in Tradition alone. Trent's wording is standardly read as leaving the question open. **Freely disputed.**

*Introduced:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-tradition.md)

### Authentic

A term of **office**, not of sincerity or of certainty: teaching with the authority the office carries, competent to bind. **Authentic does not mean infallible** — the most consequential English trap in this field.

*Introduced:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-magisterium.md)

### The two axes of magisterial acts

**Extraordinary or ordinary** (the mode of acting) crossed with **universal or particular** (the extent). Extraordinary-and-particular is empty as a matter of competence. The ordinary-and-universal cell is the surprising one: it can teach infallibly with no defining document at all.

*Introduced:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-magisterium.md)

### The four conditions for an *ex cathedra* act

Speaking as pastor and teacher of all Christians; by virtue of supreme apostolic authority; defining a doctrine of faith or morals; to be held by the whole Church. **Only 1854 and 1950 are uncontested instances**; which other acts qualify is disputed.

*Introduced:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md)

### Infallibility as a negative charism

A guarantee against error in a defined act, not an inspiration, not new revelation, and not a guarantee that the formulation is the best one available. Its **primary object** is revealed truth; its **secondary object** is truths necessarily connected with revelation.

*Introduced:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md)

### Religious submission

A real interior assent of intellect and will to authentic non-definitive teaching — **not** mere outward silence, and **not** a claim that the teaching is irreformable. Both points must be held together; dropping either is the standard error in each direction. It is not an act of faith, so denying it is not heresy (can. 752).

*Introduced:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-religious-submission.md)

### Difficulty and dissent

A theologian may have genuine difficulties with a non-definitive teaching and is to raise them in a manner that serves the Church rather than constituting a **parallel magisterium**. The Church's own instruction concedes that prudential interventions have at times needed correction.

*Introduced:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-religious-submission.md)

### The Vincentian canon

What has been believed **everywhere, always, and by all** — universality, antiquity, consent, each of which can fail independently. Vincent also supplies a procedure for contested cases. Note its limit: run strictly as a historical test, it returns a failing grade on doctrines the Church has defined, which is the problem Newman's theory answers. **The canon itself is taught by no magisterial act.**

*Introduced:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-vincent-of-lerins.md)

### Growth in the same sense and the same meaning

Vincent's condition for legitimate development — the body grows, the seed becomes a tree, and it stays the same doctrine *in eodem sensu eademque sententia*. This clause, unlike the canon, **was taken up by Vatican I** and carries conciliar weight.

*Introduced:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-vincent-of-lerins.md)

### Newman's seven notes

Marks for telling a true development from a corruption — **preservation of type, continuity of principles, power of assimilation, logical sequence, anticipation of its future, conservative action upon its past, chronic vigour**. Marks, not a decision procedure: each can be argued in a given case, which is why development disputes are genuine. Newman's *Essay* carries no magisterial authority.

*Introduced:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md)

### Historically conditioned formulas

Dogmatic formulas are expressed in the language of their time and can be stated better later, while remaining true and determinate in meaning. Overstated in one direction it dissolves dogma; in the other it freezes a single vocabulary.

*Introduced:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md)

### *Sensus fidei*

The instinct for the truth of the faith: *sensus fidei fidelis* in the individual believer, connected to the virtue of faith and to charity; *sensus fidei fidelium* in the whole body, which cannot err when it shows universal agreement in faith and morals. **It is not a majority opinion, not a poll, and not a rival authority to the Magisterium.** Authentic participation has dispositions — living the Church's life, listening to the word of God, docility, holiness and charity.

*Introduced:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### Reception

The faithful's living appropriation of a teaching. Reception does not **confer** authority on a definition: definitions are irreformable of themselves, and not from the consent of the Church.

*Introduced:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### Sacred doctrine as a science

Theology is a science by **subalternation**: it proceeds from principles known by a higher knowledge, as optics takes its principles from geometry. It does not follow that its conclusions are provable to someone outside the faith, or that faith is a hypothesis.

*Introduced:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-sacred-doctrine-as-a-science.md)

### Arguing from authority in theology

Proper to sacred doctrine. With an opponent who grants some revealed premises, argue from those; with one who grants none, you can only answer objections, not prove the articles. Aquinas ranks the authorities: scripture is proper and conclusive, the doctors of the Church proper but probable, the philosophers extrinsic and probable.

*Introduced:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-sacred-doctrine-as-a-science.md)

### The loci theologici

Cano's map of where theological premises come from: **proper** loci (Scripture; apostolic tradition; the Catholic Church; the councils; the Roman Church; the Fathers; the scholastic theologians and canonists) and **extrinsic** loci (natural reason; the philosophers and jurists; human history).

*Introduced:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-the-loci-theologici.md)

### The weakest-locus rule

A conclusion follows the weaker part: an argument's theological note is capped by its weakest load-bearing premise. An argument resting on a Father and a philosopher does not yield a conclusion *de fide*.

*Introduced:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-the-loci-theologici.md)

## Pitfalls

### Levels

- "The Church teaches X" covers at least five different things — always ask: taught by whom, in what act, at what level? *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-fundamental-theology-asks.md))*
- Overstating a level is the characteristic error of this field, and step 4 of the procedure exists to stop it: when the level is not manifest, default down. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md))*
- Understating is the mirror error: "not infallible" does not mean "an opinion." *([4.3](lessons/04-03-religious-submission.md))*
- Authentic does not mean infallible; a motu proprio is a mode of issuance, not a rung; an interview is not a teaching act. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-the-magisterium.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md))*
- The hierarchy of truths is about distance from the foundation, not firmness of teaching — a different axis entirely. *([4.5](lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md))*
- "The Catechism says so" locates a claim; it does not fix its level. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-fundamental-theology-asks.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md))*

### Texts

- The canons of a council are usually where the definition bites; not every conciliar sentence defines. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-fundamental-theology-asks.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md))*
- *Dei Filius* says God **can be** known by reason, not that any given proof works. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-natural-knowledge-of-god.md))*
- "Authentic" applied to the Vulgate is juridical, not a claim of superiority to the originals. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-the-canon-as-defined.md))*
- Trent's wording on unwritten traditions is standardly read as leaving material sufficiency open — do not enlist it for either side. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-tradition.md))*

### Faith, development and the faithful

- Credibility is not evidence of the mystery; a faith that waited for the latter would not be faith. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-credibility-and-the-preambles-of-faith.md))*
- An approved apparition adds no doctrine and binds no one in faith. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-public-and-private-revelation.md))*
- Vincent's canon is a private theologian's test, not the Church's rule, and run strictly it fails on defined dogmas. *([5.1](lessons/05-01-vincent-of-lerins.md))*
- Newman's notes are marks to argue with, not a procedure that settles a case. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md))*
- The *sensus fidei* is not a poll, and an advisory body is not the Magisterium. *([5.3](lessons/05-03-the-sensus-fidei.md), [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-fundamental-theology-asks.md))*
- An argument is capped by its weakest load-bearing locus, however impressive its other premises. *([5.5](lessons/05-05-the-loci-theologici.md))*
