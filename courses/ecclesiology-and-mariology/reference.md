# The Church & Mary · Reference Card

> Open book. This card is meant to be open while you work — including during
> quizzes. Nothing here is something you're expected to recall cold.

This course teaches what the Catholic Church holds about herself and about Mary, from within the tradition, with every claim's weight marked. Its machinery is a set of readings: find the act behind a sentence about the Church, read off its level, say what it does **not** assert, and state other traditions as their own theologians state them, with no reply attached. The card holds the levels of every text the lessons cite, the positions and distinctions in the wording the lessons verified, the other traditions' accounts, and the traps. Levels come from [`fundamental-theology` 4.4](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md), which owns the ladder; where that course and this card differ, that course wins.

## Authority and citations

### Levels of authority

**The ladder, as this course uses it.** Rung 1: proposed as divinely revealed, either solemnly defined or taught infallibly by the ordinary universal magisterium; owed theological faith. Rung 2: definitive, to be held (dogmatic facts sit here). Rung 3: authentic ordinary magisterium, owed religious submission of intellect and will. Rung 4: theologically certain or common teaching of the schools. Rung 5: freely disputed among Catholics. The procedure: find the **act**, not the book you read the sentence in; ask what the act proposes the claim **as**; read off the assent; and if the level is not manifest, **default down** (can. 749 §3: nothing is held infallibly defined unless that is manifestly evident). The ladder grades acts, not topics, and overstating and understating are both errors, graded strictly.

**Phrases this card uses.**

- *At least rung 3*: conciliar teaching that defined nothing new. Vatican II's notification of 16 November 1964 says the council defines as binding only what it openly declares so, and Vatican II defined no new dogma; a conciliar sentence then carries the weight of what it restates ([`fundamental-theology` 4.5](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md)), so restated dogma keeps rung 1 and new formulations sit at rung 3.
- *Rung 3, light grade*: a dicastery's act approved by the pope in the ordinary form, or a lighter genre such as an allocution or an audience catechesis.
- *Taught, not defined*: a chapter, prologue, history or reasoning inside a defining act. The canon or the definition bites; the explanation around it does not.
- *Below the seam*, *no magisterial level*: not a teaching act of the Magisterium (theologians, the Fathers as authors, dialogue commissions, the International Theological Commission, study documents). Weighed by their arguments.
- *Law*, *discipline*: canons, norms and rulings. Binding and changeable; no doctrinal rung.
- *No rung*: historical facts, editorial acts, liturgical acts, dates of papyri.

**The levels in this course, in one place.**

- **Rung 1.** The Creed's article on the one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church; the Church's institution by Christ (by the ordinary universal magisterium); Christ the head of the Church, his body; a hierarchy instituted by divine ordinance, a visible priesthood, bishops above priests (Trent XXIII cans. 1, 6, 7); the minister in mortal sin still confers the sacrament (Trent VII can. 12); the necessity of the Church for salvation; heretics' baptism true baptism (Trent VII, Canons on Baptism can. 4); Peter's primacy of jurisdiction, its perpetuity in the Roman pontiffs, their full, supreme, ordinary and immediate jurisdiction, and infallibility under four conditions (*Pastor Aeternus*); communion with Peter's successor as a bond of the Church's unity (*Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3); the primacy as Florence defined it; that the dispersed bishops can teach infallibly (*Dei Filius* ch. 3); no justified person avoids all sin through life (Trent VI can. 23); Christ the one Mediator (Trent V can. 3); *latreia* for God alone (Nicaea II); the four Marian dogmas (table below).
- **Rung 2.** Canonization, a dogmatic fact; *Apostolicae Curae* as the 1998 CDF commentary lists it; *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis*'s teaching at least rung 2 (rung 1 against rung 2 disputed).
- **At least rung 3.** Indefectibility (presupposed by rung-1 acts, defined by none as a thesis of its own); LG 12 on the inerrancy of the whole body *in credendo* ("not defined, not open"); bishops succeed the apostles by divine institution; desire or martyrdom can supply for baptism; Mary's lifelong freedom from personal sin and her fullness of grace; LG ch. 8's own teaching on Mary's cooperation, spiritual motherhood, subordinate mediation and type.
- **Rung 3.** *Mystici Corporis* (at its heaviest grade); *Lumen Gentium*'s new formulations (sacrament, one complex reality, People of God, the college, the sacramentality of the episcopate, incorporated/joined/ordered); *Unitatis Redintegratio*'s own teaching; Rome's one-subsistence reading of *subsistit in*; the Churches / ecclesial communities criterion; the 1875 German bishops' declaration as approved by Pius IX; *Mater Populi Fidelis* (so the use of "Co-redemptrix" is no longer simply freely disputed, though nothing about it is defined); and the other papal and dicastery acts in the table.
- **Freely disputed.** The referent of "this rock"; the priority of the universal Church; whether the Church herself sins; what the commission meant by *subsistit* beyond one subsistence; whether 1928 to 1964 on ecumenism was development or reversal; one subject or two in the college; how grace reaches the unevangelized; Epiphanian or Hieronymian brothers; whether Mary died; the meaning of *charisma veritatis certum*; whether *Humanae Vitae*'s norm was already infallibly taught (its floor is rung 3); and before 1854, the Immaculate Conception itself.
- **Disputed, no level.** *Haec Sancta*'s standing; whether any papal act beyond 1854 and 1950 is *ex cathedra*; *Unam Sanctam*'s closing sentence as an act (not manifestly defined).

### The four Marian dogmas

### Citations and how to read them

| Form | Points at |
|---|---|
| LG, UR, GS, AG, DV, CD, OE, AA *n* | a section of a Vatican II document (*Lumen Gentium*, *Unitatis Redintegratio*, *Gaudium et Spes*, *Ad Gentes*, *Dei Verbum*, *Christus Dominus*, *Orientalium Ecclesiarum*, *Apostolicam Actuositatem*) |
| MC, CN, DI, RM, UUS, MA, MD, MPF § or ¶ *n* | *Mystici Corporis*, *Communionis Notio*, *Dominus Iesus*, *Redemptoris Mater* (or *Missio*, named in full where both appear), *Ut Unum Sint*, *Mortalium Animos*, *Munificentissimus Deus*, *Mater Populi Fidelis*; vatican.va numbering |
| PA ch. *n* | a chapter of *Pastor Aeternus*; the canon closing chs. 1-3 and the definition in ch. 4 are what bind |
| Trent, Session, ch. or can. | a chapter expounds; a canon is where the definition bites |
| DH *n* | a place in Denzinger-Hünermann, **not** a measure of weight; the lessons give DH numbers only where verified |
| CCC *n* | a Catechism paragraph, which keeps the weight of its source |
| can. *n*; CCEO can. *n* | the 1983 Latin Code or the 1990 Eastern Code: law, not a rung |
| ST part, question, article | Aquinas, English Dominican Province translation |
| ANF, NPNF | Schaff's *Ante-Nicene* and *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, public domain; Percival is NPNF second series vol. 14 |
| DR | Douay-Rheims (Challoner); bracketed numbers give its book and verse where it differs, e.g. 2 Samuel 6 [DR 2 Kings 6] |

## The Church as mystery

Positions and distinctions from Module 1: what the Church is, where the word comes from, and the four images that do most of the work.

### Ecclesiology

The treatise on the Church: the Church was believed long before she was studied.

She entered theology as a clause of the Creed of 381, and the Fathers and schoolmen treat her inside other questions; Aquinas wrote no question *On the Church* (she appears at ST III q.8, as Christ's body, and II-II q.1 a.9, in the Creed). The first works with the Church as their whole subject came out of the quarrel between Boniface VIII and Philip IV: Giles of Rome, *De ecclesiastica potestate* (most probably 1302), and James of Viterbo, *De regimine christiano* (1302), which its 1926 editor titled the oldest treatise on the Church. The conciliar crisis added Juan de Torquemada's *Summa de ecclesia* (about 1453). So the treatise was born arguing about power; after the Reformation the argument moved to visibility ([Bellarmine](#bellarmine-on-the-visible-church)), and the twentieth century recovered the sources ([*ressourcement*](#ressourcement)).

**Does not imply:** that a doctrine is as young as its treatise. The Church has been confessed as long as the creeds.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md)

### Bellarmine on the visible Church

A definition built so the Church can be found: membership by visible bonds, against an invisible Church of the elect.

Robert Bellarmine, *Controversies*, "De conciliis et ecclesia", bk 3 (*De ecclesia militante*), ch. 2 (published from the 1580s). The one true Church is the company of people bound together by profession of the same Christian faith and communion in the same sacraments, under legitimate pastors and chiefly the Roman pontiff: three bonds. She is "as visible and palpable as the assembly of the Roman people, or the kingdom of France, or the republic of the Venetians" (lesson's translation). He rejects five positions, from Wyclif and Hus to the Augsburg Confession and Calvin, which he reads as placing the true Church among the truly believing or elect.

The same chapter takes Augustine's soul (faith, hope, charity) and body (profession, sacraments): the definition takes in only the outward way of belonging, "as a minimum for someone to be called part of the visible Church" (lesson's translation). The cost, on the critics' account: the manuals read a membership minimum as the Church's essence, and the treatise became a *hierarchology*.

**Level:** a theologian's definition, **no magisterial level** (his doctorate of 1931 does not change that); the three bonds recur in LG 14 at LG's own weight.

**Does not assert:** that the Church is only her structure.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md)

### Ressourcement

The return to the sources, over the manuals' heads, to the Fathers, the liturgy and Scripture.

Antecedents: Johann Adam Möhler of the Catholic Tübingen school, *Die Einheit in der Kirche* (1825), the Church as the visible form of the Spirit's life, and *Symbolik* (1832), the Church set beside the Incarnation; Newman on development ([`fundamental-theology` 5.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md)) and on consulting the faithful ([5.3](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-03-the-sensus-fidei.md)). The twentieth century: Henri de Lubac, *Catholicisme* (1938), on the social aspects of dogma; Yves Congar, *Chrétiens désunis* (1937), *Vraie et fausse réforme dans l'Église* (1950), *Jalons pour une théologie du laïcat* (1953). Both worked under Roman restriction around 1950 and served Vatican II as experts. *Lumen Gentium* bears their mark but is weighed as the Council's text. De Lubac's quarrel is [`thomistic-synthesis` 6.4](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/06-04-transcendental-thomism-and-the-ressourcement-quarrel.md)'s.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md)

### Models of the Church

Lenses, not parties: each model lights something up and, used alone, distorts it.

Avery Dulles, *Models of the Church* (1974; a sixth model, the community of disciples, added in 1987), sorting ecclesiologies by root image, as his *Models of Revelation* sorts theories of revelation ([`fundamental-theology` 1.3](../fundamental-theology/lessons/01-03-supernatural-revelation.md)).

| Model | Root image | Risk used alone |
|---|---|---|
| Institution | a visible society | the structure taken for the whole |
| Mystical communion | Body of Christ, People of God | a fellowship with no visible shape |
| Sacrament | sign and instrument (LG 1) | a Church absorbed in her own sign |
| Herald | the Word proclaimed | an event with no abiding body |
| Servant | Christ who serves | one more relief agency |

Models sort emphases, not authors: Bellarmine fits the institution model on membership and the communion model on the Church's life.

**Level:** a theologian's taxonomy, **no magisterial level**; not the Church's official options.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md)

### Credo ecclesiam

The Church is professed in the Creed, inside the Spirit's article; faith *in*, strictly, is owed to God.

Aquinas, ST II-II q.1 a.9 ad 5: "in" the holy Catholic Church holds only as faith directed to the Holy Ghost who sanctifies the Church; it is better to omit the "in" (he credits "Pope Leo"; New Advent's bracket traces it to Rufinus). The Greek Creed of 381 repeats the preposition (*eis*), and Percival's English keeps it; Aquinas allows that only if the "in" runs through to the Spirit. CCC 750: we profess the Church rather than believing in her, so as not to confuse God with his works. So ecclesiology's first question is what the Spirit is doing here.

**Does not assert:** that the Church is a second object of faith beside God, or that she is dropped from the Creed.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md)

### Church as sacrament

The Church is like a sacrament: a visible sign and instrument of union with God and of the unity of the human race, in Christ.

LG 1: the Church is in Christ *veluti sacramentum*, "like a sacrament" (*veluti*, "as it were", lesson's gloss), sign and instrument of intimate union with God and of the unity of the whole human race. LG 9: the "visible sacrament of this saving unity", citing Cyprian's "sacrament of unity" (ANF Ep. 75.6 = Hartel 69.6). LG 48: the "universal sacrament of salvation". CCC 774-776, 780. Otto Semmelroth, *Die Kirche als Ursakrament* (1953), called her the "primordial sacrament", a theologians' term with no magisterial level. Read LG 1 phrase by phrase: *in Christ*, since Christ is the mystery (CCC 774); *like*, so no eighth sacrament; *sign and instrument*, both halves; *union with God, then unity of the human race*, in that order (CCC 775).

**Level:** conciliar teaching, **at least rung 3**, defined nowhere.

**Does not assert:** an eighth sacrament (Trent's seven, rung 1, are untouched); a sacrament in her own right apart from Christ; a humanitarian agency. The sacramental side is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md) [1.1](../sacraments-and-liturgy/lessons/01-01-what-a-sacrament-is.md)'s. Dulles's sacrament model: [models](#models-of-the-church).

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)

### Institution of the Church by Christ

The Church exists because Christ willed and founded her, not because his followers later organized.

*Dei Filius* ch. 3 (1870, Schaff): "God has instituted the Church through his only-begotten Son, and has bestowed on it manifest notes of that institution". *Pastor Aeternus*'s prologue: the eternal Pastor "determined to build up the holy Church"; LG 18 restates it, following Vatican I. The foundation is by a series of acts: the kingdom preached, the Twelve chosen with Peter, the Supper and the cross (CCC 763-766); see [stages of the Church](#stages-of-the-church). The boundary: *Lamentabili* (Holy Office decree, 3 July 1907, approved by Pius X) condemned proposition 52, that it was foreign to Christ's mind to establish a Church meant to last through a long series of centuries; the 1910 Oath made clergy profess that the historical Christ founded the Church directly, "as he dwelt with us".

**Level:** **rung 1**, by the ordinary universal magisterium. The CDF's 1998 commentary on the *Professio fidei* lists the foundation of the Church by Christ's will among truths proposed as revealed. Vatican I states it in a chapter and a prologue, which teach without defining, and defined canons presuppose it (Trent XXIII can. 6; *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 1). The manuals' *de fide* note is testimony, not the act. *Lamentabili* and the Oath are authentic papal acts, not definitions: they mark the boundary and do not raise the level.

**Left open:** the timetable and shape of the offices (presbyter and bishop seem one office in Titus 1:5-7; one bishop per city first in Ignatius about 110), how Jesus pictured the time before the end, the wording of particular sayings (critical scholars still argue Matthew 16:18 and 18:17). These are historians' questions, with no magisterial standing ([`new-testament` 3.3](../new-testament/lessons/03-03-what-historians-broadly-agree-on.md), [6.1](../new-testament/lessons/06-01-the-disputed-pauline-letters-and-the-pastorals.md)).

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md)

### Ekklesia

The assembly word: *ekklesia* means an assembly, not "the called-out ones".

The Septuagint renders the *qahal*, Israel assembled before God, by *ekklesia* (Deut 9:10, "the day of the *ekklesia*", lesson's translation) or by *synagoge* (Num 20:4, which the Douay, after the Vulgate, renders "the church of the Lord", and LG 9 cites). Acts uses the word for the city assembly of Ephesus (19:32, 39, 41 [DR 19:40]). CCC 751 mentions the *ek-kalein* derivation but gives the meaning as a convocation or assembly; what was new was who convenes it. In the Gospels only Matthew has it: 16:18 for Jesus's people as a whole, 18:17 for a congregation ([`new-testament` 2.2](../new-testament/lessons/02-02-matthew.md)). Paul uses it in three inseparable senses, the liturgical assembly, the local community and the universal community (CCC 752; 1 Cor 1:2, 15:9; Col 1:18); LG 26 says the Church of Christ is truly present in every legitimate local congregation.

**Does not license:** "a sect apart" from the etymology, or "two verses show Jesus intended no Church": the teaching rests on acts, not a word count. The "called-out ones" gloss is not doctrine at all.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md)

### Kingdom and Church

The Church is the kingdom's seed and beginning on earth: neither the finished kingdom nor its replacement.

Jesus preached the kingdom, "at hand" (Mark 1:15) and "come upon you" (Luke 11:20). LG 5 cites both and says he set his Church on her course by preaching it; she receives the mission to proclaim it and is on earth its *germen et initium*, seed and beginning (lesson's translation), straining toward its completion. LG 3: the kingdom of Christ already present in mystery. Loisy's sentence, "Jesus foretold the kingdom, and it was the Church that came" (*The Gospel and the Church*, Home's translation, 1903; [`church-history` 8.3](../church-history/lessons/08-03-leo-xiii-and-the-modernist-crisis.md)), sets the problem. On the Catholic account growth unfolds what Christ willed and began; the condemned position draws legitimacy from development alone.

**Level:** conciliar teaching, **at least rung 3**.

**Does not assert:** identity with the finished kingdom.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md)

### The Twelve

The Twelve are a claim about Israel regathered, and the first element of the structure Christ gave his Church.

Jesus "made that twelve should be with him" (Mark 3:14), to sit "on twelve seats judging the twelve tribes of Israel" (Matt 19:28). CCC 765: the Twelve, with Peter at their head, are the first element of that structure, foundation stones of the new Jerusalem (Rev 21:12-14). LG 19: formed after the manner of a college, or stable group. Matthias restores the number before Pentecost (Acts 1:15-26; criterion 1:21-22), but when Herod kills James no one replaces him (Acts 12:2): a number that marks a founding generation, not an office to be refilled. What does pass on is [apostolic succession](#apostolic-succession). That Jesus called twelve is near-consensus history; the restoration reading is a live dispute among historians ([`new-testament` 3.3](../new-testament/lessons/03-03-what-historians-broadly-agree-on.md)).

**Level:** LG 19 at least rung 3.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md)

### Images of the Church

LG 6 gathers more than a dozen biblical images; each says one thing.

- **Flock and sheepfold** (John 10:16): there is one shepherd.
- **Field and vine** (John 15:5): the branches live from Christ.
- **Building and temple** (1 Cor 3:16-17; Eph 2:19-22): God dwells in this people.
- **Bride and mother** (Eph 5:25-27; Rev 21:2, 9): Christ loves and cleanses her, and she is not he.

The body is the [mystical body](#mystical-body); the people of God (1 Pet 2:9-10) is [People of God](#people-of-god). No magisterial act ranks the images; as a map needs several projections, the Church needs several images ([1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)).

**Level:** at least rung 3; "dogma" overstates, "pastoral opinion" understates.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)

### Stages of the Church

Founded by Christ, manifested at Pentecost: the Church was not born on one day.

LG 2 (CCC 759): the Church was foreshadowed from the world's beginning, prepared in Israel, constituted by Christ in the last times, manifested by the outpouring of the Spirit, and will be completed at the end (LG ch. 7). The founding is a series of acts (CCC 763-766): the kingdom preached, the little flock and the Twelve, the Supper and the cross; she is born above all from his self-giving, from his pierced side (CCC 766, LG 3). At Pentecost she was "openly displayed" (CCC 767).

**Level:** conciliar teaching, **at least rung 3**, not defined in those words.

**Does not license:** "the Church was born at Pentecost" read as the Spirit and the apostles beginning something Jesus had not, which runs into [institution by Christ](#institution-of-the-church-by-christ).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md)

### Mystical body

The Church is Christ's body: members with their head, living from his Spirit, and a real society with something inside it no other society has.

Paul uses the image two ways: in 1 Corinthians 12 and Romans 12 the body is a congregation of many members in mutual need (the head there is one member among the rest, 1 Cor 12:21); in Colossians and Ephesians Christ is "the head of the body, the church" (Col 1:18; Eph 1:22-23, 4:15-16). Augustine fused them in the *totus Christus*; for Aquinas head and members are as it were one mystical person ([`christology` 5.3](../christology/lessons/05-03-satisfaction-anselm-and-aquinas.md)). "Mystical" marks the Church off from Christ's physical body, born of the Virgin, at the Father's right hand and under the Eucharistic veils, and from every merely physical or moral body, whose bond is a common end under authority; this body also has an uncreated inner principle, the Spirit (MC 60-62). Acts 9:4 shows the head-body use: Christ is persecuted in his members, a mystical, not a hypostatic, identity.

**Level:** that Christ is head of the Church, his body, is **rung 1**: Scripture's own statement, used as a premise by Trent (Session VI ch. 16: Christ infuses his virtue into the justified "as the head into the members", Schaff) and restated in LG 7. **Used alone**, the image ties the Church so tightly to Christ that her sins and her unfinished journey drop out ([1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)

### Mystici Corporis

Pius XII's encyclical of 29 June 1943: the Mystical Body is visible because it is a body, and it is the Roman Catholic Church.

In paraphrase. The Church is visible because she is a body (§14); visible, juridical and mystical at once, and MC condemns setting a Church of charity against a "juridical" Church (§§63-65). "The Mystical Body of Christ" is the noblest description of the true Church, which MC names the One, Holy, Catholic, Apostolic and Roman Church (§13). *Humani Generis* (12 August 1950, §27) summed the doctrine up as the two being "one and the same thing" and complained that some held themselves not bound. Membership is §22; not every grave sin severs (§23); those outside are ordered by desire (§103): see [members of the Church](#members-of-the-church). Christ and his vicar constitute one only head (§40); bishops receive ordinary jurisdiction directly from the pope (§42, read now through the Nota); the Church is spotless in sacraments, faith and laws, and members' falls are not laid to her charge (§66).

**Level:** authentic ordinary magisterium, **rung 3 at its heaviest**: *Humani Generis* insisted on the identification and teaches that a deliberate papal judgment takes a question out of free debate (§20). It is no higher: no definition, no "definitively held", and "based on the Sources of Revelation" (HG 27) names the teaching's ground, not a mode of proposal (can. 749 §3).

**Not:** "MC defined that the Mystical Body is the Roman Catholic Church" (overstates); "Pius XII's opinion, which the council dropped" (understates: LG 8 footnotes both MC and *Humani Generis* at its "one complex reality" sentence). The council chose a different verb, [*subsistit in*](#subsistit-in); what that did is argued among Catholics.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md); used in [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Soul of the Church

The Spirit is to the Church what the soul is to a body: a comparison, taught at rung 3, with rung-1 limits.

Augustine, *Sermo* 267.4 (Pentecost): what the soul is to a man's body, the Holy Spirit is to Christ's body, the Church (lesson's translation); the soul is whole in each member, working differently through each, and a member cut off loses the life. Leo XIII, *Divinum Illud Munus* (9 May 1897, §6): Christ the head, the Spirit the soul. MC 57 repeats it. LG 7 restates it more cautiously: the Spirit, one and the same in head and members, gives life, unity and movement, so that the Fathers *could compare* his work to the soul's. Aquinas can liken the Spirit to the heart instead (ST III q.8 a.1 ad 3); his rule for metaphor: "we must not expect a likeness in all respects; for thus there would be not likeness but identity" (a.1 ad 2).

**Level:** a comparison in two encyclicals, **rung 3**, restated by the council as a comparison.

**Limits, rung 1:** the Spirit is not incarnate in the Church (only the Son assumed a nature, [`trinity-and-god` 6.1](../trinity-and-god/lessons/06-01-the-missions-of-the-son-and-the-spirit.md)); he is not her form and makes no one substance with her; the outward works are undivided, so "soul" is an appropriation ([`trinity-and-god` 4.5](../trinity-and-god/lessons/04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md); MC 78); the members keep their own personality (MC 61). And the Spirit is not the subject of a member's sin.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md)

### Head and members

Christ is head of the Church by order, perfection and power; only he is head by inward influx of grace.

Aquinas, ST III q.8. Christ is head by **order** (his grace is first), **perfection** (it is full) and **power** (it flows to every member) (a.1). That this capital grace is his own habitual grace is [`christology` 3.4](../christology/lessons/03-04-the-grace-of-the-head.md)'s (a.5). Only Christ is head by giving grace from within; bishops and the pope are heads only by outward government, in his place (a.6); MC 40: the pope as vicar makes no second head. The mystical body spans all time, from the world's beginning to its end, so its members are counted in act and in potency, in grades (a.3): by glory, by charity, by faith alone, by a potency that will be actualized, and by one that never will be. Aquinas's a.3 and MC 22 answer different questions: union with the head across all time, and membership of the visible body on earth now.

**Level:** headship **rung 1** (see [mystical body](#mystical-body)); Aquinas's analysis is Thomist theology, with no magisterial act.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md)

### Members of the Church

In 1943 membership was fixed by visible bonds; a relation by desire is real and a different category.

*Mystici Corporis* 22: only those are "really" (*reapse*, lesson's gloss) members who are baptized, profess the true faith, and have neither cut themselves off from the body's unity nor been excluded by legitimate authority for grave faults; those divided in faith or government cannot be living in its unity. §23: not every grave sin cuts a man off; schism, heresy and apostasy do, by their nature. §103: those outside the visible body are "ordered" (*ordinentur*, lesson's gloss) to it by an unconscious desire and longing, yet lack many helps found only within. A Catholic in grave sin who keeps the faith is a member; MC's criteria count who is in the body, not who is holy.

**Level:** **rung 3** (MC). The later grading of belonging (the 1949 letter; LG 14-16) is [Feeney case](#feeney-case) and [*Lumen Gentium* 14-16](#lumen-gentium-14-16).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md)

### One complex reality

The visible society and the Mystical Body are not two Churches but one reality, human and divine.

LG 8: the society furnished with hierarchical organs and the Mystical Body, the visible assembly and the spiritual community, are not two realities but "one complex reality" of a divine and a human element, compared "by no weak analogy" to the Incarnation: as the assumed nature serves the Word, the Church's social structure serves the Spirit of Christ. No real Church hides behind the institution.

**Level:** **at least rung 3**.

**Limits:** no hypostatic union of the Spirit with the Church; the human element includes sinners, so the same section calls the Church holy and always in need of purification ([*semper purificanda*](#semper-purificanda)). The section's next sentence is [*subsistit in*](#subsistit-in).

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md); met in [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md)

### People of God

God saves people not merely one by one but by making them a people: Israel, and now the people gathered in Christ, still on the road.

LG ch. 2 (LG 9-17). God chose Israel as preparation and figure of the new covenant in Christ's blood, which gathers Jew and Gentile into one people in the Spirit; LG 9 quotes 1 Peter 2:10 (DR: "Who in time past were not a people: but are now the people of God"), and Israel was already called "the church of God" (Neh 13:1 [DR 2 Esdras 13:1]). This people has Christ for its head, love for its law, the kingdom for its end. LG 30: what is said of the People of God applies to laity, religious and clergy alike. LG 16: the Jews remain most dear to God for the sake of the fathers (Rom 11:28-29). LG never says that the Church replaces Israel or that Israel's election has lapsed, and defines no theory of the relation ([`new-testament` 5.3](../new-testament/lessons/05-03-romans-5-11-adam-spirit-israel.md); [`apologetics-foundations` 6.4](../apologetics-foundations/lessons/06-04-supersessionism-nostra-aetate-and-the-jewish-people.md)).

**Level:** **at least rung 3**; LG 16 and *Nostra Aetate* 4 rung 3.

**Used alone:** sociology, a "people" in the political sense with sovereignty and parties (Ratzinger's address of 27 February 2000; theology, no level).

**Not:** "People of God means the laity".

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)

### Chapter order of Lumen Gentium

The council argued by layout: what is common to all the baptized comes before the offices.

LG's chapters: 1 the mystery (1-8); 2 the People of God (9-17); 3 the hierarchy (18-29); 4 the laity (30-38); 5 the universal call to holiness (39-42); 6 religious (43-47); 7 the pilgrim Church (48-51); 8 Mary (52-69). The schema sent to the bishops went from the mystery straight to the hierarchy; Cardinal Suenens, presenting it to the Coordinating Commission in 1963, proposed the People of God first, meaning "pastors as well as the faithful" (*Criterion*, Spring 1964). LG 32: one dignity from rebirth, the same filial grace, the same call to perfection, a "true equality"; within that people, ordered by divine institution, some are made pastors by Christ's will.

**Level:** the order is an editorial act, **no rung**; LG 30 and 32 at least rung 3; Suenens's account is testimony. A hierarchy by divine ordinance stays **rung 1** (Trent XXIII can. 6), and LG ch. 3 grounds the episcopate in Christ's institution and consecration.

**Does not assert:** that the hierarchy was demoted, or that offices are delegated from below.

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)

### Communio

Communion is first a gift, union with Father, Son and Spirit, then a bond among those who share it, made visible in faith, sacraments and order.

*Koinonia*: a sharing in something held in common (lesson's gloss); the Douay renders it "communication" (Acts 2:42), "fellowship" (1 John 1:3), "communion" and "partaking" (1 Cor 10:16). Cyprian: "a people united in one in the unity of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit" (*On the Lord's Prayer* 23, ANF), quoted in LG 4. The churches lived it as practice: "No stranger shall be received without letters pacifical" (Synod of Antioch, 341, can. 7, Percival). The 1985 Extraordinary Synod's Final Report (II.C.1) called the ecclesiology of communion "a central and fundamental concept" of the conciliar documents, as quoted in *Christifideles Laici* 19 (1988). The CDF's [*Communionis Notio*](#communionis-notio) (1992) calls it "a key" (§1), gives it a vertical and a horizontal dimension and an invisible and a visible side (§§3-4), makes the visible bonds part of it (§4), and faults ecclesiologies that fail to integrate it with People of God, Body and sacrament (§1).

**Level:** communion's central place weighs **rung 3** (CL, CN); the Synod report is advisory (can. 343).

**Used alone:** an invisible or merely horizontal fellowship, a slogan flattened (Ratzinger, 2000). Its visible bonds are [unity of the Church](#unity-of-the-church); its degrees are [full and partial communion](#full-and-partial-communion).

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md); used in [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md)

## The four marks

Positions and distinctions from Module 2. Each mark is asserted of the Church as such, from her source, and not of every member.

### Four marks

One, holy, catholic, apostolic: four things the Creed says the Church *is*, not four achievements she reports.

The Creed of 381 confesses "one, holy, Catholic and Apostolic Church" (Percival, NPNF second series vol. 14; some texts omit "holy"). Each mark has its entry: [unity](#unity-of-the-church), [holiness](#holiness-of-the-church), [catholicity](#catholicity), [apostolicity](#apostolicity). Vatican I named the Church's visible notes, her "eminent holiness" among them, as signs of credibility (*Dei Filius* ch. 3, chapter text).

**Level:** each mark **rung 1**, an article of the Creed.

**Does not assert:** anything about every member: the members are not each one in communion, holy in conduct, everywhere, or faithful in office.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

### Unity of the Church

The Church is one by gift, in faith, sacraments and communion; division wounds Christians' communion, not the Church's unity.

**Source** (CCC 813, mostly from UR 2): the Trinity as pattern, Christ who reconciled all in one body, the Spirit as the Church's "soul". Cyprian, *On the Unity of the Catholic Church* 23 (ANF 5): "God is one, and Christ is one, and His Church is one, and the faith is one", the people's concord the last link; ch. 7, Christ's seamless coat, "woven from the top throughout" (John 19:23), which UR 13 also uses. Cyprian's own quarrel was over communion, not creed (Novatian was orthodox); the Church kept his premises on unity and not his conclusion that nothing given outside the communion is real ([`patristics` 2.4](../patristics/lessons/02-04-cyprian-of-carthage.md)).

**Bonds.** Charity "above all" (CCC 815; Col 3:14), then three visible bonds: the profession of one apostolic faith; common worship, above all the sacraments; apostolic succession, or in LG 14's words "ecclesiastical government and communion". Can. 205 defines full communion by them. LG 14 adds that full incorporation also needs the Spirit of Christ: one who does not persevere in charity is in the body only "bodily", not in heart, and is not saved. The bonds measure communion; they do not certify holiness.

**Gift and task.** UR 4: the unity Christ gave subsists in the Catholic Church as something she can never lose, and is to grow. "That they all may be one" (John 17:21) asks a unity the world can see: [ecumenism](#unitatis-redintegratio).

**Level:** that the Church is one, **rung 1** (the Creed). That her unity is visible and held by the three bonds, **at least rung 3** (LG 14 restating older teaching; the Catechism and the Code add no weight). The communion-with-Peter's-successor part stands at **rung 1**, by *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3 ([4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md)). UR 3, 4, 15, 22: rung 3. Cyprian: a Father's argument, no magisterial weight.

**Does not assert:** that Christians are already one as Christ willed (UR 3 denies it); that the differences are merely administrative; that anyone is half a Christian; that schism is abolished as a category.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md)

### Schism, heresy, apostasy

Three different breaks, named by can. 751: of part of the faith, of all of it, of communion. None reaches baptism.

Can. 751, paraphrased.

**Heresy:** the obstinate denial or doubt, after baptism, of a truth "to be believed by divine and Catholic faith", that is, a rung-1 truth and nothing lower.

**Apostasy:** the "total repudiation of the Christian faith".

**Schism:** refusal of submission to the pope, or of communion with the members of the Church subject to him. Heresy cuts part of the bond of faith, apostasy all of it, schism the bond of government and communion; schism needs no false doctrine (Novatian held the whole creed). Baptism, the second bond's floor, cannot be undone: a "sacramental bond of unity" (UR 22).

**Acts of a will.** "Obstinate" and "refusal" describe a person, not a position. A position at odds with a defined truth held without that will is what theologians call *material* heresy or schism; the willed version is *formal*. Augustine, Letter 43.1 (about 397, NPNF1 1): those who do not maintain an error "with passionate obstinacy" but received it from misguided parents "are not to be counted heretics". UR 3: those born and raised in faith in Christ in the separated communities cannot be charged with the sin of separation. Categories built for individuals who leave strain on communities apart for centuries, such as the Orthodox, so the texts speak of a separated *condition* (UR 13: communion "dissolved").

**Level:** can. 751 is **law**, not a rung; it borrows heresy's object from rung 1. Material and formal are theologians' terms. UR 3 rung 3.

**Does not assert:** that every non-Catholic Christian is a heretic or schismatic in the canon's sense.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md)

### Full and partial communion

Communion has degrees because its bonds are several; the Church's unity has none.

Full communion is all three visible bonds (LG 14; can. 205). Those who believe in Christ and are truly baptized are in communion with the Catholic Church "even though this communion is imperfect" (UR 3; *Ut Unum Sint* 11 reports the Council's phrase as "a certain, though imperfect communion"). Differences in doctrine, discipline and structure are obstacles, sometimes serious (UR 3). The gifts beyond the visible boundaries "belong by right to the one Church of Christ" (UR 3); the Eastern churches keep true sacraments and, by succession, priesthood and Eucharist (UR 15). The baptized outside full communion are "joined" (LG 15) in real though imperfect communion (DI 17); a body called an "ecclesial community" does not put its baptized outside the Church ([5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)). Ecumenism's task is to turn that real but imperfect communion into full visible unity in faith, sacraments and governance, shown at one altar (UR 4; *Ut Unum Sint* 77): not federation, not absorption ([5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)).

"Partial communion" is this course's shorthand; the texts say imperfect, or not full.

**Level:** rung 3.

**Does not assert:** half-membership; that the differences are merely administrative.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Holiness of the church

The Church is holy because Christ holds her, not because her members are good: holy in her source, means and fruit, spotless only at the end.

The Creed of 381 confesses a holy Church; LG 39 restates it as belief: the Church *indefectibiliter sancta creditur*, is believed to be indefectibly holy.

**Source:** Christ loved the Church as his bride and gave himself up to sanctify her (Eph 5:25-27; LG 39), joined her to himself as his body and gave her the Spirit; the Church does not possess her marks of herself (CCC 811). She is holy as a sanctuary is holy, because of who is present and what is given there.

**Now and not yet:** a holiness real though imperfect (*vera sanctitate licet imperfecta*, LG 48), shown in the fruits of grace, above all the saints (LG 39), complete only in glory, except in Mary, in whom the Church already has that perfection (LG 65). Every member is called, and no member has arrived (LG ch. 5, 39-42; charity its soul, LG 42; [`moral-theology` 1.4](../moral-theology/lessons/01-04-the-new-law.md)). And she holds sinners: [*semper purificanda*](#semper-purificanda).

**Level:** that the Church is holy, **rung 1** (the Creed; LG 39 restates). That her holiness is Christ's gift and that sinners are truly inside her, constant teaching, **at least rung 3** (MC 23; LG 8). LG 40 and 48 at least rung 3.

**Does not assert:** that the members are better people; that a grave sinner has left the Church (MC 23); that being inside is being holy (LG 14: "bodily", not "in his heart"). What ecclesiology must say about grave sin done through the offices is [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)'s.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md)

### The Donatist principle

The Donatists made holiness pass through the minister; Augustine moved its source to Christ.

The Donatists, after Diocletian's persecution, refused communion with bishops consecrated by men who had handed over the Scriptures: a martyrs' Church, with a text, a bride "not having spot or wrinkle" (Eph 5:27). They also kept Cyprian's denial that baptism given outside the Church is baptism. Petilian, Donatist bishop of Cirta, put the principle in one line: what we look for is "the conscience of the giver" to cleanse the recipient's (in Augustine, *Answer to the Letters of Petilian* I.1.2, NPNF1 4, King). Augustine's reply (I.7.8): "The conscience of man is often unknown to me, but I am certain of the mercy of Christ ... My origin is Christ, my root is Christ, my head is Christ." If holiness ran through the minister, no believer could know he had it. Against the Donatist claim of a Church already without spot or wrinkle, he puts that perfection at the end, "till ... 'O death, where is thy sting?'" (Letter 185 §38). The texts as texts: [`patristics` 4.4](../patristics/lessons/04-04-augustine-against-the-donatists.md).

**Level:** the sacramental corollary, that a minister in mortal sin still confers the sacrament, is **rung 1** (Trent, Session VII, *On the Sacraments in General*, can. 12), owned by [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md). Augustine's argument: a Father's, no magisterial level.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md)

### Corpus permixtum

The mixed body: good and bad really inside one visible Church, sorted by the Lord at the harvest and by nobody before it.

The phrase is a later shorthand. Tyconius, a Donatist, titled the second of his seven rules *De Domini corpore bipertito*, the twofold body of the Lord; Augustine calls the name unsuitable and proposes "the true and the mixed body of the Lord" (*vero atque permixto*), or "Concerning the mixed Church" (*De doctrina christiana* III.32.45). His images: the net with good fish and bad (Matt 13:47-48), the cockle among the wheat, "Suffer both to grow until the harvest" (Matt 13:30, DR "cockle").

**What it limits:** who sorts, and why; not discipline: MC 22 counts as outside those excluded by legitimate authority for grave faults. **Where it stops** ([5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)): it explains why sinners, abusers included, are inside; it stops at policy. Transfers, confidential settlements and closed files were acts of governance, not cockle among the wheat. Matthew 13:30 concerns the final sorting, and Augustine never used it against discipline, so citing it to keep an abuser in ministry inverts it.

**Level:** Augustine's theology, **no magisterial level**; that sinners are inside the Church is constant teaching (MC 23, LG 8), at least rung 3.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Semper purificanda

"At once holy and always to be purified": the Church herself, not only her members, needs purifying for as long as this age lasts.

LG 8 sets Christ, who knew no sin, beside the Church, which holds sinners in her bosom and is *sancta simul et semper purificanda*, constantly following the way of penance and renewal. UR 6: Christ calls the pilgrim Church to continual reformation, needed insofar as she is a human, earthly institution: failures of conduct, of discipline, even of how teaching was formulated, as distinct from the deposit. It commits the tradition to three things: the Church is the one purified, so failures cannot all be filed as members' private sins; penance is her permanent condition; and the scope is limited, since the deposit, the sacraments and the Spirit's gifts are not what is purified. *Mystici Corporis* §66 calls the Church spotless in sacraments, faith and laws and says members' falls cannot be laid to her charge; LG 8 does not repeat that second clause.

**Where it stops** ([5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)): it is the furthest-reaching standard answer, because the Church herself is purified; it stops where it stays a phrase: purified of what, by whom, answerable to whom? LG 8 does not say.

**Level:** **at least rung 3** (LG 8, UR 6). ***Ecclesia semper reformanda*** is a slogan of Reformed Protestant theology, in no conciliar text. Whether the Church herself can be called sinful is [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)'s question, freely disputed.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Casta meretrix

Ambrose's "chaste harlot": a Church gathered from sinners and made chaste by Christ, not a patristic verdict that the Church is a sinner.

Ambrose, *Exposition of Luke* III (PL 15, 1681), on Rahab in Christ's genealogy: "a harlot in type but the Church in mystery", who did not refuse union with many strangers, "the more joined to many, the more chaste ... a chaste harlot, a barren widow, a fruitful virgin" (lesson's translation). The "harlotry" is the Church's openness to the Gentiles; the stress falls on *casta* (Giacomo Biffi; H. M. Giudice, 2012, who reports the phrase occurs only once in the Fathers). Balthasar's essay "Casta Meretrix" (in *Sponsa Verbi*; English *Spouse of the Word*, 1991) gathered the Fathers' texts on the Church as harlot under the phrase, and it has since been invoked for a Church of sinners.

**Level:** **no magisterial level**.

**Does not license:** "the Church is a sinner" outright ([5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)).

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md)

### Canonization

The Church's solemn judgment that a named person is in glory and may be venerated: a dogmatic fact, rung 2, never rung 1.

By canonizing, the Church proclaims that a person practised heroic virtue and lived in fidelity to grace, and proposes that person as model and intercessor (CCC 828). The CDF's *Doctrinal Commentary on the Concluding Formula of the Professio fidei* (29 June 1998, §11) lists "the canonizations of saints (dogmatic facts)" among truths held definitively that cannot be declared divinely revealed; its examples are illustrations, not a complete list. Aquinas, *Quodlibet* IX q.8: the judgment stands midway between judgments of faith and of particular fact, "piously to be believed" (*pie credendum*).

**Level:** **rung 2**; **never rung 1**. Whether the canonization formula is itself an *ex cathedra* act is **disputed** among theologians: the commentary fixes the level, not the organ.

**The guarantee is narrow:** the person is in glory and may be venerated by the whole Church; it covers no particular act, opinion or page of the biography (the acts approving Alphonsus made his moral system safe to follow, not the Church's: [`moral-theology` 4.1](../moral-theology/lessons/04-01-casuistry-and-the-probabilism-controversy.md)). Heroic virtue is not sinlessness. Its reach is still discussed.

**Not:** a dogma of faith (overstates); only an honour, like a papal knighthood (understates).

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md)

### Catholicity

Catholic is intensive before it is extensive: whole first, then sent to every people, at home in each and belonging to none.

*Katholikos*, from *kath' holou*, "according to the whole" (lesson's gloss). Like temperature rather than mass: a small community with the whole faith, the whole sacramental life and the apostolic ministry has all of it. CCC 830-831 gives the double sense: (1) wholeness, because Christ is present, the [fullness of the means of salvation](#fullness-of-the-means-of-salvation), so the Church was catholic on the day of Pentecost; (2) mission to the whole human race (Matt 28:19; LG 13). It includes the power to receive every culture without being bound to one: LG 13 (customs taken up insofar as good, purified, strengthened, elevated); GS 58 (bound exclusively to no race, nation or way of life); AG 22 (no syncretism, no false particularism). The [particular churches](#particular-church) are where the one Church exists, not branches of it (LG 23, 26). Division wounds catholicity: UR 4 says divisions keep the Church from realizing the fullness of catholicity proper to her in the separated, and make it harder to express.

**Early witnesses:** Ignatius, about 110, first attested use, "wherever Jesus Christ is, there is the Catholic Church" (*Smyrnaeans* 8): a description, not yet a name ([`patristics` 1.3](../patristics/lessons/01-03-ignatius-of-antioch.md)); Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechetical Lectures* 18.23 (about 348): four "because" clauses, the last with two limbs, only the first about extension (a catechist's witness, on no rung); Vincent of Lérins, about 434, "everywhere, always, by all" ([`fundamental-theology` 5.1](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-01-vincent-of-lerins.md)). The mark was tested before the word existed, when the Jerusalem meeting declined to require circumcision of Gentile converts (Acts 15).

**Level:** the mark **rung 1**; Vatican II's exposition (UR 3-4; LG 13, 23, 26; GS 58; AG 22) **at least rung 3**. A percentage sits on no rung.

**Not:** "catholic means big, or everywhere"; "a figure of speech" (understates).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md)

### Fullness of the means of salvation

The Catholic Church has all the means: whole faith, full sacramental life, ordained ministry in apostolic succession. Having the means is not living by them.

UR 3 and CCC 830: because Christ is present, the Church receives the right and complete confession of faith, full sacramental life, and ordained ministry in apostolic succession. This is bound up with [*subsistit in*](#subsistit-in), whose rung is [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)'s.

**Does not assert:** fullness of realization (UR 4: division keeps the Church from the fullness of catholicity proper to her, and Catholics fail to live by what they have); that Catholics are holier; that others have nothing (UR 3: the Spirit uses the separated communities as means of salvation).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md)

### Inculturation

Anything good may come in; nothing the Gospel excludes, and nothing at the price of communion.

John Paul II, *Redemptoris Missio* (1990) 52-54: a two-way movement, the Gospel transforming a culture's authentic values from within and the culture brought into the Church's communion; two principles, compatibility with the Gospel and communion with the universal Church (54, quoting *Familiaris Consortio* 10); the bishops discern. LG 13: customs taken up insofar as good, purified, strengthened, elevated; AG 22 excludes syncretism and false particularism.

**The case:** Rome forbade the Chinese rites in 1715 (*Ex illa die*) and permitted them in 1939 (*Plane compertum est*), holding that their meaning had become civil: the rule against idolatry never moved, the judgment of what a bow meant did ([`church-history` 7.2](../church-history/lessons/07-02-accommodation-in-asia.md)).

**The strain:** for most of the second millennium the Church's universal forms were largely one culture's; GS 58 says she is bound to none, and the doctrine gives criteria, not the undoing.

**Level:** RM 54 **rung 3**; LG 13, GS 58, AG 22 at least rung 3; the Chinese-rites rulings **disciplinary**, no rung, which is why the second could reverse the first. Liturgical inculturation is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md)'s.

**Not:** "1939 reversed a doctrine"; "Vatican II defined inculturation".

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md)

### Apostolicity

The Church is apostolic in origin, doctrine and succession at once: built on the apostles, keeping their teaching, shepherded through their successors.

An *apostolos* is one sent with a commission (lesson's gloss). CCC 857's three senses: **origin**, built on the foundation of the apostles (Eph 2:20; Rev 21:14); **doctrine**, keeping and handing on their teaching, as the first community persevered in "the doctrine of the apostles" (Acts 2:42), with the teaching office serving the word, not above it (DV 10); **succession**, taught, sanctified and guided through the college of bishops with Peter's successor: [apostolic succession](#apostolic-succession). Not handed on: the apostles' role as chosen witnesses of the Resurrection and foundation stones (CCC 860), and new public revelation (DV 4). The first defence of succession was an argument from **visibility**: Irenaeus's public lists of bishops teaching one rule of faith ([`patristics` 2.1](../patristics/lessons/02-01-irenaeus-against-the-gnostics.md)); Tertullian daring heretics to unroll their roll of bishops (*Prescription* 32, [`patristics` 2.3](../patristics/lessons/02-03-tertullian.md)); behind both, 1 Clement 42 and 44 ([`patristics` 1.2](../patristics/lessons/01-02-first-clement-and-the-didache.md)). The lists were evidence of where the teaching was publicly kept. A diocese erected this year is apostolic in the same three senses as Rome.

**Level:** that the Church is apostolic, **rung 1** (the Creed). A hierarchy of divine ordinance, of bishops, presbyters and ministers, **rung 1** (Trent XXIII can. 6), the defined floor. That bishops succeed to the apostles' place by divine institution, **at least rung 3**. LG 25's denial of individual infallibility, at least rung 3. Other traditions' accounts are in [Other traditions](#other-traditions-in-their-own-words). Other churches' orders (*Apostolicae Curae*) are not judged here.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

### Charisma veritatis certum

Irenaeus's "certain gift of truth", received with the succession: what the gift is remains freely disputed.

Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 4.26.2 (ANF 1): obey the presbyters "who, together with the succession of the episcopate, have received the certain gift of truth". The same sentence calls the same men presbyters and bishops ([`new-testament` 6.1](../new-testament/lessons/06-01-the-disputed-pauline-letters-and-the-pastorals.md)). Two readings: a charism of teaching given to the office-holder, or the truth itself, the deposit handed over with the chair. Irenaeus at once concedes that some who hold the chief seat serve their own lusts (4.26.3), and asks for the apostles' doctrine, the order, and sound speech with blameless conduct together (4.26.4). Succession tells you where to look; it does not certify every holder.

**Level:** the meaning is **freely disputed**; *Dei Verbum* 8 reuses the pairing of episcopal succession and the sure gift of truth but defines no reading of it.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

## The constitution of the Church

Positions and distinctions from Module 3: bishops and succession, the local and the universal Church, the laity, charisms and the *sensus fidei*. The college's supreme power, collegiality and synodality are under [The Petrine office](#the-petrine-office).

### Apostolic succession

The college of bishops succeeds the college of apostles in the office of shepherding: an office passes on, the founding role does not, and holiness and orthodoxy are not guaranteed.

LG 20: the apostles provided for successors so that their mission would outlast them, and the council "teaches" that bishops by divine institution have succeeded to the place of the apostles as shepherds of the Church; its footnote cites Trent, Session XXIII ch. 4 (15 July 1563: bishops, "who have succeeded to the place of the Apostles, principally belong to this hierarchical order", Schaff) and *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3. What passes is an **office**, the mission to teach, sanctify and govern ([three munera](#three-munera)), with the sacramental power of orders (ceded to [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md)); one enters it by sacramental consecration and hierarchical communion (LG 22). Succession exists for the doctrine, and the doctrine is held to be kept through the succession (LG 20). The lists of bishops were public evidence of where the teaching was kept, not the thing itself.

**What it does not claim.** The college succeeds the apostolic college (LG 22); no bishop inherits a particular apostle (Rome's succession to Peter is a separate claim, Module 4). The Nota (§1) calls the parallel a proportion, *pari ratione* and not *eadem ratione*, transmitting none of the apostles' extraordinary power. Their role as chosen witnesses of the Resurrection and foundation stones does not pass on (CCC 860), and public revelation closed with them. Individual bishops are not infallible (LG 25), and consecration does not make a bishop holy: the promise is that the apostolic faith will not fail in the whole Church ([indefectibility](#indefectibility)).

**The hard case.** Presbyters seem to have led Rome for part of the second century; "bishop" and "presbyter" name one office in 1 Clement and the Pastorals; one bishop over presbyters and deacons first appears in Ignatius about 110 ([`church-history` 1.2](../church-history/lessons/01-02-bishops-canon-and-the-parting-of-the-ways.md); Francis Sullivan, *From Apostles to Bishops*, 2001). The doctrine requires that the shepherding office did not lapse and that the episcopate is its legitimate bearer; it requires no date for the one-bishop form. Sullivan holds that a development the Spirit guided and the Church recognized can be "of divine institution" without a blueprint from Christ; whether that gives the phrase its full sense is argued. The record alone neither establishes nor refutes the doctrine. The second-century texts are [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) Boss 1's.

**Level:** bishops succeed the apostles by divine institution (Trent's doctrinal ch. 4, *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3, LG 20): **at least rung 3**, reported widely held to be rung 1 by the ordinary universal magisterium; not to be called defined without showing the act. The **defined floor** is Trent XXIII can. 6, a hierarchy by divine ordination, **rung 1**. LG 25's denial of individual infallibility: at least rung 3.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md)

### Sacramentality of the episcopate

Episcopal consecration confers the fullness of the sacrament of Orders: a bishop is not a priest promoted; a priest shares the bishop's priesthood.

**The dignity view, at its strength.** Peter Lombard's *Sentences* (IV d.24), the schools' textbook, counts seven orders rising to the priesthood, then lists names "not of orders but of dignities or offices" (lesson's gloss of *non ordinum, sed dignitatum vel officiorum*), "bishop" among them. The reason: order as a sacrament is directed to the Eucharist, and a priest consecrates as truly as a bishop. Aquinas, *Supplement* q.40 a.5: "every Order is directed to the sacrament of the Eucharist", so the episcopate is an order as an office, not as a sacrament; the bishop's higher power over the mystical body "has not the nature of a character". Jerome, Letter 146: "what function, excepting ordination, belongs to a bishop that does not also belong to a presbyter?" On this picture governing is jurisdiction, which can be granted: Aquinas let the pope commission a simple priest to confirm (*Super Sent.* IV d.25), and *Mystici Corporis* 42 (1943) said bishops receive ordinary jurisdiction directly from the pope.

**Vatican II.** LG 21 teaches that consecration confers the "fullness of the sacrament of Orders", the "high priesthood" of the liturgy and the Fathers, resting this on the rites of East and West; priests share the bishops' priesthood without its highest degree (LG 28); deacons are ordained for service, not priesthood (LG 29). With sanctifying, consecration confers teaching and governing, exercisable only in [hierarchical communion](#hierarchical-communion). Matter and form were fixed by Pius XII's *Sacramentum Ordinis* (1947); the sacrament itself is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md) [5.1](../sacraments-and-liturgy/lessons/05-01-holy-orders-the-three-degrees.md)'s. Vatican II pulls apart three things the dignity view had fused into two: **sacrament** (what he is), **communion** (with whom he can act), **mission** (where, and over whom).

**Level:** LG 21 is **not a definition** (the notification of 16 November 1964; LG 21 "teaches"): **rung 3**, owed religious submission. Because a council deliberately taught on a disputed question, the dignity view is **no longer free**. Trent XXIII cans. 6-7 (**rung 1**) define the hierarchy and the bishops' superiority with a power to confirm and ordain, **not** the episcopate as a distinct sacramental order. *Mystici Corporis* 42: rung 3, read through the Nota. Lombard's and Aquinas's view: a former school opinion, no magisterial level.

**Not:** "Vatican II defined the sacramentality of the episcopate" (overstates); "the medieval view is still an open option" (understates).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md)

### Three munera

Sanctifying, teaching, governing: conferred on bishops by consecration, shared by all the baptized in their own way.

The threefold scheme is [`christology` 5.1](../christology/lessons/05-01-the-paschal-mystery-and-the-threefold-office.md)'s: adopted in authentic magisterium; "exactly three" not taught.

**In bishops:** all three conferred by episcopal consecration, exercisable by their nature only in hierarchical communion with the head and members of the college (LG 21; can. 375 §2); LG 25-27 treat each. The Nota (§2): consecration gives an "ontological participation" in the *munera*; power *ad actum expedita*, ready to act, needs a canonical determination, an office or an assignment of subjects.

**In the laity:** LG 31 defines the laity as all the faithful except the clergy and the approved religious state; by baptism they share, in their own way, Christ's priestly, prophetic and kingly functions (can. 204 §1, of all the faithful); their proper mark is the secular character, *indoles saecularis* (LG 31). Inside the Church they may take on certain ecclesiastical functions (LG 33) and are permitted, sometimes obliged, to speak on the Church's good (LG 37). "Secular" says where the lay vocation is lived, not that it lives outside the Church; their mission in the world is [`catholic-social-teaching`](../catholic-social-teaching/syllabus.md)'s.

**The contrast:** *Mystici Corporis* 42 (bishops' jurisdiction received directly from the pope), rung 3, now read through the Nota.

**Level:** LG 21 rung 3; the lay *munera* and the secular character at least rung 3, within christology 5.1's grading of the scheme.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md), [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### College of bishops

The bishops with their head and never without him: consecration plus hierarchical communion makes a member.

As Peter and the apostles formed one college, so do the pope and the bishops (LG 22). Membership comes by sacramental consecration and hierarchical communion with head and members (LG 22; can. 336); a diocese is not a condition, so titular, auxiliary and emeritus bishops are members, and a bishop between consecration and canonical possession is a member who cannot yet govern (can. 382). Each bishop governs his own [particular church](#particular-church) and no other; his care for the whole Church is not an act of jurisdiction (LG 23). LG 22 also grants to the college with its head the binding and loosing given to Peter (Matt 16:19 with 18:18), while Simon alone is rock and key-bearer. The college with its head is subject of supreme and full power over the universal Church; the pope's power remains full, supreme, universal and always free (LG 22). It acts solemnly in an ecumenical council, ecumenical only if the pope confirms or at least accepts it, or dispersed, when he calls the bishops to collegial action or approves or freely accepts their united action (can. 337 §§1-2). An Orthodox bishop is consecrated but, lacking hierarchical communion, not a member; his governing power is left to theologians (Nota, N.B.).

**Level:** authentic conciliar magisterium, **rung 3 at its heaviest, not defined** (the Theological Commission's declaration of 16 November 1964; can. 749 §3). Whether this is one subject acting in two ways or two subjects not adequately distinct is **freely disputed**. The college's share in binding and loosing: rung 3 at its heaviest. Collegiality in the strict and wider senses: [collegiality](#collegiality).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md); met in [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Hierarchical communion

The organic, juridically formed communion with the pope and the other bishops without which a bishop's *munera* cannot be exercised.

LG 21: teaching and governing, conferred with consecration, can by their nature be exercised only in hierarchical communion with the head and members of the college; the Nota (§2) explains the wording, chosen "almost unanimously". Consecration plus that communion makes a member of the college (LG 22). The Nota's closing N.B.: without hierarchical communion the *munus* cannot be exercised; whether acts outside it are valid or licit, and the power exercised de facto in the separated Eastern Churches, are left to theologians. An unmandated consecration incurs a *latae sententiae* excommunication reserved to the Apostolic See for consecrator and consecrated (can. 1387, 2021 numbering; can. 1013).

**Level:** LG 21 rung 3; the canons are law.

**Not decided:** what a validly consecrated bishop outside hierarchical communion validly or lawfully does.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md)

### Particular church

The whole Church present in a place: a diocese is not a branch office, a share, or a member of a federation.

A diocese and its equivalents (can. 368): a portion of the People of God entrusted to a bishop, in which the one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church of Christ is truly present and operative (*Christus Dominus* 11; can. 369; of an eparchy, CCEO can. 177 §1). LG 23: particular churches are formed after the model of the universal Church, and *in quibus et ex quibus*, "in and out of" them (lesson's translation), the one Catholic Church exists; John Paul II added the converse (address to the Curia, 20 December 1990), and *Communionis Notio* 9 calls the pair "mutual interiority". LG 26: in every legitimate altar community round its bishop, however small, poor or scattered, Christ is present and the Church gathered; Ignatius said it first, "one Eucharist ... one altar; as there is one bishop" (*Philadelphians* 4). Paul VI warned against a federation of essentially different churches (*Evangelii Nuntiandi* 62, in CCC 835). Each bishop is the visible principle of unity of his church and governs it with power proper, ordinary and immediate (LG 23, 27); bishops are not "vicars of the Roman Pontiffs" (LG 27, citing Pius IX's March 1875 letter to the German bishops).

**"Local church" has no fixed sense:** LG 26 uses it of altar communities, LG 23 of the ancient patriarchal groupings (*Ecclesiae locales*), *Communionis Notio* as a synonym of "particular", and OE 2 calls whole rite-churches *particulares Ecclesias*. One city can hold three particular churches at once, a Latin archdiocese and two Eastern eparchies, each the whole Church present.

**Level:** **at least rung 3** (LG 23, 26; CD 11; restated in both Codes); the federation picture is excluded at that weight. The priority of the universal Church is a separate, freely disputed question: [priority](#priority-of-the-universal-church). A priest apostolic administrator governs in the pope's name (can. 371 §2).

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md); met in [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md)

### Universal Church

The Church confessed in the Creed, the worldwide community of the disciples of the Lord: not the Holy See, not the dioceses added up.

*Communionis Notio* 7: the universal Church is "the worldwide community of the disciples of the Lord". It is not the sum or federation of particular churches (CN 9, quoting John Paul II, 1987), nor the result of their mutual recognition (CN 8, rejecting "ecclesiological unilateralism"). One belongs to it immediately, though entry always happens in a particular church (CN 10). Primacy and college are interior to each particular church (CN 13). The one eucharistic Body makes any particular church's self-sufficiency impossible (CN 11). Ratzinger called Rome itself a local church.

**Level:** CN rung 3, light grade.

**Not:** "the universal Church means the Vatican".

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md)

### Eastern Catholic churches

Churches *sui iuris*, each living a rite, equal in dignity with the Latin Church: not "rites of the Latin Church".

A church *sui iuris* ("of its own law", lesson's gloss) is a body of the faithful joined by its hierarchy and recognized by the supreme authority as *sui iuris* (CCEO, 1990, can. 27). Each lives a **rite**, a liturgical, theological, spiritual and disciplinary patrimony (can. 28), from the Alexandrian, Antiochene, Armenian, Chaldean or Constantinopolitan tradition. Forms: patriarchal (cann. 55-56), major archiepiscopal (can. 151), and others. All are equal in dignity (OE 3); a patriarch with his synod is the highest authority in his patriarchate, saving the pope's right to intervene (OE 9). Recent *Annuario Pontificio* editions list twenty-three Eastern churches *sui iuris*, twenty-four with the Latin Church. The sacramental and liturgical side is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md)'s.

**Level:** the CCEO is law; OE cited, not separately graded.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md)

### Communionis Notio

The CDF's letter of 28 May 1992 on the Church as communion: the universal Church comes first, ontologically and temporally.

The Congregation's act, approved by the pope in the ordinary form. §1: communion "a key", to be integrated with People of God, Body and sacrament. §§3-4: vertical and horizontal, invisible and visible; the visible bonds belong to it. §7: the universal Church, the worldwide community of disciples. §8: rejects "ecclesiological unilateralism", the universal Church as the result of mutual recognition among self-sufficient particular churches. §9: the universal Church, in her essential mystery, is "ontologically and temporally prior" to every particular church (citing Hermas, *Vision* 2.4, and 2 Clement 14), with "mutual interiority": ontologically, because the one Church precedes creation and is the churches' mother; temporally, because she appears at Pentecost already speaking every language. §10: belonging is immediate. §11: the one eucharistic Body excludes self-sufficiency. §13: primacy and college interior to each particular church. §17: the Orthodox churches are particular churches in a "wounded" condition ([5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)).

**Level:** authentic teaching, **rung 3, light grade**; *Apostolos Suos* 12 quoting §9 adds frequency within rung 3, not a higher rung. What the priority formula asserts is [freely disputed](#priority-of-the-universal-church).

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md); met in [1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

### Priority of the universal Church

Does the one Church come before the many churches? Catholics may still argue it; two cardinals did, in print.

**The formula** (CN 9, 1992) was Ratzinger's before it was the Congregation's: he used it in books of 1987 and 1991 and signed the letter as prefect (Kilian McDonnell, *Theological Studies*, 2002). **Kasper**, then bishop of Rottenburg-Stuttgart (1999 Festschrift essay; *Stimmen der Zeit*, 2000; *America*, 23-30 April 2001), grants that no particular church is self-sufficient, that the universal Church is no federation, and that the Church pre-exists in God's eternal plan. He denies that the pre-existent Church is the universal Church apart from the local churches, which with de Lubac he calls an abstraction: the Pentecost church was universal and local in one reality, and the two are simultaneous and indwell each other. His practical fear: once "universal Church" quietly means pope and curia, the formula reverses Vatican II into Roman centralism. **Ratzinger** (*Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung*, 22 December 2000; *America*, 19 November 2001): God's design is one bride and one body; the Fathers carried Israel's pre-existence over to the Church; at Pentecost the Twelve stand for renewed Israel, not a Jerusalem congregation; in baptism one is born into the one Church, not a local group; Rome is itself a local church, so the priority has nothing to do with centralism. Kasper's closing letter (*America*, 26 November 2001) accepted this restatement, a priority of inner unity, and called the rest speculative. Both affirm pre-existence and mutual interiority. Hermas's old woman, the Church "created first of all" and for whose sake "the world was made" (*Vision* 2.4.1), is an allegory about order in God's design, which CN 9 cites; it does not settle which Church it means. A baptism shows the same split: Ratzinger reads it as birth into the one Church, Kasper as entry into a specific, episcopally structured church. The Orthodox accounts: [eucharistic ecclesiology](#orthodox-eucharistic-ecclesiology); the Catholic-Orthodox Munich text (1982) called the two necessarily simultaneous.

**Level:** **freely disputed** (syllabus Note 6). The formula is authentic teaching at rung 3: "dogma" overstates; "one man's opinion" understates. The cardinals' articles carry no magisterial weight.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md); met in [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md)

### Common priesthood

Every baptized person holds a real priesthood, exercised in a whole life and in joining in the Eucharist.

LG 10: by rebirth and the Spirit's anointing the baptized are consecrated as a spiritual house and a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices through the whole of Christian life; by their royal priesthood the faithful join in the offering of the Eucharist. The warrant: "a kingly priesthood" (1 Pet 2:9, DR). It is real, not a figure of speech. How it relates to the ordained priesthood: [essence, not degree](#essence-not-degree).

**Level:** **at least rung 3**.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### Essence not degree

The common and the ministerial priesthood differ in kind, not in amount, and each exists for the other.

LG 10: the two differ *essentia et non gradu tantum*, "in essence and not in degree only" (lesson's translation), yet are ordered to one another, each sharing in its own way in Christ's one priesthood. The ordained priest acts in the person of Christ, teaching and ruling the priestly people and offering the Eucharist in the name of all; the faithful join in that offering. *Christifideles Laici* 22: the ministerial priesthood has the royal priesthood of all the faithful "as its aim". The formula shuts three doors: the **ladder** (ministry a higher grade of the same Christian life) and the **delegate** (the people's priesthood concentrated in one man; Trent XXIII cans. 1 and 7) by "not in degree only"; the **metaphor** (the common priesthood a figure of speech) by "ordered to one another". A conductor is not a better violinist, and an orchestra is not an audience. It distinguishes priesthoods, not holiness: all share one vocation to perfection (LG 32).

**Level:** **at least rung 3**; the rejection of the delegate carries Trent's **rung 1** (a visible priesthood with power to consecrate and absolve; ordination not dependent for validity on the people's call). The sacrament itself is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md)'s. Misread as rank in holiness, it becomes [clericalism](#clericalism). *Vehementer Nos* (1906, §8) called the Church an unequal society whose multitude must "allow themselves to be led"; LG 32 and 37 do not repeat it, and whether they develop or correct it is argued among Catholics.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### Charisms

The Spirit gives gifts to the faithful of every rank; the office judges them and does not issue them.

LG 12 (second paragraph): special graces, extraordinary or simple and widespread, for building up the Church; extraordinary gifts are not to be sought rashly (*temere*), nor fruits presumptuously expected; judging their genuineness and ordered exercise belongs to those who preside, whose task is not to extinguish the Spirit (1 Thess 5:19-21, DR: "Extinguish not the spirit ... prove all things"). AA 3: a charism brings the right and duty to use it, in communion with the pastors. The CDF's *Iuvenescit Ecclesia* (2016): hierarchical and charismatic gifts are "co-essential" (§10, John Paul II's word); recognizing founding charisms is a pastoral duty (§17); eight criteria (§18): the primacy of holiness, evangelization, profession of the Catholic faith, communion with the whole Church, esteem for other charisms, acceptance of trial, spiritual fruits, the social dimension. Extinguishing fails the office as surely as rubber-stamping. Not every charism must become a ministry (Synod Final Document, 2024, §66).

**Level:** LG 12 and AA 3 **at least rung 3**; *Iuvenescit Ecclesia* carries what it restates, and its criteria are norms of discernment.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### Sensus fidei

The faith lives in the whole body, pastors included; a majority is a fact about opinion.

Owned by [`fundamental-theology` 5.3](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-03-the-sensus-fidei.md), which teaches its dispositions and tests. LG 12 places the *sensus fidei* in the whole people "from the bishops down to the last of the lay faithful", a phrase from Augustine, *On the Predestination of the Saints* 14.27 (NPNF1 5), where it describes the reception of the book of Wisdom "by all Christians, from bishops downwards, even to the lowest lay believers". The whole body cannot err *in credendo* when it agrees universally in faith and morals (LG 12). The ITC's *Sensus Fidei* (2014, §§113-118): public opinion belongs to societies built on popular sovereignty, which the Church is not, so the two are never simply identical. The 2024 Final Document (§22) restates LG 12 and denies that the *sensus fidei* is public opinion. In the 1946 consultation on the Assumption, the faithful's sense entered as testimony gathered and judged by the pastors ([6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)).

**Level:** LG 12 on the whole body's inerrancy *in credendo*: **at least rung 3, not defined, not open**; no act manifestly proposes it as revealed (can. 749 §3). The ITC: no magisterial level.

**Not:** a poll, a majority, a rival authority; nor "a pious opinion".

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)

### Reception

The people of God recognizing a teaching as apostolic and making it its own: reception recognizes; it does not vote.

The ITC (*Sensus Fidei* §78): the people of God, guided by the Spirit, recognizing insights as in accord with the apostolic Tradition and integrating them into its life and worship. Yves Congar, "La 'réception' comme réalité ecclésiologique" (1972): a church body taking as its own a decision it did not originate.

**Two limits:** papal definitions are irreformable "of themselves, and not from the consent of the Church" (*Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4; [*ex sese*](#ex-sese-non-ex-consensu-ecclesiae)); conciliar decrees bind once the pope approves, confirms and promulgates them (can. 341 §1).

**The strain:** the council of Ephesus of 449, which Leo called a *latrocinium*, was never received ([`church-history` 2.2](../church-history/lessons/02-02-ephesus-chalcedon-and-leos-rome.md)); but Egypt, much of Syria and Armenia never received Chalcedon either, and Catholic theology does not conclude that Chalcedon was unmade. So non-reception is read with communion with Rome and consonance with the faith; it does not decide alone.

**Level:** the ITC and Congar, **no magisterial level**.

**Not:** ratification.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)

## The Petrine office

Positions and distinctions from Module 4: the texts, the development of the claim, Vatican I, infallibility in exercise, and Vatican II's college. The infallibility machinery (conditions, objects, assent) is [`fundamental-theology` 4.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md)'s, reloaded here.

### Petrine texts

Three sayings to one fisherman: each singles Peter out, gives him something, and sets the gift beside his failure.

- **Matthew 16:17-19** (every "you" singular): the name, the rock, the *ekklesia* the gates of Hades will not overpower, the keys, binding and loosing. Five verses later Peter is "Satan" and a *skandalon*, a stumbling-stone (16:23). See [*Petros* and *petra*](#petros-and-petra), [the keys](#power-of-the-keys).
- **Luke 22:31-32** (DR): "Satan hath desired to have you" (*hymas*, all of you) "... But I have prayed for thee" (*sou*, you alone) "that thy faith fail not: and thou, being once converted, confirm thy brethren" (*sterison*, make firm, lesson's gloss), after a quarrel over greatness settled by "he that is the leader, as he that serveth" (22:26).
- **John 21:15-17:** three questions after three denials (John 18:17, 25, 27), three charges: *boske*, feed (vv. 15, 17), and between them *poimaine*, shepherd (v. 16), the verb Matthew 2:6 uses for the ruler from Bethlehem (DR "rule"); the Douay, after the Vulgate's single *pasce*, says "feed" each time.

**The record.** Peter heads every list of the Twelve ("The first, Simon", Matt 10:2); in Acts he leads the choice of Matthias, preaches at Pentecost, receives Cornelius and speaks first at the council (1:15; 2:14; ch. 10; 15:7). Yet James gives the judgment (15:19), the apostles send Peter to Samaria (8:14), Acts drops him after chapter 15, and Paul's "pillars" are James, Cephas and John (Gal 2:9). At Antioch Paul "withstood him to the face" (Gal 2:11). The New Testament names no successor, says nothing of a bishop of Rome, and records no handing on of his place (ARCIC, 1981: "no explicit record of a transmission of Peter's leadership"). From function to office: [Petrine function and Petrine office](#petrine-function-and-petrine-office).

**The Fathers on "this rock"** (public-domain translations): Tertullian, Peter personally (*On Modesty* 21); Origen, Peter by the letter, every confessing disciple by the spirit (*Comm. Matt.* XII.10-11); Chrysostom, "the faith of his confession", though Christ "makes him a shepherd" (*Hom. Matt.* 54.3); Augustine, Christ, with Peter representing the universal Church (*Tractates on John* 124.5); Leo, Peter named for what Christ is, "Peter's solid rock" (Letter 10.1). Whether these reach Trent's "unanimous consent" is [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) Boss 5's question. Other traditions' readings: [Orthodox](#orthodox-reading-of-the-petrine-texts), [Melanchthon](#melanchthon-on-the-petrine-texts), [Calvin](#calvin-on-the-keys), [Cullmann](#cullmann-on-the-rock).

**Level:** the texts' scriptural reasoning in *Pastor Aeternus* is the act's reasoning, not its definition; what is defined is in [*Pastor Aeternus*](#pastor-aeternus). The authenticity of Matthew 16:17-19 is a historical question, no rung ([`new-testament` 2.2](../new-testament/lessons/02-02-matthew.md)).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Petros and petra

*Petra* is the ordinary word for rock; a man's name needs the masculine *Petros*; the Aramaic *Kepha* stands behind both.

Matthew 16:18: *su ei Petros, kai epi taute te petra*. "Cephas, which is interpreted Peter" (John 1:42); Cephas is Paul's usual name for him (1 Cor 15:5; Gal 2:9, 11; at Gal 1:18 the Greek has *Kephan*, the Douay the Vulgate's "Peter"). Grammar alone does not decide whether the pair is one word or two, and the Lutheran Cullmann read the rock as Peter. The Catechism uses both readings: the rock of the faith Peter confessed (CCC 424), and Peter the unshakable rock because of that faith (CCC 552).

**Level:** the referent of "this rock" is **not defined**: *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 1 defines the primacy, not the exegesis; two Doctors, Chrysostom and Augustine, did not read it as Peter's person alone. Which reading is primary is **freely disputed**, inside the defined primacy.

**Not:** "*Petros* a pebble, *petra* a cliff, so Peter is not the rock"; "the Church has defined that this rock is Peter's person"; "the Fathers disagree, so the primacy is open".

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Power of the keys

The keys are given to Peter alone; binding and loosing is given to Peter and also to the disciples.

Matthew 16:19, "I will give you the keys" (*doso soi tas kleidas*), singular; binding and loosing (*deses*, *luses*) singular there, plural to the disciples in 18:18 (*desete*, *lusete*), which does not mention keys. Many readers hear Isaiah 22:20-22 (DR Isaias): "the key of the house of David" laid on the royal steward Eliacim, "over the house" (4 Kings 18:18 [2 Kings 18:18]), who opens and none shuts; v. 25, the peg falls. Revelation 3:7: Christ holds the key of David. Binding and loosing: forbidding and permitting, or imposing and lifting a ban (lesson's gloss); whether the future perfect "will have been bound" means heaven ratifying or heaven having already decided is argued. LG 22: Simon alone is rock and bearer of the keys; binding and loosing is also granted to the college of apostles with its head (CCC 881). CCC 553: the keys are authority to govern the house of God, entrusted specifically to Peter alone. Melanchthon (*Treatise* §§23-24) gives the keys to the whole Church; Calvin (IV.6.4) makes them the preaching of the Gospel.

**Not:** "Matthew 18:18 gives the keys to all": treating the two as one grant is an argument, not a reading. The college's share: rung 3 at its heaviest.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Petrine function and Petrine office

A function is what Peter does in the New Testament; an office is that function made permanent, held by successors, located in Rome.

The US Lutheran-Roman Catholic dialogue, *Differing Attitudes Toward Papal Primacy* (1973), §4: the "Petrine function", a ministry serving the oneness of the church as a whole; §13: a "trajectory" of images of Peter, not yet primacy "in its later technical sense" (quoted in *The Bishop of Rome*, 2024, §43), drawing on *Peter in the New Testament* (eds. Brown, Donfried, Reumann, 1973). From function to office takes three steps: **perpetuity**, **succession**, **Rome**. The New Testament shows the function; perpetuity only by inference from purpose (a foundation for a Church Hades will not overpower); succession and Rome not at all. *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 2 takes the steps from Christ's institution: what Christ established in Peter for the Church's perpetual welfare must remain "by the same institution", and Peter lives and presides in his successors at Rome. Luke 22:32 shows the function clearly; *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4 applies it to the successors in its preamble, which explains and does not define.

**Level:** the dialogues carry **no magisterial weight**. That Peter has perpetual successors by divine right, and that the Roman pontiff is that successor, is **rung 1** (*Pastor Aeternus* ch. 2 canon), held from Christ's institution as the tradition knows it, not from the page alone.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Development of papal claims

The papal claim grew, sometimes on forged evidence: for every era ask what Rome claimed, what other churches conceded, and what was later defined.

**Three questions, never one.** A claim is not a concession, a concession is not a definition, and 1870 does not certify every earlier claim. Development is the Church's own category for this history: she holds an origin and a core, not that every era's exercise was the core.

| Era | Claimed | Conceded, or not |
|---|---|---|
| c. 96-200 | the Roman church writes to Corinth in the church's name (1 Clement); Victor I tries to cut off Asia over Easter | Roman communion mattered; Irenaeus rebukes Victor; no head of all Christians |
| 440-461, Leo I | care converging on Peter's one seat (Letter 14); a vicar to "share our responsibility, not to take plenary powers" | Chalcedon's canon 28 refused by Leo, kept by the East |
| 595-602, Gregory I | refuses "universal" as taking from his brother bishops, while approving his predecessor's annulment of an Eastern synod by Peter's authority | his letters are not definitions; reading them is [`patristics` 5.3](../patristics/lessons/05-03-gregory-the-great.md)'s |
| 9th c. | forgeries: [Pseudo-Isidore](#forged-decretals), [the Donation](#donation-of-constantine) | no authority of any kind |
| 1075-1302 | the claim at its fullest, temporal power included: [*Dictatus Papae*](#dictatus-papae), Innocent III, [*Unam Sanctam*](#unam-sanctam) | the temporal claims later shed |
| 1415-1516 | a council above the pope claimed ([*Haec Sancta*](#haec-sancta)); the primacy defined ([*Laetentur caeli*](#laetentur-caeli)); the pope over councils (Lateran V) | Florence's definition renewed in 1870; the superiority reading closed |

**Concessions a Catholic account makes** (Schatz, as [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) reports him): no one in 100 or 200 would have called Rome's bishop head of all Christians; the forgeries served claims that were older; the balance of 1870, the bishops, came only at Vatican II. **Newman's notes** ([`fundamental-theology` 5.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md)), run on the trajectory: the type is what LG 18 names, Peter as the permanent, visible source and foundation of the unity of faith and communion; anticipation runs on authentic texts only, thinner than the forgeries made them look; assimilation is where Rome borrowed most, Roman law's heirs, vicars and fullness of power (Leo's limit on a deputy, "not ... plenary powers", became the canonists' *plenitudo potestatis* for the principal); chronic vigour cuts both ways. The notes do not prove the development; they assign the burdens. The 1279 and 1323-1324 Franciscan bulls (*Exiit*, *Cum inter nonnullos*) are not among the uncontested acts: a contradiction would show only that one was not a definition. Brian Tierney (*Origins of Papal Infallibility, 1150-1350*, 1972) argued irreformability was first urged by Olivi to bind future popes to *Exiit*; critics answer that canonists held versions earlier.

**Level:** Newman's notes and Schatz, **no magisterial level**. The question is not whether the 1870 office existed in 200, but whether what grew is growth. The events and the evidence by era are [`church-history`](../church-history/reference.md#roman-primacy-the-evidence-by-era)'s.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md)

### Forged decretals

The Pseudo-Isidorean Decretals: letters forged in the province of Reims, probably in the 840s, put in early popes' mouths, routing bishops' cases to Rome.

Forged by Frankish clergy, not in Rome: enlarging Rome's jurisdiction was their means of protecting bishops from archbishops and kings. Canonists built on them for centuries, and some texts were cited by the Gregorian reformers. Schatz: the forgeries served claims older than themselves.

**Level:** **no authority of any kind**.

**Not:** "the forgeries created the papacy" (they served older claims), nor "they don't matter" (they inflated exactly the early evidence anticipation needs).

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md)

### Donation of Constantine

A forgery, made between about 750 and the mid-ninth century, in which Constantine grants the pope supremacy over all churches and rule of Rome and the West.

First papal use: Leo IX to the patriarch of Constantinople, 1053-54. Doubted by Nicholas of Cusa (1430s); exposed by Lorenzo Valla (1440). Events: [`church-history` 3.3](../church-history/lessons/03-03-popes-franks-and-emperors.md).

**Level:** **no authority**.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md)

### Dictatus Papae

Gregory VII's list of propositions in his register (1075): the claim at its fullest, but a list, not a decree.

Henderson's translation (1910). Proposition 2: "That the Roman pontiff alone can with right be called universal", set against Gregory I's refusal of that title, where the crux is what "universal" means. Others: deposing and reinstating bishops, without a synod (3, 25); the greater cases of every church referred to Rome (21). Events: [`church-history` 4.1](../church-history/lessons/04-01-the-reform-papacy-and-the-investiture-contest.md).

**Level:** a list in the papal register, **no level**. Gregory I's letters on the title are not definitions either.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md)

### Unam Sanctam

Boniface VIII's bull of 18 November 1302: an opening that restates Lateran IV, and a closing sentence whose status is disputed.

**Opening sentence** (lesson's translation): "Urged by faith, we are bound to believe and to hold one holy Church, catholic and also apostolic; and this one we firmly believe and simply confess, outside which there is neither salvation nor remission of sins"; "firmly believe and simply confess" borrows Lateran IV's opening verbs. The bull calls this Church one mystical body with one head, Christ and Christ's vicar, Peter's successor ([*extra ecclesiam*](#extra-ecclesiam-nulla-salus)).

**Closing sentence** (Latin, Denzinger 1911): *Porro subesse Romano Pontifici omni humanae creaturae declaramus, dicimus, definimus et pronuntiamus omnino esse de necessitate salutis*: "Further, we declare, we say, we define and we pronounce that to be subject to the Roman pontiff is, for every human creature, altogether necessary for salvation" (lesson's translation). Four verbs, one of them *definimus*; the sentence never mentions temporal power, kings or swords, which belong to the body (the two swords are [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) Boss 5's).

**Three readings:** a definition narrowly read (Fessler, Vatican I's secretary-general, held only the last sentences infallible; Newman, *Letter to the Duke of Norfolk*, Postscript, follows him: prefaces are not part of what is defined, and *Porro* links two separate judgments); a definition broadly read, including the temporal subjection the body argues (the maximal medieval reading, and Gladstone's); not a definition in the 1870 sense. Later acts narrowed it: Clement V's *Meruit* (1306) declared France subject no differently than before; Lateran V (Session 11, 19 December 1516) renewed and approved the bull, stating the necessity for "all Christ's faithful" in a "since" clause, without prejudice to *Meruit*.

**Level:** the opening carries the weight of the Lateran IV article it restates. The closing sentence, as an act, is **a disputed candidate, not manifestly defined** (can. 749 §3). Read narrowly (the necessity of the Church with her visible head, not a census), its content is taught at **rung 1 by other acts** (the necessity of the Church; *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3). A census reading is not manifestly defined. The temporal subjection of rulers has **no rung** (see [deposing power](#deposing-power)).

**Not:** "*Unam Sanctam* defined that kings are subject to the pope in temporal matters" (two overstatements); "not manifestly defined, so communion with Peter's successor is optional" (understates).

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Laetentur caeli

Florence's decree of union with the Greeks (6 July 1439) defines the Roman primacy: rung 1, renewed at Vatican I.

It defines the primacy of the Apostolic See and the Roman pontiff over the whole world, and his full power, given in Peter, to feed, rule and govern the universal Church, *quemadmodum etiam* ("as also", or "in the way that", lesson's gloss) it is contained in the acts of the councils and the canons. Whether that clause limits or confirms is [`church-history` 4.2](../church-history/lessons/04-02-east-and-west-the-long-estrangement.md)'s close reading. *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3 renews the definition, clause included. The same decree defines, under its own *definimus*, that the purified are received into heaven at once and see God clearly, which fixes that content at rung 1 whatever is said of *Benedictus Deus* (1336) ([4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md)).

**Level:** the primacy definition **rung 1**; the original sense of the clause **disputed**.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md)

### Haec Sancta

Constance's decree of 6 April 1415: the council holds power immediately from Christ, and all, the pope included, must obey it in faith, the schism and reform. Its standing is disputed.

Three readings: a standing rule; an emergency measure for a Church with three claimants; never validly enacted, since only John XXIII's obedience was present. Martin V's approval of what was done *conciliariter* does not clearly reach it. Later acts closed the strong reading: Pius II's *Execrabilis* (1460) forbade appeals from a pope to a future council ([`history-of-political-thought` 2.5](../history-of-political-thought/lessons/02-05-marsilius-and-conciliarism.md)); Lateran V (19 December 1516) gives the pope alone authority over all councils, to convoke, transfer and dissolve them; *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3 calls appealing from the pope to a council as to a higher authority an error. Events: [`church-history` 5.4](../church-history/lessons/05-04-avignon-the-great-schism-and-the-councils.md).

**Level:** standing **disputed, no level**; a council permanently above an undoubted pope cannot stand beside *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3.

**Not:** "dogma", nor "simply void".

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md)

### Pastor Aeternus

Vatican I's First Dogmatic Constitution on the Church of Christ (Session IV, 18 July 1870): a prologue, four chapters, three canons and a definition. Its heaviest claim on daily Church life is chapter 3.

Schaff's public-domain translation throughout.

**Prologue:** Christ set Peter over the apostles "in order that the Episcopate also might be one and undivided", as "the abiding principle of this twofold unity, and its visible foundation": the primacy serves the episcopate's unity.

**Ch. 1:** Christ gave Peter a primacy of jurisdiction "immediately and directly" (Matt 16:16-19; John 21:15-17), not "of honor only", nor given to the Church and through her to Peter "as her minister"; canon.

**Ch. 2:** perpetual successors "by divine right", the Roman pontiff that successor; canon.

**Ch. 3:** [full, supreme, ordinary and immediate jurisdiction](#ordinary-and-immediate-jurisdiction) over every church, pastor and believer, in discipline and government as well as faith and morals; renews Florence; free communication with every flock against any state's *placet*; no appeal to a council; the bishops' own power "asserted, strengthened, and protected"; canon. Its sentence on this jurisdiction, and on the unity of communion and faith with the pope it secures, ends: "This is the teaching of Catholic truth, from which no one can deviate without loss of faith and of salvation".

**Ch. 4:** a history (including "ever free from all blemish of error", and the Spirit "not promised ... that by his revelation they might make known new doctrine"; Luke 22:32 cited in the narrative), then the definition: [conditions](#conditions-for-papal-infallibility), [*ex sese*](#ex-sese-non-ex-consensu-ecclesiae).

**History.** Cut from a schema on the Church distributed on 21 January 1870, whose papal chapters were taken first on 29 April. On 13 July, 88 voted *non placet* and 62 *placet iuxta modum*; some 55 to 60 then renewed their votes by letter and went home; on 18 July it passed 533 to 2. None finally refused the decrees, though some came slowly (Strossmayer of Đakovo among the slowest). The rest of the schema, the bishops included, never reached a vote; Kleutgen's further draft, *Tametsi Deus*, stayed unfinished; Pius IX suspended the council on 20 October 1870 (*Postquam Dei munere*). The council defined the head and never reached the body; the "First" Constitution waited for *Lumen Gentium*. Votes and minority: [`church-history` 8.2](../church-history/lessons/08-02-pius-ix-vatican-i-and-the-fall-of-rome.md).

**Level:** **rung 1, exactly what the three canons and the definition state**: Peter's primacy of jurisdiction; its perpetuity in the Roman pontiffs; their full, supreme, ordinary and immediate jurisdiction; infallibility under the four conditions, irreformable *ex sese*. Ch. 3's note of faith makes **communion with Peter's successor a rung-1 bond of the Church's unity** (answering [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md)'s open question).

**Taught, not defined:** the prologue (the founding is rung 1 by another route), the histories, the patristic citations, "ever free from all blemish of error", the chapters' exegesis. Ch. 3's protection of the bishops: **at least rung 3** (restated LG 27); their divine institution is rung 1 at Trent XXIII can. 6.

**Not:** "ch. 4 is the dogma and the rest is discipline" (understates).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md); met in [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Conditions for papal infallibility

Four conditions, all required: infallibility attaches to acts that meet them, not to every papal word.

*Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4 (DH 3074), as [`fundamental-theology` 4.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md) counts them: the pope (1) speaks in discharge of the office of pastor and doctor of all Christians, (2) by his supreme apostolic authority, (3) defines a doctrine of faith or morals, (4) to be held by the universal Church. He then possesses "that infallibility with which the divine Redeemer willed that his Church should be endowed", and his definitions are irreformable "of themselves, and not from the consent of the Church" (Schaff). The same chapter's history denies that popes receive new revelation, and ch. 3 protects the bishops' own power, so the text defines neither that whatever a pope teaches is infallible, nor new revelation, nor bishops as his delegates.

**Level:** **rung 1**. Which acts meet the conditions: [*ex cathedra*](#ex-cathedra). The bishops' share: [ordinary universal magisterium](#ordinary-universal-magisterium).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md)

### Ex cathedra

A papal act meeting ch. 4's four conditions. Two acts are uncontested; every other is a candidate.

*Ineffabilis Deus* (1854) and *Munificentissimus Deus* (1950) are "the two that nobody contests", one before the council and one after; no magisterial list goes further. Candidates: Benedict XII's *Benedictus Deus* (1336, "by apostolic authority we define"; its content is rung 1 anyway, by Florence's route); the closing sentence of [*Unam Sanctam*](#unam-sanctam), which Fessler counted. The lists differ because the test dates from 1870, and applying it to a medieval bull means judging that bull's address and intention. Can. 749 §3 resolves every such doubt downward. Invented acts fail it readily: a Wednesday-audience catechesis fails defining and supreme authority; a letter to one nation fails the universal Church; a "definition" of the universe's age fails faith or morals. Whether the canonization formula is *ex cathedra* is disputed ([canonization](#canonization)).

**Level:** the conditions rung 1; 1854 and 1950 rung 1, *ex cathedra*; **no act settles whether any other papal act is *ex cathedra***. Say "two, plus candidates", never a number.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md)

### Ex sese non ex consensu Ecclesiae

"Irreformable of themselves, and not from the consent of the Church": the consent excluded is juridical ratification, not the Church's agreement or consultation.

*Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4; Latin *ex sese, non autem ex consensu Ecclesiae*.

**Target:** the fourth Gallican Article of 1682: in questions of faith the pope's decrees reach all the churches, "yet his judgment is not irreformable unless the consent of the Church is added" (lesson's translation); annulled by Alexander VIII's *Inter multiplices* (1690). Ch. 4 inverts that clause almost word for word.

**What it leaves standing:** ch. 4's own history records popes defining after councils and after "asking for the mind of the Church scattered throughout the world"; a pope should consult, and popes did.

**The minority's request:** infallibility tied to the witness of the churches; Cardinal Guidi of Bologna proposed that the pope define with the counsel of the bishops manifesting their churches' tradition. The majority refused to write any such step in as a *condition* of a definition's force. Bishop Vinzenz Gasser of Brixen explained ch. 4 for the drafting committee before the 13 July vote; drawing on his *relatio*, the US Lutheran-Catholic dialogue (1978, quoted in *The Bishop of Rome* §§69-70) reads "consent" juridically, as an official approval giving force.

**Level:** the clause is part of the **rung-1** definition; Gasser's explanation is weighty evidence of the sense voted on, **no magisterial level**. Reception confirms, never ratifies ([reception](#reception)).

**Not:** "*ex sese* means apart from the Church": the infallibility is the Church's.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md)

### Ordinary and immediate jurisdiction

Two powers, both ordinary and immediate, over one flock: the pope's and the bishop's. The text gives no rule for how often the first is used.

*Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3 and its canon: the Roman pontiff's power over all churches, pastors and faithful, singly and together, is full, supreme, ordinary, immediate and "truly episcopal", in discipline and government as well as faith and morals; the canon condemns "inspection or direction" only, or "merely the principal part". The same chapter: this power does not prejudice but asserts, strengthens and protects the bishops' own ordinary and immediate power. The dialogues, drawing on Bishop Zinelli's explanations for the drafting committee, gloss **ordinary** as belonging to an office, not delegated, and **immediate** as reaching subjects without an intermediary (ARCIC 1981; Farfa Sabina Group; *The Bishop of Rome* §67).

**Clean case:** Rome overturning a bishop's parish closure on recourse acts by its own office, and the bishop is not thereby its vicar.

**Strain case:** in 1801 Pius VII asked every sitting French bishop to resign for the Concordat and replaced those who refused ([`church-history` 8.1](../church-history/lessons/08-01-enlightenment-revolution-and-napoleon.md)): ch. 3 licenses it; the 1875 reading survives it; nothing says when acts against each bishop become an act against the order.

**Level:** the jurisdiction **rung 1**; the bishops' protected power **at least rung 3** (LG 27); the dialogues' gloss, **no magisterial level**.

**Not:** "the pope is every diocese's real bishop" (overstates).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md)

### German bishops declaration of 1875

The first authoritative reading of 1870: the bishops govern by their own divine right, and Pius IX approved that reading as the council's sense.

Bismarck's circular note of 14 May 1872, on the next papal election, argued that the Vatican decrees had merged episcopal jurisdiction into papal. The German bishops' joint declaration (early 1875; DH 3112-3114), as the CDF's *The Primacy of the Successor of Peter in the Mystery of the Church* (1998, §§5, 7) and *The Bishop of Rome* §65 summarize it: primacy and episcopate are in harmony; the episcopate rests on the same divine institution as the papacy, so the primacy does not reduce the bishops' ordinary authority; the primacy is bounded by divine law and the Church's divinely given constitution; infallibility reaches exactly as far as the Church's infallible magisterium, bound to Scripture, tradition and earlier definitions. Pius IX approved it as the council's authentic sense in a March 1875 letter to the German bishops (LG 27's note dates it 12 March) and a consistorial allocution of 15 March 1875; LG 27 cites both (DH 3112-3117) for bishops not being "vicars of the Roman Pontiffs".

**Level:** **rung 3**: a national episcopate's reading made an authentic papal interpretation by Pius IX's approval. It **cannot narrow or widen the definition**. No earthly tribunal enforces the limits it names.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md); met in [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md)

### Ordinary universal magisterium

The bishops dispersed through the world, in communion with one another and with Peter's successor, agreeing on one judgment as definitively to be held: most infallible teaching comes this way.

LG 25 (paraphrased); can. 749 §2. Named in Pius IX's letter *Tuas libenter* (21 December 1863, to the archbishop of Munich: the submission of faith reaches beyond express definitions to what "the ordinary magisterium of the whole Church dispersed throughout the world" hands on as revealed, lesson's translation); adopted by *Dei Filius* ch. 3 (1870; DH 3011, Schaff): "all those things are to be believed with divine and Catholic faith ... which the Church, either by a solemn judgment, or by her ordinary and universal magisterium, proposes for belief as having been divinely revealed". Two routes to one rung. Examples held this way: Scripture's freedom from error; the grave wrong of directly killing the innocent.

**The burden is on whoever invokes it:** the bishops must agree in proposing a teaching *as definitive*, and that must be manifestly evident. Three outcomes in the library: a papal act confirms it (*Evangelium Vitae* 57, 1995, rung 1, "confirm", not "define"); a dicastery answers a doubt (the CDF's 1995 response on *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis*: at least rung 2, rung 1 against rung 2 disputed); no act settles it (*Humanae Vitae*'s norm, floor rung 3, its infallibility freely disputed, Ford-Grisez against Sullivan).

**Marian cases:** [perpetual virginity](#perpetual-virginity) is rung 1 by this route with no defining act, and the condition is the episcopate's agreement, not every earlier writer's (Tertullian strains Vincent's "always", not the rung); [*Munificentissimus Deus*](#munificentissimus-deus) 12 reads the 1946 replies as this magisterium agreeing with the faith of the people, so on the bull's own account the Assumption was infallibly taught before it was defined.

**Level:** that the dispersed bishops can teach infallibly is **rung 1** (two councils teach it; *Mysterium Ecclesiae* 3 calls *Dei Filius*'s sentence a definition). *Tuas libenter*: authentic teaching. A claim of this route with no act behind it: **no rung by mere assertion**.

**Not:** "never defined, so not infallible" (papal infallibility itself was held as definitive before 1870); "every bishop teaches it, so it is infallible" (overstates twice). A national conference calling something "the constant faith" is a particular act.

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md), [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Primary and secondary object

Infallibility covers revealed truth, to be believed, and what is needed to guard and expound it, to be held. Only the first is defined.

**Primary object:** revealed truth, believed with divine and Catholic faith.

**Secondary object:** what is required to guard and expound the deposit, held definitively. *Mysterium Ecclesiae* (CDF, 1973) §3 teaches, as "Catholic doctrine", that infallibility covers both, and claims revealed status only for the first. The CDF's 1998 commentary on the *Professio fidei* (§11) gives examples. To be believed: inerrancy, the grave wrong of directly killing the innocent, papal primacy and infallibility, immediate recompense after death. To be held: the illicitness of euthanasia and of fornication, dogmatic facts (canonizations among them). Papal infallibility was held as definitive before 1870, when Vatican I defined it as revealed: a definition can move a truth from "held" to "believed". The table is [`fundamental-theology` 4.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md)'s.

**Level:** that infallibility reaches the secondary object is **taught, not defined**; marking the extension rung 1 overstates.

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md)

### Canon 749

The Code's rule of interpretation: no doctrine is understood as infallibly defined unless that is manifestly evident.

Can. 749 (1983): §1 the pope's infallibility *ex cathedra*; §2 the college of bishops infallible in an ecumenical council or dispersed, on LG 25's conditions; §3 no doctrine is understood as infallibly defined unless this is manifestly evident. §3 is a rule of interpretation, not a doctrine: any claim that a teaching is infallible must be shown, and when it is not, default down. It decides *Unam Sanctam*'s closing sentence, *Cantate Domino*'s census reading, candidate *ex cathedra* acts, indefectibility and LG 12 in this course.

**Level:** law; owned by [`fundamental-theology` 4.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md); applied throughout

### Deposing power

The claimed papal right to depose rulers and release subjects from allegiance: never defined, taught by no act today, infallibility excluded.

Exercised in *Regnans in Excelsis* (Pius V, 25 February 1570), which excommunicated Elizabeth I, declared her deposed and released her subjects ([`church-history` 6.3](../church-history/lessons/06-03-the-english-reformations.md)). Newman (*Letter to the Duke of Norfolk*, 1875, §3) cites Pius IX's reply to a Roman academy: the deposing power was a right of the "ages of faith", resting on public law and the common consent of peoples, and to call a pope infallible in exercising it is malicious. Newman: "not necessary for any Catholic to acknowledge".

**Level:** never defined; **default down**. A sentence on a ruler is jurisdiction, not doctrine defined.

**Not:** "Pius V deposed Elizabeth, so Catholics must hold that popes can depose rulers".

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md)

### Honorius

A pope anathematized by name by an ecumenical council, and a pope confirmed it: the hardest case in the record, and no counter-example to what 1870 defined.

Honorius, pope 625-638, wrote to Sergius of Constantinople. At Session XIII of Constantinople III (681) the council judged his letter "foreign to the apostolic dogmas" and decreed that Honorius, "some time Pope of Old Rome", be expelled from the Church and anathematized (Percival); Session XVI acclaimed it. Leo II confirmed the council: in the Latin Percival prints, Honorius "tried by profane betrayal to subvert the spotless faith" (lesson's translation); a milder variant says he *permitted* it to be stained, and which is original is disputed ([`christology` 2.5](../christology/lessons/02-05-monothelitism-and-constantinople-iii.md)). The papal profession of the *Liber Diurnus* repeated it. Hefele pressed the case at Vatican I. Run through ch. 4, the letter fails every condition: one patriarch answered, not all Christians; Newman finds the letters "written almost as private instructions" (Norfolk §8); a discussion, not a definition; not addressed to the whole Church. "At the utmost it only decides that Honorius in his own person was a heretic" (Newman).

**What it does touch:** papal teaching outside the conditions is unprotected, and not hypothetically; and it presses on ch. 4's explanatory sentence that the see has remained "ever free from all blemish of error", which is not the definition. Baronius's forgery theory is rejected (Percival, citing Hefele); "the council erred" is a minority view against almost all theologians.

**Level:** the anathema is a historical fact, **not a level**; that the letters were not *ex cathedra* is the common judgment of theologians since 1870, not a magisterial act; Constantinople III's two wills rung 1.

**Not:** "Honorius wrote privately, so the case is closed" (understates the hard fact).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md)

### Collegiality

The bishops as a body with their head: strictly, only acts of the whole college; in a wider sense, the collegial spirit.

**Effective** (strict) collegiality: acts of the whole [college](#college-of-bishops) with its head, in council or dispersed. **Affective** collegiality: the collegial spirit, which LG 23 commends episcopal bodies for putting into practice ("collegiate feeling") and *Apostolos Suos* (1998) names *affectus collegialis*, expressed in [episcopal conferences](#episcopal-conferences) and the [Synod of Bishops](#synod-of-bishops), whose joint action never takes on the collegial character proper to the order of bishops. Collegiality is synodality's "some" ([synodality](#synodality)). The second holder of supreme power contains the first: the college is never the bishops minus the pope, and the real distinction, says the Nota, is between the pope alone and the pope with the bishops. *Lumen Gentium*'s own promulgation is the clean case: the pope with the Fathers approves and decrees; without his confirmation, 2,151 votes would be no act of the college.

**Level:** LG 22-23 **rung 3 at its heaviest, not defined**.

**Not:** "collegiality binds the pope to the bishops' majority"; "LG 22 defined collegiality" (overstates); "Vatican II was pastoral, so collegiality is optional" (understates).

*Lessons:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Nota explicativa praevia

The note appended to LG ch. 3, communicated "from higher authority" on 16 November 1964: the key by which ch. 3 is to be read. Not voted.

Given to the Fathers by the Secretary General in the third session's most contentious week; *Lumen Gentium* passed five days later, 21 November 1964, 2,151 to 5 ([`church-history` 9.2](../church-history/lessons/09-02-the-second-vatican-council.md)). **§1:** "college" is not a juridical body of equals delegating to a president; the Peter-apostles, pope-bishops parallel is a proportion (*pari ratione*, not *eadem ratione*), transmitting none of the apostles' extraordinary power. **§2:** consecration gives an "ontological participation" in the *munera*; power *ad actum expedita* needs a canonical determination by hierarchical authority, an office or an assignment of subjects; recent popes' statements on bishops' jurisdiction are to be read in those terms. **§3:** the distinction is the pope alone versus the pope with the bishops; whether to act personally or collegially is his judgment; the college's "also" is admitted so that the pope's fullness of power is not called into question. **§4:** he can always exercise his power at will; the college acts strictly only from time to time and only with its head's consent ("consent" excludes dependence on an outsider).

**N.B.:** without hierarchical communion the *munus* cannot be exercised; validity, liceity and the power exercised de facto in the separated Eastern Churches are left to theologians.

**Level:** **no rung of its own**: ch. 3's authorized key, it weighs what ch. 3 weighs.

**Not:** a conciliarist reading; a pope as the college's chairman.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Synod of Bishops

A body that counsels the pope: it decides only if he grants it deliberative power, and its documents teach only if he approves them.

Created by Paul VI, *Apostolica Sollicitudo* (15 September 1965). It counsels; it decides only if the pope grants deliberative power, and then he ratifies (can. 343). Under Francis's *Episcopalis Communio* (2018) art. 18, the pope decides whether to publish the final document, which shares in his ordinary magisterium only if he expressly approves it (or, with deliberative power, ratifies and promulgates it). The 2024 Final Document was weighed on that rule ([synodality](#synodality)).

**Level:** **ecclesiastical law**, changeable; not an act of the college. A Synod majority advises.

*Lessons:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Episcopal conferences

A territory's bishops acting jointly: real, the collegial spirit applied, never the college; their doctrinal declarations bind only at home, and only on strict conditions.

LG 23: such bodies can help put the collegial spirit into practice. John Paul II, *Apostolos Suos* (21 May 1998) §12: the joint action of a territory's bishops is a concrete application of the collegial spirit (*affectus collegialis*), never the collegial nature of the whole order. Complementary norms, art. 1: a conference's doctrinal declaration is its authentic magisterium only if approved unanimously by the member bishops, or by at least two-thirds of the bishops belonging to the conference with a deliberative vote and then given the Holy See's *recognitio*; even then it is particular magisterium, binding only its own territory ([`fundamental-theology` 4.1](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-01-the-magisterium.md)). The ancient patriarchates are the other intermediate structure (LG 23; OE 9).

**Level:** the norms are **law**, changeable; *Apostolos Suos*'s account of conferences is papal teaching, **rung 3**. A conference is not the ordinary universal magisterium.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Synodality

The whole People of God walking together, with college and pope inside it: all, some, one. All are heard; the pastor decides and owes reasons.

**The concept** ([4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)). Not a word of *Lumen Gentium*. It entered working vocabulary through Francis's address of 17 October 2015, for the Synod's fiftieth anniversary, and the ITC's *Synodality in the Life and Mission of the Church* (2018), whose §64 compresses it to *all* (the *sensus fidei* of the faithful), *some* (the college of bishops), *one* (the bishop of Rome's ministry of unity). It adds the consultation of the whole people and subtracts nothing from collegiality or primacy.

**The procedure** ([3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md)). The synod of 2021-2024: launched October 2021; diocesan and eparchial consultation, national and continental stages, an assembly in two sessions (October 2023, October 2024), with lay and consecrated men and women, deacons and priests as members (*Episcopalis Communio* art. 2 §2). On 26 October 2024 the assembly approved the Final Document, every part by over two-thirds, and Francis signed it that day without an exhortation. §22: the *sensus fidei* is not public opinion. §66: not every charism must become a ministry. §88: the triple "nothing without" (Ignatius, Cyprian). §90: joint decision-*making* versus decision-*taking* by the competent authority. §91: no departure from an agreed result without a compelling, explained reason. §92: decision-taking authority is inviolable but may not ignore a properly discerned direction; it proposes revising the Code's "merely consultative".

**Level:** ITC documents, **no magisterial level**. The Final Document, by Francis's Accompanying Note (24 November 2024), is part of the ordinary papal magisterium (EC 18 §1): **rung 3 at most** for its own teaching, restated doctrine at its own weight, proposals "not strictly normative" yet committing. It is **not** the ordinary universal magisterium of LG 25.

**Not:** "Francis's signature made it infallible"; "'not strictly normative' made it optional".

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Ut Unum Sint

John Paul II's encyclical on ecumenism (25 May 1995): the primacy's essentials kept, the form of its exercise opened to dialogue.

§§95-96: heeding other Christians' requests, the pope seeks a way of exercising the primacy that renounces nothing essential to its mission yet is open to a new situation, and invites their leaders and theologians into patient, fraternal dialogue on it. The distinction doing the work: the office's essentials against the form of its exercise. Elsewhere: the Church committed "irrevocably" to ecumenism (§3); in matters of faith, compromise contradicts God who is Truth (§18); full visible unity among all the baptized is the goal (§77).

**The follow-up:** the Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity's study document *The Bishop of Rome* (presented 13 June 2024, published with Francis's agreement) synthesizes the responses and dialogues; it proposes a Catholic re-reception or official interpretation of Vatican I in the light of Vatican II, and a clearer distinction between the pope's patriarchal ministry in the Latin Church and his ministry of unity, a reflection it ties to the 2006 removal of "Patriarch of the West" from the *Annuario Pontificio*.

**Level:** *Ut Unum Sint* **rung 3** for its doctrine; its invitation is a request, not a teaching. *The Bishop of Rome* says of itself that it does not summarize the magisterium: **below the seam**.

**Not:** "the whole primacy put up for negotiation"; "*The Bishop of Rome* is new teaching".

*Lessons:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md); placed in ecumenism by [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Ravenna document

The Catholic-Orthodox commission's text of 13 October 2007: primacy and conciliarity at every level, agreement on the fact of a universal primacy, disagreement on its exercise and foundations.

The Joint International Commission finds primacy and conciliarity at three levels, local, regional and universal, with a *protos* at each; it takes Apostolic Canon 34 (35 in Percival's count) as the regional pattern (§24): the bishops of every nation must "do nothing of consequence" without their first, "but neither let him ... do anything without the consent of all", a symmetry that Vatican II keeps in its first clause at the universal level and does not make the second a juridical condition of the pope's acts. Primacy and conciliarity are interdependent (§43). The fact of universal primacy is accepted by East and West, with differences on its manner of exercise and on its scriptural and theological foundations (§43); the role of the bishop of Rome, and the reading of Vatican I and II in the light of the first millennium, are left for further study (§45). Moscow's delegation was absent, having walked out over an intra-Orthodox dispute. The Moscow Patriarchate's reply: [Moscow on primacy](#moscow-on-primacy).

**Level:** by its own preface no official declaration of the Church's teaching: **below the seam**.

**Not:** "Ravenna settled primacy".

*Lessons:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

## Salvation, other Christians, and sin

Positions and distinctions from Module 5: the necessity of the Church, where she is, what ecumenism requires, and a Church of sinners.

### Extra ecclesiam nulla salus

"Outside the Church no salvation" predicates necessity of the Church as the means Christ joined to himself; it is not a census of the saved.

Put positively: all salvation comes from Christ the Head through the Church, which is his Body (CCC 846); compare Acts 4:12, which says what salvation comes *through* without listing the saved. The historic formulas did draw lines, and each named a social body. Track two variables: **what "the Church" names**, and **how a person comes to be outside**.

| Formula | "The Church" names | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Cyprian, Letter to Jubaianus (c. 256; ANF Ep. 72.21-23 = Hartel 73) | the one visible communion of the bishops, against rival communions | [baptism of desire](#baptism-of-desire) |
| Lateran IV (1215) | one universal Church of the faithful, offering Christ's sacrifice | [*Firmiter*](#firmiter) |
| *Unam Sanctam*, opening (1302) | one body with one head, Christ and his vicar | [*Unam Sanctam*](#unam-sanctam) |
| Florence (1442) | the Catholic Church | [*Cantate Domino*](#cantate-domino) |
| Pius IX (1854, 1863) | the Apostolic Roman Church, with invincible ignorance carrying no guilt | [invincible ignorance](#invincible-ignorance) |
| Holy Office (1949) | *Mystici Corporis*'s Body, joined as member or by desire | [Feeney case](#feeney-case) |
| LG 14 (1964) | the Catholic Church, with exclusion only for knowing refusal | [*Lumen Gentium* 14-16](#lumen-gentium-14-16) |

**What moved.** The necessity never did. For centuries the formulas presumed that whoever was outside had put himself there by an act of will (heresy, schism), though Cyprian already counted in the catechumen killed for Christ before baptism; from 1854 the presumption is tested; from 1949 belonging is graded. The first targets were baptized Christians in rival communions, not non-Christians. The outward question, toward those who never heard, is argued in [`apologetics-foundations` 8.2](../apologetics-foundations/lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md).

**Level:** the necessity of the Church, **rung 1** (Lateran IV professes it, Florence's core teaches it, LG 14 restates it).

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Firmiter

Lateran IV's first constitution (1215), against the Cathars: one universal Church of the faithful, outside which no one at all is saved.

"There is indeed one universal Church of the faithful, outside which no one at all is saved, in which the same Jesus Christ is himself both priest and sacrifice" (lesson's translation of *Una vero est fidelium universalis Ecclesia, extra quam nullus omnino salvatur*). The sentence runs straight on to the altar, transubstantiation and the priest rightly ordained; the Cathars' rival church is denied the name. *Unam Sanctam* later borrows its opening verbs ("firmly believe and simply confess").

**Level:** the necessity clause **rung 1**. Cite by name; the lessons give no DH number.

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Cantate Domino

Florence's bull of union with the Copts (session 11, 4 February 1442): no exception for ignorance, and its fathers meant what they wrote.

As the faith of the Roman Church: pagans, Jews, heretics and schismatics who die outside the Catholic Church go into the eternal fire unless joined to her before the end of life; the sacraments, fasts and alms profit only those who remain in her unity; "No one, however much alms he has given, even if he has shed his blood for the name of Christ, can be saved unless he has remained in the bosom and unity of the Catholic Church" (lesson's translation). The passage follows, nearly clause for clause, two rules of Fulgentius of Ruspe (*De fide ad Petrum* 38.79-39.80), where the alms-and-blood clause concerns *baptized* heretics and schismatics.

**Level** ([`apologetics-foundations` 8.2](../apologetics-foundations/lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)'s grading, kept): the core, the necessity of the Church, **rung 1**; the census reading, that everyone in the named groups who dies outside is lost, **not manifestly defined**: the bull has no "we define" and no anathema (can. 749 §3).

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Invincible ignorance

Ignorance of the true religion that is invincible carries no guilt before God: from 1854 the presumption that the outsider put himself there is tested, not assumed.

Pius IX, *Singulari quadam* (allocution, 9 December 1854): it must be held from faith that no one is saved outside the Apostolic Roman Church, the one ark, and held as equally certain that ignorance of the true religion, if invincible, carries no guilt for this before God; and who could mark the limits of such ignorance? *Quanto conficiamur moerore* (encyclical, 10 August 1863): such people can reach eternal life by divine light and grace.

**Aquinas before them:** pre-Christian gentiles without revelation had implicit faith in a Mediator through believing in providence, but after grace was revealed all are bound to explicit faith (ST II-II q.2 a.7 corpus and ad 3); for someone raised with no contact with the Gospel who follows natural reason, "it is most certainly to be held" that God will reveal by an inner inspiration or send a preacher, as he sent Peter to Cornelius (*De veritate* q.14 a.11 ad 1).

**Level:** *Singulari quadam* **rung 3 at its lightest grade**; *Quanto conficiamur* **rung 3**. How grace reaches the unevangelized: **rung 5**, freely disputed.

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Feeney case

The Holy Office's letter to Archbishop Cushing (8 August 1949): one must be joined to the Church actually as a member, or at least by desire, explicit or implicit, animated by faith and charity.

The St Benedict Center controversy in Boston. The letter (plenary 27 July; approved by Pius XII 28 July; dated 8 August; published 1952): the Church is necessary twice over, because Christ *commands* entry and because she is a *means* of salvation by divine institution, not intrinsic necessity; and, as Trent shows of baptism and penance, such a means can work through desire. So one must be joined to her actually as a member, or at least by desire: explicit in catechumens, possibly implicit in the invincibly ignorant, but always animated by perfect charity and supernatural faith. It cites *Mystici Corporis* 103 on those related to the Body by an unconscious desire. Then it turns on the rigorists: Catholics who have heard Rome and still refuse cannot plead ignorance, and to them the necessity applies without restriction. Leonard Feeney was excommunicated on 13 February 1953 for disobedience, and reconciled in 1972.

**Level:** **rung 3, light**; its weight was raised when LG 16 footnoted it.

**Not:** "the 1949 letter loosened the axiom for Catholics" (it tightened it for them); "a 1949 invention" (see [baptism of desire](#baptism-of-desire)).

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Lumen Gentium 14-16

Three relations to the Church, not one line with insiders and outsiders: incorporated, joined, ordered.

**LG 14:** the Church is necessary for salvation and is entered through baptism as through a door. Those are *fully incorporated* who have the Spirit of Christ, accept her whole ordering and means of salvation, and are joined in her visible structure by faith, sacraments, government and communion. One who does not persevere in charity remains "in body" but not "in heart", and is not saved. Catechumens are joined by their desire. The clause: those could not be saved who refuse to enter or to remain in the Catholic Church while *non ignorantes*, "not unaware" (lesson's translation), that God founded her through Christ as necessary: knowledge and refusal are both required, and the clause names no one.

**LG 15:** the Church knows herself *joined* (*coniunctam*) to the baptized who lack the whole faith or communion under Peter's successor; they hold Scripture and faith in Christ, are "consecrated by baptism", and receive other sacraments "in their own Churches or ecclesiastical communities", in real but imperfect communion.

**LG 16:** those without the Gospel are *ordered* (*ordinantur*) to the People of God; those who through no fault of their own do not know the Gospel, yet sincerely seek God and, moved by grace, try to do his will, can attain salvation; it footnotes the 1949 letter.

**Level:** LG 14's knowing-and-refusing clause **restates the rung-1 necessity of the Church** and names no one; LG 14's account of incorporation and catechumens, LG 15 and LG 16 **rung 3**; LG 16 defines nothing. LG 15's "consecrated by baptism" rests on Trent's rung-1 canon that heretics' baptism in the Trinity's name, intending what the Church does, is true baptism (Session VII, Canons on Baptism, can. 4).

**Not:** a census; LG 14 never promised insiders anything either, since membership without charity saves no one.

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

### Baptism of desire

That faith and conversion, or martyrdom, can supply where baptism is prevented is old, not a modern loophole, and taught, not defined.

Cyprian (Ep. 72.22): a catechumen killed for the Name before baptism is baptized in blood, the greatest baptism; he cites the thief on the cross. Augustine (*On Baptism* IV.22.30): faith and conversion of heart supply where baptism is prevented by the necessity of the moment, not contempt (his *Retractations* later doubted only whether the thief was unbaptized). Aquinas: three baptisms, of water, blood and Spirit (ST III q.66 a.11; ad 2, the latter two are not sacraments, not being signs), blood the most excellent (a.12). Trent: the laver of regeneration "or the desire thereof" (Session VI ch. 4; Session VII, can. 4 on the sacraments). The 1949 letter: desire explicit in catechumens, implicit in the invincibly ignorant. On every formulation's own terms the martyred catechumen is inside: what divides him from Cyprian's martyred heretic is where the will stands toward the communion, not the blood. For someone who never heard the word "Church", whether "desire" still fits is disputed.

**Level:** that desire or martyrdom can supply for baptism, **at least rung 3** (LG 14; CCC 1258-1259); whether Trent's clause puts it higher is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md) [2.2](../sacraments-and-liturgy/lessons/02-02-necessity-infants-and-baptism-of-desire-and-blood.md)'s to settle.

**Not:** "defined" (overstates); "modern loophole" (erases fifteen centuries).

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md)

### Subsistit in

The Church of Christ subsists in the Catholic Church: she continues whole in one communion, and her gifts are at work beyond it, pulling toward it.

**The sentence.** LG 8 (1964), paraphrased: this one Church of Christ, confessed in the Creed and constituted in this world as a society, *subsistit in Ecclesia catholica*, subsists in the Catholic Church governed by Peter's successor and the bishops in communion with him, though many [elements of sanctification and truth](#elements-of-sanctification-and-truth) are found outside her structure (*extra eius compaginem*), which as gifts proper to the Church of Christ impel toward catholic unity. *Subsistere*: to continue in existence, to remain (lesson's gloss); the same council uses it of the unity she can never lose (UR 4) and of Catholic traditions continuing in the Anglican Communion (UR 13). In 1950 Pius XII had summed up his teaching as the Mystical Body and the Roman Catholic Church being "one and the same thing" ([*Mystici Corporis*](#mystici-corporis)); the council chose a different verb.

**The drafting.** The 1962 draft followed *Mystici Corporis*; the 1963 draft said the Church of Christ *est*, is, the Catholic Church, and added the clause on elements. At the end of 1963 Gérard Philips's subcommission proposed *adest in*, "is present in"; Sebastian Tromp, the commission's secretary, proposed *subsistit in* and, by Karl Becker's report of the archived recording, meant it exclusively. The commission's stated reason was better accord with the statement on ecclesial elements present elsewhere (*Acta Synodalia* III/1, 177, in Francis Sullivan's translation). Thirteen bishops asked for *est* back and were refused. Philips predicted floods of ink.

**Rome's reading.** One subsistence only: the CDF's Notification on Leonardo Boff's *Church: Charism and Power* (1985); *Dominus Iesus* §16 n. 56 (2000); the 2007 *Responses* (Q2: subsistence is the perduring historical continuity, in the Catholic Church, of all the elements Christ instituted, and belongs to her alone; Q3: "full identity"; yet the Church of Christ is "present and operative" in the churches and communities not in full communion).

**The open edge.** Becker (*L'Osservatore Romano*, December 2005): the council never left the drafts' total identity. Sullivan (*Theological Studies*, 2006): *subsistit* means "continues to exist", but the identity is no longer exclusive; the Church of Christ continues in her fullness only in the Catholic Church, yet extends, less fully, beyond her. They divide on exclusivity; neither says she subsists twice.

**Level:** LG 8 as an act **at least rung 3**, a constitution that defined nothing; **no act defines *subsistit* or any theory of it**. Rome's one-subsistence reading, carried by three papally approved CDF acts, is **rung 3**: holding that the Church subsists in other churches too contradicts authentic teaching owed religious submission, but is not heresy. What the commission meant beyond one subsistence is **freely disputed**, inside that bound.

**Not:** "subsists in" as "one of several places the Church exists"; *subsistit* as a mere synonym of *est* (the verb changed on purpose; Rome denies that the change weakened the identity, not that the words differ).

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md); first met in [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md)

### Elements of sanctification and truth

Much of what makes the Church the Church is really found outside her visible structure, and it belongs to her.

LG 8 and UR 3: Scripture, baptism, the life of grace, faith, hope and charity, and in some bodies bishops and the Eucharist, are found outside the Catholic Church's visible boundaries. They "belong by right to the one Church of Christ" (UR 3) and impel toward catholic unity (LG 8). The Spirit uses the separated communities as means of salvation (UR 3).

**Level:** **rung 3**.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

### Churches and ecclesial communities

One criterion sorts bodies: apostolic succession in the sacrament of Orders, because it carries the Eucharist. It grades structure, not faith or holiness.

**The separated Eastern churches** keep apostolic succession and a valid Eucharist: "true particular Churches" (DI 17; *Responses* Q4). But communion with Peter's successor is an internal constitutive principle of a particular church, so their condition lacks something (Q4): it is "wounded" (*Communionis Notio* 17, 1992). **The communities born of the Reformation**, in Catholic doctrine, lack apostolic succession in the sacrament of Orders, so they have not kept "the genuine and integral substance" of the Eucharist (*Responses* Q5; UR 22, *propter sacramenti Ordinis defectum*), and are not Churches "in the proper sense". Their baptized are in imperfect communion (DI 17), and the Spirit uses the communities as means of salvation (UR 3). The CDF's own 2007 commentary grants these communities ecclesial character and concedes that the classification caused no little distress in them and among some Catholics. Applying the test body by body runs through judgments made elsewhere: the Church of England kept bishops, but *Apostolicae Curae* (1896) judged Anglican orders invalid, which the 1998 commentary lists as rung 2; validity is [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md)'s. UR 19 names "Churches and ecclesial Communities" in the West without sorting them. How those communities describe themselves: [Reformation responses to the 2007 Responses](#reformation-responses-to-the-2007-responses).

The full title of the 2007 document: *Responses to some questions regarding certain aspects of the doctrine on the Church* (CDF, 29 June 2007).

**Level:** **rung 3**; the 2007 commentary carries no approval of its own.

**Not:** "ecclesial community" means "outside the Church": it is a structural verdict on a body.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

### Dominus Iesus

The CDF's declaration of 6 August 2000: on Christ (christology's) and on the Church (one subsistence; Churches and ecclesial communities).

Authentic ordinary magisterium, ratified by John Paul II; it "defines nothing new". Its first half, on Christ and the unicity of salvation, is [`christology` 6.4](../christology/lessons/06-04-christology-today.md)'s. Its second half: §16 n. 56 rejects the reading that the Church of Christ could subsist in non-Catholic churches too, citing the 1985 Boff Notification; §17 calls the Eastern churches "true particular Churches", the communities of the Reformation "not Churches in the proper sense", and their baptized in imperfect communion. §16 restates that Christ founded one Church, which will never lack her unity, without adding weight to that rung-1 article.

**Level:** **rung 3**.

### Unitatis Redintegratio

Vatican II's decree on ecumenism (21 November 1964): restoring unity among Christians is one of the council's principal concerns. It defines nothing.

Used in [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md): UR 1 (division contradicts Christ's will); UR 2 (unity's Trinitarian source); UR 3 (imperfect communion; those born into separation not charged with it; obstacles of doctrine, discipline and structure; elements belong by right to the one Church); UR 4 (the unity Christ gave subsists in the Catholic Church, can never be lost, and is to grow); UR 13 ("the seamless robe of Christ"; East-Rome communion "dissolved"); UR 15 (Eastern sacraments and succession); UR 22 (baptism a "sacramental bond of unity", only a beginning). Used in [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md): UR 1 (a principal concern); UR 3 (the separations came about *non sine hominum utriusque partis culpa*, "not without fault of people on both sides", qualified by *quandoque*, "at times", lesson's gloss); UR 4 (the goal: all gathered at one Eucharist into the unity that subsists in the Catholic Church; every Catholic to take part; individual reconciliation a distinct work); UR 6 (continual reformation); UR 7 (begs pardon of God and the separated brethren); UR 8 ([spiritual ecumenism](#spiritual-ecumenism), common prayer in set circumstances); UR 9 (dialogue *par cum pari*); UR 11 ([hierarchy of truths](#hierarchy-of-truths), [false irenicism](#false-irenicism)); UR 12 (cooperation); UR 16-18 (legitimate variety; no burden beyond the essential, Acts 15:28).

**Level:** a conciliar decree that **defines nothing**, carrying the Creed's weight where it restates it (the Church's oneness, rung 1; baptism's incorporation into Christ, defined dogma). Its own teaching (shared blame, the communities as means the Spirit uses, ecumenism every Catholic's duty, imperfect communion) is **rung 3**, restated in *Ut Unum Sint*, *Mysterium Ecclesiae* and CCC 817-822. Its rules on common prayer and worship are **discipline**, no rung; UR 11's hierarchy is **no rung at all**.

**Not:** "both sides to blame" as equal blame (UR 3 says *at times*, divides nothing, judges no living person); "ecumenism gave up *subsistit*" (UR 4 says the unity subsists and cannot be lost); "Vatican II defined that other Christians are in communion".

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Spiritual ecumenism

Change of heart, holiness of life and prayer for unity: not the soft half of ecumenism but its soul.

UR 8 calls spiritual ecumenism the soul of the whole ecumenical movement, and permits common prayer in set circumstances. With dialogue (UR 9: competent experts, approved by their bishops, each side dealing with the other on an equal footing) and cooperation in service (UR 12), it is one of the activities asked of every Catholic. Catholics' first duty is to appraise what needs renewal in their own household (UR 4, 6). Ecumenism refuses two cheap imitations: **federation** (the churches already one underneath, unity as mutual recognition; excluded by *Mysterium Ecclesiae* §1, which forbids picturing Christ's Church as a mere collection of churches or a goal that exists nowhere today) and **absorption** (the others coming over one by one and dropping all that is theirs; UR 4 makes individual reconciliation a distinct work, and UR 4, 16-18 protect variety). "Neither absorption nor fusion" is John Paul II's phrase, quoted by the Balamand document (1993, §14), a commission text. Leo XIV's *In Unitate Fidei* (2025, §12): neither a return to the state before the divisions nor acceptance of the status quo.

**Level:** **rung 3**.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Hierarchy of truths

Catholic truths differ in their relation to the foundation of the faith: an axis of centrality, never of authority or assent.

UR 11 (1964): in comparing doctrines, remember that Catholic truths vary in their relation to the foundation of the Christian faith. The fence stands in the same paragraph: doctrine is presented whole, and nothing is more foreign to ecumenism than a [false irenicism](#false-irenicism). *Mysterium Ecclesiae* §4 (CDF, 1973): some dogmas rest on others, but all, being revealed, are believed with the same divine faith. It is not *Mortalium Animos* §9's rejected "fundamental and non-fundamental articles", which was a distinction of assent. Papal infallibility in dialogue is the clean case: rung 1, well out from the foundation, so explained from the foundation outward, never offered as negotiable. [`fundamental-theology` 4.5](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md) owns the axis: moving right never moves a claim down.

**Level:** **no rung**: it orders explanation, not firmness.

**Not:** "lower truths are optional".

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### False irenicism

Peace bought by blurring the truth: the named failure mode of ecumenism.

UR 11: nothing is more foreign to ecumenism than a false irenicism that harms the purity of Catholic doctrine and clouds its genuine meaning; the way of expressing the faith must not obstruct dialogue, but the doctrine is presented whole. *Ut Unum Sint* §18: in matters of faith, compromise contradicts God, who is Truth. The failure mode of "federation" ecumenism.

**The hard case:** the Joint Declaration's "differentiated consensus" sets its differences out (§§18-39, 43), the opposite of clouding; but false irenicism can also hide in calling a difference "emphasis" when it is substance, which the 1998 Catholic Response charged against parts of it. One principle says look for the same faith under different words (*Ut Unum Sint* §38); the other forbids calling different faiths the same. The doctrine supplies the test, not the verdict.

**Level:** **rung 3**.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Mortalium Animos

Pius XI's encyclical of 6 January 1928: no Catholic part in the "pan-Christian" assemblies, and union only by return. The posture and the discipline changed in 1964; the doctrine did not.

Vatican.va numbering. §6: the Church of Christ exists now, visible, not a federation. §7: rejects dealing with Rome *pares cum pari*, as equals with an equal. §8: forbids Catholics to join or support the "pan-Christian" assemblies. §9: rejects "fundamental and non-fundamental" articles (faith rests on God revealing). §10: "the union of Christians may be fostered in no other way than by fostering the return of the dissidents to the one true Church of Christ" (lesson's translation).

**What changed in 1964:** every Catholic told to take an active part (UR 4); common prayer permitted in set circumstances (UR 8); meetings *par cum pari* commended (UR 9); return as the *only* road set aside, with individual reconciliation kept as a distinct work. Balamand (§30) calls the ecclesiology of return "outdated", a commission's judgment, not magisterium.

**What did not:** Christ's Church exists now and is one, and federation is excluded (MA §6; *Mysterium Ecclesiae* §1); no revealed truth is negotiable (MA §8; *Ut Unum Sint* §18); the rejection of "fundamental articles" (restated by *Mysterium Ecclesiae* §4).

**Level:** its doctrine **rung 3**, restating rung-1 doctrine where it does (faith rests on God revealing: [`fundamental-theology` 2.1](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-01-the-act-of-faith.md)); its ban was **discipline**, replaced by a council. Whether 1928 to 1964 is development or reversal is **freely disputed**.

**Not:** "the ban still binds" (overstates a replaced discipline); "the whole encyclical was withdrawn" (understates doctrine *Mysterium Ecclesiae* restates).

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Joint Declaration on Justification

The Lutheran World Federation and the Catholic Church, Augsburg, 31 October 1999: a consensus in basic truths of justification, with differences remaining. An agreement, not a definition.

An Official Common Statement with an Annex confirmed the Declaration. It claims a consensus in *basic truths* of justification, the remaining differences acceptable (§§5, 40); the sixteenth-century condemnations do not apply to the partner's teaching *as presented in the Declaration* (§41); the condemnations remain "salutary warnings" (§42); ecclesiology, authority, ministry and the sacraments are left open (§43). Commentators call the method "differentiated consensus". The 1998 Catholic Response judged some differences substantive: clarification 1, *simul iustus et peccator* as §29 explains it "not acceptable"; clarification 5, some differences concern substance. Later associated: the World Methodist Council (Seoul, 23 July 2006), the Anglican Consultative Council (2016), the World Communion of Reformed Churches (2017). Its doctrine is [`creation-grace-last-things`](../creation-grace-last-things/syllabus.md) 5.2's; the argument is [`apologetics-catholic-claims`](../apologetics-catholic-claims/syllabus.md) 3.4's.

**Level:** an act of agreement, **not a definition**; it **did not revoke a canon**: Trent's decree keeps rung 1, bounded by "as presented in the Declaration".

**Not:** "1999 revoked Trent". Whether its method passes the [false irenicism](#false-irenicism) test is argued downstream.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Indefectibility

The Church will last until Christ returns, holding the faith and sacraments through which he saves. Indefectible is not impeccable.

Christ's promises: "the gates of hell shall not prevail against it" (Matt 16:18, DR), and his promise to be with the apostles to the end of the world (Matt 28:20). *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 2 grounds Peter's perpetual succession on the Church's permanence: the Church, "being founded upon the Rock, will stand firm to the end of the world" (Schaff). LG 39: indefectibly holy; CCC 869: indestructible; GS 43: the Church remains her Lord's faithful spouse, though members clerical and lay have been unfaithful. The whole body cannot fail in believing ([sensus fidei](#sensus-fidei)). It is promised to the whole Church, not to each diocese or successor ([apostolic succession](#apostolic-succession)). **Impeccability** ("cannot sin") is claimed by no Catholic text of any member, priest, bishop or pope: Trent VI can. 23 (rung 1) defines that no justified person avoids all sins through life without a special privilege, as the Church holds of Mary; CCC 827: all members, ministers included, must acknowledge that they are sinners. Infallibility is not impeccability either. A promise that a river will reach the sea is not a promise that its water will be clean; indefectibility is true, and it answers a question no victim asked.

**Level:** **at least rung 3**, presupposed by rung-1 acts (the Creed's article; *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 2's canon on Peter's perpetual successors), but **defined by no act as a thesis of its own** (can. 749 §3). **Says nothing** about governance or conduct.

**Not:** "the Church cannot do great wrong".

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Memory and Reconciliation

The ITC's study of the Church and the faults of the past (dated December 1999, published March 2000): holiness *of* the Church against holiness *in* the Church, and a Church that confesses her children's sins in maternal solidarity.

Holiness *of* the Church, from the missions of Son and Spirit, against holiness *in* the Church, her members' (§3.2). The Church is "in a certain sense" a sinner, because she takes on the sin of those she bore in baptism (ch. 3 introduction); she confesses herself a sinner "not as a subject who sins", but by bearing her children's faults in maternal solidarity (§3.4). Bishops may confess for local churches, the pope for the whole Church (§3.4). Historical judgment comes first, then theological (§4); imputability does not reach beyond those who consented (§1.3). Paul VI's *Credo of the People of God* (30 June 1968, §19) says the Church is holy though she holds sinners, because "she herself has no other life but that of grace", and does penance for her members' offences.

**Level:** **no magisterial level**. Its *in forma specifica* approval was the commission's own written vote on the text, not the papal approval of [`fundamental-theology` 4.5](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md); Cardinal Ratzinger, as CDF prefect and ITC president, approved publication. The *Credo* is by its own §3 not strictly a dogmatic definition; §19 rung 3. **Whether the Church herself sins** is **freely disputed**: Jacques Maritain (*On the Church of Christ*, 1970; English 1973) set the Church's person, holy and without sin, against her personnel, who sin; Karl Rahner ("The Sinful Church in the Decrees of Vatican II", *Theological Investigations* 6) read LG 8 as making the Church, still holy, in some sense a subject of sin. All three agree that a bishop who concealed abuse sinned, that the Church must repent, and that the victim's suffering is the body's; they disagree whether the Church's own act can be a sin.

**Not:** "the Church teaches that she is a sinner" (no act teaches it); "a Catholic may not say the Church sinned" (the ITC itself says "in a certain sense").

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Clericalism

A distorted understanding of the Church's authority, fostered by priests or by lay people, that helps perpetuate abuse: essence-not-degree misread as rank in holiness.

Francis, Letter to the People of God (20 August 2018), written after the Pennsylvania grand jury report (14 August 2018; 301 priests named, more than 1,000 identifiable victims). It opens with 1 Corinthians 12:26 (when one member suffers, all suffer with it); speaking as an ecclesial community, it admits "we were not where we should have been"; it names clericalism, whether fostered by priests themselves or by lay persons, as a distorted way of understanding authority; it calls the whole People of God to penitential prayer and fasting. In course terms, clericalism misreads [essence, not degree](#essence-not-degree) as caste rather than service to the common priesthood. The record: the John Jay study (2004, allegations against 4,392 US priests who served 1950-2002, about 4 percent); the Murphy Report (2009, Dublin put secrecy and reputation ahead of children's welfare); journalists, commissions and grand juries, not the Church's own processes, brought the facts out ([`church-history` 9.3](../church-history/lessons/09-03-after-the-council.md)).

**Level:** the Letter **carries what it restates**; that clericalism is the root is its **pastoral judgment**, and the causes remain disputed (church-history 9.3).

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Vos estis lux mundi

Francis's motu proprio of 7 May 2019 (definitive text 25 March 2023): clerics and religious must report, and a bishop's interference with an investigation is itself covered. Law, not doctrine.

The questions the record forces: a bishop governs with proper, ordinary and immediate power (LG 27; can. 381 §1), and in penal cases only the pope judges him (can. 1405 §1, 3°), so a concealing bishop long answered, in practice, to no one below the pope. *Vos estis* (ad experimentum for three years; definitive text in force 30 April 2023): clerics and religious must report (art. 3); its scope includes acts or omissions by bishops intended to interfere with or avoid civil or canonical investigations (art. 1 §1 b); a report about a Latin bishop goes to the Holy See and the metropolitan, who investigates (art. 8); no obligation of silence may be imposed on a reporter (art. 4 §3); authorities are to welcome, listen to and support those who say they were harmed (art. 5); state reporting laws stand. Related: a rescript published on 17 December 2019 removed these cases from the pontifical secret; *Pascite gregem Dei* (23 May 2021, in force 8 December 2021) moved a cleric's sexual offence with a minor from offences against special obligations (can. 1395, a breach of his duties) to offences against human life, dignity and freedom (can. 1398). Whether ecclesiology, and not only law, has a place for the victim is open.

**Level:** all **law**: binding, changeable, **no doctrinal rung**.

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Day of Pardon

12 March 2000: John Paul II's seven confessions of the sins of the Church's members. A liturgical act, not a teaching act, and not a response to the abuse crisis.

Among the seven: sins "in the service of truth", against unity, against the Jewish people; the seventh asked prayer for "minors who are victims of abuse" only in general terms, two years before the Boston revelations ([`church-history` 9.3](../church-history/lessons/09-03-after-the-council.md)). Vatican II had begged pardon for sins against unity (UR 7). Later: Benedict XVI's letter to the Catholics of Ireland (2010); Francis's admission of "grave errors" over Chile (2018). Acts of repentance are acts, not arguments that the Church is holy after all; each is measured by the historical truth it admits and by what changed afterwards.

**Level:** a liturgical act, **no doctrinal level**.

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

## Mary

Positions and distinctions from Module 6. The module's rule: **no Marian claim may say anything that the Christological root, *Theotokos*, would not license**; whatever is said of her must come from who her Son is and lead back to him. The four dogmas at a glance: [the table](#the-four-marian-dogmas).

### Theotokos

God-bearer: a claim first about who her son is. The one Marian dogma that is really about Christ.

Ephesus (431) recited Nicaea, judged Cyril's Second Letter to Nestorius consonant with it, and deposed Nestorius: the one born of Mary is the Word himself in his own flesh, so she is *Theotokos*. There is no separate Ephesine decree on the title, which was already in use; Chalcedon's Definition (451) is the first definition text to name her Mother of God. Both acts are [`christology` 2.2](../christology/lessons/02-02-nestorius-cyril-and-ephesus.md)'s. Scriptural seed: Elizabeth's "the mother of my Lord" (Luke 1:43; CCC 495), where Luke uses "Lord" for God through the chapter; the verse bears the title's ground, not Ephesus's precision. Its Marian content, what it makes of *her*, is [divine maternity](#divine-maternity).

**Level:** **rung 1**.

**Not:** that she is mother of the divinity; that Ephesus defined everything LG 53 says.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md)

### Divine maternity

Mary is truly Mother of God, and by that office the Father's beloved daughter and the Spirit's temple: the root of every other Marian claim.

CCC 487: what the Church believes about Mary rests on what she believes about Christ, and illumines it. LG 53: Mary is truly Mother of God and of the Redeemer, and by that office beloved daughter of the Father and temple of the Spirit, above every creature; yet as a child of Adam she is redeemed by her Son's merits and stands with all who are to be saved. LG 61: she was predestined to it in the decree that determined the Incarnation. LG 54: chapter 8 gives no complete doctrine on Mary and leaves open questions open. LG 58 reads Cana as her intercession obtaining the first sign and Calvary as Christ giving her as mother to his disciple; *Redemptoris Mater* 24 (1987) links the double "Woman" to Genesis 3:15. John's symbolism (unnamed mother, unnamed disciple, "Woman", "hour") invites these readings; the text does not state them. The harder texts, Mark 3:31-35 and Luke 11:27-28, put doing God's will above kinship; LG 58 cites both and says Mary did exactly that (Luke 2:19, 51).

**Level:** **rung 3** (LG 53, 61; chapter 8 defines nothing). The Marian readings of Cana and Calvary: **rung 3** as readings those documents propose, not definitions of what John meant.

**Not:** "dogma" (overstates); "pious opinion" (understates).

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md); the rule is [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)'s

### New Eve

Inside Christ's undoing of Adam, Mary's believing obedience undoes Eve's unbelieving disobedience.

Justin, *Dialogue* 100 (mid-second century), its first surviving form ([`patristics` 1.4](../patristics/lessons/01-04-justin-martyr.md)). Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* III.22.4 (c. 180, ANF 1), makes it structural: "In accordance with this design" (Adam the figure of Christ), virgin answers virgin, betrothed betrothed, obedience disobedience; Mary "by yielding obedience, become the cause of salvation, both to herself and the whole human race"; "the knot of Eve's disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary". "Cause of salvation" is said of her consent, and "to herself" counts her among the saved. In V.19.1 he calls Mary Eve's "patroness (*advocata*)", which the ANF note marks as ambiguous, perhaps a counterpart. Tertullian, *On the Flesh of Christ* 17 (c. 206): "As Eve had believed the serpent, so Mary believed the angel." LG 56 quotes Irenaeus's knot; LG 63 calls Mary "the new Eve". *Munificentissimus Deus* 39 makes it the ground of the Assumption ([6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)).

**Level:** patristic consensus taken up at **rung 3** (LG 56, 63); **defined nowhere**. Recapitulation as a text is [`patristics` 2.2](../patristics/lessons/02-02-irenaeus-recapitulation.md)'s.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Marian typology

A type shows fit within one plan, not proof: it carries the weight of whoever draws it, and no dogma rests on a figure alone.

Typology reads a real person or thing as prefiguring another. [`old-testament` 1.2](../old-testament/lessons/01-02-the-senses-of-scripture.md)'s rules: the spiritual sense rests on the literal; no doctrine rests on a figure alone; a type the New Testament itself draws belongs to an inspired text's literal sense, a Father's carries that Father's weight. It is an argument from correspondence: it cannot establish a fact, only show that a fact known otherwise belongs to a pattern.

- **The woman of Genesis 3:15.** Hebrew *hu'* ("he" or "it": the offspring), Septuagint *autos*, Vulgate *ipsa*, "she", which the Douay follows ("she shall crush thy head"); the Douay's note says the sense is the same either way, since the woman crushes the serpent "by her seed" ([`old-testament` 2.3](../old-testament/lessons/02-03-the-primeval-history.md)). The Marian reading rests on the enmity with *the woman*, read with John's "Woman" and Revelation's, not on the pronoun. LG 55: these earliest texts, read in the Church in the light of fuller revelation, bring the Redeemer's mother gradually into view.
- **The ark.** 2 Samuel 6 [DR 2 Kings 6] beside Luke 1:39-56: "arose and went" to Juda (6:2) and Mary "rising up" went to Juda (1:39); "How shall the ark of the Lord come to me?" (6:9) and "whence is this to me that the mother of my Lord should come to me?" (1:43); three months (6:11; 1:56); leaping (6:16; 1:41, 44). Revelation shows the ark (11:19) one verse before the woman (12:1). The Litany of Loreto: *Foederis arca*. Luke never names the ark; his intention is argued.
- **The queen mother.** The *gebirah*, "great lady" (lesson's gloss; 1 Kings 15:13; Jeremiah 13:18); Solomon sets a throne at his right hand for "the king's mother" (1 Kings 2:19 [DR 3 Kings 2:19]); Mary's son receives "the throne of David his father" (Luke 1:32). It bears dignity, nearness to the throne, a mother's standing to ask; it cannot bear that her requests are always granted (Solomon refuses Bathsheba's petition, 2:22-25), and no New Testament text draws it.

**Level:** the Marian senses of Genesis 3:15 and Revelation 12 are **spiritual-sense readings**, taught, not defined, and not the literal sense; the ark and the queen mother are **theological typology**, with **no magisterial act**.

**Not:** "proof", or "decoration".

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md)

### Woman of Revelation 12

The woman clothed with the sun is first Israel and the Church; the Marian reading names the one woman in whom that people bore its Messiah.

Three readings ([`new-testament` 6.5](../new-testament/lessons/06-05-revelation.md)): Israel, the Church, Mary. The Douay note: "The church of God. It may also, by allusion, be applied to our blessed Lady." She bears the child "who was to rule all nations with an iron rod" (12:5; Psalm 2:9), so the Messiah's mother is in view; but her birth pangs (12:2), 1,260 days in the wilderness (12:6) and "the rest of her seed" (12:17) are a people's features. The Marian reading works on the logic of Mary as type of the Church (LG 63). *Munificentissimus Deus* cites Revelation 12 only at §27, as what the scholastic Doctors saw signified there: not the bull's proof, which rests on the Church's faith (§12) and the New Eve (§39).

**Level:** a spiritual-sense reading, **not defined**; the ecclesial sense is not excluded.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Sub tuum praesidium

An early Marian prayer under the title *Theotokos*; how early is a palaeographers' question with no consensus.

The lesson's translation of the Greek, as Byzantine Vespers prays it: "Beneath your compassion we take refuge, Theotokos. Do not overlook our petitions in distress, but deliver us from dangers, you alone pure, you alone blessed." (Latin *Sub tuum praesidium*, "beneath your protection".) The vocative *Theotoke* is read on a papyrus, P.Ryl. III 470. Its editor, C. H. Roberts (1938), dated the hand to the fourth century with a query, reporting that Edgar Lobel would not place it after the third; Hans Förster (1995; 2005) argued for a date centuries later. LG 66 alludes to it, and its footnote names it. *Mater Populi Fidelis* (2025) fn 16 repeats a third-century date. Early, it is the first direct invocation of Mary we have; late, a witness to a prayer already old. Either way it shows prayer under the title, nothing more.

**Level:** the date is a **historical question**, **no rung**; a doctrinal note's footnote is not a palaeographic ruling.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md)

### Virginal conception

Jesus was conceived in Mary by the Holy Spirit, without a human father. A claim about Jesus's conception, not Mary's.

Matthew tells it from Joseph's side ("found with child, of the Holy Ghost", 1:18), Luke from Mary's ("How shall this be done, because I know not man?", 1:34), and the two narratives share no scene yet agree on this core ([`new-testament` 2.3](../new-testament/lessons/02-03-luke.md)). The creeds confess it; CCC 496 insists on the bodily fact. Isaiah 7:14, *almah* and *parthenos*, is [`old-testament` 4.2](../old-testament/lessons/04-02-isaiah-and-immanuel.md)'s, and the doctrine does not hang on it. It is the *ante partum* clause of [perpetual virginity](#perpetual-virginity). Every historic denial of Mary's virginity (Tertullian, Helvidius, Jovinian) kept this clause.

**Level:** **rung 1**, from Scripture and the creeds.

**Not:** the [Immaculate Conception](#immaculate-conception), which is Mary's own conception by her parents, without original sin, defined in 1854 by a different act; neither implies the other.

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md)

### Immaculate Conception

Mary, from the first instant of her own conception, was preserved from original sin by Christ's merits: redeemed first and best, not exempted from redemption.

The definition in *Ineffabilis Deus* (Pius IX, 8 December 1854), Schaff's translation: "the doctrine which holds the Blessed Virgin Mary to have been, from the first instant of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege of Almighty God, in view of the merits of Christ Jesus the Saviour of mankind, preserved free from all stain of original sin, was revealed by God, and is, therefore, to be firmly and constantly believed by all the faithful." She was **preserved**, not cleansed: original sin never contracted, as against contracted and washed. "Her conception" means the first instant of *her*. The merits clause makes Christ the cause of her preservation, so "my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour" (Luke 1:47) is true of her in the strongest sense; LG 53, as CCC 492 renders it, calls her redeemed "in a more exalted fashion". It presupposes Trent, Session V (17 June 1546): Adam's sin passes to all "by propagation, not by imitation"; as Adam's daughter she would have contracted it (her debt) and was kept from it. Original sin itself is [`creation-grace-last-things`](../creation-grace-last-things/syllabus.md)'s.

**History, said plainly.** Aquinas held the opposite: "the Blessed Virgin did indeed contract original sin, but was cleansed therefrom before her birth from the womb" (ST III q.27 a.2 ad 2); Bernard called the feast a presumptuous novelty (Letter 174, about 1140). Neither was a heretic: no act had proposed the doctrine, and Rome said in 1483 that it was undecided ([*Grave nimis*](#grave-nimis)). The answer that carried was Scotus's [preservative redemption](#preservative-redemption). The development: the East kept Anne's conception of Mary by the eighth century, England by the eleventh (Eadmer of Canterbury wrote in its defence); in 1387 the Paris faculty condemned the Dominican Juan de Monzón for calling the doctrine heretical; Basel's 1439 decree was never received; Sixtus IV approved a Mass and Office (*Cum praeexcelsa*, dated 28 February 1476) and then *Grave nimis*; Trent left Mary out of its decree; Alexander VII's *Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum* (8 December 1661) gave the feast's sense in nearly the words of 1854 and defined nothing; Pius IX consulted the bishops by *Ubi primum* (2 February 1849) and defined. Lourdes (1858) came after and, as private revelation, adds no weight.

**Level:** **rung 1**, every clause including the merits clause: solemnly defined *ex cathedra*, one of the two papal acts nobody contests; obstinate denial by the baptized is heresy (can. 751). Before 1854, **freely disputed** (rung 5), by Rome's own word in 1483.

**Does not assert:** that Mary needed no redemption; a virginal conception of Mary; when the soul is infused; exemption from death; lifelong freedom from personal sin (a distinct privilege, [Trent VI canon 23](#trent-vi-canon-23)).

**Not:** "the virgin birth"; "Trent defined it" or "the Church always held it" (overstate); "a pious belief to take or leave" (understates).

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md); distinguished in [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md)

### Perpetual virginity

Mary was virgin before, in and after the birth: dogma held without a single defining act, by the Church's constant teaching.

**Three moments.** *Ante partum*: Jesus conceived without a human father, the [virginal conception](#virginal-conception) itself. *In partu*: the birth did not take away her virginal integrity; Lateran 649 says she bore him "incorruptibly", LG 57 that the birth did not lessen her integrity but hallowed it, CCC 499. These texts assert a fact, not a mechanism; Aquinas explained it bodily, as a miracle of divine power (ST III q.28 a.2 ad 3), and twentieth-century Catholic theologians argued whether "integrity" must be read in bodily terms. *Post partum*: she remained a virgin for life, and Jesus is her only son (CCC 501).

**Each clause was defended when it was denied:** Tertullian denied *in partu* to defend a real birth and pictured a marriage after it (*On the Flesh of Christ* 23, c. 206; *On Monogamy* 8); Helvidius (Rome, c. 383) denied *post partum* from Matthew 1:25, Luke 2:7 and the "brethren", answered by Jerome; Jovinian (early 390s) denied *in partu*, Augustine reporting that he said she "had been corrupted in giving birth" (*On Heresies* 82, lesson's translation), and was condemned at Rome under Siricius and at Milan by Ambrose.

**Witnesses:** Constantinople II's title ([*aeiparthenos*](#aeiparthenos), 553); the [Lateran Synod of 649](#lateran-synod-of-649), can. 3; Paul IV's *Cum quorundam* (7 August 1555), against anti-Trinitarian errors, listing the denial that Mary remained always a virgin "before the birth, in the birth, and perpetually after the birth" (lesson's translation) as an error to be abandoned, by admonition; the liturgy East and West. LG 57 and CCC 499-501 restate it.

**The Reformers kept it:** the Smalcald Articles (1537), Part I art. 4, call Mary "ever-virgin" in their Latin text; Calvin, on Matthew 1:25 (*Harmony of the Evangelists*), refuses Helvidius's inference without affirming the doctrine in his own voice; the Helvidian reading is now widely held among Protestant exegetes.

**Said plainly.** No New Testament text says that Mary remained a virgin in giving birth or afterwards, and the New Testament names Jesus's brothers and sisters without comment. The witnesses cluster from the late fourth century on; the later clauses are attested from the second century (the *Protevangelium*, then Origen), contested by some writers into the late fourth, universal after. The route is a condition on the episcopate's agreement (LG 25), not on every earlier writer: Tertullian strains Vincent's "always", not the rung.

**Level:** **rung 1 by the [ordinary universal magisterium](#ordinary-universal-magisterium)**, no single defining act; assent owed is theological faith. *In partu*: **rung 1 as to the fact**, its bodily content argued. Who the brothers were: [freely disputed](#brothers-of-jesus).

**Not:** "only a pious opinion" (understates); "defined at Lateran 649" or "at the Lateran Council" (overstates the route, and misnames a Roman synod); "every early Christian held all three".

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md)

### Aeiparthenos

"Ever-virgin" (*aei*, always; *parthenos*, virgin): a name in the liturgy and the councils before it was a thesis.

Constantinople II (553) uses it in three capitula: "always a virgin" in capitulum 2, "ever-virgin" in 6 and 14 (Percival); Denzinger prints capitulum 2's Greek, *aeiparthenou*. In each the title describes Mary inside an anathema aimed at something else (capitulum 2: the Word's two nativities), so it witnesses the common faith rather than defining it; *Munificentissimus Deus* uses "ever virgin" in its subject phrase the same way. The Roman Canon names her ever-Virgin (quoted in LG 52); LG 69 notes the Eastern Christians' honour for the Mother of God, ever-virgin. Constantinople II as a council is [`christology` 2.4](../christology/lessons/02-04-the-three-chapters-and-constantinople-ii.md)'s.

**Level:** the title is a witness to rung-1 doctrine held by the ordinary universal magisterium, **not the proposition anathematized**.

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md)

### Lateran Synod of 649

A Roman synod under Pope Martin I against monothelitism, not an ecumenical council: its canon 3 confesses the three moments as grounds of *Theotokos*.

Canon 3 (lesson's translation from Denzinger, 1911): anathema on whoever does not, "in accord with the holy Fathers", confess the holy, ever-virgin and immaculate Mary Mother of God, inasmuch as she conceived God the Word of the Holy Spirit "without seed", bore him "incorruptibly", her virginity "remaining indissoluble even after the birth". The main verb is "confess ... Mother of God": a Christological canon, with the three moments inside the "inasmuch as" clause, as grounds of the claim that the one she bore is God the Word. "Immaculate" is an epithet of holiness, tied to no doctrine about her own conception. LG 57 n. 10 cites it.

**Level:** a Roman synod **carrying papal authority** ([`christology` 2.5](../christology/lessons/02-05-monothelitism-and-constantinople-iii.md)'s grade); a witness to perpetual virginity (rung 1 by the ordinary universal magisterium), not its single defining act.

**Not:** "the Lateran Council"; "the Sixth Council"; the source of the 1854 doctrine.

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md)

### Brothers of Jesus

The dogma excludes Mary's later children; whose sons the "brothers" were is left open.

The texts: Mark 6:3; Matthew 13:55-56; Galatians 1:19; Mark 15:40; the exegesis is [`new-testament` 2.1](../new-testament/lessons/02-01-mark.md)'s, and Jerome's word-by-word answer [`patristics` 4.2](../patristics/lessons/04-02-jerome.md)'s. Lexical questions: whether *heos hou*, "until" (lesson's gloss; Matt 1:25), implies a change afterwards, and whether *prototokos*, "firstborn" (Luke 2:7), implies later children. Three readings: **Helvidian**, Mary's later sons (excluded by perpetual virginity); **Epiphanian**, Joseph's sons by an earlier marriage, already in the second-century *Protevangelium of James*, where Joseph protests "I have children, and I am an old man" (ch. 9), and known to Origen from "the Book of James" (*Commentary on Matthew* X.17); **Hieronymian**, cousins. CCC 500 calls them Jesus's close relations.

**Level:** Epiphanian against Hieronymian **freely disputed**. The *Protevangelium* is an apocryphon, a witness to belief, **no magisterial weight**.

**Not:** "ever-virgin settles who the brothers were".

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md)

### Ineffabilis Deus

Pius IX's apostolic constitution of 8 December 1854, defining the Immaculate Conception: one of the two papal acts nobody contests is *ex cathedra*.

What is defined is that **the doctrine** holding Mary preserved **was revealed by God** and is to be firmly and constantly believed by all the faithful ([Immaculate Conception](#immaculate-conception)). The act invokes the authority of Christ, of Peter and Paul, and the pope's own; it concerns faith; it addresses the whole Church: the marks *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4 set out in 1870 as conditions. A penal clause follows: dissent held in the heart is shipwreck in the faith, and voiced, it incurs the law's penalties. The bishops had been consulted by *Ubi primum* (2 February 1849; read in [`fundamental-theology` 5.3](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-03-the-sensus-fidei.md)). Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom* vol. 2 has the public-domain English.

**Level:** **rung 1**, *ex cathedra* ([*ex cathedra*](#ex-cathedra)).

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md); counted in [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md)

### Preservative redemption

Scotus: keeping someone from a debt is a more perfect redemption than freeing her once she has incurred it.

**Aquinas's two reasons against** sanctification before animation (ST III q.27 a.2): grace inheres only in a rational creature, so before the soul there is no one to sanctify; and had she been sanctified before she could incur sin, she would never have incurred it and would not have needed redemption by Christ, "Saviour of all men" (1 Tim 4:10). **Scotus's answer** (*Sentences* commentary, book III, distinction 3): redemption need not be a cleansing; preserving someone from a sin she would otherwise contract is the more perfect act of mediation, and the most perfect Mediator does it for someone. Weighing three possibilities open to God, he concluded: "if it does not conflict with the authority of the Church or the authority of Scripture, it seems probable that what is more excellent should be attributed to Mary" (lesson's translation). The 1854 merits clause writes the answer in, and the definition keeps Aquinas's *first* reason: the first instant of her conception is the first instant of her. *Potuit, decuit, ergo fecit* is a tag often pinned on Scotus; it is not his wording, and his text carries a proviso.

**Level:** the merits clause of 1854 is **defined** (rung 1); Scotus's argument as such, and Aquinas's, are theology, **no magisterial level**.

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md)

### Grave nimis

Sixtus IV's constitution of 4 September 1483: neither side may call the other heretical, the question "not yet decided" by Rome.

It forbade preachers on either side to call the other heretical or in mortal sin, "since it has not yet been decided by the Roman Church and the Apostolic See" (lesson's translation). With *Cum praeexcelsa* (dated 28 February 1476: a Mass and Office of the Conception), it was renewed by Trent, Session V, which left Mary out of its decree on original sin.

**Level:** it **defined nothing**; by Rome's own word the Immaculate Conception was **freely disputed** (rung 5) until 1854. Trent's exception "states an intention"; the decree it qualifies is rung 1.

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md)

### Fullness of grace

Mary's fullness of grace is the fullest grace a mere creature has, measured by her office and received, not a source.

The angel greets her *kecharitomene* (Luke 1:28; Douay "full of grace"), a perfect passive participle: "you who have been graced" (lesson's gloss), the grace given and lasting; the word does not say when it began. Aquinas separates two fullnesses (ST III q.7 a.10, reloaded from [`christology` 3.4](../christology/lessons/03-04-the-grace-of-the-head.md)): Christ's is fullness of grace itself, overflowing to all; Mary's is measured by her office, nearest the source of grace, having given him his humanity (III q.27 a.5). Taught in LG 56 and CCC 490-492.

**Level:** **at least rung 3**. Aquinas's two fullnesses are theology.

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md)

### Trent VI canon 23

No justified person can avoid all sins, even venial ones, through a whole life without a special privilege, "as the Church holds in regard of the Blessed Virgin".

Trent, Session VI (13 January 1547), can. 23 (Schaff): the anathema strikes the general claim; Mary is the exception the Church already holds. Her lifelong freedom from personal sin is a privilege distinct from 1854's: Bernard (Letter 174) and Aquinas (III q.27 a.4) held her life free of all sin while denying her immaculate conception. The same canon is why no member of the Church is impeccable ([indefectibility](#indefectibility)).

**Level:** the general rule **rung 1** ([`moral-theology` 3.4](../moral-theology/lessons/03-04-sin-mortal-and-venial.md)); its Marian clause **presupposed, not itself defined**. Mary's lifelong freedom from personal sin is firmly taught (LG 56; CCC 493), **at least rung 3**, never separately defined.

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md); used in [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md)

### Assumption

Mary, having completed the course of her earthly life, was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory: the Church defined where she is and left how her life ended open.

*Munificentissimus Deus* 44 (1 November 1950): by the authority of Christ, of Peter and Paul, and his own, Pius XII defines as divinely revealed that the Immaculate Mother of God, Mary ever virgin, "having completed the course of her earthly life" (*expleto terrestris vitae cursu*), was assumed body and soul into heavenly glory (Vatican translation, paraphrased). Whoever wilfully denies or doubts it has fallen from the divine and Catholic faith (§45). The subject phrase ("Immaculate", "Mother of God", "ever virgin") uses the other three as titles and defines nothing new; the defined content is the predicate, and its verb is passive: Christ ascended by his own power (ST III q.57 a.3; [`christology` 6.2](../christology/lessons/06-02-resurrection-and-ascension-as-doctrine.md)), Mary was taken up. LG 59 restates the dogma; LG 68: glorified in body and soul, Mary is the image and beginning of the Church as it will be perfected in the age to come, a sign of sure hope; CCC 966: a singular share in her Son's resurrection, an anticipation of ours. Aquinas took it as given ("her body was assumed into heaven, and yet Scripture does not relate this", ST III q.27 a.1), while denying the Immaculate Conception: the link the bull draws between the two (§§4-5) is an argument of convergence, not a premise the Assumption needs.

**Said plainly.** Nothing in the New Testament, and no surviving writer of the first three centuries, tells how Mary's life ended; the first to ask, Epiphanius, says no one knows ([Dormition](#dormition)).

**Level:** **rung 1**, *ex cathedra*, one of the two papal definitions nobody contests; owed theological faith; obstinate denial is heresy (can. 751). It binds because the act meets *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4's conditions, which make no other sentence of the bull infallible. LG 59 carries the weight of the dogma it restates; LG 68 **rung 3**.

**Does not assert:** whether she died ([mortalists and immortalists](#mortalists-and-immortalists)); time, place, tomb, a third day; the bull's arguments.

**Not:** "1950 defined that Mary never died", nor that "a Catholic must hold that she died" (both overstate); "a pious opinion" (understates).

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Dormition

The Eastern feast of 15 August, the *Koimesis* or "falling asleep": a real death, a burial and a glorification. Its narratives carry no doctrinal weight.

The feast was kept in Palestine before 500 and reportedly fixed on 15 August under the emperor Maurice (c. 600); at Rome, Sergius I (687-701) added a procession and Leo IV a vigil and octave (MD 19).

**The sources:** Epiphanius, *Panarion* 78 (370s): Scripture is silent and no one knows her end; she may have died and been buried, been martyred (Luke 2:35), or remained alive, and he will not choose. The apocryphal *Transitus Mariae* narratives appear from the late fifth century, their sources possibly older (Stephen Shoemaker, *Ancient Traditions of the Virgin Mary's Dormition and Assumption*, 2002); the sixth-century Gelasian Decree lists a book of "the Assumption of holy Mary" among the apocrypha. In the West the story first appears in Gregory of Tours (*Glory of the Martyrs*, begun 587). John Damascene's homilies on the Dormition (eighth century; public-domain English, Mary H. Allies, 1898) give narrative (death, burial at Gethsemane, an empty tomb after three days) and fittingness ("It was fitting that the body of her, who preserved her virginity unsullied in her motherhood, should be kept from corruption even after death"); *Munificentissimus Deus* 21 quotes the fittingness and leaves the narrative alone. The Juvenal story (Juvenal of Jerusalem telling the emperor Marcian, around 451, that the tomb was found empty) comes from a lost *Euthymiac History* added in Greek editions, generally treated as an early interpolation, and absent from Allies. The Orthodox account: [Orthodox Dormition](#orthodox-dormition).

**Level:** the circumstances (tomb, third day, Gethsemane, Juvenal) have **no doctrinal weight**. The dogma does not rest on the *Transitus* stories and does not fall with them.

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Munificentissimus Deus

Pius XII's apostolic constitution of 1 November 1950, defining the Assumption: what the bull argues from, and what it left undefined.

**The consultation.** On 1 May 1946 *Deiparae Virginis Mariae* asked every bishop two questions: could the bodily Assumption be proposed and defined as a dogma of faith, and did they, with their clergy and people, desire it (§11)? The first asks the bishops as teachers; the second asks them to report their people's faith. The reply was almost unanimous; the bull gives no figures (§12). §12 reads it as the [ordinary universal magisterium](#ordinary-universal-magisterium) in agreement with the faith of the people it guides, an agreement that of itself shows the doctrine revealed. Some 200 bishops and theologians had petitioned for a definition at Vatican I.

**The rest of the case:** the liturgy (§§16-19); the Fathers (§§20-23, §21 quoting Damascene's fittingness); theologians down to Suárez, Aquinas among them (§§24-37; Revelation 12 only at §27, as the scholastics' reading); then the ground: Scripture as "ultimate foundation" (§38) and the [New Eve](#new-eve), joined to the new Adam in the struggle of Genesis 3:15, ending in victory over sin and death (1 Cor 15:21-26, 54-57), so that her share closes with her body's glorification (§39). The link to the Immaculate Conception (§§4-5): she was not subject to remaining in the corruption of the grave, an exemption from waiting in the grave, not from dying. **The exposition assumes her death** (§§14, 17, 20); the operative sentence (§44) avoids it. The four conditions are run on this act in [`fundamental-theology` 4.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-02-infallibility-and-its-conditions.md).

**Level:** §44 **rung 1**; the arguments, the exposition and the consultation's reading are **theology, not dogma**.

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Mortalists and immortalists

Did Mary die before her assumption? Not defined; most of the tradition says she did; the other view was never condemned.

**Mortalist:** the majority of the tradition East and West: Damascene ("She dies according to the flesh"); Aquinas (ST III q.27 a.3 ad 1: she "was not freed from death and other such penalties"); the Orthodox liturgy. John Paul II's general audience of 25 June 1997 ("Mary and the human drama of death") favoured it: Pius XII did not intend to deny her death but did not judge it opportune to affirm it solemnly; the Immaculate Conception does not entail bodily immortality.

**Immortalist:** a minority, by John Paul II's account unknown before the seventeenth century; never condemned. The question turns on whether freedom from original sin entails freedom from death; Christ, sinless, died.

**Level:** **not defined** (MD 44 and LG 59 deliberately silent); **open to Catholics**, the mortalist view the common one. An audience catechesis defines nothing: at most the lightest grade of rung 3.

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Mary in Lumen Gentium

Chapter 8 of *Lumen Gentium* (LG 52-69): Mary stands *with* the Church before Christ, not *between* the Church and Christ.

On 29 October 1963 the bishops voted, 1,114 to 1,074, to put Mary inside the constitution on the Church rather than in a document of her own (Sara Butler's ARCIC II paper, citing the *Acta Synodalia*). One instinct sees Mary chiefly beside Christ ("Christotypical", commentators' term), the other as the first of the redeemed, the Church's member and model ("ecclesiotypical"); the chapter keeps both. LG 53: redeemed by her Son's merits, she is a pre-eminent and singular **member** of the Church, its **type** and exemplar, and honoured as **mother**. LG 54: no complete doctrine; open questions left open.

**Structure:** 52-54 introduction; 55-59 Mary in the economy of salvation; 60-65 Mary and the Church; 66-67 her cult; 68-69 sign of hope.

**Its teaching:** she cooperated freely: God willed the Incarnation to wait on her consent, so that as a woman contributed to death a woman should contribute to life (LG 56); at Cana her intercession brought about the first sign, at the Cross she consented to his sacrifice (LG 58); in a singular way she cooperated, by obedience, faith, hope and charity, in restoring supernatural life to souls (LG 61). [Spiritual motherhood](#spiritual-motherhood) (LG 61-62); subordinate mediation ([one Mediator](#one-mediator), LG 60-62); type of the Church, also virgin and mother, after Ambrose, already spotless (LG 63-65); a singular cult, different in kind from adoration ([latria, dulia, hyperdulia](#latria-dulia-hyperdulia), LG 66-67, which warns against both gross exaggeration and petty narrowness); sign of sure hope (LG 68).

**Level:** the dogmas it restates keep **rung 1**; its own teaching (cooperation, spiritual motherhood, subordinate mediation, Mary as type) is authentic conciliar teaching, **at least rung 3**, defining nothing new (the 1964 notification).

**Not:** "LG ch. 8 is a dead letter"; "LG used Co-redemptrix".

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

### Spiritual motherhood

Mary is our mother in the order of grace, from the Annunciation, through the Cross, in heaven by intercession.

LG 61-62: her motherhood in the order of grace began with her consent at the Annunciation, held beneath the Cross, and continues in heaven by intercession until all the elect are gathered; hence she is invoked as Advocate, Helper, Benefactress and Mediatrix (CCC 969's rendering of LG 62; the vatican.va LG has Advocate, Auxiliatrix, Adjutrix, Mediatrix). "Behold thy mother" (John 19:27, DR) is read as said to every disciple, pastors included.

**Mother of the Church:** not used by LG; Paul VI proclaimed the title in his address of 21 November 1964 closing the third session (*Redemptoris Mater* 47); since a decree of the Congregation for Divine Worship (11 February 2018) its memorial falls on the Monday after Pentecost. *Redemptoris Mater* (25 March 1987) 38-41 calls LG 60-62's teaching "maternal mediation": mediation *in Christ*, shared and subordinate.

**Level:** the content **at least rung 3**; "Mother of the Church" **proclaimed in an address, not defined**; the 2018 memorial is **liturgical law**; *Redemptoris Mater* **rung 3**.

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

### One Mediator

There is one mediator of God and men, Christ; every title for Mary must neither take from nor add to his dignity and efficacy.

1 Timothy 2:5 (DR): "one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus". LG 60: Mary's saving influence comes from God's free choice, not from necessity; it *flows from* Christ's superabundant merits, *rests on* his mediation, *depends entirely* on it, and fosters the faithful's immediate union with him. **LG 62's test** for every title: it must **neither take away from nor add to** the dignity and efficacy of Christ the one Mediator; as his one priesthood is shared by ministers and faithful, so his one mediation gives rise to a manifold cooperation that is a share in it. Mary mediates as every Christian who prays for another does, uniquely and maternally, never alongside Christ. The test sorts senses, not sentences: "every grace comes through Mary's hands" fails if it means a channel through which all sanctifying grace must pass, and passes if it means that every help we receive is one her intercession asked for (*Mater Populi Fidelis* ¶¶67-69). The *Salve Regina*, read as the Church prays it, passes.

**Level:** Christ the one Mediator, **rung 1** (Scripture; Trent, Session V, can. 3, the merit of the one mediator). LG 60-62 **at least rung 3**; *Redemptoris Mater* 38-41 "maternal mediation" **rung 3**.

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

### Latria, dulia, hyperdulia

Worship is due to God alone; honour to a creature for God's gifts is not worship. Mary's honour is the highest of that kind, different in kind from adoration.

*Latria* (Greek *latreia*): worship due to God alone. *Dulia* (Greek *douleia*, servitude): honour paid to a creature for gifts God gave. *Hyperdulia*: Latinized Greek for "beyond dulia" (lesson's gloss), the honour due Mary as Mother of God. Aquinas: "the worship of 'latria' is not due to her, but only that of 'dulia': but in a higher degree than to other creatures, inasmuch as she is the Mother of God" (ST III q.25 a.5); it is the highest species of *dulia* taken widely, not a grade of *latria* (II-II q.103 a.4 ad 2). LG 66: her cult is altogether singular, yet differs essentially from the adoration given to the Trinity, and fosters it. Nicaea II (787) reserved *latreia* to the divine nature ([`patristics` 5.4](../patristics/lessons/05-04-maximus-and-john-of-damascus.md)); Trent (Session XXV, 1563) taught that invoking the saints does not offend the one Mediator, "our alone Redeemer and Saviour" (Schaff).

**Level:** *latreia* for God alone **rung 1** (Nicaea II); invoking the saints is Trent's Session XXV teaching (the manuals' *de fide* is testimony); *dulia* and *hyperdulia* are **school words, no rung**.

**Not:** "hyperdulia is worship, only less": *latria* is fixed by its object, God.

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

### Co-redemptrix

A title for Mary's cooperation in redemption: never defined, and since November 2025 judged by the Dicastery, with papal approval, "always inappropriate" to use.

**History.** The title arose in the fifteenth century as a correction of "Redemptrix"; some popes used it, John Paul II at least seven times; Vatican II refrained (*Mater Populi Fidelis* ¶¶17-18). In the 1990s the movement *Vox Populi Mariae Mediatrici* asked for a dogma of both disputed titles; Cardinal Ratzinger's personal *votum* at the CDF on 21 February 1996 was negative, the doctrine not mature (¶19).

**The advocates' case, at its strongest:** Mary's cooperation is real and taught (LG 56, 61); *co-* means "with", not "equal to", and they affirm her total dependence on Christ; popes used the title.

**The 2025 note.** The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith's doctrinal note *Mater Populi Fidelis* (dated 4 November 2025; approved by Leo XIV on 7 October 2025) judges it "always inappropriate" to use "Co-redemptrix" for Mary's cooperation, because the title risks obscuring Christ's unique mediation and needs constant explanation (¶22). A title that needs constant explanation to stay orthodox does not serve the faith: that is a verdict on prudence in speech, beside LG 62's test of truth.

**Level.** The title is **not defined, and never was**. *Mater Populi Fidelis* is a Dicastery doctrinal note with ordinary papal approval (its formula lacks *in forma specifica*): authentic magisterium, **rung 3**, owed religious submission, in principle reformable. Its ¶22 judges the *use* of the title. So whether to use the title, and so whether to seek its definition, is **no longer simply "freely disputed"**. But the note is **not a definition**, and it **condemns no doctrine**: Mary's cooperation, which LG ch. 8 teaches (LG 56, 58, 61), stands at least at rung 3.

**Two errors:** treating the title as dogma; treating Mary's cooperation as a forbidden opinion. Approved apparitions lend the title no weight ([Marian apparitions](#marian-apparitions)).

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

### Mediatrix of all graces

"Mediatrix" is acceptable in a clearly subordinate sense; "of all graces" has limits. Never defined.

**History.** Cardinal Mercier asked in 1921 for a definition of Mary's universal mediation; Benedict XV granted a feast of Mary Mediatrix, not a definition (*Mater Populi Fidelis* ¶23).

**The title alone:** LG 62 records "Mediatrix" among the titles under which she is invoked, and the 2025 note accepts it in a clearly subordinate sense (¶¶25-27).

**"Of all graces":** it "has limits" (¶67): Mary, the first of the redeemed, could not mediate the grace she herself received, and she is no reservoir or channel through which all sanctifying grace must pass (¶45, citing Ratzinger: the title is not clearly grounded in Revelation). "Graces" can name the helps and actual graces her motherly intercession obtains (¶¶68-69): in that sense the phrase has an acceptable meaning.

**Level:** **not defined**. *Mater Populi Fidelis* is **rung 3**, owed religious submission, in principle reformable: its limits on the phrase (¶¶45, 67-69) are an authentic judgment, so how the phrase may be used is no longer simply "freely disputed" either. It marks the limits without forbidding the phrase; it is **not a definition**, and it condemns no doctrine of Mary's cooperation or intercession that LG ch. 8 teaches.

**Not:** "Mediatrix of all graces was defined at Vatican II"; "'Mediatrix' is now banned".

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md); the clause first diagnosed in [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md)

### Marian apparitions

Apparitions apply doctrine; they never add to it. Approval is a permission, human faith at most, and a title gains no weight from a vision.

[`fundamental-theology` 1.4](../fundamental-theology/lessons/01-04-public-and-private-revelation.md) parsed approval: a permission, never a source of doctrine, binding no one to believe. The Dicastery's *Norms* (dated 17 May 2024, in force 19 May 2024) allow six conclusions: *Nihil obstat*; *Prae oculis habeatur*; *Curatur*; *Sub mandato*; *Prohibetur et obstruatur*; *Declaratio de non supernaturalitate*; as a rule none declares a phenomenon supernatural (the pope may authorize a special procedure). As institutional history, apparitions built shrines, pilgrimages and feasts; Lourdes (1858) is the sturdiest recognition, and it came after 1854. Approval language once drifted upward: the *Norms* recall a Grenoble decree (19 September 1851) telling the faithful they could hold an apparition "indubitable and certain" (lesson's translation), and disown such wording. *Mater Populi Fidelis* ¶2 ties the disputed titles to such phenomena.

**Level:** apparition approval has **no doctrinal rung**; the *Norms* are **discipline**.

**Not:** "an approved apparition lends its titles authority": approval binds no one and cannot lift a title above a doctrinal note.

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

### Devotion and doctrine

Devotion is measured by doctrine: renewal makes it more biblical and Christ-centred, not smaller, and no devotion licenses what the doctrine does not.

Paul VI's apostolic exhortation *Marialis Cultus* (2 February 1974) asks every devotion to show its Trinitarian, Christological, pneumatological and ecclesial character (MC 25-28); sets biblical, liturgical, ecumenical and anthropological guidelines for devotions old and new (MC 29-37); and rejects vain credulity, sentimentality and legend posing as history (MC 38). LG 67 commends liturgical and approved devotion and warns theologians and preachers against gross exaggeration and petty narrowness alike. Catholic devotion has at times said of Mary what no doctrine licenses: that her mercy is infinite, that it is safer to seek her than her Son. Newman answered Pusey's collection of such sayings and disowned them (*Letter to Pusey*, dated 7 December 1865); [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md) quotes him.

**Level:** *Marialis Cultus* **rung 3**, much of it pastoral direction; Newman's *Letter* is testimony, **no magisterial level**.

*Lessons:* [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md)

## Other traditions in their own words

### Orthodox eucharistic ecclesiology

The Church is where the Eucharist is: each assembly gathered at one table round its bishop is not a part of the Church but the whole of it.

**Nicholas Afanasiev** (1893-1966), a Russian Orthodox priest teaching in Paris, called his position eucharistic ecclesiology. Many such churches are one Church because each is the same Church, and no church has power over another. Some carry more authority, Jerusalem first and then Rome, as a priority of witness, not of jurisdiction. The rival "universal ecclesiology", which makes each local church a fragment of a whole governed by law, he traced chiefly to Cyprian ("The Church Which Presides in Love", in *The Primacy of Peter*, English 1963; *The Church of the Holy Spirit*, English 2007). **John Zizioulas** (1931-2023), Metropolitan of Pergamon (*Eucharist, Bishop, Church*, his 1965 Athens dissertation, English 2001), keeps the starting point and corrects it. The Eucharist that makes a church is the one Eucharist under its bishop; parish Eucharists are offshoots of it. Each episcopal church is not a part but the whole body of Christ, and all are one Church by "the mystical identity of full circles", not by adding parts; that unity shows in mutual recognition and in councils that restore communion. Whether the local or the universal comes first is, for him, a modern dilemma the first three centuries never faced; pushed to the extreme, "where the Eucharist is, there is the Church" destroys any notion of canonical unity; a permanent universal centre of unity is foreign to those centuries. The Catholic-Orthodox Munich text (1982) called the local and universal necessarily simultaneous.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md); the Catholic dispute it sits beside is [priority of the universal Church](#priority-of-the-universal-church)

### Orthodox account of apostolicity

Right faith and the laying on of hands are one apostolicity, carried by communities gathered at the Eucharist round their bishop.

**Philaret's Longer Catechism**, which Schaff printed as the Russian Church's chief standard (q. 274), asks why the Church is apostolic: "Because she has from the Apostles, without break or change, both her doctrine and the succession of the gifts of the Holy Ghost, through the laying on of consecrated hands"; in the same sense she is called Orthodox. **John Zizioulas** (*Being as Communion*, French 1981): apostolic continuity is carried by communities, not by a line of individuals: the local Church gathered at the Eucharist round its bishop, ordained within such an assembly and for that church. The apostles are men sent into history and also the college round Christ that every Eucharist images. Ordination detached from a church is foreign to this picture, as Chalcedon's canon 6 already held.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

### Orthodox reading of the Petrine texts

Peter's succession belongs to no single see: every bishop who keeps Peter's confession holds Peter's place in his church, and Rome's first place rests on the canons and the city.

The case was set out in English in *The Primacy of Peter* (London, 1963), essays by Meyendorff, Schmemann, Afanasiev and Koulomzine. Meyendorff's essay gathers Byzantine writers for whom Peter's succession belongs to no single see. The joint Catholic-Orthodox commission recorded that the East, with "a different interpretation of the Scriptures and the Fathers", never read Rome's first place as Peter's succession (Chieti, 2016, §16). Philaret's Longer Catechism (Q. 259): "The Church, being to abide through all generations of time, needs also an ever-abiding head; and such is Jesus Christ alone."

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Moscow on primacy

Primacy exists at every level of church order but differs in nature by level; the universal primacy of the first millennium was one of honour, instituted by men.

The Moscow Patriarchate's Holy Synod, *Position on the Problem of Primacy in the Universal Church* (2013), disagreeing with the last part of the [Ravenna document](#ravenna-document): a *primus*'s functions cannot be carried from one level to another; the universal primacy of the first millennium was one of honour, not of power, instituted by men and not by God (citing the fourteenth-century Nilus Cabasilas); within Orthodoxy that primacy of honour now belongs to Constantinople. Moscow's delegation had been absent from Ravenna's session, having walked out over an intra-Orthodox dispute.

*Lessons:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md)

### Orthodox Dormition

Mary died as all people die, was raised by her Son, and already shares the life promised to all who keep God's word; no Orthodox council has defined it.

Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, vol. 2, *Worship* (Orthodox Church in America). The feast commemorates "the death, resurrection and glorification of Christ's mother", taken into his kingdom in the fullness of her spiritual and bodily existence. It has no biblical or historical sources; it rests on Tradition. Mary died not voluntarily, as her Son did, but by the necessity of a mortal nature bound up with the world's corruption; without personal sin, she still truly needed to be saved by Christ. What is praised in her is offered to every believer (Luke 11:28), so Orthodox tradition calls her the image of the Church. It lives in the feast's hymns and icon.

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md)

### Augsburg Confession article 7

The Church is the congregation of saints in which the Gospel is rightly taught and the sacraments rightly administered, and agreement in these is enough for her true unity.

The Augsburg Confession (1530), article 7 (*Triglot Concordia*, 1921): "And to the true unity of the Church it is enough to agree concerning the doctrine of the Gospel and the administration of the Sacraments. Nor is it necessary that human traditions, that is, rites or ceremonies, instituted by men, should be everywhere alike." The *satis est*, "it is enough", is a sufficiency claim. Melanchthon's *Apology* (1531), articles 7-8: the Church is not only an outward society of rites, like other governments; it is first a fellowship of faith and of the Holy Spirit in hearts, which nevertheless has outward marks by which it is recognized, the pure Gospel and the sacraments administered according to it (§5). The unity meant is "spiritual unity" (§31), which differing human rites no more injure than differing lengths of day and night (§33). The Leuenberg Agreement (16 March 1973), by which Lutheran, Reformed and United churches in Europe declared church fellowship, states it as the Reformers' criterion: agreement in the right teaching of the Gospel and administration of the sacraments is the "necessary and sufficient" condition of true unity (§2); differences of worship and church order do not divide the Church (§28). On this account the Church is not invisible: its marks are public, and agreement in them can be tested.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md); recalled in [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

### Lutheran account of ministry and apostolicity

Apostolicity is the succession of the apostolic Gospel; God instituted the ministry of Word and sacraments, and bishops hold their power by the Word alone.

The Augsburg Confession: God instituted the ministry of the Gospel and the sacraments so that we may obtain faith (art. 5); no one may publicly teach or administer the sacraments unless regularly called (art. 14); article 7 defines the Church by its marks ([above](#augsburg-confession-article-7)); article 28 gives bishops, by divine right, the power to preach, forgive sins, administer the sacraments and judge doctrine, by the Word alone and without human force, with congregations owing them obedience except when they teach against the Gospel. The [Porvoo Common Statement](#porvoo-common-statement) (§57) asks its churches to affirm the apostolic continuity of churches that at some time did not use the episcopal sign.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

### Melanchthon on the Petrine texts

Peter speaks for all the apostles; the keys belong immediately to the Church; the rock is the ministry of Peter's confession.

Melanchthon, *Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope* (Smalcald, 1537), a Lutheran confession, §§22-30: what Matthew 16:19 says to Peter in the singular, 18:18 and John 20:23 say to all in the plural (§23); the keys belong principally and immediately to the Church, not to one person (§24); "this rock" is the ministry of the confession Peter made (§25); "feed" means teaching the Word, which Peter has in common with the other apostles (§30).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Calvin on the keys

The keys are the preaching of the Gospel; nothing is given Peter that his colleagues lack; Christ is the only foundation.

Calvin, *Institutes* IV.6.4-8 (Beveridge, 1845): the keys are the preaching of the Gospel, which opens heaven; binding and loosing are reconciliation by faith and bondage by unbelief (IV.6.4); Peter may be "the first among the faithful", but honour of rank is not possession of power (IV.6.5); the rock is the confession, because Christ is the only foundation (1 Cor 3:11; IV.6.6); his colleagues sent him (Acts 8:14) and Paul rebuked him (Gal 2:11) (IV.6.7); nor will Calvin allow "wresting a single fact into a perpetual enactment" (IV.6.8).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Cullmann on the rock

The rock is Peter himself, but his role is the unrepeatable one of first foundation, with no basis for successors.

Oscar Cullmann, a Lutheran exegete, *Peter: Disciple, Apostle, Martyr* (1952): the confession cannot be detached from the man who made it, so "this rock" is Peter; but the text gives no basis for successors in a foundational role laid once.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)

### Branch theory

The one Catholic Church can be divided in external communion without ceasing to be one: it exists in the Roman, the Oriental and the British churches, none alone catholic.

William Palmer of Worcester College, Oxford, *A Treatise on the Church of Christ* (1838). A society cut off by the great body of Christians is outside the Church; but the Catholic Church itself can be divided in external communion without ceasing to be one. The one Church of the Creed exists in the Roman, the Oriental and the British churches, each holding the apostolic faith with valid ordination and true mission. None may call itself alone catholic; Palmer faults the Roman side for "pretending that their branches of the church are alone catholic". Universality, for him, is moral: adherents in every known nation, never again reduced to a small part of the world.

Stated without reply in [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md); recalled in [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md) beside the "federation" picture of ecumenism.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md)

### Chicago-Lambeth Quadrilateral

Four articles as a basis for approaching reunion, the fourth "the Historic Episcopate, locally adapted".

The Lambeth Conference of 1888, after the American bishops at Chicago in 1886, named the Scriptures, the two Creeds, the two sacraments Christ ordained, and "The Historic Episcopate, locally adapted in the methods of its administration to the varying needs of the nations and peoples called of God into the Unity of His Church."

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

### Porvoo Common Statement

Apostolic succession lies primarily in the apostolic tradition of the whole Church; episcopal succession is a visible, personal sign of it, which does not by itself guarantee fidelity.

Agreed in 1992 by the Anglican churches of Britain and Ireland with Nordic and Baltic Lutherans: succession primarily in the apostolic tradition of the whole Church (§39); episcopal succession a visible, personal way of "focusing the apostolicity of the whole Church" (§46); the sign does not by itself guarantee a church's fidelity, nor the bishop's personal faithfulness (§51); the churches are asked to affirm the apostolic continuity of churches that at some time did not use the episcopal sign (§57).

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md)

### Thirty-Nine Articles article 19

The visible Church is a congregation of faithful men where the pure Word is preached and the sacraments duly ministered; no see guarantees the Church.

The Church of England's Articles (1571), article 19: "The visible Church of Christ is a congregation of faithful men, in the which the pure Word of God is preached, and the Sacraments be duly ministered according to Christ's ordinance ..." Rome, like Jerusalem, Alexandria and Antioch, has erred, not only in living and ceremonies but in matters of faith.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

### Reformation responses to the 2007 Responses

The Lutheran and Reformed bodies answered the CDF in July 2007: fully church where the Gospel is preached and the sacraments administered, by God's gift, not by the Vatican's interpretation.

Ishmael Noko, general secretary of the Lutheran World Federation (11 July 2007): Lutheran churches do not accept the document's understanding of church, "understanding ourselves to be fully church" where the Gospel is rightly preached and the sacraments rightly administered. Setri Nyomi, general secretary of the World Alliance of Reformed Churches, to Cardinal Walter Kasper (10 July 2007): their calling to be part of the church of Jesus Christ is God's gift, "not dependent on the interpretation of the Vatican"; they count the Roman Catholic Church part of that one family.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)

## Pitfalls

### Overstating a level

- **"Every papal utterance is infallible."** *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4 defines four conditions; two papal acts are uncontested; say "two, plus candidates", never a number. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md))*
- **"Co-redemptrix (or Mediatrix of all graces) is Catholic doctrine", "defined", "used by the council".** Never defined; Vatican II refrained; the 2025 note judges "Co-redemptrix" always inappropriate to use. *([6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md), [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md))*
- **"LG 14 names who is lost."** Its exclusion is a conditional with a knowledge condition (*non ignorantes*) and names no one; the axiom is not a census. *([5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md))*
- **"Vatican II defined ..."** the images, the sacramentality of the episcopate, collegiality, inculturation, that bishops succeed the apostles, that other Christians are in communion: it defined nothing new; these are at least rung 3. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md), [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md))*
- **Encyclicals and notes read as definitions:** "*Mystici Corporis* defined the identity"; "the priority of the universal Church is dogma"; "*Mater Populi Fidelis* is a definition"; "Francis's signature made the Final Document infallible". *([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md), [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md), [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md))*
- **Medieval and conciliar texts promoted:** "*Unam Sanctam* defined that kings are subject to the pope"; "*Haec Sancta* is dogma"; "the deposing power must be held"; "*Cantate Domino* defined that every non-Catholic is lost"; "Trent defined the Immaculate Conception"; "perpetual virginity was defined at Lateran 649". *([4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md), [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md))*
- **Rung 2 or 3 called rung 1:** canonization as a dogma of faith; baptism of desire as defined; the secondary-object extension as defined; LG 12 as defining the infallibility of the faithful; LG 53's claims as dogma. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md), [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md))*
- **Below the seam read as teaching:** "Ravenna settled primacy"; "*The Bishop of Rome* is new teaching"; "the Church teaches that she is a sinner" (only the ITC, which has no level, says "in a certain sense"); an approved apparition lending a title authority; a footnote dating a papyrus. *([4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md), [6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md), [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md))*
- **Agreements and acts read as definitions:** "1965 restored communion"; "1999 revoked Trent"; "*Mortalium Animos*'s ban still binds". *([5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md))*
- **Settling an open question by fiat:** "the Church has defined that 'this rock' is Peter's person"; "1950 defined that Mary never died", or "a Catholic must hold that she died"; "the many-subsistences reading is heresy". *([4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md))*
- **"Every bishop teaches it, so it is infallible."** The bishops must agree in proposing it *as definitive*, manifestly; a national conference's "constant faith" is a particular act. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md))*

### Understating a level

- **"Never defined, so optional":** institution by Christ, perpetual virginity, and what the ordinary universal magisterium teaches are rung 1 without a defining act. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md), [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md))*
- **"A pious belief to take or leave":** the Immaculate Conception and the Assumption are rung 1 since 1854 and 1950. *([6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md))*
- **"Pius XII's opinion, which the council dropped"; "*Communionis Notio* is one cardinal's opinion"; "*Mater Populi Fidelis* is one cardinal's opinion"; "Rome's reading of *subsistit* is one opinion among others":** each is rung 3, owed religious submission. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-body-of-christ.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md), [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md))*
- **"Vatican II was pastoral, so collegiality (or the images) is optional":** at least rung 3. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md))*
- **"Perfect society is discarded"; "the whole of *Mortalium Animos* was withdrawn"; "the medieval dignity view is still open":** rung-3 teaching not repealed; doctrine restated elsewhere; a council deliberately taught the contrary. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md))*
- **"Not manifestly defined, so communion with Peter's successor is optional"; "ch. 4 is the dogma and the rest is discipline":** that bond and the jurisdiction are rung 1 by *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 3. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md))*
- **"The Fathers disagree on the rock, so the primacy is open"; "Honorius wrote privately, so the case is closed":** the canons of chs. 1-2 are rung 1; the Honorius record is a hard fact the definitional answer does not erase. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-infallibility-in-exercise-and-the-ordinary-universal-magisterium.md))*
- **Marks and dogmas shrunk:** "one" as an aspiration; catholicity as a figure of speech; a hierarchy of divine ordinance as optional church order; canonization as a mere honour; the *sensus fidelium* as a pious opinion; "a Catholic may not say the Church sinned"; Mary's cooperation as a forbidden opinion. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md), [6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md))*

### Conflating the two conceptions

- **"The Immaculate Conception is the virgin birth."** The virginal conception is Jesus conceived without a human father (creeds, rung 1); the Immaculate Conception is Mary's own conception by her parents without original sin (1854). Neither implies the other. *([6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md))*
- **"Lateran 649 defined the Immaculate Conception."** Its "immaculate" is an epithet of holiness. *([6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md))*
- **"Preserved means unredeemed."** The merits clause is part of the definition: she was redeemed first and best. *([6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md))*
- **"1854 made her sinless all her life" or "exempt from death."** 1854 concerns original sin; lifelong sinlessness is taught separately (at least rung 3); death is left open. *([6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md))*

### Reading the four marks as claims about every member

- **Holy means the members are better people; a grave sinner has left the Church.** The mark is said of the Church from her source; MC 23: not every grave sin severs; but inside is not holy (LG 14, "bodily"). *([2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md))*
- **Catholic means big, or everywhere; a diocese is a branch office.** Whole first, catholic at Pentecost; the whole Church is present in each particular church. *([2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md))*
- **Succession guarantees each bishop's faith and life; succession is a list of names.** The promise is to the whole Church; LG 25 denies individual infallibility; what passes is an office. *([2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md))*
- **One means Christians are already one; schism means false doctrine; every non-Catholic Christian is a heretic or schismatic.** Unity is a gift the Church never loses; schism is refused communion; the canon names acts of a will (UR 3). *([2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md))*
- **Indefectibility means the Church cannot do great wrong.** It promises endurance in faith and sacraments; impeccability is claimed of no one. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md))*

### Treating the sensus fidelium as a poll

- **A majority of Catholics, or a survey, is the *sensus fidelium*.** It belongs to the whole body, pastors included, and is never simply public opinion (LG 12; ITC *Sensus Fidei* §§113-118; Final Document §22). *([3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md))*
- **Reception ratifies a definition.** Definitions are irreformable "of themselves"; reception recognizes, it does not vote. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md))*
- **The 1946 consultation was a vote.** The bishops reported their people's faith, and the bull reads it as the ordinary magisterium agreeing with that faith. *([6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md))*
- **A Synod majority binds the pope; collegiality means majority rule.** The Synod counsels (can. 343); the college has no authority without its head. *([4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md))*

### Corpus permixtum as an excuse

- **"Wheat and cockle until the harvest" to keep an abuser in ministry.** Matthew 13:30 concerns the final sorting; Augustine never used it against discipline; transfers and closed files were acts of governance. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md))*
- **Each standard answer is true and each stops somewhere:** indefectibility at conduct, the mixed body at policy, the holiness of the Church at the offices, *semper purificanda* at who answers. An answer that settles the theology before it speaks to the victim is the pastorally false one. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md))*
- **Ambrose's *casta meretrix* as a patristic verdict that the Church is a sinner; "*ecclesia semper reformanda*" as Vatican II.** The first is a Church made chaste; the second is in no conciliar text. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-holy.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md))*
- **The Day of Pardon as the Church's response to the abuse crisis.** It preceded the Boston revelations and prayed for abused minors only in general terms. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-a-church-of-sinners.md))*

### Volunteering a rebuttal

- **Stating another tradition and then answering it.** Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed and Anglican positions are stated as their own theologians state them, with authors, and no reply attached; the argument is `apologetics-catholic-claims`'s. A reply volunteered inside the statement is a wrong turn. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md), [2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-apostolic.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-vatican-ii-primacy-collegiality-synodality.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md))*
- **Stating it below its strength:** "the Lutheran confessions teach an invisible Church" (the *Apology* gives outward marks); "the Orthodox reject Peter" (they read his succession as every bishop's who keeps his confession); "the Reformation communities deny they are churches" (they hold themselves fully church). *([2.1](lessons/02-01-one.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md))*

### Words that mislead

- ***Ekklesia* means "the called-out ones"** (it means assembly); ***Petros* a pebble, *petra* a cliff** (*Kepha* stands behind both); **Matthew 18:18 gives the keys to all** (it gives binding and loosing, never keys). *([1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md))*
- **"Like a sacrament" means an eighth sacrament**; **"People of God" means the laity**; **communion is an invisible bond or a mood.** *([1.4](lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md))*
- **"The universal Church" means the Vatican**; **an Eastern Catholic church is a rite of the Latin Church.** *([3.2](lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md))*
- **"Successor of the apostles" means successor of an apostle; appointment makes a bishop; "not vicars of the pope" means independent of him.** *([3.1](lessons/03-01-bishops-and-apostolic-succession.md))*
- **"Essence, not degree" makes the ordained a superior kind of Christian; the secular character keeps the laity out of the Church's life.** *([3.3](lessons/03-03-the-laity-charisms-and-the-sensus-fidei.md))*
- **"Ordinary and immediate" makes the pope every diocese's real bishop; *ex sese* means apart from the Church.** *([4.3](lessons/04-03-vatican-i-and-pastor-aeternus.md))*
- ***Subsistit* is a synonym of *est*, or "one of several places the Church exists"; "ecclesial community" means outside the Church.** *([5.2](lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md))*
- **"Both sides to blame" means equal blame; ecumenism gave up *subsistit*; the hierarchy of truths makes lower truths optional.** *([5.3](lessons/05-03-ecumenism-as-doctrine.md))*
- **Hyperdulia is "worship, only less"; "Mediatrix" is now banned.** *([6.5](lessons/06-05-mary-in-the-economy-in-the-church-and-in-devotion.md))*

### History read as doctrine, doctrine as history

- **A doctrine is as young as its treatise; two verses show Jesus intended no Church; the Church was "born at Pentecost" as a new start.** *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-ecclesiology-asks.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-church-in-the-new-testament.md))*
- **The forgeries created the papacy, or don't matter.** They served older claims, and they inflated exactly the evidence anticipation needs. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-the-development-of-papal-claims-read-honestly.md))*
- **1939 reversed a doctrine** (the Chinese-rites rulings were discipline); **the axiom was aimed first at non-Christians** (at baptized rivals); **the 1949 letter loosened it for Catholics** (it tightened it); **baptism of desire is a modern loophole** (fifteen centuries old). *([2.3](lessons/02-03-catholic.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-outside-the-church-there-is-no-salvation.md))*
- **A type is proof, or decoration; "every early Christian held all three moments"; Aquinas a heretic after 1854; the Assumption stands or falls with the *Transitus* stories.** *([6.1](lessons/06-01-theotokos-and-mary-in-scripture-and-the-fathers.md), [6.2](lessons/06-02-ever-virgin.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-immaculate-conception.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-the-assumption.md))*

## Conventions

- **One card per course**, covering every lesson. The linter checks that every lesson file is cited somewhere on this card.
- **Intuition first**, as in the lessons: a plain-English line before any Latin, Greek or canon.
- **Levels exactly as the lessons mark them.** Where a lesson says "at least rung 3", "not manifestly defined" or "disputed, no level", the card says the same; it never rounds a level up or down.
- **Other traditions are stated, never answered.** The [Other traditions](#other-traditions-in-their-own-words) entries carry no reply.
- **Headings are anchors.** Renaming a `###` breaks inbound lesson links. Headings are ASCII only (no italics, parentheses, dashes, Greek or diacritics); Latin and Greek terms appear in the body.
- **Boss problems are reserved.** Entries touching boss material stay at reference level: the texts, their levels and the positions are here; numbered-premise reconstructions, side-by-side word comparisons, the marks-strain table and other ready answers are not.
- **Modern authors and post-1930 magisterial texts are paraphrased**, cited by work, year and, where verified, section; DH numbers only where the lessons verified them.
- **No prose dollar signs** (see CLAUDE.md). Length is not capped the way a lesson's is: this is a lookup surface, not a read-through.
