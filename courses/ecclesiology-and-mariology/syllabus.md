# The Church & Mary — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 2 · ~25 lessons · Prereqs: [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md), [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `ecclesiology-and-mariology`

## Goal

This course teaches what the Catholic Church holds about herself, and about the woman the tradition calls the type and the mother of the Church — taught from within the tradition, with the weight of every claim marked, and with the hard parts told straight. You will finish able to say what the Church *is* before saying what she does: mystery, Body of Christ, People of God, sacrament of salvation, and to use each image for the work it does and no more; to state the four marks precisely enough to say what each asserts of the Church as such and what it does not assert of every member; to lay out the hierarchical constitution — bishops, apostolic succession, the particular church and the universal Church — and the place of the laity, the charisms and the *sensus fidei* in it; to read *Pastor Aeternus* line by line, list every condition its definition attaches to an infallible papal act, and name the things careless readers think it defined and it did not; to trace the development of papal claims from the second century to 1870 with the difficulties conceded rather than smoothed; to hold *extra ecclesiam nulla salus* together with *Lumen Gentium* 14–16 and say exactly what changed in the words and what did not; to say what *subsistit in* claims about the churches and ecclesial communities not in full communion, and to state their own self-understanding as their theologians state it; to say what the tradition can and cannot say about a Church whose members, including her ministers, have done grave evil; and to state each of the four Marian dogmas with its defining act, its precise content, its level, and the history of its development — including the centuries in which great theologians denied what is now defined.

It deliberately skips a great deal that neighbouring courses own. **It does not argue.** Every dispute with Orthodoxy and the Reformation traditions — primacy, the filioque, Mary, purgatory, *sola scriptura* — is argued in `apologetics-catholic-claims`, which takes this course as a prerequisite for exactly that reason; here those positions are *stated fairly and left standing*, because you cannot argue with a position you cannot state. The events — the councils, the schism, the Reformation, Vatican I's convocation, the abuse crisis — belong to [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md), which is read and cited. The sacraments, the liturgy, Holy Orders as a sacrament, the Eucharist as the act that builds the Church, and the reservation of priestly ordination belong to `sacraments-and-liturgy`. Christ as Head and the grace of the Head belong to [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md); the levels of doctrinal authority to [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md); grace, the communion of saints and the last things to `creation-grace-last-things`. It also skips canon law as a discipline, the Church's social mission (owned by [`catholic-social-teaching`](../catholic-social-teaching/syllabus.md)), religious life and the consecrated state, particular-church governance, and any survey of Protestant or Orthodox ecclesiology for its own sake.

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md). Every part declares its kind; every solution is a rubric (must-hit moves, wrong turns) plus a model answer.

- **No apologetic problems.** This is the course's defining constraint and the reason `apologetics-catholic-claims` exists downstream. Where another tradition's position appears — the Orthodox account of primacy, the Reformation communities' self-understanding, the Anglican and Lutheran readings of apostolic succession — the student is asked to **state it as its own best defenders state it, and stop.** That statement is graded strictly, as exegesis; a reply earns nothing, and volunteering one is a wrong turn the rubric names.
- **Exegetical parts are strict, and authority is the thing most often got wrong.** What *Pastor Aeternus* defines and what it merely says; whether the Assumption's definition settles that Mary died; that the Immaculate Conception is *de fide* and Co-redemptrix is not a defined title; that *Lumen Gentium* 14's clause about those who know and refuse is not a claim about any named person. Overstating a level is an error, and so is understating one.
- **Evaluative parts, verdict-neutral, are kept for questions genuinely open among Catholics:** whether the move from *Mystici Corporis* to *subsistit in* is development or change; whether the universal Church is ontologically prior to the particular churches (the Ratzinger–Kasper exchange); which features of the papal office are of divine institution and which are historically conditioned and could change (the question *Ut Unum Sint* itself puts); whether the laity's share in the prophetic office amounts to a share in teaching; whether "Co-redemptrix" should ever be defined; how much of Marian devotion's expansion is growth and how much accretion.
- **Close reading (archetype 7), public domain only:** *Pastor Aeternus* in the translation in Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*; the Creed and conciliar texts in Percival's *The Seven Ecumenical Councils* (*Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, second series, vol. 14); Cyprian, Augustine against the Donatists, and the other Fathers in the *Ante-Nicene* and *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*; Aquinas in the English Dominican Province translation; Newman's *Letter to Pusey* (1866) and *Letter to the Duke of Norfolk* (1875); Florence and Trent in pre-1930 translations; *Ineffabilis Deus* (1854); scripture in the Douay–Rheims (Challoner), with the lesson's own translation where a Greek word decides the reading (*ekklēsia*, *petra*, *poimaine*, *hyperdulia* is Latinized Greek and gets a note). **Vatican II, *Mystici Corporis*, *Munificentissimus Deus*, *Marialis Cultus*, *Redemptoris Mater*, *Ut Unum Sint* and every Dicastery document are paraphrased and cited by name**, never quoted from a copyrighted translation.
- **Diagnose (archetype 4), exegetical.** Invented parish bulletins, homilies, diocesan press releases and catechist's handouts that get the ecclesiology wrong — "the institutional Church is a human add-on to the real Church"; "the pope can define anything he likes"; "the Church is holy, so the scandal was a media invention"; "we honour Mary the way we honour God". Invented material is never attributed to a real person.
- **Find the crux (archetype 5), exegetical.** Cyprian against the lapsed party on where the Church is; Augustine against the Donatists; Aquinas against Scotus on the Immaculate Conception; Ratzinger against Kasper on the priority of the universal Church; the Vatican I majority against the minority on *ex sese*.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Say what the Church is before saying what she does, and use each of the great images — Body of Christ, People of God, temple of the Spirit, sacrament of salvation — for the work it actually does, naming the error each one alone would produce
- [ ] State the four marks precisely: what each asserts of the Church as such, what it does not assert of every member, and which mark each historic division puts under strain
- [ ] Explain the hierarchical constitution — the sacramentality of the episcopate, the three *munera*, apostolic succession, the college and its head — and say what succession requires and what it does not
- [ ] Distinguish the particular church, the local church and the universal Church, and lay out the Ratzinger–Kasper dispute over priority with each side at full strength
- [ ] State what the common priesthood of the faithful is, how it differs from the ministerial priesthood in kind and not only in degree, what a charism is and who judges it — and decide, on the Church's own criteria, whether a given widespread opinion counts as an expression of the *sensus fidelium*
- [ ] Read the Petrine texts (Matthew 16:17–19, Luke 22:31–32, John 21:15–17) and say what each is argued to establish, stating the Orthodox and Reformation readings as their own theologians state them
- [ ] Trace the development of papal claims from the second century to 1870 honestly, conceding what the historical record makes hard, and apply Newman's notes to the trajectory
- [ ] Read *Pastor Aeternus* line by line: every condition attached to an infallible act, what "of themselves and not from the consent of the Church" denies, what chapter 3 claims for jurisdiction, and the three things readers wrongly think were defined
- [ ] Say which papal acts are uncontestedly *ex cathedra*, what the ordinary universal magisterium is, and how Vatican II sets primacy and collegiality together
- [ ] Hold *extra ecclesiam nulla salus* together with *Lumen Gentium* 14–16 — saying what necessity is predicated of whom in each historic formulation — and say what *subsistit in* claims about the churches and ecclesial communities not in full communion, stating their own self-understanding accurately
- [ ] Say what the tradition can and cannot claim about a Church whose members and ministers have done grave evil, without a defensive move the facts do not support
- [ ] State each of the four Marian dogmas with its defining act, its exact content, its level, and the two or three things it does not assert — and tell the history of the Immaculate Conception including the centuries in which Aquinas denied it

## Modules

### Module 1: The Church as mystery

Before the Church is an institution she is something believed. This module fixes the images the rest of the course runs on, and the two texts — one from 1943, one from 1964 — that every later lesson cites.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What ecclesiology asks | Say what the treatise is for, why it is young, and how to weigh an ecclesiological claim | the Church as an article of the Creed before a subject of study; why there was no separate treatise before the late Middle Ages, and what Bellarmine's definition of the Church as a society as visible as the Republic of Venice bought and cost; the nineteenth-century recovery (Möhler and the Tübingen school, Newman) and the *ressourcement* (Congar, de Lubac) behind Vatican II; Dulles's five models — institution, mystical communion, sacrament, herald, servant — as models rather than parties; assigning a level to an ecclesiological claim ([`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4); the map of what this course cedes |
| 1.2 | The Church in the New Testament | Say what the New Testament means by *ekklēsia*, and state what the Church holds about her own foundation | *qahal* and *ekklēsia*: the assembly called by God; Jesus's preaching of the kingdom and the relation of kingdom to Church; the calling of the Twelve as the seed of a structured community, and the number twelve as a claim about Israel; Pentecost as the Church's manifestation; the images Paul and John use and the local-universal double reference of *ekklēsia* in Paul; what the Church teaches about her institution by Christ, and what is left to historical inquiry ([`new-testament`](../new-testament/syllabus.md) 2.1–2.2 for the exegesis) |
| 1.3 | The Body of Christ | State what the Body of Christ image asserts, and read *Mystici Corporis* precisely | 1 Corinthians 12 and Romans 12 (the members and the one body) beside Ephesians and Colossians (Christ as Head over the body); Christ as Head and the grace of the Head taken from [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md) 3.4; the Spirit as the soul of the Church in the tradition's language, and what that analogy may not be pressed to mean ([`trinity-and-god`](../trinity-and-god/syllabus.md) 4.5); Pius XII's *Mystici Corporis* (1943): the Mystical Body as visible and juridical as well as mystical, the identification it makes with the Roman Catholic Church, membership and the categories it uses for those outside; the level at which an encyclical teaches ([`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.5) |

**Boss problem 1:** (a) From *Summa Theologiae* III q.8 aa.1–3 (English Dominican Province), reconstruct in numbered premises Aquinas's argument that Christ is the Head of the Church, and state exactly whom he counts as belonging to the body on that account. Mark the premise that carries the grace of the Head ([`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md) 3.4). *(Exegetical.)* (b) In paraphrase, state what *Mystici Corporis* teaches about the relation between the Mystical Body and the Roman Catholic Church, and what *Lumen Gentium* 8 says with *subsistit in*. Set the two sentences side by side and say precisely what differs in the words — not yet what follows. *(Exegetical, strict.)* (c) Diagnose an invented parish bulletin paragraph arguing that "the real Church is the community of people who love each other; the institution with its offices and rules is a later human addition." Overstating either document's level fails the part even if the verdict is well defended. *(Evaluative.)*

### Module 2: The four marks

One, holy, catholic, apostolic. Each is confessed in the Creed, each is under visible strain, and each says something about the Church as such that it does not say about every member.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | One | Say what the Church's unity consists in, and what divides Christians without dividing Christ | unity of faith, of sacramental life, and of governance as the tradition's three bonds; unity as a gift and as a task; Cyprian's *On the Unity of the Catholic Church* (the images of ch. 5: one sun with many rays, one tree with many branches) and what his controversy was actually about; John 17:21 and unity as Christ's own prayer; schism distinguished from heresy and from apostasy; full communion and partial communion as categories, set up here and used in 5.2–5.3 |
| 2.2 | Holy | Explain how a Church whose members sin can be confessed as holy | the source of the Church's holiness: Christ and the Spirit, not the virtue of the members; the Donatist crisis and Augustine's answer — the *corpus permixtum*, the wheat and the tares, holiness that does not depend on the minister (the sacramental-validity question ceded to `sacraments-and-liturgy`, the texts to [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) 4.4); *Lumen Gentium* ch. 5 on the universal call to holiness; *Lumen Gentium* 8 on the Church as at once holy and always in need of purification, and what *semper purificanda* commits the tradition to; the patristic language of the Church as *casta meretrix*, gathered in the twentieth century by Balthasar; canonization as the Church's judgment that holiness is real and achieved |
| 2.3 | Catholic | Say what catholicity claims, and separate the claim from the statistics | *kath' holou*: wholeness before geography; the earliest attested use of "catholic Church" in Ignatius's *Smyrnaeans* and what it means there (the textual question ceded to [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) Boss 1); catholicity as fullness of means of salvation, and as the capacity to receive every culture without being captured by one; the particular churches as the places in which the one Church exists, not as branches of it; inculturation and its limits; catholicity strained: a Church of European shape becoming a Church of the global South |
| 2.4 | Apostolic | State what apostolicity requires — in doctrine, in worship, and in ministry | apostolicity of origin, of doctrine, and of succession; Irenaeus and the public succession lists as an argument from visibility against secret tradition ([`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) 2.2); what succession is held to transmit, and what it is not held to guarantee; the apostolic college and the episcopal college as its continuation; the Orthodox and the Anglican and Lutheran accounts of apostolic succession stated as their own theologians state them, and left standing; the closure of public revelation with the apostolic age ([`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 1.4) |

**Boss problem 2:** Supplied passages, all public domain: Cyprian, *On the Unity of the Catholic Church*, chs. 5–6 (*Ante-Nicene Fathers*) — **not** the disputed ch. 4, whose two recensions belong to [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) Boss 2 — and a passage from Augustine against the Donatists (*Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*). (a) Close-read Cyprian chs. 5–6: state his argument that unity is not optional, list the images he uses and say what each is doing, and name the words that carry the claim. *(Exegetical.)* (b) Reconstruct in numbered premises Augustine's argument that the Church's holiness does not depend on the holiness of her ministers or members, and state the ecclesiological principle it establishes. Say which premise a Donatist must deny. *(Exegetical.)* (c) For each of the four marks, state in one sentence what it asserts of the Church as such and in one sentence what it does not assert of every member; then say which mark each of these puts under most visible strain: the East–West schism, the Reformation, the Church's cultural provincialism, and a bishop who teaches error. *(Exegetical.)* (d) In 300 words, any verdict: is "holy" a claim about the Church's present condition or only about her source and her destiny? State the strongest form of a Donatist-style objection in contemporary dress, give the tradition's answer, and say what the answer licenses and what it does not. A reply that avoids naming real failures fails the rubric. *(Evaluative.)*

### Module 3: The constitution of the Church

Who holds office, where the Church exists, and what the baptized who hold no office actually are. The papacy waits for Module 4; everything else about the Church's structure is here.

### Module 4: The Petrine office

The course's hardest module, and the one the prerequisite in [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) exists for. The texts, then the development told without flinching, then the definition read word by word, then how it is actually exercised, then what Vatican II did with it.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |

## Notes

- **Stance.** Taught from within the tradition, with authority marked, and historically honest. The hard things are said as hard: that the papacy's claims grew, and that documents used in their service included forgeries; that a pope was condemned by an ecumenical council; that a majority at Vatican I got its definition over the objection of a minority that left rather than vote against it; that Aquinas denied what is now defined about Mary's conception; that *Mystici Corporis* and *Lumen Gentium* do not say the same words about the Mystical Body and the Catholic Church; and that a Church confessed holy concealed the sexual abuse of children for decades. Not one of these is softened before it is stated, and none of them is stated in order to score a point.
- **The no-argument rule, and why it matters.** This course states and does not argue. That is not timidity: `apologetics-catholic-claims` takes this course and `sacraments-and-liturgy` as prerequisites precisely so that it can argue from a base where the Catholic doctrine has been taught properly and the other traditions' positions have been stated without a reply attached. Problems here therefore have **no apologetic parts at all**, which is unusual in this field and deliberate. Where a rubric asks for another tradition's position, it grades fidelity alone and names "volunteering a rebuttal" as a wrong turn.
- **Authority discipline for this course's hardest claims.** (1) The Church's institution by Christ, her four marks, the primacy and infallibility as defined by Vatican I, and the four Marian dogmas are taught at the levels the acts themselves fix, using the ladder in [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4 and nothing else. (2) *Pastor Aeternus* ch. 4 defines conditions; it does not make every papal utterance infallible, and lessons say so in the same breath as the definition. (3) Beyond 1854 and 1950 the list of *ex cathedra* acts is disputed, and lessons say so rather than list. (4) *Subsistit in* is conciliar teaching; what precisely it changed relative to *Mystici Corporis* is argued among Catholic theologians and is therefore evaluative. (5) Co-redemptrix and Mediatrix of all graces are **not** defined; treating them as dogma is an exegetical error graded strictly, and so is treating them as forbidden opinions. (6) Whether the universal Church is prior to the particular churches is freely disputed. (7) The *sensus fidei* is never invoked in this course as a warrant for a conclusion without the criteria from [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 5.3 being applied first.
- **Accuracy: verify before writing each lesson, never assert from memory.** Section numbers in *Lumen Gentium* beyond those corroborated across the field's syllabi (1, 8, 12, 14–16, 22–23, 25, and the chapter divisions) and in *Unitatis Redintegratio* beyond 11; every DH number; the exact wording and chapter structure of *Pastor Aeternus* in Schaff; the date, addressee and content of the German bishops' 1875 collective declaration and the form of Pius IX's approval; the date and addressee of the 1949 Holy Office letter in the Feeney case; the canon or session numbers for Lateran IV's *Firmiter*, Florence's *Laetentur Caeli* and *Cantate Domino*, and the Lateran Synod of 649 on Mary ever-virgin; whether Constantinople II's anathemas use *aeiparthenos* and in which anathema; the exact operative clauses of *Ineffabilis Deus* and *Munificentissimus Deus*; the article numbers within ST III q.8 and q.27; the title, publishing body and date of the 2024 study document on the bishop of Rome and of the Ravenna document (2007); the name and date of the 1992 CDF letter on the Church as communion; the date and title of the 2018 Letter to the People of God and of the 2019 motu proprio on reporting abuse; and every Catechism paragraph. Where a number cannot be confirmed, the document is named without it — the rule the whole field follows.
- **Ownership collisions found and resolved.** (1) [`apologetics-foundations`](../apologetics-foundations/syllabus.md) 8.2 already teaches *extra ecclesiam nulla salus* with Cyprian, *Cantate Domino*, the Feeney letter and *Lumen Gentium* 14–16, and its Boss 8 (c) sets *Lumen Gentium* 16 beside *Cantate Domino*. This is the one real overlap in the field. The split adopted: that course argues the axiom **outward**, against pluralism and toward those who have never heard the Gospel, and takes *Dominus Iesus* as its instrument; this course teaches it **inward** — what "the Church" names in the sentence, what *subsistit in* does to it, and where baptized non-Catholics stand, which that course does not treat at all. Module 5's centre of gravity is 5.2 and 5.3, neither of which that course touches; 5.1 covers the same texts from the ecclesiological side and cites 8.2 rather than repeating its argument. Neither course is a prerequisite of the other, so 5.1 restates the minimum and points there. (2) [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) Boss 1 already works the offices in 1 Clement, the Didache and Ignatius, and Boss 2 the two recensions of Cyprian ch. 4 and Irenaeus 3.3.2; this course reads Cyprian chs. 5–6 and Augustine against the Donatists instead, and cites those boss problems for the textual work. Its Boss 5 (d) gathers the patristic readings of "this rock"; 4.1 cites that gathering rather than redoing it. (3) [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) cedes the theology of the papacy, conciliarism's theological status and the drafting of *Pastor Aeternus* here, and its Boss 5 hands over the theological status of *Unam Sanctam*'s closing sentence; both are in 4.2, and 4.3 reads the constitution its 8.2 declines to read. (4) [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md) cedes capital grace worked out as ecclesiology, the Marian dogmas and *Theotokos* as a Marian title; 1.3 and 6.1 take them up in those exact terms. (5) [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) keeps the conditions for infallibility as a rule of assent and cedes the papacy as an institution and the history of *Pastor Aeternus*; 4.3 reads the text, 4.4 teaches the exercise, and neither re-derives the assent logic. (6) [`new-testament`](../new-testament/syllabus.md) and [`old-testament`](../old-testament/syllabus.md) both name this course in their scope tables for Matthew 16, the Marian dogmas, Revelation 12 and Marian typology. (7) [`catholic-social-teaching`](../catholic-social-teaching/syllabus.md) owns the lay apostolate in the world; 3.3 keeps only the lay state within the Church's constitution. (8) No built lesson anywhere in the library touches an ecclesiological or Marian topic.
- **Where this goes next.** `sacraments-and-liturgy` takes this course whole as its prerequisite: 3.1 for the episcopate, 3.2 for the Eucharistic ecclesiology it will complete, 2.2 for the Donatist principle behind sacramental validity. `apologetics-catholic-claims` takes 4.1–4.5 and Module 6 and argues what this course only states, and takes 5.2's fair statements of other traditions' positions as its starting material. `creation-grace-last-things` takes 1.4's pilgrim Church, 5.1's necessity of the Church, and 6.3's presupposition about original sin.
- **Lesson count.** The roadmap estimated ~22; the syllabus runs 25 (+14%, inside the band). The steering names two full treatises. The papacy needs five lessons because the texts, the development, the definition, the exercise and Vatican II's rebalancing are five separate skills, and collapsing any pair of them is how this topic is usually taught badly. Mary needs five because each of the four dogmas has a distinct defining act and a distinct development, and LG ch. 8 plus devotion is a fifth question. Merges already made to hold the line: the four marks are four lessons and not eight, with holiness carrying the Donatist material and the sinful-Church question deferred to 5.4 where the abuse crisis belongs; collegiality sits in 4.5 rather than doubling a lesson in Module 3, which is therefore three lessons; ecumenism is one lesson, not a module. If a lesson overflows when built, split it and merge elsewhere rather than grow past 26.
