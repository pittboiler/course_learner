# Organic Chemistry I & II — Syllabus

> Chemistry · Tier 1 · ~23 lessons · Prereqs: [general-chemistry](../general-chemistry/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `organic-chemistry`

## Goal

This course makes you fluent in the logic of carbon: given a structure you can predict how it reacts, and given a reaction you can push the arrows that show *why*. You start where all of organic chemistry actually lives — the electrons — building hybridization, resonance, and acidity into instincts, then reading three-dimensional shape well enough to tell two mirror-image molecules apart. From there it is one idea, relentlessly reused: nucleophiles find electrophiles, and a curved arrow tracks every pair of electrons that moves. You will run that idea through substitution, elimination, and addition; through aromatic rings and the whole carbonyl family; and finally backward — retrosynthesis — to plan a molecule instead of merely explaining one. It closes by letting spectra (IR, NMR, mass spec) hand you an unknown structure, and by tasting how these same rules build sugars, amino acids, and lipids. It deliberately skips advanced total synthesis and the depth of transition-metal organometallic catalysis — here the toolkit is the classical mechanisms, used well.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Assign hybridization and predict geometry, then draw the resonance structures that show where charge and reactivity really sit
- [ ] Rank acids or bases by comparing conjugate-base stability, and read a $\mathrm{p}K_a$ table to predict which way a proton transfer goes
- [ ] Draw Newman projections and chair conformations and identify the lowest-energy conformer
- [ ] Assign $R/S$ configuration and classify any pair of stereoisomers as enantiomers, diastereomers, or a meso compound
- [ ] Push curved arrows through a multistep mechanism, tracking every electron pair, charge, and intermediate
- [ ] Predict whether a substrate reacts by SN1, SN2, E1, or E2 from substrate, nucleophile/base, and solvent
- [ ] Predict the regiochemistry and stereochemistry of electrophilic additions to alkenes and alkynes (Markovnikov, anti vs. syn)
- [ ] Judge whether a ring is aromatic with Hückel's rule and predict the product and orientation of electrophilic aromatic substitution
- [ ] Predict the outcome of nucleophilic addition to aldehydes/ketones and nucleophilic acyl substitution on carboxylic-acid derivatives
- [ ] Use enolate chemistry (aldol, alkylation) to form new carbon–carbon bonds at the α-carbon
- [ ] Determine an unknown structure from its degrees of unsaturation, IR bands, and ¹H/¹³C NMR spectra
- [ ] Plan a short synthesis by retrosynthetic analysis, choosing reactions that set the right bonds and functional groups

## Modules

### Module 1: Structure, Bonding & Stereochemistry

Everything downstream is a consequence of where the electrons are and how the atoms sit in space. This module builds the two literacies the rest of the course assumes: reading electronic structure (hybridization, resonance, acidity) and reading three-dimensional shape (conformation, chirality).

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Bonding, hybridization & molecular shape | Assign $sp^3/sp^2/sp$ and predict geometry and bond angles | atomic vs. hybrid orbitals, sigma vs. pi bonds, hybridization–geometry–angle map, bond-line structures |
| 1.2 | Resonance, formal charge & delocalization | Draw and weight resonance structures and locate real charge | formal charge, curved arrows for delocalization, major vs. minor contributors, resonance hybrid |
| 1.3 | Acids & bases, the organic way | Rank acidity/basicity by conjugate-base stability | $\mathrm{p}K_a$, conjugate acid/base, ARIO (atom, resonance, induction, orbital), predicting equilibrium direction |
| 1.4 | Alkanes & conformational analysis | Draw Newman/chair forms and pick the lowest-energy conformer | rotation about C–C, staggered vs. eclipsed, torsional/steric strain, chair flips, axial vs. equatorial |
| 1.5 | Chirality & the $R/S$ system | Find stereocenters and assign absolute configuration | chirality, stereocenter, Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priorities, $R/S$, wedge/dash notation |
| 1.6 | Diastereomers, meso compounds & optical activity | Classify stereoisomer relationships and predict optical activity | enantiomers vs. diastereomers, meso & internal mirror planes, optical rotation, Fischer projections |

**Boss problem 1:** Consider 2,3-dibromobutane, $\text{CH}_3\text{-CHBr-CHBr-CH}_3$. (a) How many distinct stereoisomers exist? (b) Assign $R/S$ to each stereocenter of every isomer and identify the meso compound. (c) State every pairwise relationship (enantiomers vs. diastereomers) and say which isomers are optically active. *(Target: two stereocenters would allow $2^2=4$, but internal symmetry collapses this to **three** stereoisomers. The $(2R,3R)$ and $(2S,3S)$ forms are a pair of enantiomers, both optically active (they rotate plane-polarized light equally and oppositely). The $(2R,3S)$ form is **meso**: it has an internal mirror plane, so it is superimposable on its mirror image and is optically inactive. Each of the chiral pair is a **diastereomer** of the meso form. Priorities at C2: $\text{Br} > \text{C3(bearing Br)} > \text{C1(}\text{CH}_3) > \text{H}$, so meso requires opposite descriptors — $(2R,3S)$.)*

### Module 2: Reactions I — Substitution, Elimination & Addition

Now the payoff of Module 1: one idea drives it all — electron-rich nucleophiles attack electron-poor electrophiles, and a curved arrow records each pair of electrons that moves. This module installs that language and runs it through the four workhorse aliphatic reactions and the additions of alkenes and alkynes.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Functional groups & the language of mechanisms | Read the functional-group map and push clean curved arrows | functional-group landscape, nucleophile/electrophile, curved-arrow bookkeeping, bond-making/breaking, leaving groups |
| 2.2 | Nucleophilic substitution: SN2 | Predict SN2 rate and the stereochemical inversion | backside attack, concerted mechanism, second-order kinetics, Walden inversion, steric effects, nucleophile strength |
| 2.3 | SN1 & carbocation rearrangements | Predict SN1 via carbocation stability and racemization | ionization, carbocation stability (3° > 2° > 1°), racemization, 1,2-hydride/methyl shifts, solvent effects |
| 2.4 | Elimination (E1/E2) & choosing among the four | Predict E1 vs. E2 and settle the substitution–elimination competition | β-hydrogen, Zaitsev vs. Hofmann, anti-periplanar E2, base strength/bulk, the SN1/SN2/E1/E2 decision grid |
| 2.5 | Alkenes: electrophilic addition | Predict regio- and stereochemistry of additions to C=C | Markovnikov's rule, carbocation vs. bridged-ion pathways, hydrohalogenation, acid hydration, halogenation (anti), hydroboration & oxymercuration contrasts |
| 2.6 | Alkynes & a first look at radicals | Add across triple bonds and read a chain-radical mechanism | terminal vs. internal alkynes, addition to alkynes, keto–enol tautomerism, radical initiation/propagation/termination, anti-Markovnikov HBr |

**Boss problem 2:** (a) Predict the major product and name the mechanism when 2-bromo-2-methylbutane is treated with (i) sodium ethoxide in hot ethanol and (ii) warm ethanol alone. (b) Take the alkene from (a)(i) and add HBr; separately, subject it to acid-catalyzed hydration. Give both products and justify the regiochemistry. *(Target: (a)(i) tertiary substrate + strong base → **E2**, Zaitsev major = **2-methyl-2-butene** (the more-substituted alkene) over 2-methyl-1-butene. (a)(ii) weak nucleophile/base in polar-protic solvent → **SN1/E1** through the tertiary carbocation, giving 2-ethoxy-2-methylbutane plus 2-methyl-2-butene. (b) HBr adds **Markovnikov** — proton to the less-substituted carbon, $\text{Br}^-$ to the tertiary carbocation — regenerating **2-bromo-2-methylbutane**; hydration likewise puts OH on the tertiary carbon → **2-methyl-2-butanol**. Both are governed by forming the most stable (tertiary) carbocation. The loop back to the starting halide is the point: addition and elimination are microscopic reverses.)*

### Module 3: Reactions II — Aromatics, Carbonyls & Functional Groups

The same nucleophile-meets-electrophile logic, now on the two richest platforms in organic chemistry: the aromatic ring and the carbonyl group. This module covers what makes a ring aromatic and how it substitutes, the full carbonyl family from aldehydes to acid derivatives, α-carbon (enolate) chemistry, and the heteroatom functional groups that tie it together.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Aromaticity & Hückel's rule | Decide whether a ring is aromatic, antiaromatic, or neither | cyclic conjugation, planarity, $4n+2$ π electrons, benzene's stability, aromatic ions & heterocycles |
| 3.2 | Electrophilic aromatic substitution | Run the EAS mechanism and predict rate and orientation | arenium (sigma) intermediate, nitration/halogenation/Friedel–Crafts, activators vs. deactivators, o/p- vs. m-directors |
| 3.3 | Aldehydes & ketones: nucleophilic addition | Predict addition products at the carbonyl carbon | carbonyl polarity, tetrahedral intermediate, hydrate/acetal formation, imine/enamine, cyanohydrin, hydride & Grignard addition |
| 3.4 | Carboxylic acids & derivatives: nucleophilic acyl substitution | Rank derivatives and predict addition–elimination outcomes | acyl chloride/anhydride/ester/amide reactivity order, addition–elimination, Fischer esterification, saponification, hydrolysis |
| 3.5 | Enols & enolates: α-carbon chemistry | Form C–C bonds at the α-carbon | α-hydrogen acidity, keto–enol tautomerism, enolate formation, aldol addition/condensation, α-alkylation |
| 3.6 | Alcohols, ethers & amines | Convert among and react the key heteroatom groups | alcohols as acids/nucleophiles & their oxidation, ether synthesis & epoxide opening, amine basicity & nucleophilicity |

**Boss problem 3:** (a) Rank phenol, toluene, benzene, and nitrobenzene by reactivity toward electrophilic aromatic substitution, and give the major mononitration product of toluene with its regiochemical reason. (b) Write the product of the base-catalyzed self-aldol of acetaldehyde ($\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}$), then its dehydration product, and name the C–C bond-forming step. *(Target: (a) reactivity **phenol > toluene > benzene > nitrobenzene** — $\text{–OH}$ is a strong activator, $\text{–CH}_3$ a weak activator, $\text{–NO}_2$ a strong deactivator. Both $\text{–OH}$ and $\text{–CH}_3$ are **ortho/para directors**; toluene's major product is **p-nitrotoluene** (para favored over ortho by sterics), with $\text{–NO}_2$ itself meta-directing for any later substitution. (b) One molecule's enolate adds to the carbonyl of another (the **aldol addition**) → **3-hydroxybutanal**; loss of water gives the α,β-unsaturated **2-butenal (crotonaldehyde)**. The new $\text{C}_\alpha\text{–C}$ bond is the heart of enolate chemistry.)*

### Module 4: Structure Determination & Synthesis

You can now predict reactions; the final skills are the inverse problems. Spectroscopy hands you an unknown molecule and asks what it is; retrosynthesis hands you a target and asks how to build it. The module closes with a taste of the biomolecules these same rules assemble.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | IR & mass spectrometry | Read functional groups from IR and clues from the mass spectrum | degrees of unsaturation, diagnostic IR bands (C=O, O–H, N–H), molecular ion, fragmentation, isotope patterns |
| 4.2 | ¹H NMR spectroscopy | Deduce connectivity from chemical shift, integration, and splitting | shielding & chemical shift, integration = proton count, $n+1$ multiplicity, coupling, equivalent protons |
| 4.3 | ¹³C NMR & the structure-determination workflow | Combine all spectra to pin down an unknown | ¹³C shifts & carbon count, DEPT overview, a repeatable formula → fragments → structure workflow |
| 4.4 | Retrosynthetic analysis & multistep synthesis | Plan a synthesis backward from the target | disconnections & synthons, functional-group interconversion, protecting groups, forward vs. retro reasoning |
| 4.5 | A taste of biomolecules | See the course's rules build life's molecules | carbohydrates (anomers, glycosides), amino acids & peptide bonds, lipids & esters, nucleotide base-pairing |

**Boss problem 4:** An unknown has molecular formula $\text{C}_4\text{H}_8\text{O}_2$. Its IR shows a strong band near $1740\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ and no broad O–H. Its ¹H NMR: a triplet near $1.2\ \text{ppm}$ (3H), a singlet near $2.0\ \text{ppm}$ (3H), and a quartet near $4.1\ \text{ppm}$ (2H). (a) Compute the degrees of unsaturation and identify the structure. (b) Propose a one-step synthesis from an acid and an alcohol. *(Target: degrees of unsaturation $= \tfrac{2(4)+2-8}{2} = 1$, consistent with one C=O and no ring. IR $\sim1740\ \text{cm}^{-1}$ with no O–H points to an **ester**, not an acid. The ethyl pattern — $\text{OCH}_2$ quartet (2H, ~4.1) coupled to a $\text{CH}_3$ triplet (3H, ~1.2) — plus an isolated acetyl singlet (3H, ~2.0) gives **ethyl acetate**, $\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_2\text{CH}_3$ (¹³C: ~171 C=O, ~60 $\text{OCH}_2$, ~21 and ~14). (b) **Fischer esterification**: acetic acid + ethanol under acid catalysis, water removed to drive the equilibrium.)*

## Sources of truth

- **Clayden, Greeves & Warren, *Organic Chemistry*** — the primary spine for the mechanism-first, curved-arrow-centered viewpoint: nucleophile/electrophile framing, conformational and stereochemical rigor, and the reactivity logic used throughout.
- **Klein, *Organic Chemistry*** — for the skills-based treatment of arrow pushing, the SN1/SN2/E1/E2 decision framework, and the acidity (ARIO) heuristics.
- **Carey & Sundberg / McMurry** — for reaction scope, the carbonyl and aromatic-substitution reference reactions, and spectroscopy (IR/NMR/MS) conventions.
- **Cross-links:** builds directly on `general-chemistry` (bonding, Lewis structures, equilibrium, acid–base); the carbonyl, stereochemistry, and biomolecule threads feed `biochemistry`, and the addition/chain reactions feed `polymer-chemistry`.
