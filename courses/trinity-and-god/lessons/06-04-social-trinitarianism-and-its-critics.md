# The Triune God · Lesson 6.4: Social trinitarianism and its critics

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 6: The missions, and modern Trinitarian theology · Builds on: [6.3](06-03-rahners-rule-and-the-immanent-trinity.md), [5.2](05-02-the-greek-doctrine-and-the-addition.md), [4.5](04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md), [1.3](01-03-simplicity-as-doctrine.md) · Unlocks: Boss problem 6, and the dogmatic sequence that starts with [`christology`](../../christology/syllabus.md)

## Why this matters

For the last third of the twentieth century the liveliest question in Trinitarian theology was not whether the doctrine is true but what it is *for*. One answer swept through seminaries, pulpits and political theology: the Trinity is a communion of persons, and so the pattern for every human communion — the Church, the family, the just society. Anyone who has heard a homily calling the Trinity "a community of love we are meant to imitate" has met it. This is the course's last lesson, and it is where the defined faith of Modules 1, 3 and 4 stops being a conclusion and starts working as a constraint. The movement recovered something real. It also ran into walls that are not negotiable, and telling those apart is the whole exercise.

## The idea

Every Trinitarian theology has to answer two questions: where do you start, and what makes God one?

The manual tradition starts from the one God and his simple essence, and reaches the three ([1.1](01-01-the-treatise-and-its-order.md)). Unity is located in the essence: one *what*, wholly possessed by three *whos*. [Social trinitarianism](../reference.md#social-trinitarianism) inverts both. Start from the three — that is what the gospels actually show, a Son praying to a Father and promising a Spirit — and locate unity not in a prior essence but in the *communion* of the three: their being wholly in and for one another, what the tradition calls [mutual indwelling](../reference.md#mutual-indwelling) or *perichoresis* ([4.5](04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md)).

The payoff is the movement's real engine. If God's own life is a communion of equals, then domination and isolated individualism are not merely bad, they are *unlike God*, and a Church ordered as a communion is an image of the Trinity. A doctrine that had been an appendix becomes the most practical thing in Christianity.

## The argument

The movement's case, at the strength its best readers would recognize, before any critique.

1. **The revealed data is the economy.** *In words:* what scripture gives is three in relation — one who sends, one who is sent and prays, one who is poured out — not an undifferentiated essence.
2. **The economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity** ([6.3](06-03-rahners-rule-and-the-immanent-trinity.md)). *In words:* the communion we see in the economy is not a mask; it is how God is.
3. **To be a person is to be in relation.** John Zizioulas, the movement's most serious metaphysician, argues that the Greek Fathers made *hypostasis* — the concrete someone — prior to *ousia*, so that being itself is communion rather than closed substance (*L'Être ecclésial*, 1981; English as *Being as Communion*). *In words:* nobody is a person first and related afterwards; being related is what makes a person.
4. **God is one because one person is the source.** Zizioulas grounds this on the [monarchy of the Father](../reference.md#monarchy-of-the-father), the Cappadocian settlement of [3.2](03-02-one-ousia-three-hypostaseis.md) and the Greek doctrine of [5.2](05-02-the-greek-doctrine-and-the-addition.md). *In words:* this is the point at which he is not a modern innovator at all — the Father as sole *aitia* is the Eastern tradition's own answer, stated long before the modern quarrel.
5. **Perichoresis is what unity means.** Jürgen Moltmann's *The Trinity and the Kingdom* (German 1980, English 1981) makes the mutual indwelling of the three, not a shared substance, the form of divine unity, and calls the doctrine open to history: the cross is an event within the divine communion ([1.5](01-05-impassibility-and-the-god-who-suffers.md)), and history is taken into God's own life.
6. **Therefore the Trinity has social consequences.** Moltmann argues that a strictly monarchical monotheism has in fact been used to sanction monarchs and patriarchs, and offers the Trinity as the antidote. Leonardo Boff turns this to liberation: the Trinity as the pattern of a society of equals, under the slogan that the Trinity is our social programme — traced to the Russian thinker Nikolai Fedorov, an attribution this lesson has not verified. Catherine Mowry LaCugna's *God for Us: The Trinity and Christian Life* (1991) makes the programmatic version: the doctrine decayed when speculation about God in himself was cut loose from the economy of salvation, and it is recovered as a *practical* doctrine about God's life shared with us.

Two things critics are regularly unfair about. None of these writers is a tritheist by intention: Moltmann's unity is perichoretic and eternal, Boff denies tritheism explicitly, Zizioulas's whole account of unity is the one cause. And LaCugna is not abolishing the immanent Trinity; her claim is that treating immanent and economic as two *objects* is what went wrong.

## Map of positions

| Position | Where unity is located | What it is for | The charge against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manual Latin tradition** (Modules 1, 4) | one altogether simple essence; distinction only by [relative opposition](../reference.md#relative-opposition) | holding the mystery without contradiction | leaves the doctrine practically idle (Rahner's complaint, [6.3](06-03-rahners-rule-and-the-immanent-trinity.md)) |
| **Moltmann** | perichoresis of three subjects; God open to history | freeing theology from political monotheism | three subjects with three centres of will; simplicity set aside |
| **Zizioulas** | the Father as sole cause, the monarchy of [5.2](05-02-the-greek-doctrine-and-the-addition.md) | an ontology in which person precedes substance | whether the Cappadocians said this; whether the essence can depend on a person's freedom |
| **Boff** | communion as the form of divine life | a political and ecclesial programme | the inference from God's life to a polity |
| **LaCugna** | the economy, in which God is God for us | reuniting doctrine with Christian living | whether anything of God in himself survives |

The critics come in three kinds: method, history, dogma.

**Karen Kilby**, in "Perichoresis and Projection: Problems with Social Doctrines of the Trinity" (*New Blackfriars* 81, 2000), makes the objection that has stuck. Her argument has three steps. First, *perichoresis* in the Fathers is a nearly formal term — it names that the three are in one another, without explaining how. Second, modern writers fill it with content drawn from somewhere else: an ideal of human relationality, mutuality, non-competitive love. Third, the filled-in concept is then presented back as a revealed truth about God's inner life, and as a norm binding on us. So the movement projects a social vision onto God and then receives it as revelation. Kilby is not calling anyone a heretic; she is naming a *method*, and she proposes instead that we hold the doctrine more apophatically — the grammar of what may be said, not a picture of what God is like inside.

**Stephen Holmes** (*The Quest for the Trinity*, 2012) and **Lewis Ayres** (*Nicaea and its Legacy*, 2004) attack the history. On their reading the pro-Nicene settlement, Greek and Latin alike, holds divine simplicity, inseparable operation, and distinction by relations of origin alone — so the story in which Cappadocian personalism was smothered by Augustinian essentialism is a modern construct, the de Régnon paradigm that [1.1](01-01-the-treatise-and-its-order.md) already flagged. If they are right, the movement's retrieval retrieves nothing.

**Bruce Marshall** (*Trinity and Truth*, 2000; "The Unity of the Triune God", *The Thomist* 74, 2010) and **Neil Ormerod** (*The Trinity: Retrieving the Western Tradition*) press the dogmatic question instead: what do the defining texts actually permit?

### The honest scorecard

| Recovered | Where the constraints bite |
|---|---|
| The Trinity is the central mystery of Christian life, not an appendix to *De Deo Uno* | Simplicity is defined, twice, at [1.3](01-03-simplicity-as-doctrine.md). A unity *constituted* by the relating of three is composition in God, and *omnino* excludes it |
| Relation and communion are serious ontological categories — which is, after all, what [subsistent relation](../reference.md#subsistent-relation) already claimed | Florence: everything is one where there is no opposition of relation ([3.4](03-04-the-latin-rules-of-speech.md)). Intellect, will and operation carry no such opposition, so they are one, not three |
| The monarchy of the Father was taken seriously in the West again, which is an ecumenical gain | Three centres of consciousness is not the dogma; it is the classic route into [tritheism](../reference.md#tritheism) |
| A real pastoral abuse was named: preaching that leaves believers practically monotheist, appropriations hardened into departments ([4.5](04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md)) | Lateran IV: no likeness between creator and creature without a greater unlikeness — a standing tax on every inference from God's life to a polity |

## Worked examples

**Example 1 — the machinery on a clean case.** Take the homily sentence: *"God is a community of three who love each other, and the Church should be ordered the same way."*

Run the filters of [3.4](03-04-the-latin-rules-of-speech.md). Does "love each other" mark a relation of origin? No — so by Florence's clause it cannot distinguish, and the mutual love of the three is not three acts of three wills; each person is the one divine love. Does "community" posit an essence held in common ownership? Lateran IV says the essence is not a fourth thing beside the three; each person *is* it, entire. Does "ordered the same way" state a likeness? Then the greater-dissimilarity rule applies before any conclusion is drawn.

The sentence survives — read strictly. Three wholly in one another, with one intellect, one will and one operation, whose reciprocity the Church images from an immeasurable distance. What it may not mean is three who each decide to love, which is three wills and so three natures. Notice how little work the dogma does in the second clause: it licenses an image, not a constitution.

**Example 2 — where it strains.** Zizioulas is the hard case precisely because his starting point is unimpeachable. The Father as sole cause is the Greek doctrine of [5.2](05-02-the-greek-doctrine-and-the-addition.md); "God is one because the Father is" is not a modern slogan.

The strain comes one step later, when he makes the Father's *free personal love* the ground of the divine being — so that God's being is constituted by a person's freedom rather than given as an essence. A Latin reader hears two alarms. If the essence results from an act of will, simplicity is in trouble, since in God nothing is caused by anything ([1.3](01-03-simplicity-as-doctrine.md)); and Lateran IV's ruling that the essence neither begets nor is begotten was aimed at exactly this traffic between essence and person, from the other direction. His defenders answer that "cause" here is the Cappadocian *aitia* of [5.2](05-02-the-greek-doctrine-and-the-addition.md), which communicates the whole undiminished Godhead and implies no priority of will over nature. Ayres and Holmes answer the historical half: the Cappadocians were not making person prior to substance in anything like the modern sense. Both answers can be right at once, which is why this, and not the homily above, is the case worth arguing.

## Watch out

- **You might think a "social" doctrine is automatically unorthodox. It is not.** The monarchy of the Father is the Eastern tradition's own teaching, and communion language is older than the movement. What crosses the line is a specific claim: three centres of consciousness, each with its own will, deliberating and agreeing. That is **tritheism**, because will follows nature, and three wills means three natures. The premise is the one Constantinople III used to confess two wills in the incarnate Son, and it belongs to [`christology`](../../christology/syllabus.md), not here.
- **Do not promote the perichoretic model, and do not demote the dogma.** That there are three really distinct persons in one altogether simple essence is rung 1. That communion is the form of divine unity, or that a divine person is a subsistent relation, are *explanations* — see [dogma and its explanation](../reference.md#dogma-and-its-explanation), and the ladder at [`fundamental-theology` 4.4](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md). No magisterial act proposes either.
- **Kilby's objection is not a censure.** [The projection objection](../reference.md#the-projection-objection) is an argument about theological method, and it cuts at any doctrine whose content arrives from our own ideals — including, its targets reply, the psychological analogy of [4.2](04-02-augustine-the-image-in-the-mind.md), which is an image drawn from a creature too. Whether the two cases are parallel is the live question, and Boss 6(d) is where you answer it.
- **"The Trinity is our social programme" is nobody's defined teaching.** No council has inferred a politics from the processions; the Church's social doctrine is argued on other grounds, in `catholic-social-teaching`.

## One-liner

> Social trinitarianism put the Trinity back at the centre of Christian life, and the bill it has to pay is that in God intellect, will and operation are one, because only relative opposition distinguishes anything at all.

(ii) The unity of God consists in the mutual indwelling of the three.
(iii) The communion of the three persons is the model on which human society should be ordered.

**P2 (🟡) *(Exegetical.)*** An invented parish handout for a parents' evening:

> "Think of the Trinity as the first family. The Father proposes, the Son agrees, the Spirit carries it out — three minds in perfect harmony, never once disagreeing. That agreement is what makes them one God. And that is the model for your home: three who each want the good of the others, and so become one."

(a) Name the error and the heresy it lands in, and cite the rule from [3.4](03-04-the-latin-rules-of-speech.md) or [4.5](04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md) that corrects it. (b) Say what true thing the handout is reaching for, and give a sentence that keeps it without the error. **Two sentences per part.**

**P3 (🔴, optional) *(Exegetical.)*** Reconstruct Kilby's projection objection as three numbered premises and a conclusion, marking which premise is historical and which is about method. Then name the single premise a defender of Moltmann must deny, and state what he would have to show to deny it. **150 words or fewer in total.** (Do not give a verdict on whether the objection succeeds — that is Boss 6(d).)

<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Exegetical.)*

**Must hit, strict:** the level *and* the act, for each.

- (i) **Rung 1**, defined dogma. Fixed by the solemn confession of an ecumenical council — Lateran IV's *Firmiter* ("three persons indeed, but one essence, substance or nature altogether simple", this course's translation of the Latin), with Vatican I's *Dei Filius* chapter 1 repeating the simplicity ([1.2](01-02-the-one-true-god-and-what-reason-can-reach.md), [1.3](01-03-simplicity-as-doctrine.md)). Obstinate denial is heresy.
- (ii) **Rung 5**: no magisterial act proposes it. The mutual indwelling itself is taught (ST I q.42 a.5, and the tradition before it); what no act proposes is that the unity *consists* in it. Credit for adding that, taken strictly, the claim does not merely lack support but competes with the defined simplicity of (i).
- (iii) **Rung 5**, and no act proposes it. It is an inference from a theological model, standing under Lateran IV's greater-dissimilarity rule; the Church's social teaching is argued on other grounds.

**Wrong turns:** calling (ii) or (iii) dogma because a pope or a catechism has used communion language devotionally — devotional use proposes nothing; demoting (i) to "the Latin tradition's view" because the *explanation* of it is disputed, which is the course's standing error in the other direction; assigning a level without naming an act.

**Model answer:** (i) Rung 1 — proposed as the faith by the solemn confession of an ecumenical council, Lateran IV's *Firmiter*, and repeated at Vatican I. (ii) Rung 5 — no act proposes it; the mutual indwelling is taught, but that the divine unity *consists* in it is a modern theological thesis, and taken strictly it competes with the defined simplicity. (iii) Rung 5 — no act; an inference from a model, and Lateran IV's greater-dissimilarity rule applies to every such inference.

---

**P2** *(Exegetical.)*

**Must hit, strict (a):** the error is **three centres of will** — proposing, agreeing, carrying out are three acts of three deliberating subjects, and the handout then makes their *agreement* the ground of the unity. The heresy is **tritheism**. The correcting rule: Florence's clause, that everything is one in God where there is no opposition of relation ([3.4](03-04-the-latin-rules-of-speech.md)) — willing carries no relative opposition, so the will is one; with [4.5](04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md)'s undivided outward works, the three are not three principles of the creature but one principle, so "the Spirit carries it out" is at best an appropriation, here preached as a property. Credit for adding that agreement between wills would make the unity moral rather than substantial, which is unity of the wrong kind entirely.

**Must hit, strict (b):** what it reaches for is real — the three are genuinely distinct, genuinely turned toward one another, and family life does image them. The repaired sentence must locate distinction in relations of origin, not in roles or decisions, and must not make human likeness the measure: something on the order of *"The Father eternally begets the Son, and the Spirit proceeds from both; they are one God with one mind and one will, and a family that lives for one another images that at an immeasurable distance."*

**Wrong turns:** diagnosing modalism — the handout errs in the opposite direction, multiplying subjects; objecting to the family image as such, when the difficulty is the three deliberating minds and the agreement-as-unity; naming the error without naming a text.

**Model answer:** (a) It gives each person his own mind and decision and then makes their agreement the ground of their unity, which is tritheism: by Florence's clause only relative opposition distinguishes, and willing is not a relation of origin, so in God there is one intellect and one will; "the Spirit carries it out" also divides the outward works, which [4.5](04-05-common-proper-appropriated.md) forbids. (b) It is reaching for the real distinction of the three and their being wholly for one another, which a family can image. Better: the Father begets the Son and with him breathes the Spirit; the three are one God of one mind and one will, and a household that lives for one another is a distant likeness of that, distant in the way Lateran IV insists all such likenesses are.

---

**P3** *(Exegetical.)*

**Must hit, strict:** the three premises, correctly sorted, plus the crux.

- **Premise 1 (historical):** in the Fathers *perichoresis* is a nearly formal term — it asserts that the three are in one another and supplies no content about what that inner life is like.
- **Premise 2 (method):** modern social doctrines fill the term with content taken from elsewhere, an ideal of mutual, non-competitive human relationality.
- **Premise 3 (method):** that filled-in content is then presented as revealed knowledge of God's inner life, and as a norm binding on us.
- **∴** the doctrine so used is a projection received back as revelation.

The premise a defender of Moltmann must deny is **premise 2** — he must show the content is *derived*, not projected: that mutual indwelling as he uses it is read out of the economy (the Son's relation to the Father in the gospels, the cross, the sending of the Spirit) rather than read in from a social ideal. Denying premise 1 instead is possible but weaker, since it is a claim about patristic usage that Ayres and Holmes are also pressing.

**Wrong turns:** naming premise 3 as the crux — a defender who grants 1 and 2 has already conceded the projection and is only disputing what follows; treating the objection as a charge of heresy; giving a verdict on whether it succeeds, which is reserved for Boss 6(d).

**Model answer:** Premise 1 (historical): patristic *perichoresis* states that the three are in one another and says nothing about what that is like. Premise 2 (method): social doctrines supply the missing content from an ideal of human mutuality. Premise 3 (method): the result is then offered as revealed truth about God and as a norm for us. Therefore the doctrine is a projection returned as revelation. A defender of Moltmann must deny premise 2: he has to show the content comes from the economy — the Son's prayer to the Father, the cross, the sending of the Spirit — and not from a social ideal, which means exhibiting the exegetical route from those events to the specific relational content he ascribes to God.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [6.2](06-02-the-trinity-in-the-economy-liturgy-and-prayer.md) (The Trinity in the economy, the liturgy and prayer):** *(Exegetical.)* The Gloria Patri, in its traditional English form:

> Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost. As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

(a) Which words of the first sentence carry the assertion the form exists to make, what is that assertion, and what does the sentence say nothing about? (b) The second sentence is a Western addition. Say what it is for, and name the sixth-century synod that reports its use against that error and orders it said in Gaul. **Two sentences per part.**

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Must hit, strict (a):** the three datives joined by a bare "and" — to the Father, *and* to the Son, *and* to the Holy Ghost — governed by one undistributed "glory". The assertion is **equal glory, and therefore equal nature**: the same glory is given to each, nothing is parcelled out among them, and no preposition puts one under another. What the sentence says nothing about is the economy and the order of origin — who is from whom, who sends whom — which is what the prepositional shape carries instead. Credit for adding that this is why the coordinate form was added to the older prepositional one rather than put in its place.

**Must hit, strict (b):** it asserts that the equality is **not new**: the glory was so in the beginning, is so now, and will be so always, which denies the Arian claim that the Son came to be. The **Second Synod of Vaison (529)** reports that *Sicut erat in principio* was used at Rome and in Africa as a protest against Arianism, and orders it said in Gaul likewise. Credit for the sharper reading: Fortescue's *Catholic Encyclopedia* article on the doxology (1909) notes that although everyone now takes the glory as the subject of "was", the clause was probably meant originally of the Son — as *he* was in the beginning — which makes *in principio* an allusion to the opening of St John's gospel and the anti-Arian point explicit.

**Wrong turns:** treating the coordinate form as a replacement for the prepositional one, when the Church kept both and for different work; taking the addition to be aimed at the East — the Greeks did complain of it and it has never been used in an Eastern rite, but the error it answers is Arianism; reading the wording as itself dogma, when the Mozarabic rite said *Gloria et honor Patri et Filio et Spiritui sancto* by order of the Fourth Synod of Toledo in 633 — liturgical law, which the Church makes and can change, while what the form confesses is rung 1.

**Model answer:** (a) The assertion sits in the repeated "and" joining three datives under a single "glory": equal glory given to each, nothing divided among them, no preposition subordinating any one. It says nothing whatever about the economy or the order of origin, which is the other shape's business. (b) It says the equality is not new — the glory was so in the beginning and always will be — against the Arian claim that the Son came to be. The Second Synod of Vaison in 529 reports *Sicut erat in principio* in use at Rome and in Africa against Arianism and orders it said in Gaul.
