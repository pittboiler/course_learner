# Apologetics I: God, Christ & the Religions · Reference Card

> Open book. This card is meant to be open while you work — including during
> quizzes. Nothing here is something you're expected to recall cold.

The course argues openly for the foundations of Catholic Christianity against atheism, naturalism, Judaism, Islam and pluralism, and is built so the argument can lose. Every entry gives the Catholic argument as the lesson states it, the other side as its own defenders state it, the concession the lesson makes, and the premise the case turns on. Start with the [Map of the case](#map-of-the-case) to find a module's claim and its strongest opponent; mark every Church text at its true weight with [Levels of authority used in this course](#levels-of-authority-used-in-this-course); the odds-form arithmetic is in [Formulas](#formulas). The argument skeletons themselves belong to [philosophy-of-religion](../philosophy-of-religion/syllabus.md) and the ladder of authority to [fundamental-theology](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md): see [Assumed, not taught here](#assumed-not-taught-here).

### Levels of authority

The five-rung ladder of [fundamental-theology 4.4](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md), applied to every Church text above; it grades acts, not topics, and defaults down when the level is not manifest.

Overstating a level is an exegetical error, graded strictly; so is understating (treating a rung-1 capacity claim as one school's opinion). The recurring cases are in [Pitfalls](#overstating-the-level-of-a-document). For the full table see [Levels of authority used in this course](#levels-of-authority-used-in-this-course).

## Module 1 Giving a reason for the hope

What apologetics is for, the three strategies, and who carries the burden. The governing distinction: argument supplies motives of credibility; it never supplies the ground of faith.

### Apologetics

A reasoned defence, given when asked, of the reasonableness of believing: it argues *toward* faith on ground the questioner shares, and answers objections.

The word is the courtroom *apologia*, a speech for the defence (Plato's *Apology*; Acts 22:1, DR "the account"; Acts 25:16, DR "liberty to make his answer"). The charter text is 1 Peter 3:15-16, in two halves: the **job** (be ready to give a *logos*, an account, to whoever asks, DR "to satisfy") and the **manner** (DR "with modesty and fear", Greek *meta prautetos kai phobou*, gentleness and reverence, with a good conscience). The manner is part of the duty, and the asker sets the agenda.

| Neighbour | Does what |
|---|---|
| Evangelization | proclaims what God has done; invites trust in a witness |
| Theology | argues *from* the articles of faith as from principles (ST I q.1 a.8) |
| Apologetics | argues *toward* the reasonableness of believing; answers objections |

Its reach is fixed by the act of faith ([fundamental-theology 2.1](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-01-the-act-of-faith.md)): it works on the [motives of credibility](#motives-of-credibility), the [preambles of faith](#preambles-of-faith) and objections, so it can make faith reasonable but cannot produce it. *Dei Filius* ch. 3, can. 5 on faith (rung 1) anathematizes the claim that the assent is necessarily produced by arguments of human reason. Aquinas's job description: against one who accepts nothing revealed there is no means of proving the articles, only of answering objections, which "are difficulties that can be answered" (ST I q.1 a.8).

**The rationalization objection.** Russell, *A History of Western Philosophy* (1945), on Aquinas: the conclusion is known in advance from the faith, so finding arguments for it is "special pleading", not philosophy. Sharpened from the Church's own text: *Dei Filius* ch. 3, can. 6 forbids Catholics to suspend assent until they have finished a scientific demonstration. **The fideist form:** Kierkegaard (as Anti-Climacus, *The Sickness unto Death*, 1849) calls the first defender of Christianity a second Judas; Barth's *Nein!* (1934, against Brunner): a God reached by our arguments is not the God who reveals himself.

**The reply (1.1):** (1) faith's motive is God's authority, its assent free and moved by grace; (2) so apologetics claims only real signs, knowable preambles, answerable objections; (3) soundness is settled by public standards independent of the arguer's motive; (4) an argument held to public standards (opposing view first, concessions, no conclusion required for a pass) can be checked and can fail; (5) so rationalization is a charge against conduct, curable by (4), not against the arguments. Against the fideist: (1) grants his point; *Dei Filius* can. 3 on faith condemns the view that revelation cannot be made credible by outward signs, and 1 Peter commands the account.

**Concession:** the genre has earned its bad name (caricatures, invented quotations, uncounted numbers; the Lady Hope story of Darwin's deathbed return to faith, published 1915, denied by his daughter Henrietta, who was present, in 1922). **Turns on** premise 4: can. 6 makes the apologist and the skeptic asymmetric (one may suspend judgment, the other may not; the canon is usually read as aimed at Hermes's positive doubt and forbids suspending assent, not examining arguments). The public rules are the substitute, and work only if kept.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md); manner returns in [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md)

### Motives of credibility

Signs (miracles, prophecy, the Church herself) that bear on *whether God has spoken*, never on the mystery spoken; they make assent reasonable without making its content evident.

*Dei Filius* ch. 3 and can. 3 on faith (rung 1): revelation can be made credible by outward signs. That is a **capacity claim**: no particular argument is canonized. The chapter text (not a canon) calls the Church, by her wonderful propagation and holiness, a great and perpetual motive of credibility. Every historical argument in Module 5, the prophecy argument of 6.1 and the success argument of 7.5 have this status: a motive, not a demonstration. An argument that never moves its ablest hearers (6.3) still works as one. Owned by [fundamental-theology 2.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-02-credibility-and-the-preambles-of-faith.md).

### Preambles of faith

Truths reason can reach that faith presupposes: that God exists and that he cannot deceive or be deceived.

ST I q.2 a.2 ad 1. A cumulative case for a preamble (Module 2) is itself a motive of credibility, not a demonstration of the faith. Owned by [fundamental-theology 2.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-02-credibility-and-the-preambles-of-faith.md).

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md)

### Ethics of persuasion

Four rules the course enforces, each graded: an assent got by distortion or humiliation is not the free assent sought.

1. **No caricature.** State the view so its defenders would sign it.
2. **No invented quotation.** Quote only what you have seen in the source.
3. **No inflated number.** No unchecked statistic; no head-count of scholars as a premise.
4. **No winning by embarrassing someone.** The aim is the questioner's free assent, not a crowd's verdict on a person.

Grounded in 1 Pet 3:16 (the manner) and *Dignitatis Humanae* (1965): everyone is bound to seek religious truth and has a right to immunity from coercion (§§1-2); truth is sought by free inquiry and dialogue and imposes itself only by its own truth (§3); the act of faith is of its nature free (§10). DH is a conciliar declaration, rung 3 (some theologians read its core as definitive); §1 leaves the traditional teaching on duty toward the true religion untouched. It does not teach that all religions are equally true.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md)

### Classical apologetics

Two steps in order: natural theology from premises any reasoner can grant, then the historical signs that God has revealed in Christ.

The Catholic manuals' route, ending with the Church as bearer of revelation; Garrigou-Lagrange, *De Revelatione per Ecclesiam catholicam proposita* (Rome, 1918) is the classic Thomist version. **Order matters:** whether a resurrection report is believable depends on whether there is a God who could raise anyone (the prior of Module 5 is set by Modules 2-4). The Catholic warrant: *Dei Filius* ch. 2 and can. 1 on revelation (reason can know God with certainty from created things; rung 1, a capacity claim) and ch. 3, can. 3 (signs; rung 1). Choosing between the classical and cumulative routes is freely disputed (rung 5).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md)

### Cumulative case

No single argument demonstrates, but independent modest strands, each raising the odds, can together make theism more probable than not.

Mitchell, *The Justification of Religious Belief* (1973); Swinburne, *The Existence of God* (1979; 2nd ed. 2004): several **C-inductive** arguments (raise the probability) can be jointly **P-inductive** (make it more probable than not); his conclusion is that the other arguments leave theism neither very probable nor very improbable and religious experience tips it over one half. Newman's ancestor (*Grammar of Assent*, 1870, ch. VIII §2): certainty about the concrete comes from "the cumulation of probabilities, independent of each other"; his theory returns as the [illative sense](#illative-sense).

Rules the lessons enforce:

1. Multiply each item's likelihood ratio **given the items already counted** ([Cumulative odds](#cumulative-odds)).
2. A dependent strand or a shared crux counts once; a datum with ratio 1 (a rival predicts it as well, e.g. the moral datum under the parity claim, 3.4) adds nothing.
3. Unfavourable evidence (evil, hiddenness) enters the same product: [total evidence](#total-evidence).
4. The 2.1 audit for each strand: is it evidence? is it independent? where does it land (what $X$ does it reach)?

**Opposing view:** Flew's [ten leaky buckets](#ten-leaky-buckets). **Turns on** the primed ratios: arguments for God are rarely independent in the needed sense, and several draw their ratio from one value premise (that God would have reason to make embodied, conscious, moral agents). In 4.3 the cosmology feeds the kalam and fine-tuning strands, so the foundation should be an argument that needs no beginning; in 7.5 al-Ghazali's *Munqidh* knows a prophet as one knows Galen is a physician, by many indications, not one miracle; in 8.3 Newman's informal inference is the theory behind the cable, the illative sense judges convergence that odds only display.

### Presuppositionalism

The Reformed strategy (Van Til, Bahnsen) that refuses shared premises and argues instead that nothing, logic included, is intelligible unless the God of Scripture is presupposed.

**Stated as its defenders state it** (Cornelius Van Til, 1895-1987, Westminster Theological Seminary; Greg Bahnsen, 1948-1995, "The Great Debate: Does God Exist?" against Gordon Stein, UC Irvine, 11 February 1985):

1. **No neutral ground.** Every reasoner reasons from an ultimate commitment; the unbeliever's is autonomy, which is the point in dispute.
2. **No [brute facts](#brute-facts).** Every fact is created and already carries God's interpretation.
3. **[Noetic effects of sin](#noetic-effects-of-sin).** The unbeliever knows God and holds the knowledge down (Rom 1:18-21).
4. **The transcendental argument.** Logic, the uniformity of nature and obligation make sense only if the God of Scripture is presupposed: the proof is the impossibility of the contrary. Not fideism: an argument, and a bold modal one.

**The Catholic argument (1.2):**

1. Reason can know God with certainty from created things (*Dei Filius* ch. 2, can. 1 on revelation; rung 1, capacity).
2. Revelation can be made credible by outward signs (ch. 3, can. 3; rung 1).
3. Sin wounds reason but does not destroy it (ST I-II q.85 a.1, a.3; q.109 a.1).
4. ∴ C1: there is common ground.
5. Some truths, the mysteries, cannot be known unless revealed (ch. 4 with its canons; rung 1); the Trinity is the paradigm.
6. ∴ C2: "the triune God is a precondition of logic" claims to reach a mystery from natural principles, which proves too much (toward what *Dei Filius* calls semi-rationalism).

C1 and C2 are theological conclusions, **rung 4 at most**: no magisterial act names Van Til. Stripped of "no neutral ground" and aimed at theism, the transcendental argument survives as the [argument from reason](#argument-from-reason). Frame (2012) questioned whether Bahnsen's debate argument differed from Aquinas's. **Concession:** the brute-fact complaint is right that evidence does not come pre-weighed (see [brute facts](#brute-facts)). **Turns on** premise 3: if sin's damage is graver than q.85 allows, Kuyper's two sciences follow and C1 collapses; the Catholic leans on doctrine and on the observation that unbelievers prove theorems and reject bad arguments (Warfield pressed the same point against the Dutch school).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md)

### Brute facts

Van Til's term for facts available uninterpreted to believer and unbeliever alike as raw material for a verdict; he denied there are any.

**Conceded kernel:** evidence does not come pre-weighed. The same empty-tomb report is strong for someone with a moderate prior for a living God and nearly worthless for someone whose prior is vanishingly small, and background beliefs are not settled by the evidence they shape ([epistemology 5.4](../epistemology/lessons/05-04-the-problem-of-priors.md)). Presenting the Resurrection to a convinced naturalist as a neutral fact waiting to be read is the mistake Van Til named.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md)

### Noetic effects of sin

What sin does to the mind's power to know God: Reformed accounts make it grave; the Catholic account says reason is wounded, not destroyed.

| Position | Claim |
|---|---|
| Calvin, *Institutes* II.ii.12 | natural gifts corrupted, supernatural withdrawn; reason a ruin with sparks remaining |
| Kuyper | regenerate and unregenerate minds build two kinds of science |
| Van Til | the unbeliever knows God and suppresses it (Rom 1:18-21, *katechonton*, DR "detain the truth of God in injustice") |
| Warfield (Reformed) | sin affects the operation of faculties, not their nature |
| Aquinas | the inclination to virtue is diminished, never removed (ST I-II q.85 a.1); reason's wound is ignorance (a.3); natural truth known without new light (q.109 a.1) |

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md)

### Burden of proof

The procedural onus on whoever argues against the presumption; the apologist accepts it as a method and refuses it as a verdict.

1. Flew's presumption is procedural: it fixes who speaks first, not what is true ([philosophical-method 4.2](../philosophical-method/lessons/04-02-steelmanning-and-the-burden-of-proof.md)).
2. Faith does not wait on winning a debate; argument supplies motives, not the ground of assent.
3. But 1 Peter 3:15 asks for a reason whenever one is demanded.
4. ∴ Take the onus in argument, while denying that losing the exchange would make atheism true or unargued belief irrational.

Plantinga's two replies and the "double waiver" are in philosophical-method 4.2, Example 2, reloaded not rerun.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md)

### Presumption of atheism

Flew's proposal (1972) that inquiry start from non-belief, with the onus on whoever affirms God; "atheist" here is negative, a non-theist.

Antony Flew, "The Presumption of Atheism", *Canadian Journal of Philosophy* (1972), reprinted in the book of that title (1976). Like the presumption of innocence: defeasible, a rule of method, not a verdict; pressed as what any inquiry into any existence claim requires, not a special handicap for theists. Related: Scriven, *Primary Philosophy* (1966). Stating it as "atheists claim to know there is no God" is the caricature the course fails outright.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md)

### Chain and cable

A chain is no stronger than its weakest link; a cable of many slender strands can hold what no strand could, provided the strands fail independently.

Peirce, "Some Consequences of Four Incapacities", *Journal of Speculative Philosophy* (1868), public domain: reasoning should be a cable whose fibres may be slender "provided they are sufficiently numerous and intimately connected". The proviso: strands bear on the same conclusion, but their *failures* must not be connected; five strands cut from one thread are one strand. Chain arithmetic: links at 0.8, 0.85, 0.9 give $0.8 \times 0.85 \times 0.9 = 0.612$. Independent strands multiply (cable); dependent ones collapse toward a chain (5.5, the McGrews' factor). The cable image is Peirce's, not Newman's (no "cable" in the *Grammar*).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### Total evidence

Condition on every relevant item, favourable or not; for theism that means suffering and hiddenness enter the same product as the strands for God.

Carnap's name for the requirement. An apologist who leaves out evil has not made a weaker case but a different calculation, about a world without suffering. Uses across the course:

- 1.3, 2.1, 2.3: evil and hiddenness enter as factors below 1, not as vetoes.
- 2.2: worldviews are compared as total theories on all the data (conceded to Oppy).
- 5.1: background from Modules 2-4 may set the prior for God, but may not raise the likelihood ratio or be counted twice.
- 5.5: the prior for a miracle is set by the total evidence about God, not by a base rate of the event type.

**What would falsify a case:** a strand's ratio shown near 1 drops it; two strands sharing a premise count once; suffering's ratio shown far below 1 sinks the total. A case no finding could lower is not a cumulative case.

## Module 2 The case for God and the strongest replies

The arguments themselves are assessed in [philosophy-of-religion](../philosophy-of-religion/syllabus.md) (its card holds every skeleton). This module audits them into one case, answers Mackie and Oppy on their own terms, and lets evil and hiddenness into the same sum.

### Dialectically spent

An argument whose key premise can be credited only by someone already persuaded of the conclusion: it may be sound, but it adds nothing to a case addressed to the unpersuaded.

The ontological arguments are the course's example. In S5 the modal argument's "possibly God" is equivalent to its conclusion; Mackie: grounds for the premise can be no better than grounds for God, and "possibly no maximal being" looks just as conceivable ([philosophy-of-religion 2.2](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/02-02-the-modal-ontological-argument.md)). Plantinga himself claimed only rational acceptability. So they enter the cable at a ratio of about 1. "Spent" is not "unsound".

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md)

### The gap problem

Every argument reaches some $X$ (a necessary being, a designer, a ground of obligation); getting from $X$ to God is a separate step with premises of its own.

Owned by [philosophy-of-religion 1.1](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/01-01-which-god-and-what-an-argument-must-do.md) (its card has the per-argument table). This course's use: a cumulative case from the world reaches at most a necessary, personal, powerful, intelligent creator. It cannot reach a creator who has *said* anything, because whether God has spoken is a question about events, so the case must become historical (Module 5). One gain noted in 2.1: escaping modal collapse by denying that explanation entails already narrows the gap toward a *personal* ground, since only an agent explains without necessitating.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md)

### Ten leaky buckets

Flew's image against cumulative cases: if one leaky bucket will not hold water, ten will not either.

Attributed to Antony Flew (*God and Philosophy*, 1966, per 1.3). **At full strength (2.1):** (1) a deductive argument with a doubtful premise establishes nothing, and more such arguments establish nothing more; (2) recasting as probabilities does not help, since the fine-tuning likelihood may be undefined (normalizability) and the designer's unassessable (Sober's inscrutable designer); (3) the strands are not independent: contingency, per se regress, kalam, fine-tuning and the moral argument all demand explanation of what the naturalist treats as brute, so deny that once and all weaken together; (4) the gap, with Oppy's necessary initial physical state.

**Reply:** failing as a proof is not having a likelihood ratio of 1 (Swinburne's C-inductive vs P-inductive); the image holds only for arguments carrying *no* evidence. **Concession:** right about a pile of zero-evidence arguments, and right that a case built on one contested premise presented three times is exactly a stack of leaky buckets.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md), [2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md)

### The load-bearing pair

Contingency and fine-tuning: the two pillars of the course's cumulative case for God, largely but not fully independent.

**The case (2.1):**

1. An argument that fails as a proof can still be evidence.
2. The ontological arguments are [dialectically spent](#dialectically-spent): ratio about 1.
3. The per se regress and the kalam borrow contingency's explanatory demand (the regress's critics say its demand is the PSR; the kalam rests on the A-theory and no actual infinite), so they are counted inside it.
4. Pillar one, contingency: the BCCF has an explanation; if explanation need not entail (avoiding van Inwagen's modal collapse), it is the free act of a necessary being.
5. Pillar two, fine-tuning: contingency asks why any world, fine-tuning why *these* constants; a likelihood comparison needing no PSR.
6. Further strands: consciousness, reason, morality (Module 3), religious experience (counted last, after Swinburne).
7. Evil and hiddenness enter the same product.
8. ∴ A necessary, personal, powerful, intelligent ground of the contingent order is more probable than its naturalist rival.

**Level:** *Dei Filius* can. 1 (1870) defines that God *can* be known with certainty by natural reason (rung 1, a capacity, not a performance); whether any of these arguments is sound is freely disputed (rung 5). Ch. 2 concedes the road is not easy: revelation lets everyone know even these truths readily. **Opposing view:** [ten leaky buckets](#ten-leaky-buckets), Oppy's parity. **Concession:** each pillar faces a reply serious philosophers think succeeds: Hume-Edwards and modal collapse against contingency; the multiverse and normalizability against fine-tuning. **Turns on** step 5's independence: a naturalist content with a brute contingent world (Hume-Edwards) has a tolerance for brute facts that also blunts fine-tuning, unless the likelihood argument really works without any explanatory demand.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md)

### Mackies verdict

J. L. Mackie, *The Miracle of Theism* (1982): taken one family at a time, the arguments leave theism, on the balance of probabilities, much less probable than its denial.

The title is borrowed from Hume (*Enquiry* §X, end): the Christian religion cannot be believed by any reasonable person without a miracle. Mackie finds no argument proves God or makes God more probable than not. **Concessions he makes that readers forget:** evil does not make theism's core doctrines inconsistent ([philosophy-of-religion 4.1](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/04-01-the-logical-problem-of-evil.md)); objective values, if there were any, would raise the probability of God. He keeps his atheism on that strand by denying objective values (error theory); the concession is a conditional with a denied antecedent, not an admission that the moral argument works. Wielenberg and Enoch deny the conditional itself.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-answering-the-atheist-philosophers.md)

### Oppys parity strategy

Graham Oppy: compare the best theism with the best naturalism on all the data; whatever explanatory shape theism gives reality, naturalism copies with natural items, and every tie goes to naturalism as simpler.

**Two levels, which must not be blurred.** *Arguing about Gods* (2006): a fully successful argument would persuade every reasonable person; none for or against God does; reasonable people can be theists, agnostics or atheists. *The Best Argument against God* (2013): Naturalism beats Theism on the [theoretical virtues](#theoretical-virtues) because it is simpler (O1: fewer commitments, natural causes only) and explains every datum at least as well (O2). *Naturalism and Religion* (2018) adds that those unpersuaded are not irrational. Parity: theism ends the regress in a necessary God, naturalism in a necessary initial natural state; a brute contingent fact for one, a brute fact in the same place for the other. Schmid (*Religious Studies*, 2023): the naturalist's ontology is a proper part of the classical theist's. In 3.3 parity becomes brute psychophysical laws (Mackie; Chalmers's naturalistic dualism) set against a divine intention.

**The Catholic reply (2.2):**

1. Oppy's verdict needs O1 and O2.
2. O1 counts kinds of commitment, a ledger on which any addition is a cost.
3. The virtue that matters is fewest unexplained primitives: classical theism has one (a simple being whose existence is not received), naturalism leaves the world with its laws, constants and initial state as primitive.
4. O2 is contested datum by datum: fine-tuning, reason, consciousness, obligation (Module 3).
5. ∴ At best a stalemate on simplicity; the outcome turns on O2.

**Concession:** Oppy is right that theism must be compared as a total theory on [total evidence](#total-evidence), suffering included. **Turns on** premise 3. Oppy's rejoinder: God must explain *this* world, either necessarily (modal collapse: the world as necessary as the naturalist's initial state) or freely (a brute contingent choice back at the bottom); if it holds, the stalemate is real and O1 decides for naturalism. Oppy does not say there is no evidence for God or that believers are irrational.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-answering-the-atheist-philosophers.md), [3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md)

### Theoretical virtues

Simplicity, fit with the data, explanatory scope and power: the criteria by which whole worldviews are compared.

Oppy counts simplicity as fewest **kinds** of commitment; the theist counts fewest **unexplained primitives**. Neither is neutral: both build simplicity into the prior (the "smuggled prior" charge cuts both ways; [epistemology 5.4](../epistemology/lessons/05-04-the-problem-of-priors.md)), and whether the virtues track truth is disputed even in physics (the base-rate objection to the no-miracles argument, philosophy-of-science 5.1). In 3.4 the virtue becomes premise 4: predicting a datum's features beats positing them as brute, other things equal.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-answering-the-atheist-philosophers.md), [3.4](lessons/03-04-morality.md)

### Noseeum inference

Rowe's step from "we know of no good that would justify God in permitting this suffering" to "probably there is none".

Named by Wykstra (1996). **Rowe** ("The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism", 1979), E1 the fawn burned in a forest fire: P1 there is intense suffering an omnipotent, omniscient being could have prevented without losing a greater good or permitting an equal or worse evil; P2 a wholly good being would prevent it; ∴ no such being. P1 rests on N1 (after reflection we know of no justifying good) → N2 (probably there is none). Rowe's "friendly atheism": a theist with other grounds may be rational. **Conceded (2.3):** "I see no reason" is ordinary good evidence in almost every domain, and purposeless intense suffering counts against theism. Skeleton owned by [philosophy-of-religion 4.2](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/04-02-the-evidential-problem-of-evil.md). The free will defense answers Mackie's logical problem, not Rowe.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md)

### Hypothesis of indifference

Draper's rival to theism (1989): neither the nature nor the condition of sentient beings on earth results from benevolent or malevolent actions by nonhuman persons.

Draper, "Pain and Pleasure" (1989). $O$ = what we know about who feels pain and pleasure and when. D1 $O$ is known; D2 $\Pr(O \mid \text{HI}) \gg \Pr(O \mid T)$; D3 $T$ is not intrinsically much more probable than HI; ∴ other evidence equal, $T$ is more probably false than true. Needs no noseeum step: pain tracking biological use is what HI predicts. **Conceded (2.3):** that pattern is better predicted by indifference than by *bare* theism. The reply changes the hypothesis to $T_C$ (below, [answer by participation](#answer-by-participation)).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md)

### Nonresistant nonbelief

Not believing in God without having brought that state about oneself; Schellenberg's premise 4, which the Catholic concedes.

Schellenberg, *The Hiddenness Argument* (2015): (1) necessarily, if God exists, God perfectly loves every finite person; (2) such love is always open to a meaningful conscious relationship with each capable person; (3) if God is always so open, no capable person is ever in nonresistant nonbelief; (4) at least one is or was; (5) so God does not exist. The test is resistance, not blame. **Catholic move:** concede (4), since Lumen Gentium 16 speaks of people who "without blame on their part" lack explicit knowledge of God (its weight is weighed in 8.2); deny (3): grace can work a real relationship below explicit belief. Isaiah 45:15 (DR "Verily thou art a hidden God") and Pascal (*Pensees* 584, 242, Trotter: a religion that does not say God is hidden is not true) make partial hiddenness a prediction of $T_C$. **Turns on** the denial of (3): Schellenberg answers that a relationship you cannot recognize is not *conscious*, so the denial changes the topic.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md)

### Skeptical theism

God's reasons may lie beyond our ken, so failing to see a justifying good is no evidence there is none (ST1-ST3 of [philosophy-of-religion 4.4](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/04-04-skeptical-theism.md)).

**Its price for a cumulative case (2.3):** if $\Pr(O \mid T)$ is inscrutable, so are the theist's own likelihoods, fine-tuning's above all. The cumulative-case apologist may use only a modest version (our view of providence is partial, CCC 314), which shrinks the evil ratio toward 1 without cancelling it. In 5.5 the same cost bites: it removes the right to read $P(E \mid R)$ off God's purposes.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### Horrendous evils

Marilyn McCord Adams: evils so bad they seem to defeat the sufferer's life as a good; love must defeat each one within that sufferer's own life.

*Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (1999), *Christ and Horrors* (2006): defeat through intimacy with God and God's identification with horror-participants in Christ's passion. **Two Catholic costs:** her universal salvation collides with the teaching that hell exists (CCC 1035; whether one may *hope* all are saved is disputed); and "the God who suffers" must be said as Constantinople II (553, capitulum 10, rung 1) says it: one of the Trinity crucified *in the flesh*, not suffering in the divine nature ([trinity-and-god 1.5](../trinity-and-god/lessons/01-05-impassibility-and-the-god-who-suffers.md)). Privation (evil as lack of due good, ST I q.48 a.1) says what evil is, not why God permits it, so it does not touch Rowe's P1.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md)

### Answer by participation

The cross answers evil not by explaining it but by God undergoing the worst and promising to bring good from it.

CCC 309 gives no theory: the Christian faith as a whole is the answer. Augustine, *Enchiridion* 11, as Aquinas's reply to the objection from evil (ST I q.2 a.3 ad 1): God would not allow evil unless his omnipotence and goodness could bring good even out of evil. That God permits evil only to draw good from it is presented as of faith (CCC 311, 324); every specific theodicy (free will, soul-making) is freely disputed.

**The argument (2.3):** (1) the free will defense shows consistency, not $\Pr(O \mid T)$; (2) the fawn and $O$ have a ratio below 1 against bare theism; (3) on total evidence the evil term is a factor, not a veto (the Moore shift as argued case); (4) only modest skeptical theism is affordable; (5) $T_C$ adds that God enters suffering, so some of $O$ and of hiddenness is expected; ∴ evil and hiddenness weaken the case without defeating it, provided $T_C$'s content has independent support. **Objection:** it changes the subject (Rowe asked for a reason). **Fair reply:** granted as a likelihood, the gain reappears as a lower prior for $T_C$ unless its extra content is supported; but changing the hypothesis is legitimate, since Christians never claimed bare theism. **Turns on** premise 5: if Module 5 fails, the costly replies become the cheap ones.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md)

## Module 3 The burdens of naturalism

Naturalism stated at its best first, its record conceded in full; then three candidate burdens (reason, consciousness, obligation), each argued with the naturalist answer stated first. The philosophy of mind and metaethics are owned elsewhere ([Assumed, not taught here](#assumed-not-taught-here)).

### Methodological and metaphysical naturalism

Methodological naturalism is a rule about how to inquire; metaphysical naturalism is a thesis about what exists. Neither sense of the first entails the second.

| Sense | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Methodological, narrow | inside the natural sciences, explain natural phenomena by natural causes and say nothing either way about God | Paul de Vries (Wheaton), coined in a 1983 conference paper, published in *Christian Scholar's Review* (1986): a Christian coined it |
| Methodological, broad | the methods of science have general authority over inquiry | Papineau, SEP "Naturalism" |
| Metaphysical (ontological) | reality has no room for the supernatural | Oppy, comparatively ([parity](#oppys-parity-strategy)) |

Conflating them is the commonest error on the topic, made by both sides. **Uses:** 4.1 places Coyne's method-incompatibility thesis; 4.2 notes that *Humani Generis* §5 and the 1996 papal message reject only the metaphysical thesis (evolution as a total monist philosophy; theories making spirit emerge from matter); 5.1 shows a discipline's rule not to invoke divine agency cannot make the Resurrection improbable. **Level:** *Dei Filius*, canons on God the Creator, can. 2 (nothing exists besides matter) and can. 1 (denial of the one true God), rung 1, condemn *metaphysical* materialism only; no act condemns methodological naturalism or causal closure as a working hypothesis.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md)

### Physicalism

Everything is physical: any world that duplicates ours physically duplicates it in every respect.

Supervenience formulation (Stoljar, SEP "Physicalism"). **Hempel's dilemma** (1969): if "physical" means current physics, physicalism is probably false (physics is incomplete); if it means a completed physics, it is close to empty. Its main support is [causal closure](#causal-closure). Sellars ("Philosophy and the Scientific Image of Man", lectures 1960, published 1962) set the manifest image beside the scientific image; in "Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind" (1956, §41) science is the measure of all things (paraphrased; he aimed to join the language of norms to the scientific image, not to eliminate it).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md)

### Causal closure

Every physical effect has a fully physical cause.

Papineau's history: conservation of energy accepted mid-nineteenth century; twentieth-century physiology found no vital or mental forces; Feigl (1958) and Oppenheim and Putnam ("Unity of Science as a Working Hypothesis", 1958) appeal to it explicitly. An empirical generalization, and earned; calling it dogma is caricature. The Thomist does not compete with it on God's ordinary action ([primary and secondary causation](#primary-and-secondary-causation)). In 4.4 it becomes the naturalist dilemma for special providence: divine action either intervenes (breaks the laws) or is idle.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md)

### God of the gaps

God invoked as one cause among causes to fill a phenomenon not yet explained naturally; the gaps tend to shrink.

Drummond (*The Ascent of Man*, 1894) warned against it; Bonhoeffer (prison letter, 1944) called God as stop-gap a mistake; Coulson (*Science and Christian Belief*, 1955) is usually credited with the phrase. **The track-record argument** (the naturalist's strongest induction; Aquinas states it against himself, ST I q.2 a.3 obj. 2): lightning, disease, orbits, heredity, cell chemistry were all explained naturally, and no explanation was ever handed back. **Concession (3.1):** every gap apologists filled and science closed was a deserved defeat; methodological naturalism in de Vries's sense is good practice. The Thomist avoids the move: God is the whole cause at another level (ST I q.105 a.5). In 4.4 the Thomists turn the charge on quantum divine action as a gap theology at the quantum scale, though its authors do not argue from indeterminacy to God.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md)

### Burden versus unfinished project

3.1's criterion: a gap is merely not-yet-explained and is evidence of nothing; a burden resists the scientific image because of what the phenomenon is, or is presupposed by science itself.

1. A gap is a phenomenon not yet explained naturally; the track record makes its closing likely, so it is evidence of nothing.
2. A genuine burden is one the naturalist's own resources predict will *not* yield, because of what it is; or one the naturalist must presuppose to do science.
3. Reason, consciousness and obligation are candidates.
4. ∴ If any survives, naturalism owes an account the track record cannot pay. Argue from what things are, never from what we have not found.

**Turns on** premise 2: clear on paper, contested in every application; every past gap also looked like an essence to those who lived with it.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md)

### Primary and secondary causation

God, the first cause, gives creatures their being and their causal power and acts through them as true secondary causes: the whole effect is from both, at different levels, so God is not a cause on the creatures' level.

Owned by [thomistic-synthesis 5.2](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/05-02-providence-and-secondary-causes.md) (ST I q.105 a.5). Uses across the course:

| Lesson | What it does |
|---|---|
| 3.1 | avoids the god of the gaps; leaves closure of secondary causes standing |
| 3.2 | screens God off in the EAAN (Fitelson and Sober's footnote): the likelihood version gives a Thomist nothing, so the argument moves to natural finality (Lewis, Ross) |
| 4.2 | a complete natural explanation of evolution neither removes nor competes with God |
| 4.4 | God causes deterministic and indeterministic processes alike (ST I q.105 a.5 ad 2; q.19 a.8), so special providence needs no physical gap |

Creatures as true causes sits below the seam (theology); how primary causality works is freely disputed (rung 5).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md)

### Argument from reason

Naturalism cannot account for the reasoning by which it is reached: three versions target inference (Lewis, Reppert), reliability (Plantinga's [EAAN](#evolutionary-argument-against-naturalism)) and determinacy (Ross).

**History.** Lewis, *Miracles* (1947) ch. 3, "The Self-Contradiction of the Naturalist". **Anscombe**, a Catholic Thomist, at the Oxford Socratic Club, 2 February 1948 ("A Reply to Mr C. S. Lewis's Argument that 'Naturalism' is Self-Refuting", *Socratic Digest* 4): Lewis confused *irrational* causes with *non-rational* ones; "valid" applies to arguments, not causal histories; "why do you believe?" asks for a cause or a ground, which do not compete. Lewis rewrote the chapter for the 1960 edition as "The Cardinal Difficulty of Naturalism" (cause-effect vs ground-consequent). She disputed his argument, not his conclusion.

**Reppert** (*C. S. Lewis's Dangerous Idea*, 2003):

1. Rational inference occurs: some beliefs are caused by being seen to follow from others.
2. If naturalism is true, every cause works through physical properties, and logical relations are not physical properties.
3. ∴ On naturalism no belief is caused by a logical relation as such; rational inference does not occur.
4. ∴ Naturalism is false.

**Concession:** Anscombe was right against the 1947 form; Fitelson and Sober are right about the EAAN's arithmetic. **Turns on** premise 2: the nonreductive physicalist says logical relations are causally relevant through the physical states that realize them (the exclusion problem, philosophy-of-mind 2.4). In 1.2 this is what the presuppositional transcendental argument becomes once it is assessable. **Level:** no magisterial act adopts it; freely disputed.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md)

### Evolutionary argument against naturalism

Plantinga: if naturalism and evolution are both true, the probability that our faculties are reliable is low or inscrutable, so whoever accepts both has a defeater for every belief, including that conjunction.

Plantinga, *Warrant and Proper Function* (1993) ch. 12; *Naturalism Defeated?* (ed. Beilby, 2002); *Where the Conflict Really Lies* (2011) ch. 10. $R$ = our faculties are reliable; $N$ = naturalism; $E$ = our faculties arose as current evolutionary theory describes.

1. $\Pr(R \mid N \,\&\, E)$ is low or inscrutable.
2. Whoever accepts $N \,\&\, E$ and sees P1 has a [defeater](#defeater) for $R$.
3. A defeater for $R$ defeats every belief those faculties produce, $N \,\&\, E$ included.
4. ∴ $N \,\&\, E$ is self-defeating.

The target is the conjunction, not evolution: $E$ plus theism is untouched. The 2011 form: under materialism content is a neural structure, and selection sees only what it does. **Opposing view:** Fitelson and Sober, "Plantinga's Probability Arguments Against Evolutionary Naturalism", *Pacific Philosophical Quarterly* 79 (1998): selection works on variants actually available; inscrutability is ignorance, not a defeater; a naturalist may have other grounds for $R$; parity (the theist has no non-question-begging answer to global skepticism either); the numbers are inconsistent ([EAAN consistency check](#eaan-consistency-check)); [teleosemantics](#teleosemantics). **Self-defeat rejoinder** (the defeater defeats itself): Plantinga's drug case answers it, since every reassuring argument is output of faculties you have reason to distrust. **Turns on** premise 1. For a Thomist, the footnote on screening off ([primary and secondary causation](#primary-and-secondary-causation)) moves the argument from probability to natural finality: use the EAAN as a challenge to the naturalist, not as evidence for theism.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md)

### Defeater

A belief that removes the rational standing of another belief, either by undercutting its grounds or by rebutting it.

In the EAAN, $N \,\&\, E$ plus P1 is claimed to defeat $R$, and with it every belief $R$ underwrites.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md)

### Teleosemantics

Content is fixed by biological function: what a state means is the condition its consumers need to obtain in order to succeed, as selection set them up.

Millikan, *Language, Thought, and Other Biological Categories* (1984); Papineau (1987; *Philosophical Naturalism*, 1993). The naturalist reply to EAAN premise 1: content and truth cannot come apart as Plantinga's scenarios need, since a system whose contents bore no relation to the conditions of its success would not have those contents.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md)

### Determinacy argument

James Ross: some thinking is determinate (I add, not "quadd"); no physical process is determinate among incompossible functions; so some thinking is not wholly physical.

Ross, "Immaterial Aspects of Thought", *Journal of Philosophy* 89 (1992); Feser, *American Catholic Philosophical Quarterly* (2013). Every finite physical history fits rival functions as "grue" fits green emeralds; Ross reads Kripke's plus/quus as showing machines do not determinately add, not that nobody does. **Turns on** the proves-too-much charge: Kripke's sceptic found no fact *of any kind*, mental or physical, fixing plus over quus, so the argument works against an immaterial intellect too or fails for brains as well. Feser: Kripke's candidates are images and dispositions, which the Thomist already counts indeterminate; concepts are neither. That reply is the crux.

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md)

### Argument from consciousness

Consciousness and its law-like correlation with brain states are more to be expected if reality's ground is a mind than if it is mindless: a C-inductive inference to the best explanation, not a gap argument.

Swinburne (*The Evolution of the Soul*, 1986; *The Existence of God*, 2nd ed. 2004, ch. 9): brain-mind correlations are too many and specific for science to explain; a God with reason to make embodied knowers explains them. Moreland (*Consciousness and the Existence of God: A Theistic Argument*, 2008). Background assumed: Nagel (1974), Levine (1983), Chalmers (1995, 1996).

1. Phenomenal consciousness exists (denied by the [illusionist](#illusionism)).
2. Phenomenal facts are not entailed by physical facts as physics states them (granted by Chalmers, Strawson, Goff).
3. So on naturalism consciousness is fundamental in matter, or tied to it by brute psychophysical laws, or an unexplained emergent.
4. On theism, reality's ground is a conscious agent with reasons to create embodied knowers.
5. Consciousness and its orderly correlation with brains is more expected on 4 than on any branch of 3.
6. ∴ Consciousness confirms theism (raises, does not demonstrate).

**Opposing views (four bills):** illusionism (is a seeming an experience?); [panpsychism](#panpsychism) (the [combination problem](#combination-problem)); brute psychophysical laws (Mackie 1982; Chalmers's naturalistic dualism, not itself a reply to theism: many arbitrary laws; this is [Oppy's parity](#oppys-parity-strategy) on one datum); theism (says *that*, not *how*). **Concession:** illusionism and panpsychism are serious, not evasions. **Turns on** premise 5 against the panpsychist: if consciousness is fundamental in matter it is not surprising on naturalism, and the contest collapses back to priors and simplicity. The Catholic need not be a substance dualist ([hylomorphism](#hylomorphism-is-not-substance-dualism)). No magisterial weight.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md)

### Illusionism

Keith Frankish: phenomenal properties in the philosophers' loaded sense do not exist; introspection misrepresents experiences, whose real properties are quasi-phenomenal (functional properties represented as phenomenal).

"Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness", *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 23 (2016). The research task becomes the illusion problem, close kin to Chalmers's meta-problem (2018): why we judge there is a hard problem. Removes the argument's datum at the cost of one claim, that introspection is fallible about its objects, which psychology supports. **Objection:** an illusion is a way things seem, and a seeming looks like the very phenomenal fact denied. **Frankish's answer:** a seeming can be a representational state without phenomenal glow; whether that works is the live dispute. It does not say nobody feels anything.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md)

### Panpsychism

Consciousness (or proto-consciousness) is a fundamental feature of matter, filling the intrinsic nature physics leaves unstated.

Russellian monism: Russell (*The Analysis of Matter*, 1927), physics gives structure and dispositions, not intrinsic nature. Strawson ("Realistic Monism", 2006): experience cannot emerge brutely from the wholly non-experiential. Goff (*Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, 2017; *Galileo's Error*, 2019): the simplest theory taking physics and consciousness at face value. Chalmers ("Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism", 2015). Needs no emergence, extra laws or God; takes the hard problem more seriously than most theists. Not "rocks have opinions": aggregates are mostly not subjects. The Thomist hylomorphist sits closer to the Russellian monist than to Descartes.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md)

### Combination problem

How many micro-subjects, each with its own point of view, add up to one macro-subject with one point of view.

Pressed by William James (*The Principles of Psychology*, 1890, ch. 6), named by Seager (1995). The panpsychist's main burden; a research programme, not a contradiction.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md)

### Hylomorphism is not substance dualism

The soul is the form of the body, soul and body one nature; so a Catholic need not adopt Swinburne's or Moreland's substance dualism, and sensation is an act of the living composite.

Council of Vienne (1311-12): the rational soul is of itself and essentially the form of the body (its rung is creation-grace-last-things's to assign); CCC 365: not two natures united but one. Sensing has the composite as its subject (ST I q.77 a.5), so a dog's pain is bodily and qualia do not prove an immaterial substance; immateriality is argued only for intellect (q.75 a.2), which is 3.2's argument. **Cost:** premise 2 cannot prove a soul. **Gain:** the hard problem looks like a symptom of stripping form and quality from matter; the argument becomes "matter with intelligible natures, including natures that feel, fits a creating intellect" (perhaps the Fifth Way in new clothes). The instrument picture (pianist and piano) is Cartesian, not Catholic; hylomorphism still affirms a subsistent soul. See [thomistic-synthesis 5.3](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/05-03-the-human-soul-in-the-system.md).

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md)

### Moral argument

The abductive moral argument narrowed to obligation: obligation's categorical, overriding and answerable features are more probable on theism than on godless realism.

Skeleton owned by [philosophy-of-religion 3.3](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/03-03-moral-arguments.md); not Kant's practical postulate. Anscombe ("Modern Moral Philosophy", *Philosophy*, 1958): the law-like "ought" makes sense only with a legislator; she concluded philosophers should drop it, not that God exists. $G$ = theism, $R$ = godless robust realism, $O$ = obligation's features.

1. Obligation is categorical, overriding, answerable (warrants guilt, owed to someone).
2. On $G$ in Thomist form all three are expected: God is goodness itself (ST I q.6 a.4); natural law is the rational creature's participation in eternal law (ST I-II q.91 a.2).
3. On $R$ the first two are posited and the third unexplained (Cuneo's review of *Robust Ethics*, NDPR 2015, on overridingness).
4. Other things equal, predicting a datum's features beats positing them as brute.
5. ∴ $\Pr(O \mid G) > \Pr(O \mid R)$: ratio above 1 on obligation, even if not on bare moral value.

**Euthyphro** ([ethics 6.5](../ethics/lessons/06-05-god-and-morality-the-euthyphro-dilemma.md)) goes through God's reason and nature, not will; one level up, divine simplicity (God good by essence, ST I q.6 a.3). **Against the parity:** no one is answerable to $7 + 5 = 12$. **Debunking cuts both ways** (Street vs third factors; the parallel debunking of religious belief). **Concession:** atheists are not less moral; Wielenberg's reasonable-nonbeliever objection is correct against any divine command theory that needs recognition of the commander. **Turns on** premise 1's third clause: the realist can deny answerability is a further datum (guilt fits flouted decisive reasons; owed to the person wronged), and the ratio falls toward 1. **Level:** command (Adams) vs eternal law through reason (Aquinas) is freely disputed (rung 5).

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-morality.md)

### Grounding and knowing

The moral argument is about what makes obligation obtain, not about who can know it; Catholics are committed to unbelievers knowing the moral law.

Romans 2:14-15: the Gentiles show the work of the law written in their hearts, conscience bearing witness. Natural law binds without recognition of the lawgiver because it is promulgated in reason. "Atheists cannot be good" contradicts the tradition.

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-morality.md)

### Godless normative realism

Basic ethical facts are objective, non-natural, irreducible, brute and metaphysically necessary; obligation is having decisive reasons; no God needed.

**Wielenberg** (*Value and Virtue in a Godless Universe*, 2005; *Faith and Philosophy*, 2009; *Robust Ethics*, 2014): a robust "making" relation from natural to moral properties, compared to divine conservation; every view countenances brute relations somewhere; third-factor epistemology (evolved faculties both ground rights and generate beliefs about them); the reasonable-nonbeliever objection to divine command theory (Craig, Adams). **Enoch** (*Taking Morality Seriously*, 2011): deliberative indispensability; a third-factor reply to Street, candidly incomplete. **Parity claim:** arithmetic is necessary and brute, and basic moral truths are no worse off; theism stops at a brute necessity too, so the ratio on the moral datum is 1. Non-naturalist, not "morality is evolved feeling". Reply: [moral argument](#moral-argument).

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-morality.md)

## Module 4 Science and faith

What evolution, cosmology and quantum mechanics do and do not settle, with the myths corrected on both sides. One rule runs through the module: find the act, find what was actually claimed, find what was known at the time. The science itself is owned elsewhere ([Assumed, not taught here](#assumed-not-taught-here)).

### Conflict thesis

Religion and science are opposed by nature, so their history is a war.

Built by John William Draper, *History of the Conflict between Religion and Science* (1874), aimed chiefly at Catholicism, and Andrew Dickson White, *A History of the Warfare of Science with Theology in Christendom* (1896), whose enemy was "dogmatic theology". Historians have largely abandoned it (a report about a field, not a premise): see the [complexity thesis](#complexity-thesis). **The serious modern form is about method:** Jerry Coyne, *Faith Versus Fact: Why Science and Religion Are Incompatible* (2015): science treats every claim as revisable and publicly testable; faith settles empirical-in-form claims (a man rose, prayer is answered, there is a soul) by revelation, authority or private experience; religions have no shared procedure to settle disagreements; "accommodationism" mistakes coexistence for compatibility. His strongest card: the veto was not given up after 1633 but defined (*Dei Filius* ch. 4 and can. 2).

**The Catholic reply (4.1):**

1. The warfare story needs opposition *by nature*, every episode an instance of one conflict.
2. The record shows conflict, alliance and indifference, driven by particular acts, institutions and states of knowledge (Brooke; Numbers).
3. ∴ The historical thesis fails; what remains is argued episode by episode.
4. *Dei Filius* ch. 4 teaches the [double order of knowledge](#double-order-of-knowledge); apparent contradictions come from a dogma misunderstood or an opinion taken for reason; can. 2 binds only against assertions opposed to *revelation*.
5. ∴ By the Church's own defined principle, 1616 made the first error (a reading of Joshua and the Psalms taken for doctrine), and a Catholic who claims science proved a dogma risks the second.

**Concessions:** [the Galileo concession](#the-galileo-concession); [the 1951 address](#the-1951-address) overclaimed. **Turns on** can. 2's application: the Church remains the judge of what is opposed to revelation, and in 1616 the judges were sure; the principle diagnoses a mistake only afterwards, which Coyne can call his point. Defeating Draper does not defeat Coyne.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md)

### Complexity thesis

Science and religion have related as rivals, allies and strangers, and each episode must be read on its own terms.

John Hedley Brooke, *Science and Religion: Some Historical Perspectives* (1991); it cuts against apologetic "harmony" histories as much as the warfare story. Ronald Numbers (ed.), *Galileo Goes to Jail and Other Myths about Science and Religion* (2009), one myth a chapter (e.g. Lesley Cormack on the medieval flat earth).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md)

### Double order of knowledge

Faith and reason are distinct in source and object, and one God is the author of both, so truth cannot contradict truth; an apparent conflict means a dogma misread or an opinion mistaken for reason.

*Dei Filius* ch. 4 (Vatican I, 1870), guarded by canons: rung 1 for the double order and the impossibility of real conflict ([fundamental-theology 2.3](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-03-fideism-and-rationalism.md)). Ch. 4 also asserts the Church's right and duty to condemn knowledge falsely so called, and can. 2 anathematizes saying the sciences may hold as true assertions opposed to revelation which the Church may not forbid. Ch. 4 with its canons also defines that some truths, the mysteries, cannot be known unless revealed (used against presuppositionalism in 1.2). The principle sorts the 1616 error and the 1951 overclaim into the same two bins.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md)

### The Galileo concession

The condemnation of Galileo was an error by Church authority, really made, and an apologist should say so plainly.

A Roman congregation declared a true proposition about nature false and contrary to Scripture (1616 Index decree), and a papal court made a man abjure it (1633 sentence). Both were congregational acts, a disciplinary decree and a judicial sentence, not definitions. John Paul II's address of 31 October 1992 attributes the error to "the majority of theologians" who did not distinguish Scripture from its interpretation (§9), speaks of a "tragic mutual incomprehension" and of a case become "a sort of myth" (§10, the lesson's translations from the French), and never says the Congregation, the court or the pope erred. It did not "rehabilitate" Galileo in those words; the affair as event is [church-history 7.3](../church-history/lessons/07-03-the-galileo-affair.md)'s.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md)

### The 1951 address

Pius XII to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences, 22 November 1951 (*Un'ora*): science seemed to bear witness to the primordial *Fiat lux*, yet the facts were no absolute proof of creation in time.

He set out Hubble's recession of the galaxies and Whittaker's convergent age estimates; climax (lesson's translation from the Italian): "Creation in time, therefore; and hence a Creator; therefore God!" But he called science's voice "neither explicit nor complete", held that metaphysics and revelation establish creation and revelation alone a beginning in time (Aquinas's position, ST I q.46 a.2), and said the theories needed development. He named neither Lemaître nor the primeval atom. **Concession:** it overclaimed all the same: rhetoric outran its caveat, it tied papal words to a cosmology still contested (steady state alive to the mid-1960s), and it was received as endorsing the Big Bang. Lemaître, by the historians' report, was disturbed; the Louvain archives suggest, without proving, that he worked through Fr Daniel O'Connell to keep the theory out of the 1952 address to the IAU (Kragh, *Cosmology and Controversy*, 1996). **Level:** an address, the lightest genre; defines nothing, and its scientific content carries no magisterial weight.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md)

### Humani Generis

Pius XII's encyclical of 12 August 1950: permits research on the human body's origin from living matter, rebukes treating it as proved, restates the immediate creation of souls, and says the faithful cannot embrace polygenism.

**Rung 3** (authentic ordinary magisterium). §36: does not forbid research and discussion by the competent on evolution "in as far as it inquires into the origin of the human body as coming from pre-existent and living matter", arguments on both sides weighed, outcome submitted to the Church's judgment; rebukes those who treat the evolutionary origin of the body as already certain. A **permission**, not an endorsement; nor a condemnation. Same paragraph: the faith obliges us to hold that souls are immediately created by God (presented as restated; its own weight is argued in creation-grace-last-things 2.3, not conferred by HG). §37: [polygenism](#polygenism). §5: rejects evolution as a total monist or pantheist philosophy of the origin of all things (metaphysical, not methodological, naturalism). **Concession to ID's proponents:** the Church does reject a purely unguided process in the metaphysical sense; on that point Behe and Dembski stand closer to Pius XII than Dawkins does.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md)

### Polygenism

The view that true men existed after Adam not descended from him, or that Adam stands for many first parents.

*Humani Generis* §37: the faithful cannot embrace it, since it is not apparent how it can be reconciled with original sin. A rung-3 judgment; whether the 1950 judgment still closes the question is disputed ([fundamental-theology 4.3](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-03-religious-submission.md), Example 2). The doctrine is ceded to creation-grace-last-things 3.4.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md)

### More than a hypothesis

John Paul II's message to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences (dated 22 October 1996), §4: new knowledge leads to recognizing in the theory of evolution *plus qu'une hypothese*, more than a hypothesis.

Ground given: the unsought convergence of independent results. The first English *L'Osservatore Romano* rendering, "more than one hypothesis", reversed the point and was later acknowledged as an error (as reported at the time). The message distinguishes *theories* of evolution (materialist, reductionist, spiritualist readings), whose judgment belongs to philosophy and theology. §5 restates immediate creation of the soul and rejects theories making spirit emerge from matter or an epiphenomenon of it. §6: an ontological leap with the human being; the observational sciences cannot observe the passage to the spiritual, though they detect its signs. **Level:** a papal message to a particular body, a light genre at the low end of rung 3; it defines nothing, and its doctrinal content carries the weight of what it restates. Benedict XVI (Auronzo di Cadore, 24 July 2007): the supposed opposition of creation and evolution is an absurdity; informal remarks, no magisterial weight.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md)

### Intelligent design

The claim, presented as science, that design is detectable in biology by an inference already used in forensics and cryptography.

Michael Behe (*Darwin's Black Box*, 1996; accepts common descent and an old earth): [irreducible complexity](#irreducible-complexity). William Dembski (*The Design Inference*, 1998): the [explanatory filter](#explanatory-filter). Its rival is Dawkins (*The Blind Watchmaker*, 1986): before Darwin adapted complexity was the best evidence for a designer and Paley was right to be impressed; selection removes the evidence (a likelihood argument in reverse). *Kitzmiller v. Dover* (2005, Judge John E. Jones III): ID is not science, and teaching it in a public school violated the Establishment Clause (whether courts can settle demarcation is philosophy-of-science 2.6's question).

**The Catholic argument (4.2):**

1. Biology's causes are secondary causes.
2. God is the primary cause of every secondary cause's being and acting, not a cause on their level.
3. So a complete secondary-cause explanation of adaptation neither removes nor competes with God.
4. The Fifth Way argues from natures acting always or nearly always toward an end, which selection uses; it is not Paley ([thomistic-synthesis 3.3](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/03-03-the-third-fourth-and-fifth-ways.md)).
5. So Dawkins defeats Paley, not the Catholic case.
6. ∴ Placing God where secondary causes fail treats him as a cause on their level: on premise 2, ID's picture of divine action is the wrong one.

**Concession:** Catholic critics of ID (Kenneth Miller) are right that a gap-based inference makes each closed gap a retreat; ID's proponents are right that the Church rejects a purely unguided process in the metaphysical sense. **Turns on** premise 2, which is untestable: a primary cause with no empirical difference looks idle to the naturalist, so the case rests on 2.1's metaphysics. The argument shows evolution cannot refute God; it gives no positive evidence for him. ID and Dawkins share one assumption: if natural causes suffice, God has nothing left to do.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md)

### Irreducible complexity

Behe: a system of several well-matched parts, all needed for its basic function, cannot be built by direct, slight, successive improvements.

**Biological reply:** Miller (*Finding Darwin's God*, 1999): parts have functions of their own elsewhere and can be co-opted along indirect routes the definition does not exclude.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md)

### Explanatory filter

Dembski's eliminative method: attribute an event to regularity if a law makes it probable; else to chance if it is of intermediate probability or unpatterned; else, if it is both below a universal probability bound and specified, to design.

The bound: 1 in $10^{150}$. Specified: matching a pattern given independently of the event. **Methodological reply:** Fitelson, Stephens and Sober, "How Not to Detect Design", *Philosophy of Science* (1999): the filter eliminates rivals without ever assessing the probability of the evidence on the design hypothesis, which a likelihood comparison requires. It does not show the world undesigned, and the Thomist does not want it to.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md)

### Creatio ex nihilo

Creation as the total dependence of everything other than God on God for its whole being at every moment: a relation, not an event at a first moment.

The analysis is owned by [thomistic-synthesis 5.1](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/05-01-creation-and-conservation.md) (theology); the doctrine of creation from nothing is rung 1. So no cosmological result, first moment or infinite past, confirms or refutes it: "science proved creation" and "science disproved God" are the same species of error, reading dependence as an edge. **The fence:** a beginning in time is also rung 1 (Lateran IV, 1215, ch. 1; *Dei Filius* ch. 1), held by faith; that it cannot be demonstrated is Aquinas's thesis (ST I q.46 a.2: by faith alone, by no demonstration), rung 5, freely disputed (Bonaventure denied it). An eternal universe would not touch creation as dependence, but would press on the rung-1 beginning. **Cost:** if an infinite past would not refute creation, a first moment cannot confirm it.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md)

### Kalam

Whatever begins to exist has a cause; the universe began; so it has a cause.

Assessment owned by [philosophy-of-religion 2.5](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/02-05-cosmological-arguments-the-kalam.md). Cosmology bears only on premise 2, and can raise or lower its probability without settling it. What is established: expansion and cooling for about 13.8 billion years from a hot dense state; "first moment" is an extrapolation through unknown physics. **Concession:** the scientific premise is weaker than popular apologetics says. The Hawking-Penrose singularity theorems assume classical general relativity and energy conditions; the Borde-Guth-Vilenkin theorem (2003) drops the energy conditions but stays classical, and concludes only that inflation needs other physics at its past boundary. Whether there was an absolute beginning is open. In the cumulative case the kalam is counted inside contingency (2.1), and the case's foundation should be an argument that needs no beginning, with a beginning as a bonus.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md)

### Fine-tuning

The constants, and the initial low-entropy state, lie in ranges improbable without design: evidence bearing on likelihoods in odds form, not proof.

Assessment owned by [philosophy-of-religion 3.1-3.2](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/03-02-fine-tuning-the-objections.md). The second pillar of the [load-bearing pair](#the-load-bearing-pair). Carroll's objections (paraphrased, "Does the Universe Need God?", 2012, and his 2014 debate with Craig): the Big Bang is the edge of understanding, not of the universe; even a first moment needs no cause (physics deals in states and laws); theism is too elastic to predict anything; and [the low-entropy objection](#the-low-entropy-objection). A multiverse attacks one likelihood, not creation: it is as dependent as a universe, and its mechanism has parameters of its own. **Turns on** (C2 of 4.3) the metaphysics: Carroll's self-contained universe is Oppy's brute-fact position against contingency ([metaphysics 3.3](../metaphysics/lessons/03-03-brute-facts-and-necessary-existence.md)); cosmology cannot decide whether existence needs explaining.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md)

### The low-entropy objection

Sean Carroll: the early universe's entropy is far lower than observers need, so the excess favours naturalism over a God aiming at life.

Penrose (*The Emperor's New Mind*, 1989) put its improbability at about one in $10^{10^{123}}$. One galaxy in a sea of equilibrium would serve observers; a God aiming at life has no evident reason for such excess, while an indifferent cosmos predicts it no worse. The low-entropy boundary is the source of the arrow of time ([stat-mech 6.2](../stat-mech/lessons/06-02-entropy-information-arrow.md)) and a clue for physics, not a gap for theology. **Reply:** theism is not minimal design (ST I q.47 a.1: the universe represents the divine goodness better by many diverse creatures than any single one could), and the overkill also counts against selection explanations.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md)

### Boltzmann brains

Observers fluctuating out of thermal equilibrium; a theory in which most observers are such fluctuations is, for Carroll, cognitively unstable.

Carroll, "Why Boltzmann Brains Are Bad" (arXiv, 2017): such a theory cannot be both true and justifiably believed. A constraint on cosmology (philosophy-of-physics 5.5), not an argument for God.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md)

### General and special divine action

General: God creating, conserving and governing everything at once. Special: God bringing about *this* outcome for *this* purpose (answered prayer, the call of Abraham, the Resurrection).

The modern debate is almost all about special action, because deism grants the general. **The naturalist's dilemma** ([causal closure](#causal-closure)): a God who brings about a physical outcome either adds a cause physics leaves no room for (intervention, an intruder in his own world) or adds nothing (idle). **Level:** providence (*Dei Filius* ch. 1) and miracles as possible signs (ch. 3) rung 1; creatures as true causes below the seam; [NIOD](#niod) and the Thomist account of how primary causality works, rung 5.

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md)

### Miracle on the Catholic account

God acting outside the order he gave secondary causes, producing their effects without them or effects they do not reach; outside the created order, not against nature.

ST I q.105 a.6 ("by producing the effects of secondary causes without them, or by producing certain effects to which secondary causes do not extend"); a.7: a miracle's cause is hidden from everyone. *Dei Filius* ch. 3 and its canons: miracles are possible and *can* rightly prove the divine origin of the Christian religion (rung 1, a **capacity** claim endorsing no particular argument). The Thomist does not deny miracles; he denies that ordinary special providence needs a gap. Whether testimony can establish one is 5.1's question; Hume's machinery is in [philosophy-of-religion 3.5](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/03-05-miracles.md).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md)

### NIOD

Non-interventionist objective divine action (NIODA): God really makes a difference to this outcome without suspending any law.

The Vatican Observatory and CTNS (Berkeley) series *Scientific Perspectives on Divine Action* (five volumes, 1993-2001). **Russell:** under ontological indeterminism the laws give only probabilities, so God can fix outcomes without breaking anything; God acts in all quantum events, mattering classically in some; central case genetic mutation, so variation becomes a channel of providence (*Evolutionary and Molecular Biology*, 1998; *Quantum Mechanics*, 2001); he says it locates divine action without explaining how. **Murphy** (*Chaos and Complexity*, 1995): created powers incomplete without God's participation in every basic event, cooperating not overruling. **Polkinghorne:** chaotic systems. **Peacocke:** whole-part influence (not quantum). Macroscopic effects need amplification. Not a god-of-the-gaps argument *for* God: its authors assume theism and ask where God acts.

**The case against (4.4):**

1. The proposals need one interpretation of quantum mechanics (the [causal joint](#causal-joint) exists under collapse or GRW, not under Bohm or Everett, which are empirically equivalent).
2. Even where there is a joint it is cramped (Saunders).
3. A causal joint is a place where one cause leaves an effect open for another: two causes of the same order.
4. On the Thomist account God is not a cause of that order; he causes deterministic and indeterministic processes alike, and wills contingency (ST I q.19 a.8).
5. ∴ Special divine action needs no indeterminacy, and miracles need no disguise.

Carroll (many essays) and his student Ignacio Silva, with Dodds and Stoeger: quantum divine action makes God's causality one more natural causality. **Concession:** speculative theology on which the Church has decided nothing; NIODA, the Thomist alternative and the choice between them are rung 5, and Russell and Murphy are serious theologians answering a real question. **Turns on** premise 4: it replaces a "how" with a "that", leaves special action physically invisible, and rests on analogical predication of "cause" ([thomistic-synthesis 4.4](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/04-04-analogy.md)), which univocalists deny.

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md)

### Causal joint

The place where God's act meets nature on the NIODA proposals.

Exists only under indeterministic readings of quantum mechanics (textbook collapse, GRW), not under Bohmian mechanics (deterministic dynamics, open initial positions) or Everett (every outcome occurs). Saunders (*Divine Action and Modern Science*, 2002) sorts four options (add possibilities, alter probabilities, act as the measurer, pick outcomes ignoring the probabilities) and finds none coherent on current understandings: changing probabilities is changing the law; choices inside the statistics are hemmed in and wash out at large scales unless amplification is established. The Thomist critique: a joint makes God a cause of the same order as creatures.

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md)

## Module 5 Jesus and the resurrection as history

The historical case the new-testament course declines to argue. The facts, criteria and texts are assumed from [new-testament](../new-testament/syllabus.md); the resurrection as doctrine is [christology 6.2](../christology/lessons/06-02-resurrection-and-ascension-as-doctrine.md)'s. Every conclusion here is a motive of credibility; the numbers are in [Formulas](#formulas).

### Two-level verdict

Keep $H$ (historical: the death, the experiences, the tomb) apart from $R$ (God raised Jesus bodily): history can settle $H$, but getting from $H$ to $R$ needs a background prior for God.

**The reply to Ehrman (5.1):**

1. A historian needs the probability of $R$ *given the evidence*, not before it (Craig pressed this in the 2006 debate; the naturalist Jeffery Jay Lowder, *Secular Frontier* 2012, judged Craig right on it).
2. A low prior can be overcome by a large enough likelihood ratio (Price, Babbage; [philosophy-of-religion 3.5](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/03-05-miracles.md)), so E2 blocks the conclusion only if $R$'s prior is zero or no evidence could carry a large ratio.
3. $R$'s prior is not that a corpse revives naturally; it is $\Pr(R \mid B) = \Pr(G \mid B) \times \Pr(\text{raise} \mid G, B)$.
4. So "least probable" reports a background in which $\Pr(G \mid B)$ is near zero; it smuggles a prior in as a method.
5. ∴ History alone cannot take us from $H$ to $R$; history plus a defensible background can.

**What Modules 2-4 may do:** set $\Pr(G \mid B)$, and remove a bad reason for a zero (a miracle breaks nothing God's causality depends on, 4.4); they may **not** raise the likelihood ratio, or count any strand that already leaned on Easter testimony. **Counter-reply** (Lowder's charitable reading of Ehrman, which he rejects): historians argue to colleagues of every belief, so a historical verdict may use only shared premises, and the historian as historian sets the prior of a divine act to zero by rule. **Answer:** a stipulated zero yields "historians-by-this-rule cannot assert $R$", not "$R$ is improbable". **Concession:** "God raised Jesus" is not a historical conclusion in the sense "Pilate crucified Jesus" is; "history proves the resurrection" is the wrong slogan (cf. CCC 639: real event with historically verified manifestations; CCC 647: it surpasses history). **Turns on** premise 3's second factor: nobody has an agreed way to estimate how likely God was to raise *this* man, so the reply shows Ehrman's zero is a choice without fixing a positive prior.

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md)

### Ehrmans objection

Bart Ehrman: the historian, as historian, can never conclude that a miracle probably happened, since history deals in the probable and a miracle is by nature the least probable occurrence.

Stated most crisply in his debate with Craig (College of the Holy Cross, 28 March 2006) and the methodological excursus of *The New Testament: A Historical Introduction to the Early Christian Writings* (later editions):

- E1. History establishes only what probably happened.
- E2. A miracle is by nature the least probable occurrence.
- E3. The historian has no access to supernatural forces; only the public record, open to any observer.
- ∴ The historian as historian cannot conclude a miracle probably happened.

Not a claim that miracles never happen: a believer may hold $R$ on other grounds. He grants much of $H$ (*How Jesus Became God*, 2014: some disciples had visions and sincerely believed), while doubting the honourable burial and the tomb. E3 is [methodological naturalism](#methodological-and-metaphysical-naturalism) applied to history. Stating him as Hume, or as an atheist proof, is caricature. Reply: [two-level verdict](#two-level-verdict).

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md)

### Minimal facts

Habermas and Licona (*The Case for the Resurrection of Jesus*, 2004): argue only from facts that are well evidenced and accepted by most scholars, skeptics included.

| Fact | Evidence (5.2) |
|---|---|
| F1 Jesus died by crucifixion | Tacitus, Josephus, all four gospels |
| F2 the disciples had experiences they took to be appearances, and came to believe he was raised | the early formula of 1 Cor 15:3-7 |
| F3 Paul, a persecutor, changed course after an experience he took to be an appearance | Paul's letters |
| F4 James, not a follower during the ministry, became one and a leader | 1 Cor 15:7, Gal 1:19, Josephus *Ant.* 20.9.1; earlier unbelief Mark 3:21, John 7:5 |
| +1 the empty tomb | argued for, but with less agreement: "4+1", not a minimal fact on their own terms |

**The cost, in one sentence:** a fact everyone grants is usually a fact every hypothesis predicts, so its likelihood ratio is near 1; consensus buys security and spends leverage. **Objections:** (1) a head-count is testimony, and scholars of one guild are not independent witnesses; Habermas's survey counts (e.g. roughly three in four favouring the tomb) come from a bibliography and coding not released, so nobody can check them; (2) consensus varies by fact: weaker for F4, weaker still for the tomb; Licona 2010 narrows his "historical bedrock" to F1-F3; (3) the field is not neutral (writers disproportionately clergy or seminary-linked: a reason to discount the count as a count, not evidence of bias in any argument).

**The method restated (5.2):** (1) F1-F4 by ordinary method on inspectable evidence, the head-count only a pointer; (2) prefer the hypothesis explaining all four without ad hoc additions; (3) the resurrection does, each rival strains on at least one (argued in 5.4); (4) given Modules 2-4, it is not ruled out before the evidence; ∴ best available explanation, a motive of credibility. **Concession:** "most scholars accept" should never appear as a premise. **Turns on** premise 3: the facts are explanation-neutral, so the weight falls on excluding rivals and on the background; James discriminates best and rests on the thinnest evidence. Skeptics grant experiences *taken to be* appearances, not that they saw him. **Level:** that Christ rose is defined (rung 1, the creeds); no apologetic method is endorsed.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md)

### Allison on the resurrection evidence

Dale Allison, *The Resurrection of Jesus: Apologetics, Polemics, History* (2021): believes Jesus was raised, yet holds that neither apologists nor polemicists can do what they claim.

On the tomb: a decent case for, a respectable case against, leaning slightly toward empty. On the appearances: grieving minds produce vivid experiences, yet the disciples' experiences cannot be shown illusory without first assuming naturalism; he knows no close parallel to the series as a whole. He gathers reports of collective visions and thinks apologists deny group visions too quickly (5.4). His verdict (paraphrased): the historical evidence is not good enough to make disbelief unreasonable nor bad enough to make faith untenable; worldview decides. Neither skeptic nor ally: quoting one half is caricature.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md)

### Maximal evidential case

N. T. Wright (*The Resurrection of the Son of God*, 2003): take as data the *shape* of early Christian belief, more contested but more discriminating than the minimal facts.

In Second Temple usage "resurrection" meant a body; the Christians held it modified: one man raised ahead of the general resurrection, the body transformed rather than restored, and the raised one the Messiah; other movements whose messiah was killed made no such claim. An empty tomb plus appearances is sufficient to explain the belief, and he argues necessary. A vision hypothesis predicts these data poorly, so their ratio is well away from 1. It spends security to buy leverage. Wright's [exaltation objection](#exaltation-objection) belongs to the same case.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md)

### Empty tomb

The "+1" of the minimal-facts scheme, weighed in 5.3 as three separate claims: burial in a known tomb, discovery that it was empty, and the explanation that God raised him.

Only burial and discovery are historical; discovery depends on burial, which is why the strongest skeptical case attacks the burial ([Crossan's burial objection](#crossans-burial-objection)). **The case (5.3):**

1. Burial was possible: Deut 21:23 (DR: buried the same day); Josephus, *War* 4.5.2 (Jews buried even the crucified before sunset); the Giv'at ha-Mivtar ossuary (1968), a crucified man's heel bone with the nail, given a family burial; Magness (*Stone and Dung, Oil and Spit*, 2011): the gospel accounts accord with Jewish law and tomb archaeology; compare John 19:31.
2. Joseph, a member of the condemning council (Mark 15:43), is an odd invention; Raymond Brown (*The Death of the Messiah*, 1994) judged burial by Joseph historically probable.
3. Women as discoverers: the [criterion of embarrassment](#criterion-of-embarrassment).
4. Preaching in Jerusalem within weeks (Acts 2:29-31: David's sepulchre is with us; this flesh saw no corruption).
5. The [stolen-body counter-story](#stolen-body-counter-story) disputes why, not whether.
6. Paul: 1 Cor 15:4 puts burial and rising in sequence; for a Pharisee rising was done to the buried body; silence about the evidence is not silence about the claim.
7. ∴ Discovery of an empty tomb is more probable than not: a motive of credibility.

**Opposing points:** Mark is earliest (c. 70) and the later gospels expand it (guards, two angels, the running disciples), the shape of legend; Paul never mentions the tomb or the women. **Concession:** the tomb is more disputed than the appearance experiences; crucifixion usually did mean no burial and one ossuary is one case; Joseph's role grows with apologetic motive; the guard story is in Matthew only and may answer the counter-story; Justin may be reporting Matthew back. **Turns on** premises 1-2: if nobody knew where the body lay, premises 4-5 prove nothing. **Levels:** Christ rose in his own body is defined (creeds; Lateran IV "rose in the flesh"), and an empty tomb follows from it; the tomb's evidential force is not defined: CCC 640, not by itself a direct proof.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md)

### Crossans burial objection

John Dominic Crossan (*Jesus: A Revolutionary Biography*, 1994, "The Dogs beneath the Cross"): denial of burial was part of Rome's terror, nobody who cared knew where the body went, and Joseph of Arimathea is Mark's creation.

Bodies were left for birds and dogs or thrown in a shallow grave the dogs reached; the disciples had fled; the later gospels make Joseph steadily more sympathetic (disciple, rich, new tomb), so the tomb stories are late narrative, not memory. Ehrman (*How Jesus Became God*, 2014) reaches a similar verdict with less certainty: Pilate had no reason to except a would-be king. Crossan holds the crucifixion certain and argues from Roman practice and the tradition's shape; meet him there. It is the objection to answer first, because everything about the tomb rests on the burial.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md)

### Criterion of embarrassment

A detail awkward for the tellers is unlikely to have been invented: here, women as the tomb's discoverers when women's testimony counted for little (Josephus, *Ant.* 4.8.15).

It shows that a detail belongs to the tradition, not that it is early. The criteria themselves are taught in [new-testament 3.2](../new-testament/lessons/03-02-sources-and-criteria.md).

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md)

### Stolen-body counter-story

The polemic that the disciples came by night and stole the body (Matt 28:13), still current in Matthew's day (28:15) and reported by Justin (*Dialogue with Trypho* 108).

It disputes **why** the body was gone, not **whether**. But it is Matthew's report of what opponents were saying, evidence about the polemic of his day, as strong as his report is accurate; it is not Jewish testimony to the empty tomb.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md)

### Ophthe

Greek *ophthe*, "he appeared" or "he was seen", the verb repeated through the witness list of 1 Cor 15:5-8.

In the Greek Old Testament it describes God appearing to Abram (Gen 12:7), a theophany; Paul uses it of his own experience, which Acts calls a "heavenly vision" (26:19) and Paul a revelation of the Son "in me" (Gal 1:16). It does not settle visionary versus physical seeing: both sides must admit that. The formula itself (received, core dated by most scholars to the 30s) is read in [new-testament 3.4](../new-testament/lessons/03-04-the-passion-and-resurrection-narratives.md).

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md)

### Vision hypothesis

The appearances were real psychological experiences, of ordinary sane people, with no object outside the seers.

**Gerd Ludemann** (*The Resurrection of Jesus: History, Experience, Theology*, 1994): Peter's grief and guilt after the denial produced a vision experienced as forgiveness; Paul's resolved the persecutor's inner conflict; the others followed in a chain of enthusiasm; the five hundred were a mass ecstasy, perhaps Pentecost; the body decayed. **Michael Goulder** ("The Baseless Fabric of a Vision", in D'Costa (ed.), *Resurrection Reconsidered*, 1996): conversion visions; group appearances as collective delusion (a 1977 Bigfoot scare in South Dakota). **The psychology is real:** W. Dewi Rees ("The Hallucinations of Widowhood", *BMJ* 1971): of 293 widowed people, almost half had hallucinations or illusions of the dead spouse, judged normal and helpful, mostly undisclosed. Strauss too appealed to visions for the appearances.

**The Catholic case (5.4):**

1. The list includes the Twelve, more than five hundred at once, all the apostles, and James (1 Cor 15:5-7).
2. Documented grief visions are individual and private; group visions with shared content are reported (Allison) but the literature is thin, no settled finding.
3. Paul (persecutor) and James (unbeliever, John 7:5, Mark 3:21) lack the grief motive.
4. Wright's [exaltation objection](#exaltation-objection).
5. [Cognitive dissonance](#cognitive-dissonance) explains persistence, not formation of a new specific belief.
6. ∴ Each rival explains part of the data; the conjunction covering all of it pays for every part.

**Concession:** grief visions are common and real; the hypothesis explains Peter's appearance well (there the ratio is near 1, and an apologist who calls it evidence against the vision hypothesis overreaches); *ophthe* does not exclude a vision. **Turns on** premise 2, which rests on silence about the five hundred: if the group reports are later tradition (Ludemann's Pentecost reading), the strain shrinks to James and Paul. A combined hypothesis can cover the data at the price of a conjunction of moderate, mutually dependent probabilities; whether that price exceeds the resurrection's low prior is 5.5's dispute. Not "the crazy-disciples theory". **Level:** that Christ truly rose (rung 1) rules out the vision hypothesis as the *whole* cause (CCC 644: not produced by faith or credulity; 645: a real, glorified body); the mode of the witnesses' perception is undefined.

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md)

### Legend hypothesis

The stories grew in the telling: the resurrection narratives as myth or legendary development.

D. F. Strauss (*Das Leben Jesu*, 1835-36): the community's faith put into narrative form; legendary growth explains the tomb stories and the physical detail (fish, wounds, Luke 24), while he too appealed to visions for the appearances. **The time-for-legend argument** (A. N. Sherwin-White, *Roman Society and Roman Law in the New Testament*, 1963: even two generations too short to erase a hard core): formula in the 30s, Paul in the 50s, Mark c. 70. It protects the early list, not the later narrative detail. In 5.3 the expansion from Mark to John is the skeptic's legend argument against the tomb.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md)

### Cognitive dissonance

Belief survives a crushed hope because believers cannot let it go, and proselytizing increases after disconfirmation.

Festinger, Riecken and Schachter, *When Prophecy Fails* (1956), as the authors reported it; John Gager, *Kingdom and Community* (1975), applied to early Christian mission (a crucified messiah as disconfirmation, mission as response). It explains persistence of a belief, not the formation of a new specific one: that one man has been raised ahead of the general resurrection.

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md)

### Exaltation objection

Wright: in a Second Temple setting a vision of a dead man yields "exalted", a ghost or angel ("It is his angel", Acts 12:15) or a martyr's vindication, not "raised", which meant a body leaving death behind.

**The reply (5.4), a good one:** Jewish expectation was more varied. Daniel 12:2 gave a live hope of bodily resurrection at the end ([old-testament 6.3](../old-testament/lessons/06-03-daniel-and-apocalyptic.md)); if the end had begun, a vision of the vindicated Jesus could be read as its *firstfruits* (1 Cor 15:20; cf. Matt 27:52-53); and Herod says John the Baptist "is risen again from the dead" with no tomb inspected (Mark 6:14-16). The objection lowers the vision hypothesis's likelihood, not to zero.

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md)

### Bayesian apologetic

Any argument that puts the resurrection into odds form; three terms (prior, likelihood ratio, posterior), and each author loads a different one.

Swinburne loads the prior (natural theology as background, then a modest $L$); the McGrews load the likelihood (an enormous $L$, the prior left to the reader). Critics aim at the loaded term: where did that prior come from? are those witnesses independent? Beneath both: [Philipse](#philipse-on-predictive-power), that the numbers cannot be assigned at all.

**The course's argument (5.5):**

1. The prior for "God raised Jesus" is fixed by the [total evidence](#total-evidence) about God, not by the frequency of corpses rising (Hume's base rate answers the wrong question).
2. On the data of 5.2-5.4 the resurrection hypothesis carries a substantial $L$ against the best naturalistic combination.
3. That $L$ is far below $10^{44}$: the strands share sources and the witnesses a community, so the cable is partly a [chain](#chain-and-cable).
4. For any prior a reasonable theist can defend after Modules 2-4, the posterior is substantial; for a prior near zero, nothing historical moves it.
5. ∴ A motive of credibility: reasonable for someone whose total evidence keeps theism live; it compels no one, and the disagreement sits mostly in the prior.

**Concession:** nobody knows how to assign the prior; Swinburne's one half is a judgment, the McGrews' $10^{39}$ an artefact of multiplication; refusing the inputs breaks no rule of probability. The numbers locate the disagreement, and that is the only gain claimed. **Turns on** the likelihood under the resurrection hypothesis: an apologist who used [skeptical theism](#skeptical-theism) against evil must explain why God's reasons become readable at Easter; the answer must come from revelation's content, not neutral ground. **Level:** *Dei Filius* ch. 3 certifies no calculation. Numbers: [Prior sensitivity](#prior-sensitivity), [Independence and the Bayes factor](#independence-and-the-bayes-factor).

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### Swinburnes calculation

Swinburne, *The Resurrection of God Incarnate* (2003), as summarized in Otte's NDPR review: a prior of one quarter and a likelihood ratio of 100 give about 0.97.

$P(\text{God} \mid k) = \tfrac12$ from natural theology; $P(\text{incarnation} \mid \text{God}) = \tfrac12$; so the prior for $c$ (God became incarnate) is $\tfrac14$. Evidence $f$: Jesus met the "prior requirements" (perfect life, moral teaching, claiming divinity, teaching an atonement, founding a church) and the "posterior" one, a vindicating super-miracle. $P(f \mid c\,k) = \tfrac1{10}$ (provisional), $P(f \mid \neg c\,k) = \tfrac1{1000}$, so $L = 100$ and $P = \tfrac{100}{103} \approx 0.971$. **Otte's criticism:** the half for incarnation given God is unsupported when rival possibilities are open, and the low $P(f \mid \neg c\,k)$ leans on God's not deceiving us, which many reject.

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### The McGrews Bayes factor

Timothy and Lydia McGrew (*The Blackwell Companion to Natural Theology*, 2009): the testimony yields a Bayes factor of $10^{44}$, not a posterior.

Women's report of the tomb $10^{2}$ × the disciples' testimony $10^{39}$ (counted witness by witness) × Paul's $10^{3}$ = $10^{44}$; they note it would overcome a prior as low as $10^{-40}$, leaving a posterior above 0.9999, but that prior is only illustrative. They argue collusion does not much weaken the factor. **The weak point is independence** (Hume's Part II): would the second disciple's conviction have appeared without the first's? Quoting "99.99 percent" as their verdict misstates them.

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### Philipse on predictive power

Herman Philipse (*God in the Age of Science?*, 2012) grants the Bayesian form and argues theism cannot supply its inputs.

(1) **Meaning:** psychological predicates get their sense from embodied agents, so "incorporeal person" cancels their conditions of application. (2) **Predictive power:** with no background knowledge of what a bodiless omnipotent being would want, theism predicts nothing specific, so nobody can fix $P(E \mid \text{God raised Jesus})$. (3) **Rivals:** secular hypotheses explain the evidence better. The dilemma he calls "the Tension": borrow science's standards and fail them, or decline them and lose the claim to rationality. He does not set the prior to zero by fiat or argue miracles impossible; calling him a naturalist by assumption is caricature.

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

## Module 6 Christianity and Judaism

The Christian messianic claim argued, the Jewish reading stated as Jewish readers state it, and the Church's present teaching on the Jewish people weighed at its true level. The texts are read side by side in [old-testament](../old-testament/syllabus.md); the record is [church-history](../church-history/syllabus.md)'s.

### Messiah

Hebrew *mashiach*, "anointed": a title given to kings, priests and even Cyrus, and in Jesus's day attached to plural hopes, all of which knew a messiah by what he does in history.

Kings (1 Sam 24:6, DR 1 Kings 24:7), the high priest (Lev 4:3), Cyrus (Isa 45:1). The Hebrew Bible almost never uses it of a future deliverer (Dan 9:25-26 disputed). Second Temple hopes ([new-testament 1.2](../new-testament/lessons/01-02-second-temple-judaism.md)): a royal son of David (*Psalms of Solomon* 17); messiahs of Aaron and of Israel (1QS 9.11); the prophet like Moses (Deut 18:15); or no messiah at all. Known by deeds: the Jerusalem Talmud (Taanit 4, 68d) reports Akiva hailing Bar Kosiba as King Messiah (Num 24:17), and the claim failed with the revolt (132-135); Deut 18:22 gives the same test for a prophet. No widely attested expectation was of a crucified messiah, so "the Jews rejected the Messiah" misdescribes the first century.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md)

### Prophecy argument

A text-event fit is evidence only if the text was fixed first, the reading was available without the event, the event could not be enacted or narrated to fit, and the misses are counted; the Christian claim survives as a pattern seen in hindsight.

**Opposing view (6.1), four parts:** (1) context: the proof-texts mean something else in their chapters (Hosea 11:1 the Exodus; Isa 7:14 a sign for Ahaz; Rashi's *peshat*; Isaac of Troki, *Hizzuk Emunah*, 1593, printed by Wagenseil with Latin and a refutation, 1681); (2) retrospect: only a believer sees Jesus there; (3) text: some proofs rest on the Greek; (4) the world is not redeemed (6.3). The Biblical Commission grants (2): Christians see the movement toward Christ retrospectively (*The Jewish People and their Sacred Scriptures in the Christian Bible*, 2001, no. 21), and the Jewish reading is "a possible one" (no. 22).

**The argument (6.1):**

1. A fit carries evidence ($L$ well above 1) only if it passes the four tests above.
2. [Proof-texting](#proof-texting) fails tests 2-4 for most items.
3. The Christian claim is not a checklist but that the scriptures' patterns find their unity in one person (Luke 24:27, 44; Dei Verbum 16), by [typology](#typology) and the disputed [sensus plenior](#sensus-plenior).
4. Some of the pattern was forced on the first Christians: a crucified Messiah (1 Cor 1:23) was nobody's choice, so test 3 is passed for those elements.
5. *Dei Filius* ch. 3 names prophecies among the exterior signs; can. 3 defines only that signs can make revelation credible (rung 1).
6. ∴ Done well, a modest strand: a motive of credibility, with ratio above 1 only for elements Christians did not control.

**Concession:** the "300 fulfilled prophecies" lists and the calculations on them are bad arguments. **Turns on** premise 3: "unity" is judged from inside a reading; a Jewish reader finds the unity in Torah and covenant (the Commission calls the readings "irreducible", no. 22), so the prior returns by the back door. **Levels:** can. 3 rung 1 (capacity); Dei Verbum 15-16 rung 3; the 2001 Commission document advisory, not magisterial (it also says an apologetic overstressing prophecy as proof should be discarded); the mode of fulfilment rung 5.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md)

### Proof-texting

Citing single verses as predictions ticked off: it fails in context, by enactment or narration, and by selection.

A colt for Zechariah 9:9 can be ridden on purpose; a detail known only from a gospel that quotes the verse may be "prophecy historicized" (Crossan, *Who Killed Jesus?*, 1995); verses are chosen after the fact, and the New Testament admits the retrospect (John 12:16). Conceded as bad arguments: the lists of "300 prophecies" and Peter Stoner's *Science Speaks* (one man fulfilling eight prophecies at 1 in $10^{17}$), which pass non-predictions, multiply dependent items and never count misses. Justin's checklist is their ancestor. Conceding them removes premise 2's items and leaves the pattern argument standing.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md)

### Typology

Events and persons in the Old Testament themselves point forward (the Exodus, the beloved son, the Davidic king), so fulfilment is a pattern, not a prediction.

The mode of fulfilment (type, fuller sense, prediction) is freely disputed (rung 5). That Christ fulfils the Servant prophecies is Church teaching (CCC 601), but no Church act defines the literal referent of any contested text. The senses of Scripture are taught in [old-testament 1.2](../old-testament/lessons/01-02-the-senses-of-scripture.md).

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md)

### Sensus plenior

A fuller sense intended by God but not clearly by the human author (the term's modern statement is Raymond Brown's, 1955).

Taught in [old-testament 4.3](../old-testament/lessons/04-03-the-servant-of-the-lord.md); a legitimate Catholic option, freely disputed (rung 5). Psalm 110 is the course's case: if its literal sense is a court oracle to the Davidic king, the Christian reading (Mark 12:35-37 assumes David speaks) needs an enthronement oracle God meant to be filled full in the Son. **Its price in an argument:** a fuller sense is visible only from the side of fulfilment; it explains how a Christian reads, and cannot be a premise against a Jewish reader.

*Lessons:* [6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md), [6.2](lessons/06-02-the-contested-texts.md)

### Collective reading

The Servant of Isaiah 52:13-53:12 is Israel (Jacob, the righteous within Israel), whose suffering the astonished nations confess in the "we" of chapter 53.

Grounds: the context names Israel God's servant (41:8; 44:1; 49:3). Rashi reads 53:8 as the nations speaking of Israel's exile and 53:10 ("he shall see seed") as children; Ibn Ezra glosses *lamo* in 53:8 as *lahem*, "to them", a plural. The Targum reads "my servant the Messiah" at 52:13 yet gives the suffering to Israel. Origen already met the collective reading in debate around 248 (*Contra Celsum* I.55): not a dodge invented against Christians. Parallel Jewish readings of the other texts (6.2): Daniel 7:13 is the King Messiah (Rashi), a man, and the kingdom goes to "the saints of the most high" (7:18, 27); Psalm 110 has *la-doni*, "to my lord", a human master (Rashi: Abraham, Gen 23:6); Zechariah 12:10 mourns those the nations slew (Rashi), with *Sukkah* 52a's Messiah son of Joseph or the evil inclination, neither divine. The readings themselves are owned by [old-testament 4.3](../old-testament/lessons/04-03-the-servant-of-the-lord.md) and [6.3](../old-testament/lessons/06-03-daniel-and-apocalyptic.md).

**The Christian convergence argument (6.2):** (1) several texts describe a figure their context does not exhaust (a Servant sent *to* Israel; one like a son of man riding the clouds as God does; a king who is priest for ever; one pierced and mourned like an only son); (2) independent Jewish interpretation read several messianically (Targum, Rashi, the Talmud); (3) no figure before Jesus combines the features; (4) the proclaimed Jesus does (Mark 14:62 joins Ps 110 to Dan 7); ∴ a motive of credibility that the pattern finds its unity in him. **Turns on** premise 3: the composite is assembled by a reader who already believes premise 4; one at a time, the Jewish reading usually has the plainer Hebrew; and the narratives were written by readers of these texts. Premise 2 proves only that the messianic reading is old and Jewish: a human Messiah still to come fits it as well.

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-the-contested-texts.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-jewish-reply-at-full-strength.md)

### Almah and parthenos

Isaiah 7:14's Hebrew *almah* means a young woman of marriageable age, not "virgin"; the virginal sense rests on the Septuagint's *parthenos* and Matthew 1:23.

Conceded in 6.2. The sign had a deadline inside Ahaz's reign (7:16); Rashi makes her the prophet's wife (8:3-4). Jerome read *almah* in his Hebrew and argued what it implies ([old-testament 4.2](../old-testament/lessons/04-02-isaiah-and-immanuel.md)). **Level:** the virginal conception is defined dogma (rung 1), resting on Matthew and Luke, not on the word.

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-the-contested-texts.md)

### Beloved son pattern

Jon D. Levenson (*The Death and Resurrection of the Beloved Son*, 1993): a recurring pattern of the beloved son given up to death and restored (Isaac, Joseph, Israel as firstborn, Exod 4:22), from which the gospel christology was built.

The step is in the texts: Greek Genesis calls Isaac the "beloved" son (22:2), the baptismal voice's word (Mark 1:11); Rom 8:32 echoes Gen 22:16; Zechariah's "only son" and "firstborn" are the same pattern. Levenson concludes Judaism and Christianity are **sibling rivals** for one blessing, not parent and child. Neither side can wave him away: the cross is not foreign to Israel's scriptures, yet the pattern belongs to Israel too and does not single out Jesus.

*Lessons:* [6.2](lessons/06-02-the-contested-texts.md)

### Maimonides criteria

Maimonides, *Mishneh Torah*, Laws of Kings 11-12: the Messiah is identified by a completed, public redemption, not by signs.

| Text | Criterion (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 11:1 | a king of David's line restores the kingdom, rebuilds the Temple, gathers the dispersed, restores full observance |
| 11:3 | no signs or wonders required: Akiva hailed Bar Kokhba without asking a miracle; when he was killed, the sages concluded he was not the one |
| 11:4 | presumed messiah: a Davidic king who studies Torah, leads Israel in it, fights the wars of the Lord; certain messiah: one who succeeds, rebuilds the Temple, gathers the exiles; one who fails or is killed is not he |
| 12:1 | nature unchanged; the wolf and lamb a figure for peace among the nations |
| 12:2-5 | the age ends subjection to foreign kingdoms, famine and war, so the world can give itself to knowing God |

The uncensored text of the same chapter names Jesus: a stumbling block that brought Israel to the sword and led much of the world to worship another beside God; yet Christianity and Islam spread talk of the Messiah, Torah and commandments, preparing the world to serve God together (Zeph 3:9). Severe, not contemptuous: report both halves. The criteria gather the prophets' own promises (Deut 30:3-5; Isa 2, 11), so calling them a late dodge is caricature.

**The Jewish case in five strands (6.3):** (1) the criteria are unmet; (2) the texts mean something else in context ([collective reading](#collective-reading); Kimhi, *Sefer ha-Berit*, twelfth century; Troki, late sixteenth); (3) the world is unredeemed ([Barcelona](#barcelona-disputation), Isaiah 2:4); (4) the Incarnation breaches the Shema (Deut 6:4); (5) modern voices: [carnal election](#carnal-election), [Neusner's refusal](#neusners-refusal). The setting matters: the medieval disputations were summoned by kings and friars, and Paris 1240 ended with the Talmud burned.

**The Catholic reply (6.3), whose job is to locate the disagreement:** (1) the criteria identify the Messiah by a completed redemption; (2) the Church claims a Messiah who has come and will come again (creedal, rung 1; CCC 840: Jews and Christians both await; Biblical Commission 2001 no. 21, advisory: Jewish waiting is not in vain); (3) whether two comings is a fair reading or a rescue depends on whether the scriptures hold a rejected, suffering or delayed figure (Isa 53, Zech 12:10, Dan 9:26; 6.2); ∴ the dispute is whether the checklist is the whole scriptural pattern. **Concession:** the world is unredeemed exactly as Maimonides names; the Christian answer reinterprets the criteria rather than meeting them; Christendom was not more peaceful; no argument here has persuaded the other side at scale, and many conversions were coerced. **Turns on** premise 3: each needed text has a Jewish reading at least as natural, and "he will come again" is an explanation no state of the world could refute. An argument that never moves its ablest hearers is a motive of credibility, not a demonstration ([epistemology 4.5](../epistemology/lessons/04-05-peer-disagreement.md)).

*Lessons:* [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-jewish-reply-at-full-strength.md)

### Barcelona disputation

July 1263, before James I of Aragon: the Dominican Pablo Christiani, a convert from Judaism, argued from the Talmud that the Messiah had come; Nahmanides answered under a royal guarantee of free speech.

Nahmanides's Hebrew account, the *Vikuah*, records three moves (paraphrased): **turn the proof-text** (the aggadah of a Messiah born on the day the Temple fell, told in the Jerusalem Talmud and Lamentations Rabbah, does not bind a Jew, and taken literally puts the birth in 70, against Christiani); **look at the world** (Isaiah 2:4 promises an end to war, and since Jesus the world has been full of violence, Christian nations not least); **name the real divide** (the root is the claim that God became man, which he held reason cannot accept; keeping Torah in exile under a Christian king earns more merit than under the Messiah). By his account the king gave him 300 gold coins; a Latin protocol reports the outcome differently. The Dominicans prosecuted him in 1265 over his account, and by 1267 he had left Aragon for the land of Israel.

### Carnal election

Michael Wyschogrod (*The Body of Faith*, 1983): God elected Abraham's bodily descendants and dwells in a people of flesh.

An Orthodox philosopher unusually open to the Incarnation's *form*: it develops something already in the Hebrew Bible and differs from Judaism in degree, not kind. Jews decline it because the Word of God as Israel hears it does not tell it: not impossible, but not Israel's story, and the commandments still bind.

### Neusners refusal

Jacob Neusner (*A Rabbi Talks with Jesus*, 1993) places himself at the Sermon on the Mount, listens with admiration, and would not have followed, because Jesus puts himself where the Torah stands, the Sabbath above all.

Engaged at length and with admiration by Benedict XVI, *Jesus of Nazareth* vol. 1 (2007), ch. 4, who grants that the question is who Jesus is. That book's foreword says it is not an exercise of the magisterium and readers may disagree: **no magisterial level**. Strand 5 of the Jewish case has no Catholic premise-level reply: it is a refusal to be persuaded, answered only by the whole case.

### The record conceded

For most of Christian history Christian teaching and power were turned against Jews; 6.4 states it first and without mitigation.

Chrysostom's eight homilies at Antioch (386-387), invective against the synagogue, later reprinted by Nazi propagandists ([patristics 3.6](../patristics/lessons/03-06-john-chrysostom.md)); the Rhineland massacres of May 1096 (Speyer about a dozen, Worms about 800, Mainz 700 to 1,100 by the sources' counts; [church-history 4.3](../church-history/lessons/04-03-the-crusades.md)); Lateran IV (1215) can. 68, distinguishing dress and Holy Week restrictions; the Talmud trial (Paris 1240) and burning (1242); expulsions from England (1290), France (1306), Spain (1492); the Shoah, some six million murdered in a Europe taught contempt for centuries ([church-history 9.1](../church-history/lessons/09-01-the-church-and-the-totalitarian-states.md)). Beneath it ran a theology: Israel rejected, collectively guilty, Matt 27:25 read as a curse; the 2015 reflection says replacement theory became the standard basis in the Middle Ages (§17). Papal letters (*Sicut Judaeis*) also forbade forced baptism and violence; that does not offset the rest. The record is not mitigation, and nothing in the argument answers it.

### Supersessionism

The view that the Church has superseded the Jewish people as God's covenant people; Soulen distinguishes three claims a Church text can reject separately.

R. Kendall Soulen (*The God of Israel and Christian Theology*, 1996): **punitive** (God revoked the covenant as punishment), **economic** (Israel's role was preparatory and passed to the Church), **structural** (the Christian story skips from Adam to Christ). **Opposing view:** the historical charge (the contempt was what the theology said; changing only after Auschwitz must be shown to be more than manners) and David Novak's conceptual charge ("Supersessionism Hard and Soft", *First Things*, 2019): hard supersessionism has Christians elected to displace the Jews; soft says Christianity brings something new while the Jews' covenant goes on; Christians cannot do without some soft supersessionism (else why not return to Judaism?), and Judaism has its own versions; its cost is the suspicion that Christians expect Jews to become Christians; "fulfilment" is economic supersessionism with better manners.

**The argument (6.4):**

1. Rom 11:28-29: enemies as regards the gospel, most dear as regards election; the gifts and calling of God are irrevocable (*ametameleta*).
2. [Nostra Aetate](#nostra-aetate) 4 builds on it and denies the [deicide charge](#deicide-charge) in substance.
3. So **punitive** supersessionism is ruled out at rung 3 (CCC 121: the Old Covenant never revoked; CCC 597 applies NA 4 to Matt 27:25).
4. **Structural** supersessionism contradicts the dogma that one God authors both Testaments (Trent) and the rejection of Marcion (rung 1).
5. What remains is the unicity of Christ (Acts 4:12; rung 1 by Ephesus and Chalcedon, not by [Dominus Iesus](#dominus-iesus)), which does not require Israel's election to have lapsed: not hard supersessionism.
6. ∴ Christ as universal saviour and Israel's unrevoked election can be affirmed together, and the theology behind the record contradicts present teaching.

**Economic** supersessionism is only *contested*, by a commission's formula: fulfilment, not replacement (2015 §§23, 32). **Concession:** Novak is right that a soft supersessionism remains, and Jews may find it unwelcome. **Turns on** how Jews share in salvation without explicit faith in Christ: the 2015 reflection calls it an unfathomable mystery (§36), honest but not an argument. **Levels:** see [We Remember and the 2015 reflection](#we-remember-and-the-2015-reflection); the mission question is freely disputed (rung 5).

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-supersessionism-nostra-aetate-and-the-jewish-people.md)

### Nostra Aetate

Vatican II's declaration on the Church's relation to non-Christian religions, 28 October 1965: rung 3, defines nothing.

**§4 (the Jews):** the Church received the Old Testament through the people of the ancient covenant and draws life from the olive tree onto which the Gentiles were grafted; God holds the Jews most dear for the fathers' sake and does not repent of his gifts and call (Rom 11:28-29); the passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today"; Jews are not to be presented as rejected or accursed as if Scripture taught it; the Church decries antisemitism at any time and by anyone. "Never revoked" is **not** in it: the 2015 reflection calls reading it there an over-interpretation (§39) and traces it to John Paul II at Mainz (17 November 1980), then CCC 121. **§3 (Muslims):** esteem for Muslims, who adore the one God, living and subsisting, merciful and almighty, Creator, who has spoken to men; who submit like Abraham; revere Jesus as a prophet, not as God, and honour Mary; await judgment; value prayer, almsgiving and fasting; it acknowledges "not a few quarrels and hostilities". It names neither Muhammad nor the Qur'an, and it is descriptive: rung 3 at most. Denying it is not heresy (can. 751 concerns rung 1).

## Module 7 Christianity and Islam

Islam stated as its theologians state it, then the Trinity, the Incarnation, the crucifixion and the prophethood of Muhammad argued. The Qur'an is cited by surah:ayah and paraphrased, never quoted. The record of conquest, crusade and polemic is conceded first (7.1, 7.5); the events are [church-history 3.2](../church-history/lessons/03-02-the-rise-of-islam-and-the-christian-mediterranean.md)'s.

### Tawhid

*Tawhid*, "making one": God's absolute oneness in being and in worship; he has no partner, equal or parts, and nothing is like him.

Surah 112 (God is One, the eternal refuge; he neither begets nor is begotten; nothing is comparable to him); 42:11 (nothing is like him). Its opposite is [shirk](#shirk) (4:48). In 7.3 it becomes *tanzih*, holding God clear of every likeness to creatures: God is not a body, cannot enter a creature by indwelling (*hulul*) or be united with one (*ittihad*), since either makes the eternal depend on the temporal; and a son would share the divine nature and be a partner. **Concession (7.1):** Islam's doctrine of God is not a cruder monotheism awaiting correction; Aquinas's God whose essence is existence comes through Avicenna, and the Mu'tazili worry about distinct eternal attributes is close to Thomist simplicity ([trinity-and-god 1.3](../trinity-and-god/lessons/01-03-simplicity-as-doctrine.md)). "Allah" is the Arabic word for God, used by Arab Christians.

*Lessons:* [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md), [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md), [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md)

### Kalam schools

The schools of *kalam*, dialectical theology, divided over whether God's attributes (knowing, powerful, willing, living, speaking) are real and distinct from his essence.

| School | Attributes | The Qur'an |
|---|---|---|
| Mu'tazila (from the eighth century) | identical with God, or negations of defects; distinct eternals would multiply eternal divine things | created |
| Ash'ari (al-Ash'ari, 874-935/6, broke with his teacher al-Jubba'i in Basra) | real and eternal, neither identical to God nor other than him | God's eternal, uncreated speech; recitation and writing created |
| Maturidi (al-Maturidi, d. 944) | with the Ash'aris, the main Sunni theology | uncreated |
| Traditionalists (Ibn Hanbal's heirs) | affirm every attribute the Qur'an affirms without asking how | uncreated |

Alongside ran *falsafa* (Avicenna's Necessary Existent). Al-Ghazali (c. 1056-1111), an Ash'ari who left the Nizamiyya in 1095, in *The Incoherence of the Philosophers* found twenty theses undemonstrated and charged unbelief on three (eternal world, God ignorant of particulars, no bodily resurrection; [ancient-medieval-philosophy 6.3](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/lessons/06-03-averroes-and-maimonides.md)). On simplicity the Thomist sits nearer the Mu'tazila than the Ash'aris. In 7.2 the Ash'ari formula offers a contested parity with the Trinity (Muslims answer that attributes are not entities, persons are).

*Lessons:* [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md), [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md)

### Mihna

The "ordeal" (833 to about 851): the caliphs from al-Ma'mun enforced the createdness of the Qur'an on judges and scholars.

Ahmad ibn Hanbal refused and was imprisoned and flogged under al-Mu'tasim; al-Mutawakkil abandoned the policy from 847, and it was finished by about 851 (sources differ). The state enforced the rationalist (Mu'tazili) side; the traditionalist who refused became a Sunni hero. An internal Muslim argument about *tawhid*, on Muslim texts, not orthodox clerics persecuting rationalists. The Mu'tazila were theologians (*mutakallimun*), not *falasifa*.

*Lessons:* [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md)

### Uncreated Quran

The Sunni outcome of the mihna: the Qur'an is God's eternal, uncreated speech (9:6 calls it God's speech).

The Ash'aris distinguish the eternal speech subsisting in God from its recitation, ink and paper, which are created. Revelation (*wahy*) is God's speech sent down (*tanzil*) to Muhammad through Gabriel (2:97), not his own word (53:4-5). Structural parallel (7.1): the Qur'an's place in Islam is Christ's in Christianity, not the Bible's. **Argument (7.1):** (1) Islam's account of revelation is coherent, with its own criterion: God's speech, sent down and preserved word for word; (2) Catholic teaching locates revelation chiefly in a person (Dei Verbum 2, 4), with Scripture inspired and its human writers true authors (DV 11); (3) each criterion applied to the other scripture finds it lacking; ∴ applying one criterion to the other begs the question: argue about God and history, not whose book looks more like a book. **Concession:** by the Islamic criterion the Gospels really are testimony written by others, and Catholic teaching agrees (DV 19). **Turns on** the reply that Jesus was given a book and the question is what became of it: [tahrif](#tahrif), a historical claim.

*Lessons:* [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md)

### Seal of the prophets

*Khatam al-nabiyyin* (33:40): Muhammad is the last prophet, the last restatement of the one message given to every community.

God sent a messenger to every community (16:36); the believer makes no distinction among the prophets (2:285); Jesus was given the *Injil* (5:46); the Qur'an confirms and guards what came before it (5:48). Islam understands itself as the restored religion of Abraham, a *hanif*, neither Jew nor Christian (3:67). **Foretold (7.5):** those who follow the unlettered prophet find him in their Torah and Gospel (7:157); Jesus announces a messenger named *Ahmad* (61:6); the texts adduced are Deuteronomy 18:18 (read of a prophet from Israel's brethren, the Ishmaelites) and the Paraclete of John 14-16; Ibn Ishaq's *Sira* glosses the Syriac *Munahhemana* of John 15 as Muhammad; Ali ibn Rabban al-Tabari's *Book of Religion and Empire* (ninth century) collected the proof-texts. **Christian readings, in context:** "brethren" means Israel (Deut 18:2; 17:15) and Acts 3:22 applies the prophet like Moses to Jesus; the Paraclete is the Spirit (John 14:16-17, 26). A Greek reading *periklytos* ("praised") for *parakletos* is a conjecture by some Muslim writers, attested in no manuscript.

*Lessons:* [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md), [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md)

### Shirk

Associating anything with God in his divinity or in the worship owed him; the sin God does not forgive (4:48; 4:116).

The Qur'an's charge against Trinitarian belief: 4:171 (Jesus a messenger, God's word cast to Mary and a spirit from him; do not say "three"; God is too exalted to have a son); 5:73 (unbelief to make God one of three, *thalith thalatha*); 5:72 (whoever associates partners is barred from paradise); [5:116](#quran-5-116). In 7.3, sonship itself is partnership (19:35; 6:101; 19:88-92): *walad* means offspring, and any son shares his father's nature, whatever kind of begetting is meant, so hearing 6:101 as a merely physical misunderstanding is too quick. **The Catholic reply (7.2):** shirk needs a second being beside God; the dogma posits none ([tritheism charge](#tritheism-charge)).

*Lessons:* [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md), [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md)

### Quran 5 116

God asks Jesus whether he told people to take him and his mother as two gods besides God; Jesus disowns it. What Christianity it has in view is disputed.

No known Christian body counted Mary a person of the Trinity. Readings: the Collyridians, whom Epiphanius (d. 403) accused of offering cakes to Mary (Goudarzi notes Epiphanius seems unsure what they believed); Michel Cuypers: a divine Mary as the absurd corollary of a divine Jesus, sharpened by *Theotokos*; Gabriel Reynolds: a deliberate *reductio* of Marian veneration; Goudarzi (2025), after Blachere and Nasr's *Study Quran* (2015): aimed at worship, a devotional "trinity" of Father, Son and Mary. Muqatil ibn Sulayman (d. 767) took the three of 5:73 as God, Christ and Mary and assigned it to the Melkites, wrongly by Goudarzi's account. "The Qur'an thought the Trinity was Father, Mother and Son" is one reading among several, not the dominant modern one.

*Lessons:* [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md)

### Tritheism charge

Ibn Taymiyya's dilemma: the divine persons are either attributes (then no three persons and no incarnation) or substances (then three gods, which is shirk).

Ibn Taymiyya (d. 1328), *al-Jawab al-Sahih li-man baddala din al-Masih*, replying to the *Letter from the People of Cyprus* (received 1316 or 1317), a reworking of Paul of Antioch's *Letter to a Muslim Friend* (Paul a Melkite bishop of Sidon: God one living, speaking being, essence, word and life; sun's disc, brightness, rays). Michel's partial translation (1984). **Four steps:** (1) why three? Christians disagree which attributes, and God's names are many; (2) an attribute is not a God and cannot be united to a man apart from its bearer: with the essence, Father and Spirit are incarnate too; without it, impossible; (3) "of one substance" makes the persons substances, so three gods; (4) the words were misread (Father = mercy; Son = one nurtured, John 20:17; Spirit = Gabriel; Matt 28:19 commands belief in God, messenger and angel): *tahrif al-ma'na*. Behind it, the **parts complaint**: every Christian analogy gives parts or aspects of one thing.

**The Catholic argument (7.2):**

1. Shirk ascribes divinity or worship to a being other than God: it needs a second being.
2. The three are one numerically single essence; each person *is* that whole reality; they are distinguished only by [relations of origin](#relations-of-origin) (rung 1).
3. The substance horn describes what the Church condemned: Lateran IV's second constitution against Joachim of Fiore's collective unity; the Athanasian Creed forbids three Gods; three wills is the route into tritheism ([trinity-and-god 6.4](../trinity-and-god/lessons/06-04-social-trinitarianism-and-its-critics.md)).
4. The attribute horn describes Paul of Antioch's model, not the dogma: the Father is himself wisdom, not wise by the Wisdom he begets (Augustine, *De Trinitate* VII.1-2).
5. So "attribute or substance" is incomplete: a person subsists (not an attribute) with no being, power or will beside the one essence (not a second substance).
6. ∴ The shirk charge misses the dogma; it lands on the attribute gloss and popular speech.

**The checkable test:** does a sentence count anything three except the relations? Three wills or powers is tritheist, and the Church condemns it. **Concession:** popular talk often sounds tritheist (the divine committee; clover and egg images of parts), and Ibn Taymiyya's reply to Paul of Antioch is sound; he answered what Christians sent him. **Turns on** premise 5: it shows the charge misses, not that the doctrine is understood. To outsiders one may show only that it is **not impossible** (ST I q.32 a.1), and each analogy fails in a stated way under Lateran IV's rule of greater unlikeness ([thomistic-synthesis 4.4](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/04-04-analogy.md)).

*Lessons:* [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md)

### Relations of origin

One numerically single divine essence, which each person is; the persons are distinct only by their relations of origin (begetting, being begotten, spiration).

Lateran IV (1215): undivided in essence, distinct in the persons' properties; Florence (1442): all in God is one where no opposition of relation intervenes; CCC 253-255: one God, not three, each person God whole and entire. Rung 1 ([trinity-and-god 3.2](../trinity-and-god/lessons/03-02-one-ousia-three-hypostaseis.md), [3.4](../trinity-and-god/lessons/03-04-the-latin-rules-of-speech.md)). The analysis as **subsistent relation** is Latin theology below the seam, never defined ([trinity-and-god 4.4](../trinity-and-god/lessons/04-04-four-relations-three-persons.md)); presenting it to a Muslim as defined overstates its level. In 7.2 Example 2 the assumption of a humanity is the common act of the three, terminating in the Son alone (ST III q.3 a.4).

*Lessons:* [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md)

### Incarnation

Chalcedon (rung 1): one and the same Son, perfect in Godhead and in manhood, in two natures, without confusion or change; not God converted into man.

[christology 2.3](../christology/lessons/02-03-eutyches-leo-and-chalcedon.md); CCC 464 (not part God and part man, not a mixture). Nicaea: "begotten, not made". The union is a relation real in the creature, conceptual in God (ST III q.2 a.7); the humanity has no hypostasis of its own and exists in the Word's ([christology 3.1](../christology/lessons/03-01-person-and-nature-the-hypostatic-union.md)). Aquinas, *De rationibus fidei* (1264), written against Muslim objections: rejects God converted into man (ch. 6); the Son's generation as the mind's conceiving its word (ch. 3). Immutability of the Word is defined, which closes the kenotic reply ([christology 3.3](../christology/lessons/03-03-kenosis.md)).

**Opposing view:** the transcendence objection ([tawhid](#tawhid): *tanzih*; the Messiah only a messenger who, with his mother, ate food, 5:75; unbelief to say God is the Messiah, 5:72, 5:17); sonship as partnership ([shirk](#shirk)); **Abu 'Isa al-Warraq**, *Against the Incarnation* (ninth century; ed. and tr. David Thomas, 2002), argued the Melkite, Nestorian and Jacobite accounts each incoherent; the lesson reconstructs his line as a dilemma (not his wording): either the Word changed and is not God, or nothing changed and the union is only a relation, leaving a servant of God (43:59; 4:172).

**The argument (7.3):**

1. The transcendence objection rules out God changing into a creature, contained in a body, mixed with man, or begetting a son in time.
2. Catholic doctrine denies each at rung 1 (Nicaea; Chalcedon; Aquinas, *De rationibus fidei*).
3. The union is real in the creature, conceptual in God: something new on the creature's side, nothing changed in God.
4. Against [incoherence](#incoherence-objection): contradictory predicates hold of one subject by reason of distinct natures (communication of idioms); Morris's fully vs merely human.
5. ∴ The objection refutes a doctrine Catholics also reject; what remains is a mystery shown not impossible.

**Concession:** Christians routinely misstate the [Islamic Jesus](#isa-in-the-quran). **Turns on** al-Warraq's second horn: if the union is real only on the creature's side, what makes this man God's own? "No human hypostasis" is a claim, not a model; Chalcedon fences the mystery without mapping it. *Tanzih* is about what God is, not what he can do: "God can do anything" is a caricature reply. "A word from God" (3:45) is not the Johannine Logos: classical exegesis ties it to the creative command (3:47; 3:59).

*Lessons:* [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md)

### Isa in the Quran

The Qur'anic Jesus, a high Christology that honours him uniquely and refuses him divinity.

*'Isa ibn Maryam*: virginal conception (3:47; 19:20); speaks from the cradle as God's servant, given the Book and made a prophet (19:30); *al-Masih*, the Messiah (3:45; 4:171), a title without the Davidic or divine sense; a word from God (3:45) and a spirit from him (4:171); miracles by God's permission (bird of clay, healing the blind and the leper, raising the dead: 3:49; 5:110); strengthened by the holy spirit (2:87; Gabriel in classical exegesis); pure (19:19); servant, not son (19:30; 43:59); raised to God (4:158); his return a sign of the Hour (43:61, as many read it). Mahmoud Ayoub, "Toward an Islamic Christology", in *A Muslim View of Christianity* (ed. Irfan A. Omar): a genuine Christology Christians should meet as such. Nostra Aetate 3 records that Muslims revere Jesus as prophet, not God, and honour Mary: descriptive, rung 3 at most. "Muslims regard him as just a prophet", "the Qur'an denies the virgin birth", "never calls him Messiah" are all wrong.

*Lessons:* [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md)

### Incoherence objection

Divine and human predicates contradict (necessarily uncreated, omniscient, impassible vs created, limited, able to suffer), so nothing can bear both.

Michael Martin, *The Case Against Christianity* (1991). John Hick, *The Metaphor of God Incarnate* (1993): "two natures in one person" has never been given a literal content that survives analysis, so read it as metaphor for Jesus's openness to God. **Replies:** predicates are said of one subject by reason of distinct natures (impassible as God, suffering as man; [christology 3.2](../christology/lessons/03-02-the-communication-of-idioms.md)); Thomas Morris (*The Logic of God Incarnate*, 1986): created and limited are common, not essential, human properties, so Christ is **fully** but not **merely** human; the [two-minds view](#two-minds-view). The kenotic reply (the Son lays aside omniscience) is closed to Catholics. **Critics' rejoinder:** "as God" and "as man" either change the predicates or leave the contradiction where it was.

*Lessons:* [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md)

### Two-minds view

Morris (*The Logic of God Incarnate*, 1986): the incarnate Son has a divine mind and a human mind, the divine containing the human with asymmetric access, in one person.

A model offered against the incoherence objection; theology, not dogma.

*Lessons:* [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md)

### Quran 4-157

The non-crucifixion verse: they neither killed nor crucified him, but it was made to seem so to them (*shubbiha lahum*); God raised him to himself (4:157-158).

*Shubbiha lahum* has no stated subject ("he was made to resemble another for them" or "it was made obscure to them"), and interpretation grows from the gap. Four main readings (7.4):

1. **Substitution:** another bore his likeness and was crucified; Jesus raised alive. Al-Tabari (d. 923) collects reports differing on who (a volunteer companion promised paradise; others name Judas or Simon of Cyrene). The majority reading of classical Sunni exegesis.
2. **Agency:** the verse denies the boast, not the event; God took him (cf. 8:17, Badr). Mahmoud Ayoub: the Qur'an denies that men can destroy God's Word; Todd Lawson (*The Crucifixion and the Qur'an*, 2009): it neither affirms nor denies the event's history, and the doctrine of denial built up later. Cited: 19:33; 3:55 (*mutawaffika*, disputed).
3. **Body, not person:** Isma'ili thinkers (Abu Hatim al-Razi, Abu Ya'qub al-Sijistani, tenth-eleventh centuries): the body was crucified; his true, spiritual self was not killed. "Docetic" is a Western label that fits poorly.
4. **Swoon:** the Ahmadiyya (after Mirza Ghulam Ahmad, d. 1908): crucified, did not die, recovered, died in Kashmir.

**Opposing view (revelation over report):** if the Qur'an is God's speech and its plainest sense is substitution, the reports record what *appeared* to happen, which the verse says misled. **The argument (7.4):** (1) the crucifixion under Pilate is independently attested (Paul in the 50s, 1 Cor 1:23, Gal 3:13; all four gospels; Josephus, *Ant.* 18.3.3, in the part of the *Testimonium* most scholars keep; Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44, hostile); (2) it embarrassed the movement and tops the historians' near-consensus; (3) [tahrif](#tahrif) of wording cannot remove it: it is in every stratum, including sources no Christian scribe controlled; (4) three of the four readings grant a public crucifixion; only substitution contradicts the history, by a miracle of appearance that by design leaves no trace; ∴ the disagreement is between the evidence and one reading of the Qur'an. **Turns on** premise 4 against a substitution reader: history can show the reading unsupported, not false, and the parity cuts, since history cannot certify that God raised Jesus either. "Islam denies the crucifixion" states one reading as the tradition. **Level:** crucified under Pontius Pilate is creedal, rung 1.

*Lessons:* [7.4](lessons/07-04-the-crucifixion-and-the-corruption-charge.md), [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md)

### Tahrif

The charge that the People of the Book corrupted their scriptures: of meaning (*tahrif al-ma'na*, the text survives but is misread) or of wording (*tahrif al-lafz*, the text itself changed).

Qur'anic basis: God sent down the Torah and the *Injil* (3:3); some shift words from their places (4:46; 5:13; 5:41; 2:75). Holders of corruption of meaning alone could cite 5:47 (judge by what God revealed in the Gospel) and 10:94 (a scripture that could be consulted). **Ibn Hazm** of Cordoba (d. 1064), *Kitab al-Fisal*, the classical exponent of corruption of wording: the gospels are not the *Injil* sent down but four accounts by four men after him; they contradict one another (genealogies, resurrection accounts); they lack *tawatur*, mass transmission through independent chains; the Torah was rewritten in Ezra's time. **Ibn Taymiyya** (*al-Jawab al-Sahih*) took a middle position: some wording altered in transmission and translation, but the books can still be read critically. In 7.2 his reading of Father, Son and Spirit is *tahrif al-ma'na*; in 7.5 the Qur'an's coherence with prior revelation assumes divergent earlier books were corrupted. **Concession (7.4):** the New Testament has not come down word for word: many variants, mostly trivial; Mark's oldest manuscripts end at 16:8 and the longer ending (canonical, rung 1) is judged by most critics not Mark's; the *Comma Johanneum* (1 John 5:7) is a late Latin addition, defended by the Holy Office in 1897, never as a definition, and narrowed in 1927 ([new-testament 6.4](../new-testament/lessons/06-04-peter-jude-and-the-letters-of-john.md)); and Christians made the corruption charge first (Justin against Jewish teachers). **Reply:** variants are detectable *because* the manuscripts survive; wholesale rewriting would leave a different trail. The NT text is reconstructed, not intact. Apply the same care to the Qur'an ([Uthmanic recension](#uthmanic-recension)).

*Lessons:* [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md), [7.4](lessons/07-04-the-crucifixion-and-the-corruption-charge.md), [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md)

### Uthmanic recension

The authoritative standard text of the Qur'an under the caliph Uthman (r. 644-656), after a first collection under Abu Bakr.

Uthman's committee, led by Zayd ibn Thabit, sent copies to the garrison cities and had rival codices (such as Ibn Mas'ud's) destroyed. **Sana'a palimpsest** (found 1972, Great Mosque): an erased lower text overwritten with the standard one; parchment radiocarbon-dated with 95% probability to 578-669 (Sadeghi and Bergmann, 2010); the lower text differs in wording and sura order. Sadeghi and Goudarzi ("San'a' 1 and the Origins of the Qur'an", *Der Islam*, 2012): a non-Uthmanic companion codex supporting an early fixed standard; Asma Hilali (*The Sanaa Palimpsest*, 2017): both layers school exercises. Wansbrough's late-canonization thesis (1977) has been weakened by the early manuscript evidence. Divine guarding (15:9), like the inspiration of Scripture, is a faith claim above what textual criticism can show. No Church act pronounces on the Qur'an's text. See also [qiraat](#qiraat).

*Lessons:* [7.4](lessons/07-04-the-crucifixion-and-the-corruption-charge.md)

### Qiraat

The canonical readings (*qira'at*) within the Uthmanic consonantal skeleton (*rasm*), varying in dotting and vowels.

Ibn Mujahid (d. 936) canonized seven readers; Ibn al-Jazari (d. 1429) added three to make ten; each reader has two transmitters. Most printed copies follow Hafs from 'Asim (Cairo edition, 1924); Warsh from Nafi' is widely read in North Africa.

*Lessons:* [7.4](lessons/07-04-the-crucifixion-and-the-corruption-charge.md)

### Ijaz

*I'jaz*, the inimitability of the Qur'an: the classical doctrine that the Qur'an is Muhammad's evidentiary miracle, an open challenge no one has met.

Challenge verses: produce one surah like it (2:23; 10:38), or ten (11:13); men and jinn together could not produce its like (17:88). A prophet's sign (*mu'jiza*) is a custom-breaking act + a claim of prophethood + an unmet challenge, and is then God's endorsement. Al-Baqillani (d. 1013), *I'jaz al-Qur'an*: its style fits no known genre and stays eloquent on every subject. 'Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani (d. 1078), *Dala'il al-I'jaz*: the miracle is *nazm*, composition, against *sarfa* (associated with the Mu'tazili al-Nazzam: God prevented the Arabs from answering). Weight added: Mecca's poets, opposed and best placed, did not answer; the reciter was *ummi*, unlettered, with no prior scripture (7:157; 29:48). Not circular: an open challenge joined to a theory of the evidentiary miracle.

**The Muslim case for prophethood, four strands (7.5):** (1) the Qur'an as miracle; (2) character and transformation (known among his people a lifetime, 10:16; *al-Amin*; 68:4; tribal Arabia united in worship of one God; burial of daughters condemned, 81:8-9; al-Ghazali's *Deliverance from Error*: a prophet known as Galen is known a physician, by many indications, not one miracle, which might be magic); (3) foretold ([seal of the prophets](#seal-of-the-prophets)); (4) coherence with prior revelation, divergences being [tahrif](#tahrif) (no contradiction in it, 4:82). A cumulative case with an empirical challenge at its centre.

**The Catholic argument (7.5):**

1. A claimant is tested against what God has already revealed (Deut 13:1-3; Gal 1:8); fruits (Matt 7:15-20) test false prophets, not certify true ones.
2. Public revelation is closed ([closure of public revelation](#closure-of-public-revelation)).
3. The Qur'an, as its classical commentators read it, denies that Jesus was crucified (4:157), as secure a fact as ancient history has.
4. ∴ A book presented as God's speech that denies a public, well-attested event fails Deuteronomy's own test (18:22).

Replies by strand: a unique style shows genius, not who the author is; character is granted but necessary, not sufficient; the foretelling texts read in context mean Israel and the Spirit; 4:157 against the crucifixion. **Concessions:** the polemical record (John of Damascus, *On Heresies*, Islam as heresy and Muhammad schooled by an Arian monk; Aquinas, *SCG* I.6; Dante, *Inferno* 28; Riccoldo, *Contra legem Sarracenorum*, c. 1300; Robert of Ketton's Latin Qur'an, 1143, for Peter the Venerable) was largely ignorant and often written to justify fear or war, and Aquinas's charges are not a reliable account; and the **success argument** cuts both ways (Augustine, *City of God* XXII.5; Aquinas's conversion of the world; *Dei Filius* ch. 3's propagation as a motive of credibility, chapter text, not a canon): conquest is not conversion, and success is weak evidence for both or neither. Keep apart "was he sincere and good?" from "did God speak through him a revelation binding on all?". **Turns on** premise 3, which holds only against the substitution reading; on the agency reading the contradiction largely disappears.

*Lessons:* [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md)

### Closure of public revelation

Public revelation was completed in Christ and closed with the apostles: no new public revelation is to be awaited before his return.

Dei Verbum 4 restates it and defines nothing new; CCC 66; *Lamentabili* (3 July 1907) condemned its denial as proposition 21. Commonly counted of faith: rung 1 by the ordinary and universal magisterium, with no single definition ([fundamental-theology 1.4](../fundamental-theology/lessons/01-04-public-and-private-revelation.md) owns the distinction). Against a Muslim it shows only that the two positions are incompatible, since Islam also claims finality; it argues nothing toward either's truth. The argument against prophethood rests on history (4:157), not on closure.

*Lessons:* [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md)

## Module 8 Pluralism, salvation, and apologetics in a secular age

Hick at full strength and the price of pluralism; the Church's teaching on salvation outside the visible Church, with its history unsoftened; and what a changed default does to argument. The typology and Plantinga's replies are owned by [philosophy-of-religion 5.5](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/05-05-religious-diversity.md).

### Exclusivism, inclusivism, pluralism

Exclusivism: where traditions conflict one is right, and (in its salvation form) only its path saves. Inclusivism: one tradition is normative, but its truth or saving power reaches people outside it. Pluralism: no tradition is privileged.

Race's typology (1983), owned by [philosophy-of-religion 5.5](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/05-05-religious-diversity.md) (with Hick's contentlessness problem, D'Costa's "covert exclusivism", Plantinga on arrogance and arbitrariness). Keep two axes apart: **truth** and **salvation**. Hick (*An Interpretation of Religion*, 1989; 2nd ed. 2004) reached pluralism as an evangelical, through years among Birmingham's Muslim, Sikh, Hindu and Jewish communities: the [soteriological criterion](#soteriological-criterion) supplies the datum; **the moral argument against exclusivism** (*God and the Universe of Faiths*, 1973: a God of universal love would not exclude most humans by accident of birth) the motive; [the transcategorial Real](#the-transcategorial-real) the explanation. Hick does not say all religions teach the same thing: their doctrines conflict read literally, which is why he reads them as myth.

**The Catholic argument (8.1):**

1. Hick's datum and motive are accommodated by any view letting grace and goodness work beyond the visible Church.
2. Catholic inclusivism is such a view (Nostra Aetate 2: the Church rejects nothing true and holy in other religions; [Lumen Gentium 16](#lumen-gentium-16)).
3. Both predict the datum, so its likelihood ratio between them is about 1.
4. Pluralism carries costs inclusivism avoids: (a) the **yardstick problem**: a transcategorial Real cannot measure "Reality-centred", so the yardstick is Hick's own ethic, which Christian, Theravada and Muslim conceptions of saintliness do not share; (b) **a first-order creed**: Incarnation as metaphor, resurrection not literal fact, *anatta* not literally true, so it contradicts each tradition as sharply as exclusivism; (c) [arbitrariness](#arbitrariness-charge) returned.
5. ∴ Pluralism is not the humble default but one more first-order proposal, explaining its best datum no better while paying more.

**Concession:** holiness visibly exists outside the Church, and exclusivist answers on the unevangelized have often been callous (billions consigned to hell as an inference, without grief); a reply that does not begin there is not answering Hick. **Turns on** premise 2: inclusivism reads a devout Muslim's holiness as Christ's grace, a description she would reject. The Catholic answer: inclusivism overrides a tradition's account of *how* someone is saved, pluralism its account of *what is true*; whether that difference is principled or convenient is open. Pluralism is consistent; the charge is that it is *not neutral*, a weaker charge than self-contradiction. Hinduism and Buddhism get no lesson: the disagreement starts further down (creator, self, single history), Advaita has its own inclusivism, and stating them at full strength needs Sanskrit and Pali sources the course does not read; Nostra Aetate 2 names both with respect.

*Lessons:* [8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md)

### Soteriological criterion

Hick: judge a tradition by the transformation from self-centredness to Reality-centredness it produces, since that is what it claims to be for.

Hick claims rough parity across the great post-axial traditions: each produces the transformation at rates we cannot tell apart, and each is a mixture of good and evil with none visibly morally superior across its history. Paul Knitter's soteriocentric variant (*No Other Name?*, 1985; *One Earth Many Religions*, 1995) judges by human and ecological well-being. Reply: the [yardstick problem](#exclusivism-inclusivism-pluralism).

*Lessons:* [8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md)

### The transcategorial Real

Hick's Real *an sich*, to which none of our concepts (one or many, personal or impersonal, good or evil) applies literally.

Kant's distinction of noumenon and phenomenon applied to the divine (which Kant did not do). The Real is experienced through a culture's concepts as **personae** (Yahweh, the Holy Trinity, Allah, Vishnu) and **impersonae** (Brahman, Nirvana, the Dharmakaya). Doctrines conflict only read literally; read as myth, true insofar as they evoke the right response, they need not. Hick applied it to Christianity: *The Myth of God Incarnate* (ed., 1977); *The Metaphor of God Incarnate* (1993).

*Lessons:* [8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md)

### Arbitrariness charge

Favouring one's own tradition without a neutral reason.

8.1's reply: Hick ranks too. His criterion admits only the great post-axial traditions and excludes destructive cults, favouring one standard without a neutral argument. The charge lands on every position, his included, and so decides nothing between them. Plantinga's replies are philosophy-of-religion 5.5's.

*Lessons:* [8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md)

### Dominus Iesus

CDF declaration of 6 August 2000, ratified by John Paul II (16 June 2000): authentic ordinary magisterium as an act; it sets forth again what was taught and defines nothing new.

§3: "sets forth again"; §4: rejects theories justifying religious pluralism "in principle" (*de iure*), not only as fact; §20: the necessity of the Church "must be firmly believed" (reporting a rung fixed elsewhere, not creating it); §21: contrary to the faith to treat the Church as one way of salvation alongside others; how grace reaches non-Christians remains for theologians to explore; §22: equality of persons in dialogue, not of doctrinal content; others in a "gravely deficient situation". It names no theologian. The **unicity of Christ** it restates is rung 1 by Ephesus and Chalcedon, not by DI ([christology 6.4](../christology/lessons/06-04-christology-today.md)); in 6.4 that unicity is what leaves soft supersessionism in place, and DI rejects two ways of salvation (cf. the 2015 reflection §35). It did not newly define pluralism as heresy.

*Lessons:* [6.4](lessons/06-04-supersessionism-nostra-aetate-and-the-jewish-people.md), [8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md), [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Extra ecclesiam nulla salus

"Outside the Church no salvation": a claim about how salvation is given (the Church is necessary), not a census of who is saved.

Cyprian (250s; Letter to Jubaianus, ANF Ep. 72.21 = Hartel 73.21) said it of schismatics and heretics, to deny their rites worked ([patristics 2.4](../patristics/lessons/02-04-cyprian-of-carthage.md)); Lateran IV (1215, ch. 1) made it a profession of faith (outside the one universal Church no one at all is saved); Florence ([Cantate Domino](#cantate-domino)) named the groups. **Opposing view (8.2), two camps with one charge** (Florence and LG 16 contradict): the **traditionalist** (Leonard Feeney, St Benedict Center, *From the Housetops*: Florence taught with the pope, "firmly believes, professes and preaches", and *Dei Filius* ch. 4 forbids changing a dogma's meaning, so the plain sense binds; Feeneyism also denies that baptism of desire or blood saves without water baptism) and the **critic** (pluralists and secular historians: the Church changed its mind and calls the reversal "development"; Francis A. Sullivan, *Salvation Outside the Church?*, 1992, supplies evidence without the conclusion: the medieval formulas presumed culpability, which collapsed on meeting peoples who had never heard the Gospel).

**The argument (8.2):**

1. The core is necessity (Lateran IV, Florence; LG 14: whoever *knows* the Church is necessary and refuses to enter or remain cannot be saved; DI §20 "must be firmly believed").
2. Necessity always admitted fulfilment by desire (Trent, Decree on Justification, 1547, ch. 4: the washing of regeneration *or the desire for it*; ST III q.68 a.2, Ambrose on Valentinian).
3. [Invincible ignorance](#invincible-ignorance) was taught a century before Vatican II, beside the axiom.
4. The [1949 Holy Office letter](#feeney-case) joined them.
5. Formulas are conditioned in expression, determinate in meaning (*Mysterium Ecclesiae*, 1973; [fundamental-theology 5.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md)).
6. ∴ What LG 16 teaches does not contradict what Florence *taught*: Florence taught necessity and that whoever culpably dies separated is lost; LG 16 that grace, always from Christ and related to the Church, can reach the inculpably ignorant. The religions are not parallel ways (DI §21).

The modern texts keep "two truths together" (*Redemptoris Missio*, 7 December 1990, §9): the real possibility of salvation in Christ for all, and the necessity of the Church. **Concession:** the fathers of Florence meant what they wrote about pagans and Jews, almost certainly assumed culpability, and the bull states no such condition; the 1949 reading is not what 1442 would have said it meant, and claiming the earlier text "really" taught LG 16 is forbidden. The hardest development in the course. **Turns on** separating the *proposition taught* from the *assumption shared* ([development of doctrine](#development-of-doctrine)). **Levels:** necessity rung 1; how grace reaches non-Christians rung 5 (Ad Gentes 7, ways known to God; DI §21). What "the Church" means and where baptized non-Catholics stand is [ecclesiology-and-mariology](../ecclesiology-and-mariology/syllabus.md) 5.1-5.2's.

*Lessons:* [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Cantate Domino

Florence's Bull of Union with the Copts (Eugenius IV, session 11, 4 February 1442): no one outside the Catholic Church, pagans, Jews, heretics or schismatics, can share eternal life unless joined to it before the end of life.

Operative verbs: the Roman Church "firmly believes, professes and preaches". It names Jews, and the record of 6.4 gives that sentence its weight. **Level:** a papal-conciliar doctrinal act; its core (necessity of the Church) rung 1; but with no "we define" and no anathema, the **census reading** (every member of the named groups dying outside is lost) is *not manifestly defined* under can. 749 §3, and the Church's later reading interprets it otherwise. Some traditionalists hold the whole sentence defined: report both readings.

*Lessons:* [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Invincible ignorance

Ignorance of the true religion that is without fault: it does not exclude from salvation one who keeps the natural law and is ready to obey God.

Pius IX, *Quanto conficiamur moerore* (encyclical to the bishops of Italy, 10 August 1863, §§7-8), taught it in the same document as the axiom: rung 3. Its doctrine is received by the 1949 letter and LG 16.

*Lessons:* [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Feeney case

The 1949 Holy Office letter to Archbishop Cushing of Boston: the axiom is dogma, to be understood as the Church understands it, and the Church is necessary as a means that can be used by implicit desire.

Decided in plenary session 27 July 1949, approved by Pius XII 28 July, dated 8 August (Prot. 122/49). Necessity as a commanded means; a means used only by desire can suffice; the desire may be implicit under invincible ignorance, but must be animated by perfect charity and supernatural faith (Heb 11:6); cites Trent and *Mystici Corporis* (1943: related by unconscious desire). Lumen Gentium 16's footnote cites it. It judged the teaching of Feeney's periodical not Catholic. **Level:** a dicastery letter with ordinary papal approval, to one archbishop, not printed in the *Acta Apostolicae Sedis*: rung 3, light grade; its doctrine gained weight when a council footnoted it. Feeney was excommunicated 13 February 1953 for disobedience (refusing summonses to Rome), and reconciled in 1972, reportedly without retracting: the record is mixed.

*Lessons:* [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Lumen Gentium 16

Vatican II's dogmatic constitution on the Church (21 November 1964), §16: those who, without fault, do not know the Gospel but seek God sincerely and, moved by grace, try to do his will known through conscience can attain salvation.

It also places Muslims first among those who acknowledge the Creator: "professing to hold the faith of Abraham", they adore with us the one merciful God who will judge on the last day (7.1; paraphrased in CCC 841; "professing" reports the Muslim claim without endorsing or rejecting it). Providence does not deny the helps necessary for salvation to those who "without blame on their part" have not reached explicit knowledge of God (2.3, conceding nonresistant nonbelief). LG 14 keeps the knowing-refusal clause. **Level:** rung 3; Vatican II defined no new dogma, and LG 16 defines nothing; owed religious submission, and denying it is not heresy. It says grace reaches people *despite* invincible ignorance, not that other religions save; calling it "pluralism with Catholic vocabulary" is a caricature Hick would reject too.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md), [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md), [8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md), [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Development of doctrine

Growth in the understanding of a doctrine that preserves its meaning; Newman's notes test whether a change is development or corruption.

Owned by [fundamental-theology 5.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/05-02-newmans-theory-of-development.md). The course's hardest case: Florence to LG 16. Development must show preservation of type and conservative action on the past, and that separating the proposition taught from the culpability assumed is not merely convenient. Helping evidence: Cyprian's claim was narrower than Florence's, and Pius IX held both truths at once. Against it: Florence's sentence, read alone, gives the separation no foothold, and nothing between Florence and Trent-Pius IX withdrew the strong reading; the tradition brought forward qualifications it already held.

*Lessons:* [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md)

### Secular age

Charles Taylor (*A Secular Age*, 2007): secularity 1 (religion withdrawn from public institutions), 2 (decline of belief and practice), 3 (changed conditions of belief: faith one option among several, often not the easiest).

The course's subject is the third sense. The **immanent frame**: a world intelligible in natural terms, livable as open or closed to transcendence; believer and unbeliever are **cross-pressured**. Two consequences: the burden of proof moves by mood, not argument (in 1500 the unbeliever owed reasons); and a valid argument may win only [notional assent](#notional-and-real-assent). The arguments did not become fewer or weaker; the default moved. The sociology is owned by [social-theory](../social-theory/syllabus.md) Module 7.

*Lessons:* [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md)

### Relativism as a stance

"That's true for you" is usually not a thesis that all truth is relative (which refutes itself) but a stance: a policy of not imposing, not judging, letting each choose.

Bas van Fraassen (*The Empirical Stance*, 2002) supplies the notion of a stance; applying it here is the lesson's move. Refuting a thesis nobody holds is the cheap move (the self-refutation is in [epistemology 3.4](../epistemology/lessons/03-04-contextualism-and-its-rivals.md)). **At its best** (Taylor, *The Ethics of Authenticity*, 1991, Massey Lectures: "soft relativism", rooted in the ideal of authenticity): religious argument tries to settle for someone what only they can settle; imposed belief has a record; respect forbids treating deep commitments as reasoning errors. **Pressing it:** Taylor's internal reply: authenticity is intelligible only against *horizons of significance* that matter whether or not I choose them; respect is owed whether or not anyone chooses to owe it. That does not refute the stance; it reopens the question it was adopted to close. **Concession:** the soft relativist is right that respect forbids treating commitments as puzzles, and the Church has itself confessed the record of imposed belief.

*Lessons:* [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md)

### Argument from desire

C. S. Lewis: innate desires have real objects; we find a longing no finite good satisfies; so we were probably made for another world.

Lewis, *Mere Christianity* (1952) bk 3 ch. 10, "Hope" (not marriage, not travel, not learning); earlier *The Weight of Glory* (1941). **Opposing view:** John Beversluis (*C. S. Lewis and the Search for Rational Religion*, 1985; revised edition 2007): (1) is it a natural desire, like the biological cases? (2) propositional content: a longing for what no one can describe shows no object, still less God; (3) universality: many are satisfied by careers, spouses, children; (4) provenance: God as single object of all desire is Platonic before biblical.

**Restated (8.3):**

1. Innate, widespread kinds of desire are, as a rule, for kinds of things that exist.
2. Humans commonly experience a longing no finite good satisfies even at its best (Augustine's restless heart, *Confessions* I.1; Lewis's "Joy").
3. Naturalism predicts restlessness (never being satisfied pays) but not obviously this shape: a longing that recognizes every finite good as not what it wants.
4. ∴ The longing is more expected if there is a transcendent good: one strand, ratio above 1, not a proof.

Grants Beversluis's second objection outright (the object is only "nothing finite"); **concedes** the universality premise is weaker than Lewis stated and that the content are not self-deceived. Aquinas's cousin (ST I q.12 a.1: a natural desire to know the first cause would be in vain) presupposes God, so it works inside a case, not at its door. **Turns on** premise 3: an evolutionary story on which an endlessly unsatisfiable drive is adaptive may predict the shape too, pushing the ratio toward 1.

*Lessons:* [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md)

### Notional and real assent

Newman (*Grammar of Assent*, 1870, ch. IV): notional assent is given to abstractions; real assent to things apprehended as concrete, and it is reached through imagination, facts and history more than syllogism.

Newman, quoting his own letters of 1841 (ch. IV §3), public domain: "The heart is commonly reached, not through the reason, but through the imagination, by means of direct impressions, by the testimony of facts and events, by history, by description." Not anti-intellectualism. Argument can help turn notional into real assent; it cannot produce faith, so losing an argument does not show faith false, and winning one gives no one faith.

*Lessons:* [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md)

### Illative sense

Newman's name (*Grammar of Assent* ch. IX) for the trained judgement that converging probabilities, each too fine to avail alone, have converged enough for certitude.

Informal inference (ch. VIII §2) is "the cumulation of probabilities, independent of each other", and proof is the limit converging probabilities approach. **Argument (8.3):** in concrete matters (a historical event, a character, a verdict) we rightly reach certitude by converging probabilities judged by a trained faculty; whether Christianity is true is such a matter; so certitude can be reasonable without demonstration, and the cumulative case is its right shape. What 1.3 does with odds Newman does with judgement, which can rightly go beyond what the arithmetic displays. **Turns on** the illative sense itself: a judgement that is its own standard is hard to tell from mere confidence, and two prepared minds can reach opposite certitudes ([epistemology 4.5](../epistemology/lessons/04-05-peer-disagreement.md)). **Level:** not Church teaching; Newman's analysis of faith is freely disputed ([fundamental-theology 2.2](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-02-credibility-and-the-preambles-of-faith.md)), and *Dei Filius* adopts no analysis of faith.

*Lessons:* [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md)

## Formulas

The odds-form arithmetic the lessons compute, compact. Odds form itself is owned by [philosophical-method 2.4](../philosophical-method/lessons/02-04-weighing-evidence-in-odds-form.md); conditionalization by [epistemology 5.3](../epistemology/lessons/05-03-conditionalization.md); the problem of priors by [epistemology 5.4](../epistemology/lessons/05-04-the-problem-of-priors.md). Every number below is the lessons' own; most are invented to show structure, not estimates.

### Odds form

Posterior odds are prior odds times the likelihood ratio.

$$\frac{P(H \mid E)}{P(\neg H \mid E)} = \frac{P(H)}{P(\neg H)} \times L, \qquad L = \frac{P(E \mid H)}{P(E \mid \neg H)}$$

$H$ is the hypothesis, $E$ the evidence. $L > 1$ favours $H$, $L < 1$ counts against it, $L \approx 1$ (a datum every rival predicts, e.g. the minimal facts, Peter's appearance in 5.4) adds nothing. Odds $a:b$ is the probability $a/(a+b)$. To reach even odds from prior odds $o$ needs $L = 1/o$.

*From* [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### Cumulative odds

Multiply each item's ratio given the items already counted; independent strands multiply, a shared premise counts once.

$$\frac{P(G \mid E_1 \ldots E_n)}{P(N \mid E_1 \ldots E_n)} = \frac{P(G)}{P(N)} \times L_1 \times L_2' \times \cdots \times L_n', \qquad L_k' = \frac{P(E_k \mid G, E_1 \ldots E_{k-1})}{P(E_k \mid N, E_1 \ldots E_{k-1})}$$

$G$ theism, $N$ its rival. 1.3's illustration (prior $1:6$, about 14%):

| Case | Odds | Probability |
|---|---|---|
| strand 1 alone, $L = 2.5$ | $1:6 \times 2.5$ | 29% |
| strand 2 alone, $L = 2$ | $1:6 \times 2$ | 25% |
| strand 3 alone, $L = 1.8$ | $1:6 \times 1.8$ | 23% |
| all three independent | $\frac16 \times 2.5 \times 2 \times 1.8 = 1.5$ | 60% |
| strand 3 shares strand 1's premise, $L_3' = 1.2$ | $\frac16 \times 2.5 \times 2 \times 1.2 = 1$ | 50% |
| independent, plus suffering at $0.6$ | $1.5 \times 0.6 = 0.9$ | 47% |
| shared premise, plus suffering | $1 \times 0.6 = 0.6$ | 38% |

No strand alone passes even odds; together they do, only while they fail independently, and the omitted suffering term is still in the true product. Chain contrast: links at 0.8, 0.85, 0.9 give 0.612.

*From* [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md)

### The evil term and skeptical theism

2.3, Example 1 (invented numbers): conceding the evil ratio usually beats full skeptical theism, because skepticism also erases the theist's own likelihoods.

Prior $3:2$ for $T$ against HI; one fine-tuning strand at $L = 4$ gives $6:1$; concede Draper's $O$ at $L_O = \tfrac25$:

$$6 \times \tfrac25 = 12:5 \approx 71\%$$

Full skeptical theism makes both $O$ and fine-tuning inscrutable, leaving $3:2 = 60\%$. Conceding wins whenever $4 \times L_O > 1$, i.e. $L_O > \tfrac14$; the evil term would need $L_O = \tfrac16$ to bring the whole case to even.

*From* [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md)

### EAAN consistency check

Fitelson and Sober's inconsistency point, with 3.2's illustrative numbers.

With $\Pr(R \mid N \& E) = 0.2$, $\Pr(R \mid \text{theism}) = 0.99$, the two exhaustive and priors $0.5$ each:

$$\Pr(R) = 0.5(0.2) + 0.5(0.99) = 0.595$$

far from the near-certainty Plantinga assigns $R$. To force $\Pr(R) = 0.95$, theism's prior must be $\frac{0.95 - 0.2}{0.99 - 0.2} = \frac{75}{79} \approx 0.95$: consistent only if theism is already nearly certain. If God acts *through* evolution (screened off), $\Pr(R \mid \text{theism} \& E) = \Pr(R \mid E)$ and the likelihood version gives the Thomist nothing.

*From* [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md)

### Prior for a miracle

The prior for $R$ (God raised Jesus) is a product of a prior for God and the chance God would raise this man; it is not the base rate of corpses rising.

$$\Pr(R \mid B) = \Pr(G \mid B) \times \Pr(\text{raise} \mid G, B)$$

$B$ background knowledge, $G$ God exists. 5.1, Example 2 (invented): two historians agree $L = 500$ and $\Pr(\text{raise} \mid G, B) = \tfrac1{100}$.

| Historian | $\Pr(G \mid B)$ | $\Pr(R \mid B)$ | Prior odds | Posterior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.6 | 0.006 | $3:497$ | about $3.02:1$, 75% |
| B | 0.01 | 0.0001 | $1:9{,}999$ | about $0.050:1$, 4.8% |
| Ehrman's rule | | 0 | 0 | 0 for any $L$ |

The historical work ($L$) was identical every time: the verdict lives in the prior, which cuts both ways (A cannot call 75% "what history shows").

*From* [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md)

### Prior sensitivity

Hold $L$ fixed and move the prior: Swinburne's calculation as a function of $P(\text{God})$.

$L = \frac{1/10}{1/1000} = 100$; prior for incarnation $= P(\text{God}) \times \tfrac12$.

| $P(\text{God})$ | Prior | Posterior odds | Posterior |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\tfrac12$ (Swinburne) | $\tfrac14$, odds $1:3$ | $100:3$ | $\tfrac{100}{103} \approx 0.971$ |
| $\tfrac1{10}$ | $\tfrac1{20}$, odds $1:19$ | $100:19$ | about 0.84 |
| $\tfrac1{1000}$ | $\tfrac1{2000}$, odds $1:1999$ | $100:1999$ | about 0.048 |

On a log scale of prior odds, one power of ten moves you one unit along the curve; with $L = 100$ the posterior passes even at prior odds $1:100$; changing $L$ shifts the whole curve sideways.

*From* [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

### Independence and the Bayes factor

A Bayes factor built by multiplying witnesses is only as large as their independence.

The McGrews: $10^{2}\ (\text{women}) \times 10^{39}\ (\text{disciples, witness by witness}) \times 10^{3}\ (\text{Paul}) = 10^{44}$; at their illustrative prior $10^{-40}$, posterior above 0.9999. 5.5, Example 2 (invented): recount the disciples as one community of testimony at $10^{3}$:

$$10^{2} \times 10^{3} \times 10^{3} = 10^{8}, \qquad 10^{-40} \times 10^{8} = 10^{-32}$$

Thirty-six orders of magnitude vanish. No agreed number lies between $10^{3}$ and $10^{39}$ (Paul and James stood outside the circle; the disciples talked to one another): argue the dependence structure strand by strand and report a range.

*From* [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md)

## Pitfalls

### Caricature first

- Stating the other side as its opponents describe it fails the problem before the reply is read: presumption of atheism as "atheists know there is no God"; Oppy as saying there is no evidence or believers are irrational; Ehrman as denying the resurrection; Crossan as denying the crucifixion; Philipse as assuming naturalism; Wielenberg as "morality is evolved feeling". *([1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-answering-the-atheist-philosophers.md), [3.4](lessons/03-04-morality.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md))*
- Presuppositionalism is an argument, not fideism; Kierkegaard and Barth are serious Christians who think defence betrays the faith, not anti-intellectuals. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md))*
- Plantinga's target is evolution *plus* naturalism, not evolution; Anscombe was a Catholic Thomist who disputed Lewis's argument, not his conclusion. *([3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md))*
- Illusionists do not say nobody feels anything; panpsychists do not say rocks have opinions; vision theorists do not say the disciples were deranged. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-consciousness.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md))*
- Allison is neither a skeptic nor an ally; quoting one half of him is caricature. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md))*
- The Jewish readings were not invented to evade Christians (Origen met the collective Servant c. 248; Rashi reads Daniel 7 messianically); Maimonides's criteria gather the prophets' own promises; report both halves of his judgement on Christianity. *([6.2](lessons/06-02-the-contested-texts.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-jewish-reply-at-full-strength.md))*
- *I'jaz* is an open challenge, not a circle; "Islam denies the crucifixion" states one reading as the tradition; Ibn Taymiyya answered the explanation Christians sent him; *tanzih* is about what God is, not what he can do; the Qur'anic Jesus is not "just a prophet". *([7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md), [7.3](lessons/07-03-the-incarnation.md), [7.4](lessons/07-04-the-crucifixion-and-the-corruption-charge.md), [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md))*
- Hick does not say all religions teach the same thing; "true for you" is usually a stance, not a thesis; refuting a thesis nobody holds is the cheap move. *([8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md), [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md))*

### Overstating the level of a document

- *Dei Filius* defines **capacities** (reason *can* know God; signs *can* make revelation credible; miracles *can* prove), never that any argument succeeds: no proof, no cumulative case, no calculation, no historical argument is canonized. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md), [1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md), [2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-answering-the-atheist-philosophers.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md))*
- No magisterial act condemns Van Til, adopts the argument from reason or the moral argument, endorses a theodicy, a design argument, NIODA or Newman's illative sense; the propagation sentence of *Dei Filius* ch. 3 is chapter text, not a canon. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-three-strategies.md), [2.3](lessons/02-03-evil-and-hiddenness-the-replies-that-cost-something.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-reason.md), [3.4](lessons/03-04-morality.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md), [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md), [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md))*
- *Dignitatis Humanae* does not teach all religions equally true; *Humani Generis* neither endorsed nor condemned evolution; the 1996 message defined nothing and did not say "more than one hypothesis"; the 1992 address did not "rehabilitate" Galileo; the 1951 address defined nothing. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md))*
- "The beginning cannot be demonstrated" is Aquinas's thesis (rung 5); the beginning itself is rung 1. Promoting the first or demoting the second is an exegetical error. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md))*
- Nostra Aetate defines nothing and does not contain "never revoked"; NA 3 names neither Muhammad nor the Qur'an; *Jesus of Nazareth* has no magisterial level; the 2015 reflection and the 2001 Biblical Commission document are not magisterial. *([6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md), [6.3](lessons/06-03-the-jewish-reply-at-full-strength.md), [6.4](lessons/06-04-supersessionism-nostra-aetate-and-the-jewish-people.md), [7.1](lessons/07-01-islam-on-its-own-terms.md), [7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md))*
- "Subsistent relation" is Latin theology, not dogma; the mode of the witnesses' perception is undefined; the Comma and the authorship of Mark 16:9-20 are not defined; no Church act defines the literal referent of any contested text. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md), [6.2](lessons/06-02-the-contested-texts.md), [7.2](lessons/07-02-tawhid-and-the-trinity.md), [7.4](lessons/07-04-the-crucifixion-and-the-corruption-charge.md))*
- Dominus Iesus defines nothing new; its "must be firmly believed" reports a rung fixed by Lateran IV and Florence. Lumen Gentium 16 does not say other religions save. The census reading of *Cantate Domino* is not manifestly defined. *([8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md), [8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md))*
- Understating is the mirror error: treating *Dei Filius* can. 1 as one school's opinion, or a rung-3 text as mere opinion. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md))*

### Proof versus motive of credibility

- An argument that fails as a proof is not thereby a ratio of 1; an argument that is "dialectically spent" may still be sound. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-assembling-the-case.md))*
- Every conclusion in Modules 5-7 is a motive of credibility: "history proves the resurrection" is the wrong slogan, a 75% posterior is history's ratio times a philosophical prior, and the empty tomb is not by itself a direct proof (CCC 640). *([5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-empty-tomb.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md))*
- Apologetics can make faith reasonable; it cannot produce it. Losing an argument does not show faith false; winning one gives no one faith. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md), [8.3](lessons/08-03-apologetics-in-a-secular-age.md))*
- The McGrews' $10^{44}$ is a Bayes factor, not a posterior; Swinburne's 0.97 is a calculation the Church does not certify. *([5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md))*
- Closure of revelation shows Islam and Catholicism incompatible; it argues nothing to a Muslim. *([7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md))*

### Methodological versus metaphysical naturalism

- Methodological naturalism (a rule inside the sciences, coined by a Christian) does not entail the metaphysical thesis; *Dei Filius* can. 2 condemns only metaphysical materialism. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md))*
- Defeating Draper and White does not defeat Coyne, whose thesis is about method. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md))*
- E3 of Ehrman's objection is methodological naturalism applied to history: a rule about what a discipline may assert, not evidence that $R$ is improbable. *([5.1](lessons/05-01-can-history-establish-a-miracle.md))*
- Never bet on a gap: argue from what a phenomenon is, not from what has not been found; the Thomist God was never competing for a slot. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-naturalism-at-its-best.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md))*

### "Science proved creation"

- A beginning would not prove creation and an eternal past would not refute it: creation is dependence at every moment, not an edge. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md))*
- The Big Bang is a hot dense state, not "the universe from nothing"; BGV and the singularity theorems are classical; whether there was an absolute beginning is open. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md))*
- The multiverse attacks one likelihood, not creation; quantum mechanics has not "shown God can act". *([4.3](lessons/04-03-cosmology-what-a-beginning-would-and-would-not-show.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-divine-action-miracles-and-quantum-indeterminacy.md))*
- ID's critics have not shown design false, and ID's proponents have not shown it present; the Thomist wants neither. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-evolution-design-and-the-human-person.md))*

### Head-counts as premises

- "Most scholars accept" is a pointer to evidence, never a premise; a guild's agreement is not independent testimony, and Habermas's counts cannot be checked. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md))*
- Skeptics grant experiences *taken to be* appearances, not that the disciples saw Jesus. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-minimal-facts-approach-and-its-critics.md))*
- No inflated number: "300 fulfilled prophecies", Stoner's 1 in $10^{17}$, and the McGrews' witness-by-witness multiplication all fail on independence. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-weighing-it-the-bayesian-apologetic-and-its-limits.md), [6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md))*
- Adding strands helps only if each ratio stays above 1 given the others; an omitted unfavourable item is still in the true product. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-burden-of-proof-and-the-shape-of-a-cumulative-case.md))*
- A datum every rival predicts (Peter's appearance) is not evidence against any of them. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-the-appearances-and-the-rival-hypotheses.md))*

### The record stated as mitigation

- Concede the record first and without offset: *Sicut Judaeis* forbidding forced baptism is true and does not offset Chrysostom, the Rhineland, Lateran IV can. 68 or the Talmud burnings. *([6.4](lessons/06-04-supersessionism-nostra-aetate-and-the-jewish-people.md))*
- "The Jews rejected the Messiah" misdescribes the first century: no widely attested expectation was of a crucified messiah. *([6.1](lessons/06-01-the-messianic-claim-and-how-a-prophecy-argument-works.md))*
- Christian polemic against Muhammad (John of Damascus to Riccoldo; Aquinas's *SCG* I.6 charges) is conceded, not reused; character attacks belong to that record. *([7.5](lessons/07-05-the-prophethood-of-muhammad.md))*
- The Galileo condemnation was an error by Church authority; the 1951 address overclaimed; say so plainly. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-the-conflict-thesis-tested-in-both-directions.md))*
- Florence meant what it wrote about pagans and Jews; do not claim it "really" taught Lumen Gentium 16. *([8.2](lessons/08-02-salvation-outside-the-visible-church.md))*
- Exclusivist answers on the unevangelized were often callous; a reply to Hick begins there. *([8.1](lessons/08-01-pluralism-and-its-price.md))*

## Conventions

- **One card per course**, covering all 31 lessons; the linter checks every lesson is cited.
- **Argued, honestly.** Each argument entry gives the Catholic argument as the lesson numbers it, the opposing position as its own defenders state it, the named concession, and the premise the case turns on. No entry gives a verdict the lesson does not.
- **Levels are as the lessons mark them**; the ladder itself is [fundamental-theology](../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md)'s. Where the build's checks corrected the syllabus (e.g. the 1951 address is more careful than its reputation; Benedict XVI's 2007 remarks were at Auronzo di Cadore; Peirce, not Newman, owns the cable), the card follows the lessons.
- **The Qur'an is cited by surah:ayah and paraphrased**, never quoted; Arabic terms are transliterated in the body, plain in headings.
- **Boss problems** are not answered here: entries give positions and arguments at outline level only.
- **Headings are anchors**, ASCII only. Renaming a `###` breaks inbound lesson links.
- **No prose dollar signs.**
