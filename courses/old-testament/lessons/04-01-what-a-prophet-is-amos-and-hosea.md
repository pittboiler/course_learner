# The Old Testament · Lesson 4.1: What a prophet is: Amos and Hosea

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 4: The prophets · Builds on: [3.3 Two kingdoms and two falls](03-03-two-kingdoms-and-two-falls.md), [1.2 The senses of Scripture](01-02-the-senses-of-scripture.md) · Unlocks: [4.2 Isaiah and Immanuel](04-02-isaiah-and-immanuel.md), [4.4 Jeremiah and Ezekiel](04-04-jeremiah-and-ezekiel.md), [4.5 The Book of the Twelve](04-05-the-book-of-the-twelve.md)

## Why this matters

Ask most people what a prophet does and they say "predicts the future." Read Amos and you find a man shouting at a shrine about bribed judges and cheated grain buyers. This lesson gives you the tools for every prophetic book that follows: the word *nabi*, the call, the stock forms of an oracle, and the covenant behind them. It tries them out on the two earliest prophets whose words became books, Amos and Hosea, both sent to the northern kingdom in its last decades.

## The idea

A prophet is first a **messenger**. God tells Moses, "I have appointed thee the god of Pharao; and Aaron, thy brother, shall be thy prophet" (Ex 7:1, Douay–Rheims), having said earlier that Aaron "shall be thy mouth" (4:16). A prophet is someone else's mouth. The job is **forthtelling** (speaking for God into the present) before it is **foretelling** (announcing what will happen). What the eighth-century prophets foretell is mostly what follows from the covenant if Israel keeps breaking it. Think of a covenant as a treaty with terms, and the prophet as the envoy who arrives to read out the charges.

## Source

Amos 5:18, 20–24 (Douay–Rheims):

> "Woe to them that desire the day of the Lord: to what end is it for you? the day of the Lord is darkness, and not light. ... Shall not the day of the Lord be darkness, and not light: and obscurity, and no brightness in it? I hate, and have rejected your festivities: and I will not receive the odour of your assemblies. And if you offer me holocausts, and your gifts, I will not receive them: neither will I regard the vows of your fat beasts. Take away from me the tumult of thy songs: and I will not hear the canticles of thy harp. But judgment shall be revealed as water, and justice as a mighty torrent."

Two surprises. First, Amos's hearers *longed* for "the day of the Lord" (*yom YHWH*) as the day God would beat Israel's enemies. Amos turns it against them: darkness, not light. Second, the cult is not attacked for being wrong in form. It is refused because the worshippers do injustice. "Judgment" and "justice" render *mishpat* and *tsedaqah*, fair verdicts and right dealing. The Hebrew verb means "let it roll" like water. The Vulgate's "revealed" seems to read it from a similar-looking root.

## The argument

1. **The word.** *Nabi* is Israel's usual term. Most scholars link it to a Semitic root "to call" (Akkadian *nabû*). Whether it means "one called" or "one who calls out" is disputed. Older titles survive: "he that is now called a prophet, in time past was called a seer" (1 Samuel 9:9 [Douay 1 Kings 9:9]). The Greek *prophētēs* means "one who speaks for" as much as "one who speaks beforehand." *In words:* the title names a function, carrying a word, not a gift for prediction.
2. **The call.** A prophet's authority rests on being sent, not on office. The call narratives share a pattern (Norman Habel's analysis): God confronts, commissions, meets an objection, reassures, and gives a sign, often at the **mouth**. Isaiah's lips are touched with a coal (Isa 6:5–8). Jeremiah protests "I am a child" and hears "I have given my words in thy mouth" (Jer 1:6–9). Ezekiel eats the scroll (Ezek 2:8–3:3). Amos's version is the bluntest: "the Lord took me when I followed the flock" (Amos 7:15). See [call narrative](../reference.md#call-narrative).
3. **The messenger formula.** "Thus saith the Lord" (*koh amar YHWH*) is how any ancient envoy began. Jacob's servants were to open with "Thus saith thy brother Jacob" (Gen 32:4). The Assyrian envoy opened with "Thus saith the great king" (2 Kings 18:19 [Douay 4 Kings 18:19]). Claus Westermann (*Basic Forms of Prophetic Speech*) showed that the typical oracle is a **judgment speech**. It gives an **accusation** ("because ...") and an **announcement** ("therefore ..."), with the formula marking whose verdict it is. See [messenger formula](../reference.md#messenger-formula).
4. **The covenant lawsuit.** Some oracles take the shape of a trial. Hebrew *rib* means a legal case or dispute. Hosea 4:1 says "the Lord shall enter into judgment" (Douay); literally, "the Lord has a case" against the land. Micah 6:1–8 summons the mountains as witnesses, recites what God did (Egypt, Moses), lets the people ask what sacrifice would satisfy, and gives the verdict: "to do judgment, and to love mercy, and to walk solicitous with thy God" (6:8). Herbert Huffmon and G. Ernest Wright read this as a fixed genre modelled on treaty or court procedure. Michael De Roche and others doubt that *rib* names a formal lawsuit form. A deeper dispute: Lothar Perlitt argued that covenant (*berit*) theology is Deuteronomistic, later than Amos and Hosea. Others point to Hosea 6:7 and 8:1 ("they have transgressed my covenant") as early. See [covenant lawsuit](../reference.md#covenant-lawsuit).
5. **Forthtelling before foretelling.** The threats are covenant sanctions, and they can be conditional. Twice Amos intercedes and "The Lord had pity upon this" (Amos 7:3, 6). He pleads "Seek ye me, and you shall live" (5:4). Prediction is still real. Deuteronomy 18:22 makes an unfulfilled prediction the mark of a false prophet. Amos foresaw exile (7:17), and Samaria fell in **722** (secure). Aquinas, who calls prophecy chiefly a kind of knowledge, still lists "Deal thy bread to the hungry" (Isa 58) among its objects (ST II-II q.171 a.3). *In words:* the prophet reads the present in God's light, and the future follows from it.
6. **Amos.** He was a herdsman of Tekoa in Judah (Amos 1:1). He preached in the north at Bethel, the royal shrine, under Jeroboam II. Jeroboam reigned roughly 788–747, a *reconstructed* date that chronologies set a few years apart, so Amos worked around 760 (also *reconstructed*). His book opens with oracles against seven nations, each "For three crimes ... and for four." The trap springs on the eighth: Israel (2:6). Election means accountability: "You only have I known ... therefore will I visit upon you all your iniquities" (3:2). His oracles on the poor sold "for a pair of shoes" (2:6, 8:4–6) are located here and handed off. [`history-of-debt` 1.3](../../history-of-debt/lessons/01-03-release-laws-in-ancient-israel.md) has the law, and [`theology-of-debt`](../../theology-of-debt/lessons/01-04-creditors-under-judgment.md) 1.4 has the theology.
7. **Hosea.** He is the northern kingdom's own prophet, active from Jeroboam II to about the eve of Samaria's fall (Hos 1:1; *reconstructed*). God commands him to "take thee a wife of fornications" (1:2). Her children's names are verdicts: Jezrahel (Jezreel), "Without mercy" (*Lo-ruhamah*), "Not my people" (*Lo-ammi*). The last carries the words "I will not be yours" (1:9). Many commentators hear in the Hebrew *lo ehyeh lakhem* ("I am not I-AM to you") an echo of the divine name of Exodus 3:14. Then comes the reversal. God will "allure her ... into the wilderness" and "espouse thee to me for ever ... in justice, and judgment, and in mercy" (2:14, 19). The covenant is a marriage, and Israel's worship of Baal is adultery. The key word is *hesed*, loyal covenant love. The Douay renders it "mercy." Hosea 4:1 finds "no mercy" in the land, and 6:6 gives God's demand: "For I desired mercy, and not sacrifice." See [hesed](../reference.md#hesed).

**Mark the levels.** That the Holy Spirit "spake by the Prophets" is creedal (Constantinople I, 381), and so is [rung 1](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md). The inspiration of these books is taught at the level set out in [`fundamental-theology` 3.1](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/03-01-inspiration.md). Vatican I's *Dei Filius* (ch. 3) counts prophecies among the "most certain proofs" of revelation ([`fundamental-theology` 2.2](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-02-credibility-and-the-preambles-of-faith.md)). That is teaching about fulfilled prophecy as a sign. It is not a theory of what prophets mostly did. The forms are another matter. Messenger formula, judgment speech, call pattern and *rib* are **form-critical hypotheses with no magisterial standing** ([Church teaching vs hypothesis](../reference.md#church-teaching-vs-hypothesis)). *Divino Afflante Spiritu* encouraged the study of literary forms but endorsed no particular classification. No early Biblical Commission response touches Amos or Hosea. Which lines are later additions is disputed: many critics take Amos 9:11–15 and some Judah references in Hosea as later editing, and others defend them as original. Whether Amos and Hosea already thought in terms of a Sinai covenant is a live scholarly question too (step 4). Dates: 722 is *secure*; the reigns and the prophets' careers are *reconstructed* ([dating discipline](../reference.md#dating-discipline)).

## The forms

| Form | Marker | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call narrative | commission, objection, sign at the mouth | Isa 6; Jer 1; Ezek 2–3; Amos 7:14–15 | grounds authority in being sent |
| Messenger formula | "Thus saith the Lord" | Amos 1:3, 2:6 | marks whose word it is |
| Judgment speech | "because ..." then "therefore ..." | Amos 5:11 | accusation, then sentence |
| Covenant lawsuit (*rib*) | summons, witnesses, charges, verdict | Hos 4:1–3; Mic 6:1–8 | puts Israel on trial under the covenant |
| Woe oracle | "Woe to ..." | Amos 5:18 | a funeral cry over the living |

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (clean): Amos 4:1–3.** "Hear this word, ye fat kine that are in the mountains of Samaria: you that oppress the needy, and crush the poor ... The Lord God hath sworn by his holiness, that lo, the days shall come upon you ..."

- *Summons:* "Hear this word," addressed to the "fat kine," the cows of Bashan in the Hebrew, usually taken as Samaria's wealthy women.
- *Accusation:* oppressing the needy while demanding drink from "your masters."
- *Announcement:* an oath, "hath sworn by his holiness," followed by deportation through the breached walls.
- *Closing formula:* "saith the Lord" (4:3).

There is no "Thus saith" at the start, and that is common. The forms are flexible, so name only the elements that are actually present. Literal sense first: this is an oracle against the elite of Samaria before 722. Its moral sense for any reader who lives comfortably off someone else's labour is real, but that sense is built on the literal one ([1.2](01-02-the-senses-of-scripture.md)).

**Example 2 (hard): Hosea's marriage, event or vision?** Hosea 1 narrates the marriage as fact: he "went and took Gomer, the daughter of Debelaim." Hosea 3 has him buy back "a woman ... an adulteress" for fifteen pieces of silver and some barley. Is she Gomer? The text never says. Readers split three ways.

- **Event.** The Talmud ([Jewish readings](../reference.md#jewish-readings)) (Pesachim 87a–b) takes the marriage as real. God uses it to teach Hosea: a man who cannot send away his unfaithful wife and children should understand why God will not exchange Israel for another nation.
- **Vision.** Maimonides (*Guide of the Perplexed* II.46, Friedländer's translation) says: "All this passed in a prophetic vision." He treats it like Jeremiah's buried girdle (Jer 13), as a parable seen, not done.
- **Sign-act.** Many modern commentators read it as a real marriage that the prophet came to understand as a living parable. They note that "wife of fornications" may describe what Gomer became, or her part in Baal worship, rather than her trade.

The literal event is contested; the point is not: God's covenant love outlasts betrayal. Then comes the tradition's second step. Matthew twice quotes Hosea 6:6, "I will have mercy and not sacrifice" (Matt 9:13, 12:7). He also reads Hosea 11:1, "I called my son out of Egypt," typologically of Jesus. In Hosea that verse is a memory about Israel ([`new-testament` 2.2](../../new-testament/lessons/02-02-matthew.md)). These are spiritual readings built on a literal sense that remains about Israel.

## Watch out

- **"Prophets were fortune-tellers."** Most of Amos is accusation. The predictions are covenant sanctions, sometimes revoked at intercession (7:1–6). Do not overcorrect either: prediction is real (Deut 18:22), and *Dei Filius* teaches that fulfilled prophecy is a sign of revelation.
- **Promoting form criticism to doctrine, or doctrine to form criticism.** "The covenant lawsuit is a fixed legal genre" is a scholarly hypothesis. "The Spirit spoke through the prophets" is creedal. Neither decides the other.
- **"Mercy" as pity.** Behind Hosea's "mercy" stands *hesed*: loyalty within a bond, owed both ways. Israel's *hesed* is "as a morning cloud" (6:4).

## One-liner

> A prophet is God's messenger under the covenant: called, not credentialed, he forthtells the charge before he foretells the sentence, and Amos and Hosea show the two faces of that charge, justice and loyal love.

## Problems

**P1 (🟢)** Amos 7:9–15 (Douay–Rheims):

> "... and I will rise up against the house of Jeroboam with the sword. And Amasias the priest of Bethel sent to Jeroboam king of Israel, saying: Amos hath rebelled against thee ... For thus saith Amos: Jeroboam shall die by the sword, and Israel shall be carried away captive out of their own land. And Amasias said to Amos: Thou seer, go, flee away into the land of Juda: and eat bread there, and prophesy there. But prophesy not again any more in Bethel: because it is the king's sanctuary ... And Amos answered and said to Amasias: I am not a prophet, nor am I the son of a prophet: but I am a herdsman plucking wild figs. And the Lord took me when I followed the flock, and the Lord said to me: Go, prophesy to my people Israel."

(a) *(Exegetical)* Compare verse 9 with Amaziah's report in verse 11. What has he changed about Jeroboam, and why does the change matter to a king? (b) *(Exegetical)* Amos 7:14 has no verb in Hebrew ("not a prophet I, and not a prophet's son I"). Give the two main ways to read it and the words in the passage that favour each. (c) *(Exegetical)* On what does Amos rest his right to speak at Bethel, and what two other grounds does the passage set against it? **Two sentences per part.**

**P2 (🟡)** Hosea 4:1–3 (Douay–Rheims):

> "Hear the word of the Lord, ye children of Israel, for the Lord shall enter into judgment with the inhabitants of the land: for there is no truth, and there is no mercy, and there is no knowledge of God in the land. Cursing, and lying, and killing, and theft, and adultery, have overflowed, and blood hath touched blood. Therefore shall the land mourn, and every one that dwelleth in it shall languish with the beasts of the field, and with the fowls of the air: yea, the fishes of the sea also shall be gathered together."

(a) *(Exegetical)* Label the form elements present: summons, the case, the charges, the sentence. Give the Hebrew word behind "enter into judgment" and say what it means. (b) *(Exegetical)* Does this passage contain the messenger formula? Justify in one sentence. (c) *(Exegetical)* What list of commandments does verse 2 echo, and what does the echo tell you about the standard the charge applies? **100 words or fewer in total.**

**P3 (🔴)** *(Exegetical)* An **invented** parish handout:

> "The prophets were Israel's fortune-tellers: their job was predicting the future, and the Church proves the faith from their predictions coming true. Amos, a professional prophet, foretold the fall of Samaria in 722. His remarks about justice were his own opinions, since inspiration covers the predictions. 'Thus saith the Lord' was a solemn formula that guaranteed the prediction would happen."

(a) Name four errors and, for each, the text or teaching that corrects it. (b) Say what the handout gets right and at what level. **150 words or fewer.**

<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Exegetical (a)–(c))*

**Must hit, strict (a):** verse 9 threatens "the house of Jeroboam," his dynasty. Amaziah reports a personal death sentence on the king ("Jeroboam shall die by the sword") and calls it rebellion, so he turns an oracle into sedition. The Douay's own note flags the change. Jeroboam in fact "slept with his fathers" (2 Kings 14:29 [Douay 4 Kings]), and it was his son Zachary who was killed (2 Kings 15:10).
**Must hit, strict (b):** (i) **present tense**: "I am not a (professional) prophet, nor a member of a prophetic guild," disowning the paid role Amaziah implies with "eat bread there." The "sons of the prophets" were guild members (2 Kings 2:3 [Douay 4 Kings 2:3], at Bethel itself). (ii) **past tense**: "I was not a prophet ... until the Lord took me," supported by verse 15's narrative "the Lord took me." Either way the contrast is between profession and commission.
**Must hit, strict (c):** the call, "the Lord took me ... Go, prophesy to my people Israel" (7:15). Set against it are royal authority ("the king's sanctuary," v. 13) and professional status, which Amaziah's "seer ... eat bread there" assumes.
**Wrong turns:** reading 7:14 as Amos denying he prophesies, when verse 15 commands him to. Treating Amaziah's report as an accurate quotation.
**Model answer:** (a) Amos threatened the dynasty with the sword; Amaziah makes it the king's own death and brands it conspiracy, a charge a king must act on. (b) Present: "I am no professional or guild prophet," fitting "eat bread there"; past: "I was not one until the Lord took me," fitting verse 15. (c) He rests on God's call (v. 15) against the king's sanctuary (v. 13) and the professional's livelihood (v. 12).

**P2** *(Exegetical (a)–(c))*

**Must hit, strict (a):** *Summons:* "Hear the word of the Lord, ye children of Israel." *Case:* "the Lord shall enter into judgment." The Hebrew is ***rib***, a legal case or dispute: "the Lord has a case against" the land. *Charges:* no truth, no mercy (*hesed*), no knowledge of God, then the five crimes. *Sentence:* "Therefore shall the land mourn ..."
**Must hit, strict (b):** **No.** "Hear the word of the Lord" is a summons to listen, not "Thus saith the Lord," the envoy's opening that marks the speaker.
**Must hit, strict (c):** the **Decalogue**: cursing or false swearing, lying, killing, theft, adultery (Ex 20; Deut 5). The standard is **Israel's own covenant law**, not a new or foreign morality. (Which came first, Hosea's list or the Decalogue's final form, is debated; the answer does not change the point.)
**Wrong turns:** calling "Hear the word of the Lord" the messenger formula. Glossing "mercy" as pity instead of covenant loyalty.
**Model answer:** Summons (v. 1a); case: *rib*, "the Lord has a lawsuit"; charges: no truth, *hesed* or knowledge of God, then five crimes (vv. 1b–2); sentence: "Therefore ..." (v. 3). No messenger formula: "Hear the word" summons, it does not introduce the envoy. Verse 2 echoes the Decalogue, so the covenant is the law applied.

**P3** *(Exegetical (a)–(b))*

**Must hit, strict (a):** any four of these. (1) Prophecy reduced to prediction: most of Amos is accusation (5:21–24), and the threats are covenant sanctions that can be revoked (7:1–6). (2) "Professional prophet": Amos 7:14–15. (3) Inspiration limited to predictions: the whole book is inspired ([`fundamental-theology` 3.1](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/03-01-inspiration.md)), and the Creed says the Spirit "spake by the Prophets" without restriction. (4) The formula as a guarantee: it is an envoy's opening that names the sender (Gen 32:4; 2 Kings 18:19). (5) "Amos foretold the fall of Samaria in 722": he foretold exile (7:17); the date is the historian's, not his.
**Must hit, strict (b):** prediction is genuinely part of prophecy (Deut 18:22; Amos 7:17 fulfilled in 722, a secure date). *Dei Filius* ch. 3 teaches that prophecies are among the signs of revelation. That is conciliar teaching, and it does not make prediction prophecy's main business.
**Wrong turns:** overcorrecting to "prophets never predicted." Treating the messenger-formula analysis as Church teaching.
**Model answer:** (1) Prophecy is chiefly forthtelling: Amos accuses (5:21–24), and his threats are conditional (7:3, 6). (2) Amos denies being a professional (7:14) and rests on the call (7:15). (3) Inspiration covers the whole book, justice oracles included (FT 3.1; the Creed). (4) "Thus saith" is an envoy's opening naming the sender (Gen 32:4), not a guarantee. Right: prediction is real (Deut 18:22; 7:17 and 722), and Vatican I counts fulfilled prophecy among the signs of revelation. That is conciliar teaching about signs, not a definition of the prophet's task.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [3.3](03-03-two-kingdoms-and-two-falls.md) (Two kingdoms and two falls):** *(Exegetical.)* Sennacherib's campaign of 701 is told twice (Douay–Rheims):

> **2 Kings 18:13–15 (DR 4 Kings):** "In the fourteenth year of king Ezechias, Sennacherib, king of the Assyrians, came up against the fenced cities of Juda, and took them. Then Ezechias, king of Juda, sent messengers to the king of the Assyrians, to Lachis, saying: I have offended, depart from me: and all that thou shalt put upon me, I will bear. And the king of the Assyrians put a tax upon Ezechias ... And Ezechias gave all the silver that was found in the house of the Lord, and in the king's treasures."
>
> **2 Chronicles 32:1, 22–23 (DR 2 Paralipomenon):** "After these things, and this truth, Sennacherib king of the Assyrians came and entered into Juda, and besieged the fenced cities, desiring to take them. ... And the Lord saved Ezechias and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, out of the hand of Sennacherib ... Many also brought victims, and sacrifices to the Lord to Jerusalem, and presents to Ezechias king of Juda."

Supplied facts: "these things" in 32:1 are Hezekiah's Temple reform and Passover (2 Chronicles 29–31). Sennacherib's own annals claim forty-six walled towns taken, Hezekiah shut up in Jerusalem, and tribute received. They record no capture of Jerusalem.

(a) Name three things Chronicles changes or leaves out, and say what the Chronicler's purpose predicts about them. (b) Kings and the Assyrian annals agree on two points. Name them, and for each say which witness is speaking against its own interest and why that matters. **Two sentences per part.**

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Must hit, strict (a):** any three of: Kings' **"took them"** becomes **"besieged ... desiring to take them"**; Hezekiah's submission **"I have offended"** is gone; the **tribute and the Temple silver** (and the stripped Temple doors, 18:16) are gone; the gifts now flow the other way, **"presents to Ezechias"** (32:23). The purpose: "After these things, and this truth" ties the invasion to the reform, and the Chronicler's **sharper rule of reward** does not let a faithful king be humiliated. He writes for a community rebuilding Temple worship and assumes readers know Kings, so he **omits rather than denies**.
**Must hit, strict (b):** (1) **Tribute was paid.** Kings admits it against the honour of a good king, while the annals boast of it. (2) **Jerusalem was not taken.** The annals, which exist to boast, record no capture, and so concede it by silence, while Kings claims a deliverance. A partisan witness is most credible where it has no motive to shade the story, so points each side would rather hide or could not avoid are the firmest. Each side's *reason* for the withdrawal (the angel, or nothing at all) stays its own theology.
**Wrong turns:** concluding that one book *errs*. Difference of purpose is judged by genre and intent (Dei Verbum 12), and Chronicles assumes Kings. Treating the annals as a neutral record. Calling the date reconstructed: 701 is **secure**.
**Model answer:** (a) Chronicles has the cities besieged, not taken, drops "I have offended" with the tribute and the stripped Temple, and ends with presents brought *to* Hezekiah. Placed "after ... this truth", the reform, the story obeys the Chronicler's rule that fidelity is rewarded, and it leaves Kings' humiliation to readers who already know it. (b) Both record tribute, which Kings concedes against its hero, and both leave Jerusalem uncaptured, which the boasting annals concede by silence. Agreement between partisan witnesses on points against their own interest is the strongest evidence either offers.

</details>
