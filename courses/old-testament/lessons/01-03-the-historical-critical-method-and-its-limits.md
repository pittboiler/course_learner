# The Old Testament · Lesson 1.3: The historical-critical method and its limits

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 1: How the Church reads the Old Testament · Builds on: [1.2 The senses of Scripture](01-02-the-senses-of-scripture.md), [`fundamental-theology` 3.2 Inerrancy](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/03-02-inerrancy.md), [4.5 Reading a document's weight](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md) · Unlocks: [1.4 Canonical reading](01-04-canonical-reading-and-the-unity-of-the-testaments.md), [2.2 Who wrote the Pentateuch?](02-02-who-wrote-the-pentateuch.md)

## Why this matters

Most of this course reports what historical critics think: that the Pentateuch grew from sources, that Isaiah 40–55 speaks to the exiles. First you need to know where the Church stands on the method that produces such claims. Two slogans get this wrong in opposite directions. One says "the Church condemned higher criticism." The other says "the Church now accepts modern scholarship." Both are half true, and the true halves are in different documents with very different weights.

## The idea

Think of an X-ray machine brought into an art museum. It shows the underdrawing and the repainted hand. The museum's first reaction was defensive: the machine's operators were mostly arguing that the painting was a forgery, and it banned their conclusions. Later it hired its own radiographers and wrote rules for reading the plates. Finally it said what the X-ray cannot see: the picture as a picture.

The Church's documents run through the same three phases:

1. **Suspicion of the operators (1893–1915):** criticism welcomed, rationalist premises and the critics' conclusions refused.
2. **Adoption with rules (1943–1965):** the study of ancient literary forms becomes a duty.
3. **Naming the limits (1988–2010):** indispensable, not sufficient.

The limit was never "do not look at the evidence." It was "do not let a philosophy decide the verdict before the evidence is in."

## Source

Leo XIII, *Providentissimus Deus* §17 (18 November 1893), in the Vatican's English translation. Leo lists the means of defending Scripture:

> The first means is the study of the Oriental languages and of the art of criticism. … There has arisen, to the great detriment of religion, an inept method, dignified by the name of the "higher criticism," which pretends to judge of the origin, integrity and authority of each Book from internal indications alone. It is clear, on the other hand, that in historical questions, such as the origin and the handing down of writings, the witness of history is of primary importance … and that in this matter internal evidence is seldom of great value, except as confirmation. … and seeing that most of them are tainted with false philosophy and rationalism, it must lead to the elimination from the sacred writings of all prophecy and miracle, and of everything else that is outside the natural order.

Read it for what it does *not* condemn: criticism as such is the **first means** of defence. The target is a named method, with two charges. The first is **an evidence rule**: it works from internal indications alone (style, vocabulary, doublets, anachronisms), where Leo wants external testimony to govern. The second is **a philosophy**: rationalism, which rules out prophecy and miracle before any text is read. The two have aged differently. The evidence rule was a prudential judgement that later documents quietly moved past; internal evidence is now the core of every source argument. The second charge became the permanent Catholic limit, restated in *Verbum Domini* 117 years later.

## The argument

1. **The frame (reloaded).** God is the author of Scripture through human authors who are true authors, and Scripture teaches without error what God willed to be recorded for our salvation ([`fundamental-theology` 3.1](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/03-01-inspiration.md), [3.2](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/03-02-inerrancy.md)). The literal sense is what the human author meant ([1.2](01-02-the-senses-of-scripture.md)). The [historical-critical method](../reference.md#historical-critical-method) (textual, source, form and redaction criticism) recovers that meaning *historically*. *In words:* what did this text mean when written, and how did it come to be?

2. **The Commission and its Old Testament answers.** Leo XIII founded the Pontifical Biblical Commission in 1902 (the letter *Vigilantiae*). Under Pius X it answered *dubia* (formal yes-or-no questions). Its Old Testament answers (dates as in Sutcliffe's translation):
   - **1905.** "Implicit quotations" (13 February) and narratives "historical only in appearance" (23 June) are refused, *except* where solid arguments prove the case.
   - **27 June 1906.** The critics' arguments do not justify denying Mosaic authorship of the Pentateuch. Moses may have used sources and secretaries, and later inspired additions may be admitted.
   - **29 June 1908.** Isaiah 40–66 need not be assigned to an unknown prophet among the exiles, and the arguments for several authors do not compel.
   - **30 June 1909.** Genesis 1–3 are not fables or pure allegory; facts touching the foundations of the faith keep their literal historical sense. But chapter 1 does not give scientific teaching, and *yom* ("day") may be read freely.
   - **1 May 1910.** David is the principal author of the Psalter, but not of every psalm, and some psalms were later reworked.

   Pius X's motu proprio *Praestantia Scripturae* (18 November 1907) bound all Catholics in conscience to submit to these answers as to papally approved doctrinal decrees of the Roman congregations. *In words:* real, binding acts, with exits already built into several of them.

3. **What changed their force.** [`new-testament` 1.1](../../new-testament/lessons/01-01-reading-the-new-testament-in-the-church.md) tells the gospel side and the 1955 clarification by the Commission's secretary and under-secretary (Miller and Kleinhans): answers that do not touch faith and morals leave scholars free. The Old Testament side has its own milestone, the Commission's letter to Cardinal Suhard of 16 January 1948 ([2.2](02-02-who-wrote-the-pentateuch.md) reads it). The letter said the 1905, 1906 and 1909 answers were no hindrance to further truly scientific study of these questions. *In words:* the Commission reinterpreted its own answers in the direction of freedom, without revoking them.

4. ***Divino Afflante Spiritu* (30 September 1943), paraphrased.** Grammar alone does not settle an ancient Eastern writer's literal sense. The interpreter must go back in spirit to that world and learn its [literary forms](../reference.md#literary-forms), found only by studying ancient literature (§§35–38). Exegetes on hard questions are to be judged with fairness and charity. *In words:* genre is a historical fact to be discovered, and discovering it is now a duty.

5. **Dei Verbum 12 (1965).** To find what God meant, find what the sacred writers really intended, attending to literary forms and to the ways of speaking and narrating of their time. Then read in the Spirit in which Scripture was written. The three criteria for that second step are [1.4](01-04-canonical-reading-and-the-unity-of-the-testaments.md)'s. *In words:* history first, and not history alone.

6. **After 1971: a scholars' commission.** Paul VI's *Sedula cura* (1971) remade the Commission as an advisory body of scholars attached to the doctrinal congregation. Its ***The Interpretation of the Bible in the Church*** (presented 23 April 1993) calls the method indispensable for the scientific study of ancient texts. It also says no scientific method can be fully adequate to the texts' richness, and that exclusively synchronic reading (the final text only) must not replace historical study. Cardinal Ratzinger's preface says the post-conciliar Commission is not an organ of the teaching office.

7. **The limits: Ratzinger and *Verbum Domini*.** In the Erasmus Lecture ("Biblical Interpretation in Crisis", New York, January 1988), Ratzinger argued that much form criticism (he examined Dibelius and Bultmann) rested on philosophical premises, such as the priority of preaching over event. He called for a self-criticism of the method, a "criticism of criticism". *Verbum Domini* (30 September 2010) §§34–36, paraphrased: there are [two levels of interpretation](../reference.md#two-levels-of-interpretation), historical-critical and theological, and they must be distinguished but never separated. Stop at the first and Scripture speaks only of the past. Leave out a hermeneutic of faith and a secularized one, assuming God does not act in history, takes its place; no criterion may rule out God's self-disclosure in advance. *In words:* Leo's second charge, restated with the evidence rule dropped.

**Mark the levels.** [Church teaching vs hypothesis](../reference.md#church-teaching-vs-hypothesis): *Providentissimus Deus*, *Divino Afflante Spiritu* and *Verbum Domini* (a post-synodal apostolic exhortation) are **authentic ordinary magisterium** ([rung 3](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md)). Their doctrine of inerrancy weighs more than their prudential advice (Leo's evidence rule). **Dei Verbum 12** is conciliar teaching in a dogmatic constitution, not a new definition. [The Commission's early answers](../reference.md#the-biblical-commission-and-its-weight) were **binding disciplinary-doctrinal decisions when issued**, not infallible. The 1948 letter and 1955 clarification reinterpreted their force (a reading J. E. Steinmueller disputed): neither dogma nor a dead letter. The **1993 document** is **not magisterial**, as its preface says; the **Erasmus Lecture** has **no magisterial weight**, though its thesis later entered *Verbum Domini*. Every composition verdict (Mosaic authorship, Second Isaiah) is a **scholarly position**. Document dates are secure; the biblical dates below are [reconstructed](../reference.md#dating-discipline).

## Timeline

| Year | Document | What it says about the method | Weight today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1893 | *Providentissimus Deus* | criticism yes; "higher criticism" from internal evidence and rationalism no | encyclical (rung 3) |
| 1905–1915 | Biblical Commission answers | against the critics' conclusions, with exits | binding when issued; force reinterpreted 1948/1955 |
| 1943 | *Divino Afflante Spiritu* | literary forms become a duty | encyclical (rung 3) |
| 1948 | Letter to Cardinal Suhard | old answers no hindrance to research | Commission letter, papally approved |
| 1965 | Dei Verbum 12 | literary forms + reading in the Spirit | dogmatic constitution |
| 1988 | Ratzinger, Erasmus Lecture | the method must criticize its own premises | none (a lecture) |
| 1993 | *Interpretation of the Bible in the Church* | indispensable, not sufficient | not magisterial |
| 2010 | *Verbum Domini* §§34–36 | two levels, never separated | apostolic exhortation (rung 3) |

The limit on philosophical premises holds from first row to last.

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (clean): the end of the Miserere.** Psalm 51 [Douay 50] carries a heading placing it after Nathan confronted David over Bathsheba. Verses 16–17 [Douay 18–19] say God does not want burnt offerings, only a contrite heart. Then verses 18–19 [Douay 20–21] pray: "Deal favourably, O Lord, in thy good will with Sion; that the walls of Jerusalem may be built up. Then shalt thou accept the sacrifice of justice…" Run the method. *Internal evidence:* the walls are down, which fits the period after 587/586 (secure) and not David's reign, and the sacrifice God just refused is now welcomed. *Inference:* most critics read the last two verses as a later addition that adapts a penitential psalm for the community of the exile or the return (reconstructed). The 1910 answer had already allowed that psalms such as the Miserere were slightly reworked by adding a verse or two. Method, answer and inspiration agree, and the Church still prays the psalm as one act of repentance ([5.1](05-01-the-psalms.md)).

**Example 2 (hard): Isaiah 40–55.** These chapters name Cyrus (Isa 44:28; 45:1) and comfort exiles. The majority case for a "Second Isaiah" writing near 539 (secure for Cyrus; the chapters' date reconstructed) comes in two forms. **Form A** says a prophet cannot know a name a century and a half ahead, so the text must be later. That premise excludes prediction in advance: Leo's second charge exactly. **Form B** says an oracle's audience is its setting. These chapters speak *to* exiles *as* present and argue *from* Cyrus's rise as a fact already seen, so they belong to that setting whatever God could reveal. Form B needs no premise about miracles. The strain is that the 1908 answer also rejected a premise close to Form B's: its third question refused to require that prophets always address their contemporaries. So keep two questions apart: is an argument secularist, or only unwelcome to an older answer since reinterpreted? [4.2](04-02-isaiah-and-immanuel.md) weighs the evidence.

## Watch out

- **"The Church condemned historical criticism."** Leo condemned a named method with a stated philosophy, and commended criticism in the same paragraph.
- **Hypothesis promoted to teaching, or teaching demoted.** "Catholics must hold Second Isaiah" and "Catholics must hold that Isaiah wrote all 66 chapters" both assign a composition verdict a rung it lacks. The 1908 answer was binding when issued and has since been reinterpreted. It is neither doctrine now nor something that never bound.
- **The 1993 document or the Erasmus Lecture quoted as magisterium.** The first is a scholars' commission, and its own preface says so. The second is a lecture.
- **"Limits" misread as "ignore the evidence."** The limit is on premises that decide the result in advance, not on internal evidence. Form B in Example 2 passes; Form A does not.

## One-liner

> The Church adopted the historian's tools and refused the rationalist's premises: indispensable, not sufficient, and never allowed to rule God out in advance.

## Problems

**P1 (🟢)** *(Exegetical)* Assign each statement its standing: Church teaching (which document, which level), a binding act whose present force is reinterpreted (say how), a non-magisterial document (say why), or a scholarly position. **One or two sentences each.**

1. Internal evidence about a book's origin is "seldom of great value, except as confirmation" (*Providentissimus Deus* §17).
2. The arguments for several authors of Isaiah do not compel (Biblical Commission, 1908).
3. "No scientific method … is fully adequate" to the biblical texts (*The Interpretation of the Bible in the Church*, 1993).
4. Exegesis without a hermeneutic of faith falls into a secularized hermeneutic that assumes God does not act in history (*Verbum Domini* §35, paraphrased).

**P2 (🟡)** *(Exegetical)* An **invented** parish handout:

> "Catholics should be wary of historical criticism: Leo XIII condemned it outright in 1893 as an 'inept method.' That is why the Biblical Commission ruled against Second Isaiah in 1908, a ruling Catholics are still bound to in conscience. But since the 1993 Biblical Commission document, signed by the future Benedict XVI, the method is official Church teaching, so everything the critics conclude is now safe to believe."

Name three errors. For each, give the correction and the document that supplies it. **Two sentences per error.**

**P3 (🔴)** *(Exegetical)* Close-read Deuteronomy 34:5–6, 10 (Douay–Rheims):

> And Moses the servant of the Lord died there, in the land of Moab, by the commandment of the Lord: And he buried him in the valley of the land of Moab over against Phogor: and no man hath known of his sepulchre until this present day. … And there arose no more a prophet in Israel like unto Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face.

(a) Which words are internal evidence that this passage was written after Moses, and how long after does each suggest? (b) Is an argument from these words open to either of Leo XIII's two charges in §17? **100 words or fewer in total.**

<details>
<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:**
- (1) **An encyclical's prudential judgement.** It sits in a rung-3 document, but it is an evidence rule, not the doctrine of inerrancy. Later teaching (DAS, DV 12) moved past it, so it does not bind as teaching.
- (2) **A binding early Commission answer**, owed submission when issued (*Praestantia Scripturae*, 1907), not infallible. Its practical force was reinterpreted (1948 letter; 1955 clarification: answers not touching faith and morals leave scholars free). Neither dogma nor void.
- (3) **Not magisterial.** Since 1971 the Commission is a consultative body of scholars, and Ratzinger's preface says it is not an organ of the teaching office.
- (4) **Authentic ordinary magisterium** (rung 3) in a post-synodal apostolic exhortation. It is not a definition.

**Wrong turns:** treating (1) as Church teaching of the same weight as inerrancy because it is in the same encyclical. Calling (2) "revoked" or "still binding doctrine". Raising (3) because Ratzinger signed the preface. Calling (4) infallible, or calling it Ratzinger's private opinion because it echoes his 1988 lecture.

**Model answer:** (1) An evidence rule in a rung-3 encyclical, prudential and since superseded in practice, not doctrine. (2) A 1908 Commission answer, binding in conscience when issued. Its force was reinterpreted in 1948 and 1955 for questions not touching faith and morals, but it was not revoked. (3) Non-magisterial: the post-1971 Commission advises and does not teach, as its preface says. (4) Papal ordinary magisterium in an apostolic exhortation, rung 3.

**P2** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:**
1. **Leo did not condemn criticism outright.** §17 calls "the art of criticism" the first means of defence. It condemns the "higher criticism" that works from internal evidence alone under rationalist premises.
2. **The 1908 answer's weight is overstated.** It bound in conscience when issued (*Praestantia Scripturae*). The 1955 clarification put authorship and dating questions among those scholars may now treat freely.
3. **The 1993 document is not Church teaching, and it does not bless every conclusion.** The preface says the Commission is not an organ of the teaching office. The document itself calls the method not sufficient, and *Verbum Domini* §§35–36 refuses conclusions driven by a secularized hermeneutic.

**Wrong turns:** correcting error 2 with "the 1908 ruling was abolished", which says more than the evidence shows. Missing the last clause: even endorsed methods do not make every result safe; conclusions weigh what their evidence weighs.

**Model answer:** (1) Leo praised criticism as the first means of defending Scripture and condemned only a method that relied on internal evidence alone and assumed prophecy and miracle impossible (*PD* §17). (2) The 1908 answer bound when issued, but the Commission's officials said in 1955 that such authorship questions are now free, so it is not a present obligation of conscience. (3) The 1993 document is a scholars' study, not teaching, as its preface says. It calls the method necessary but not sufficient, and *Verbum Domini* rejects results that rule God out in advance.

**P3** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict (a):** "died there" and the burial: a narrator after Moses's death. "Until this present day": a writer looking back across enough time for the grave to be forgotten. "There arose no more a prophet … like unto Moses": a writer who can survey a *series* of later prophets, so a long interval, generations at least.

**Must hit, strict (b):** neither charge applies. It is internal evidence, but the charge was against internal evidence *alone*, and here the text states the fact outright. It assumes nothing against prophecy or miracle. The Challoner note itself assigns the chapter to Joshua "or by some of the prophets", and the 1906 answer allowed later inspired additions.

**Wrong turns:** "Moses wrote his own death by prophecy, so this proves nothing": the passage's *perspective* (looking back) is the point, not whether prediction is possible. Inferring that the whole Pentateuch is late from these verses alone.

**Model answer:** "Died there" and "buried" need a narrator after Moses. "Until this present day" implies the grave has since been lost. "There arose no more a prophet" surveys later prophets, so it is generations later. The argument uses internal evidence but not internal evidence alone, since the text states it, and it needs no premise against miracle. So neither of Leo's charges applies. Even the Douay note gives the chapter to Joshua or a later prophet.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [1.1](01-01-what-the-old-testament-is.md) (What the Old Testament is):** *(Exegetical.)* Two possible last pages (Douay–Rheims):

> "Thus saith Cyrus king of the Persians: All the kingdoms of the earth hath the Lord the God of heaven given to me, and he hath charged me to build him a house in Jerusalem, which is in Judea: who is there among you of all his people? The Lord his God be with him, and let him go up." (2 Chronicles 36:23 [DR 2 Paralipomenon])
>
> "Remember the law of Moses my servant ... Behold, I will send you Elias the prophet, before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord. And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers: lest I come, and strike the earth with anathema." (Malachi 4:4–6 [Hebrew 3:22–24])

(a) Which collection ends with each passage, and what does each ending leave its reader waiting for? Name the words that decide it. (b) Give one fact showing that "the Tanakh ends with Chronicles" is a convention and not a fixed rule, and say where the Douay's Old Testament ends instead. **Two sentences per part.**

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Must hit, strict (a):** 2 Chronicles 36:23 ends the **Tanakh** in the Talmud's order (*Bava Batra* 14b) and in most printed Jewish Bibles. It leaves the reader waiting for the **return to the land and the rebuilt Temple**: "build him a house in Jerusalem", "let him go up". Malachi ends the **Protestant Old Testament**. It leaves the reader waiting for **a prophet still to come**, Elias, "before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the Lord", and the next page in a Christian Bible is the New Testament, where John the Baptist appears.

**Must hit, strict (b):** the great **medieval codices put Chronicles first among the Writings**, not last. The **Douay** (following the Vulgate) ends with **1–2 Maccabees**, placed after the prophets.

**Wrong turns:** saying Malachi ends the Tanakh. In the Jewish order it closes the Twelve, and so the Prophets, but the Writings follow. Saying the Douay ends with Malachi. Treating the Jewish ending as a rule of Jewish law rather than an order of books.

**Model answer:** (a) Chronicles closes the Tanakh in its usual printed order. It ends with Cyrus telling God's people to "go up" and build the house in Jerusalem, so the story waits for a return. Malachi closes the Protestant Old Testament. It ends by promising Elias before the day of the Lord, so the collection waits for a prophet, and John the Baptist is on the next page. (b) The great medieval codices put Chronicles at the start of the Writings, so the ending is a convention. The Douay places 1–2 Maccabees after the prophets and ends there.
