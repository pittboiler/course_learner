# Arithmetic & Number Sense — Syllabus

> Foundations · Tier F · ~8 lessons · Prereqs: none · Roadmap id: `arithmetic-number-sense`

## Goal

Rebuild the machinery every later math course silently assumes: how numbers are built from place value, how the four operations behave and interact, and how to move fluently between fractions, decimals, ratios, and percents. The real payoff is *number sense* — an internal alarm that fires when an answer is off by a sign, a decimal place, or an order of magnitude. This is the on-ramp to `algebra-foundations`: the fraction, factoring, and order-of-operations reflexes you build here are exactly what algebra manipulates with letters. Deliberately skipped: any variables or algebra (that's `algebra-foundations`), exponents beyond small whole-number powers (no negative exponents or logs), and all geometry.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Read and compare integers by place value, and place any of them correctly on the number line
- [ ] Add, subtract, multiply, and divide integers — including negatives — and predict the sign before you compute
- [ ] Evaluate a multi-part expression with the correct order of operations, and spot the classic traps
- [ ] Apply divisibility rules (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10) to test factors in your head
- [ ] Factor a number into primes, and use that to find a GCD and an LCM fast
- [ ] Add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions, and reduce any fraction to lowest terms
- [ ] Convert freely among fractions, decimals, and percents, and know which form makes a problem easiest
- [ ] Set up and solve a ratio or proportion, and compute a percent, a percent-of, and a percent change
- [ ] Round to any place and estimate a product, quotient, or sum to the right order of magnitude
- [ ] Sanity-check any answer by sign, size, and units — and catch a wrong result before trusting it

## Modules

### Module 1: Numbers & operations

The integers, the rules that make arithmetic unambiguous, and the prime structure hiding inside every number.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Place value & the integers | Read, compare, and order any integer, and add/subtract/multiply/divide with negatives while predicting the sign | base-10 place value, the number line, sign vs. magnitude, comparing integers, signed arithmetic |
| 1.2 | Order of operations | Evaluate any multi-part numeric expression the one correct way, and avoid the standard traps | grouping symbols, exponents, multiplication/division left-to-right, addition/subtraction, PEMDAS pitfalls |
| 1.3 | Factors, primes & divisibility | Test divisibility mentally, factor into primes, and read off a GCD and LCM | factor vs. multiple, divisibility rules, prime & composite, prime factorization, GCD, LCM |

**Boss problem 1:** A warehouse packs items in boxes of 24 and pallets of 36. (a) Using divisibility rules only — no long division — decide whether a shipment of 2,340 items divides evenly into boxes of 12, and whether it divides evenly into groups of 15. (b) Find $\gcd(24,36)$ and $\operatorname{lcm}(24,36)$ from their prime factorizations, and say what each one *means* for the boxes and pallets. (c) Evaluate $7 + 6 \times (3^2 - 4) \div 2$ and explain which rule forced each step.

### Module 2: Fractions, decimals, ratios & percents

Four dialects for the same idea — a part of a whole — and how to translate instantly among them.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Fractions | Add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions, and reduce any result to lowest terms | numerator/denominator, equivalent fractions, common denominators, multiply/divide (invert-and-multiply), reducing via GCD |
| 2.2 | Decimals & conversions | Compute with decimals and convert freely between fractions and decimals | decimal place value, aligning/decimal-point arithmetic, terminating vs. repeating decimals, fraction ↔ decimal ↔ percent |
| 2.3 | Ratios, proportions & percents | Set up a ratio or proportion, solve it, and compute any percent question | ratio & rate, proportion and cross-multiplication, percent as "per hundred", percent-of, percent increase/decrease |

**Boss problem 2:** A paint mix uses blue and white in the ratio $3:5$. (a) For a $4$-liter batch, how much of each color? (b) Express the blue share as a fraction of the whole, then as a decimal and a percent. (c) You then increase the *total* batch by $25\%$ while keeping the ratio fixed — how many liters of blue does the new batch contain? Check that your part-and-whole numbers still add up.

### Module 3: Estimation & number sense

The habits that turn arithmetic from a gamble into something you can trust: estimate first, compute second, then check.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Rounding & estimation | Round to any place and estimate sums, products, and quotients to the right order of magnitude | rounding rules, front-end estimation, compatible numbers, order of magnitude, powers of ten |
| 3.2 | Mental math & sanity-checking | Compute common cases in your head and reject any answer that fails a sign/size/units check | distributive shortcuts, doubling/halving, percent tricks, estimating to catch errors, checking sign and magnitude |

**Boss problem 3:** A receipt claims that $18$ items at $\$21$ each plus a $\$40$ delivery fee comes to $\$418$. (a) Estimate the item subtotal by rounding, *before* computing exactly. (b) Compute $18 \times 21$ mentally using a distributive shortcut, and state the true total. (c) The receipt is wrong — say by how much, and explain which sanity check (size, place value, or arithmetic slip) would have caught it fastest.
