# Algebraic Geometry · Reference Card

> Open book. This card is meant to be open while you work — including during
> quizzes. Nothing here is something you're expected to recall cold.

This course is one long translation exercise. A system of polynomial equations
cuts out a shape; the functions vanishing on that shape form an ideal; and the
Nullstellensatz makes the translation exact. The spine of this card is the
dictionary table below — geometry in the left column, algebra in the right, and
the hypothesis each row actually needs in the third. Everything after it is the
machinery for running the dictionary in a particular direction: radicals,
projective coordinates, dimension, tangent spaces, sheaves, $\operatorname{Spec}$,
divisors.

## The algebra–geometry dictionary

The one table the course exists to install. Read a row left-to-right to compute
geometry by algebra, right-to-left to compute algebra by geometry. **The third
column is not decoration** — most wrong answers in this subject come from using a
row outside its hypotheses.

| Geometry | Algebra | Exactly what the row needs |
|---|---|---|
| a system of equations $S$ | the ideal $\langle S\rangle$ it generates — $V(S)=V(\langle S\rangle)$ | nothing ([1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md)) |
| bigger shape | **smaller** ideal (both maps reverse inclusions) | nothing ([1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md)) |
| affine variety $X\subseteq\mathbb{A}^n$ | **radical** ideal $I(X)$, and the pairing is a bijection | $k=\bar k$; strong Nullstellensatz ([1.5](lessons/01-05-nullstellensatz.md)) |

## Definitions

### Affine space

The set of points polynomials are evaluated at — deliberately *not* a vector
space, since a morphism may move the origin anywhere.

$$\mathbb{A}^n=\mathbb{A}^n_k=\{(a_1,\dots,a_n):a_i\in k\}$$

*Introduced:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md)

### Vanishing set

The shape carved out by a batch of equations. More equations means a smaller
shape.

$$V(S)=\{p\in\mathbb{A}^n : f(p)=0\ \text{for all}\ f\in S\},\qquad V(S)=V(\langle S\rangle)$$

*Introduced:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md)

### Ideal of a set

Everything that dies on the shape. A bigger shape is harder to vanish on, so it
admits fewer functions.

$$I(X)=\{f : f(p)=0\ \text{for all}\ p\in X\}$$

It is always a **radical** ideal, because a field has no nonzero nilpotents.

*Introduced:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md)

### Radical

Throw into $J$ everything some power of which is already there — the operation
that undoes $V$'s blindness to multiplicity.

$$\sqrt J=\{f : f^m\in J\ \text{for some}\ m\ge 1\},\qquad J\subseteq\sqrt J,\qquad \sqrt{\sqrt J}=\sqrt J$$

*Introduced:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md)

### Radical ideal

An ideal with no redundancy for $V$ to discard: $J=\sqrt J$. Equivalently the
quotient $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/J$ is **reduced**, and $\sqrt J/J$ is exactly that
quotient's nilradical.

*Introduced:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md)

### Noetherian ring

A ring where you cannot enlarge an ideal forever. Three equivalent faces: the
ascending chain condition, every nonempty family of ideals has a maximal member,
and **every ideal is finitely generated**.

*Introduced:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-noetherian-hilbert-basis.md)

### Zariski topology

Declare the subvarieties to be the closed sets. Coarse, non-Hausdorff, and built
entirely out of the algebra.

$$\{\text{closed sets}\}=\{V(J):J\ \text{an ideal}\},\qquad \bigcap_\alpha V(J_\alpha)=V\Big(\sum_\alpha J_\alpha\Big),\qquad V(J)\cup V(J')=V(JJ')$$

On $\mathbb{A}^1$ it is the cofinite topology: the closed sets are the finite sets
and the whole line.

*Introduced:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md)

### Irreducible

A space with no seams: it is not the union of two proper closed subsets.
Equivalently, any two nonempty opens meet; equivalently, every nonempty open is
dense. Strictly stronger than connected.

*Introduced:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md)

### Irreducible components

The atoms a variety decomposes into: its maximal irreducible closed subsets. In a
Noetherian space every closed set is a finite union of them, and once you discard
redundant pieces the list is unique.

*Introduced:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md)

### Noetherian topological space

Descending chains of closed subsets stabilize — the topological shadow of the
ascending chain condition, and what makes finite irreducible decomposition work.

*Introduced:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-noetherian-hilbert-basis.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md)

### Coordinate ring

The genuine ring of polynomial functions on $X$: two polynomials count as one
function when their difference vanishes on $X$.

$$k[X]=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I(X)$$

Always a finitely generated reduced $k$-algebra; a domain exactly when $X$ is
irreducible.

*Introduced:* [1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md)

### Regular function

A function that is, near every point, an honest ratio $f/g$ of coordinate-ring
elements with $g$ nonvanishing *there*. Regularity is a **local** condition — which
is why the structure sheaf is a sheaf.

*Introduced:* [1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md), [3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md)

### Polynomial map

A tuple of regular functions whose values land in the target. The last clause is
the only real condition, and it is algebraic.

$$\phi=(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_m):X\to Y,\qquad \phi(X)\subseteq Y \iff g\circ\phi\in I(X)\ \text{for every}\ g\in I(Y)$$

*Introduced:* [1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md)

### Pullback

Transport functions against the arrow: do $\phi$ first, then measure. It is
determined by where it sends the target's coordinate functions.

$$\phi^{*}:k[Y]\to k[X],\qquad \phi^{*}(\bar g)=\overline{g\circ\phi},\qquad (\psi\circ\phi)^{*}=\phi^{*}\circ\psi^{*}$$

*Introduced:* [1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md)

### Function field

Allow yourself to divide by anything not identically zero on $X$. The result is a
field, and it is the deepest birational invariant a variety has.

$$k(X)=\operatorname{Frac}\big(k[X]\big),\qquad \frac{f}{g}\sim\frac{f'}{g'}\iff fg'=f'g$$

Needs $X$ **irreducible**, or $k[X]$ has zero-divisors and this is not a field.

*Introduced:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md)

### Domain of definition

Where a rational function actually has a value: the points at which *some*
representative keeps its denominator alive. Always open and dense.

$$\operatorname{dom}(\varphi)=\{p : \varphi=f/g\ \text{for some representative with}\ g(p)\neq 0\}$$

*Introduced:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md)

### Local ring at a point

Zoom in on $p$ and keep only what doesn't blow up there. The unique maximal ideal
is the functions vanishing at $p$; everything else is a unit.

$$\mathcal{O}_{X,p}=\{f/g\in k(X):g(p)\neq0\}=k[X]_{\mathfrak{m}_p},\qquad \mathcal{O}_{X,p}/\mathfrak{m}_p\cong k$$

*Introduced:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md)

### Rational map

A partial map given by fractions: a morphism on some dense open set, with two
such identified when they agree on the overlap. Written with a dashed arrow.

*Introduced:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md)

### Dominant

The image is Zariski-dense in the target. This is exactly the hypothesis that
makes pullback on function fields injective, and the one that lets you compose
rational maps at all.

*Introduced:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md)

### Birational equivalence

"The same away from a thin bad set": a dominant rational map with a rational
inverse. It sees exactly the function field and nothing else.

$$X\ \text{birational to}\ Y\iff k(X)\cong k(Y)\ \text{over}\ k$$

*Introduced:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md)

### Projective space

Every line through the origin of $k^{n+1}$, collapsed to a single point — so that
directions become honest points and parallel lines meet.

$$\mathbb{P}^n=\big(k^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\big)/\!\sim,\qquad v\sim\lambda v\ (\lambda\in k^{\times}),\qquad \mathbb{P}^n=\mathbb{A}^n\ \sqcup\ \mathbb{P}^{n-1}$$

*Introduced:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md)

### Homogeneous coordinates

An $(n+1)$-tuple where only **ratios** carry information. An individual $x_i$ is
not a number attached to the point, but "$x_i=0$" is a well-defined condition.

$$[x_0:\cdots:x_n]=[\lambda x_0:\cdots:\lambda x_n],\qquad \text{not all}\ x_i=0$$

*Introduced:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md)

### Affine chart

The piece of $\mathbb{P}^n$ where one coordinate is nonzero, made into an honest
$\mathbb{A}^n$ by scaling that coordinate to $1$.

$$U_i=\{x_i\neq0\}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathbb{A}^n,\qquad \mathbb{P}^n=U_0\cup\cdots\cup U_n$$

*Introduced:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md)

### Homogeneous ideal

An ideal that respects the grading: every homogeneous component of a member is a
member. Equivalently, it has homogeneous generators.

*Introduced:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md)

### Projective variety

The shape cut out in $\mathbb{P}^n$ by homogeneous equations — the only kind whose
vanishing is scale-stable, since $f(\lambda a)=\lambda^d f(a)$ for $f$ homogeneous
of degree $d$.

$$V_{+}(T)=\{[a]\in\mathbb{P}^n : f(a)=0\ \text{for all homogeneous}\ f\in T\}$$

*Introduced:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md)

### Irrelevant ideal

The ideal of everything of positive degree. In $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$ it cuts out the
origin — but $\mathbb{P}^n$ has no origin, so projectively it cuts out nothing.

$$\mathfrak{m}_0=(x_0,\dots,x_n)=S_{+},\qquad V_{+}(\mathfrak{m}_0)=\varnothing$$

*Introduced:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md)

### Projective closure

Add the points at infinity: take the Zariski closure of an affine $X$ inside
$\mathbb{P}^n$, computed by homogenizing.

$$f^{h}(x_0,\dots,x_n)=x_0^{\,d}\,f\!\left(\frac{x_1}{x_0},\dots,\frac{x_n}{x_0}\right),\qquad \overline{X}=V_{+}\big(\{f^{h}:f\in I(X)\}\big)$$

Homogenize the **whole ideal**, not the generators one at a time.

*Introduced:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md)

### Morphism to projective space

A list of forms of one common degree with no common zero on the source: the
common degree kills the scaling, the no-common-zero clause keeps the output a
legal point.

$$\varphi([a])=[F_0(a):\cdots:F_m(a)],\qquad F_i\in S_d,\qquad V_{+}(F_0,\dots,F_m)\cap X=\varnothing$$

Only *locally* must $\varphi$ have this form — different charts may need different
tuples that agree on overlaps.

*Introduced:* [2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md)

### Veronese embedding

Record **all** degree-$d$ monomials of a point at once. It loses no information, and
it turns degree-$d$ hypersurfaces into hyperplanes.

$$\nu_d:\mathbb{P}^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^N,\quad [a]\mapsto[M_0(a):\cdots:M_N(a)],\qquad N=\binom{n+d}{d}-1$$

*Introduced:* [2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md)

### Segre embedding

Multiply coordinates pairwise. A pair of points becomes a rank-one matrix, and
"rank $\le 1$" is cut out by the $2\times 2$ minors — which is what makes the
product a projective variety.

$$\sigma:\mathbb{P}^m\times\mathbb{P}^n\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{(m+1)(n+1)-1},\quad ([x],[y])\mapsto[x_iy_j],\qquad z_{ij}z_{kl}-z_{il}z_{kj}=0$$

*Introduced:* [2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md)

### Dimension

How many independent directions $X$ has, defined without any metric: the length
(number of $\subsetneq$ steps) of the longest tower of irreducible closed subsets.

$$X_0\subsetneq X_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq X_d\subseteq X$$

*Introduced:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md)

### Krull dimension

The same count read in the ring: the longest strictly ascending chain of prime
ideals, $\mathfrak{p}_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_d$. Not vector-space
dimension — $k[x]$ is infinite-dimensional over $k$ but has Krull dimension $1$.

*Introduced:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md)

### Transcendence degree

The number of elements of $k(X)$ that are algebraically independent over $k$ — the
count of genuinely free coordinates, and the computable face of dimension.

*Introduced:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md)

### Localization

Formally invert a multiplicative set. It **grows** the ring
($R\subseteq S^{-1}R\subseteq k(X)$) while shrinking the ideal structure.

$$S^{-1}R=\{r/s\},\qquad \frac{r}{s}=\frac{r'}{s'}\iff t(rs'-r's)=0\ \text{for some}\ t\in S$$

Localization is exact and commutes with quotients: $S^{-1}(R/I)\cong S^{-1}R/S^{-1}I$.

*Introduced:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md)

### Local ring

A ring with exactly one maximal ideal — the non-units form an ideal. Localizing at
a prime always produces one, and the residue field is $R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}$
(equal to $k$ at a point of a variety over $k=\bar k$).

*Introduced:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md)

### Zariski tangent space

The flat space that best hugs $X$ at $p$: the directions killed by every
linearized equation, equivalently the dual of the cotangent space. It never
undershoots, $\dim_k T_pX\ge\dim_p X$.

$$T_pX=\ker J_p=(\mathfrak{m}_p/\mathfrak{m}_p^2)^{*},\qquad \dim_k T_pX=n-\operatorname{rank}J_p$$

*Introduced:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md)

### Jacobian

The matrix of first-order coefficients of the defining equations at $p$ — formal
partial derivatives, no limits required.

$$J_p=\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}(p)\right)_{i,j}$$

Feed it generators of $I(X)$; a non-radical ideal gives the wrong answer.

*Introduced:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md)

### Smooth point

Where the linear approximation is honest — it has exactly the dimension it should.
Anything else is **singular**, meaning the linearization overshot.

$$p\ \text{smooth}\iff \dim_k T_pX=\dim X\iff \operatorname{rank}J_p=n-\dim X$$

*Introduced:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md)

### Regular local ring

The coordinate-free version of smoothness: a Noetherian local ring whose maximal
ideal needs exactly $\dim$-many generators.

$$\dim_k\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2=\dim\mathcal{O}_{X,p}$$

*Introduced:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md)

### Tangent cone and multiplicity

What $X$ looks like under infinite zoom at $p$ (translated to the origin): the
zeros of the **lowest**-degree form, not the linear one. Its degree is the
multiplicity.

$$f=f_m+f_{m+1}+\cdots,\ f_m\neq0:\qquad C_pX=V(f_m),\quad \operatorname{mult}_p(X)=m$$

Always $C_pX\subseteq T_pX$, with equality exactly at smooth points ($m=1$).

*Introduced:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md)

### Node and cusp

The two simplest plane-curve singularities, told apart by factoring the lowest
form over $k=\bar k$: **two distinct lines** is a node (two branches crossing), a
**doubled line** is a cusp (one pinched branch).

$$\text{node: } f_2=(y-x)(y+x)\qquad\qquad \text{cusp: } f_2=y^2$$

*Introduced:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md)

### Presheaf

Data attached to each open set, plus a "forget the extra part" map for each
shrinking — a contravariant functor on the open sets.

$$\rho^U_V:\mathcal{F}(U)\to\mathcal{F}(V)\ (V\subseteq U),\qquad \rho^U_U=\mathrm{id},\qquad \rho^V_W\circ\rho^U_V=\rho^U_W$$

*Introduced:* [3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md)

### Sheaf

A presheaf whose data is genuinely local: sections are determined by their
restrictions to a cover (**locality**), and compatible local sections assemble
into a global one (**gluing**). Properties checkable near each point give sheaves;
global constraints like "bounded" or "constant" do not.

*Introduced:* [3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md)

### Stalk and germ

The value of a sheaf at a point after zooming in infinitely far: pairs $(U,s)$
with $p\in U$, identified when they agree on *some* neighborhood of $p$.

$$\mathcal{F}_p=\varinjlim_{U\ni p}\mathcal{F}(U)$$

For sheaves (not presheaves), a morphism is an isomorphism iff every stalk map is.

*Introduced:* [3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md)

### Structure sheaf

The variety's ring of functions computed open set by open set — the object that
holds the coordinate ring, every intermediate ring, and every local ring at once.

$$\mathcal{O}_X(U)=\{\text{regular functions}\ U\to k\},\qquad \mathcal{O}_{X,p}=\varinjlim_{U\ni p}\mathcal{O}_X(U)$$

*Introduced:* [3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md)

### Distinguished open

The complement of one hypersurface — where you have bought exactly the poles
along $g=0$ and no others.

$$D(g)=\{p\in X : g(p)\neq0\},\qquad \mathcal{O}_X\big(D(g)\big)=k[X]_g$$

*Introduced:* [3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md)

### Ringed space, locally ringed space

A space plus a sheaf of rings; **locally** ringed when every stalk is a local ring
— the structure that remembers, at each point, which functions vanish there. A
morphism must also pull functions back, and (in the locally ringed case) send the
maximal ideal into the maximal ideal.

*Introduced:* [3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md)

### Spec of a ring

Promote **every** prime to a point. Works over any commutative ring — no
algebraically closed field, no Nullstellensatz — and remembers nilpotents.

$$\operatorname{Spec}R=\{\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq R\ \text{prime}\},\qquad V(I)=\{\mathfrak{p}\supseteq I\},\qquad D(f)=\{\mathfrak{p} : f\notin\mathfrak{p}\}$$

"$f$ vanishes at $\mathfrak{p}$" *means* $f\in\mathfrak{p}$.

*Introduced:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md)

### Generic point

A non-maximal prime: a point that spreads out. Its closure is a whole irreducible
closed set, on which it is dense.

$$\overline{\{\mathfrak{p}\}}=V(\mathfrak{p});\qquad \mathfrak{p}\ \text{closed}\iff\mathfrak{p}\ \text{maximal}$$

*Introduced:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md)

### Affine scheme, scheme

An affine scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}R$ with its structure sheaf. A scheme is a
locally ringed space that looks like one of those near every point — "locally
affine", exactly as a manifold is locally Euclidean.

*Introduced:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md)

### Gluing data

An atlas: open pieces of each chart, isomorphisms between them, and agreement on
triple overlaps. With only two charts the cocycle condition is free.

$$\varphi_{ij}:U_{ij}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }U_{ji},\qquad \varphi_{ii}=\mathrm{id},\quad \varphi_{ji}=\varphi_{ij}^{-1},\quad \varphi_{ik}=\varphi_{jk}\circ\varphi_{ij}$$

*Introduced:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md)

### Uniformizer and DVR

At a smooth point of a curve the maximal ideal is principal, $\mathfrak{m}_p=(t_p)$,
and every nonzero rational function factors uniquely as a power of $t_p$ times a
unit. Such a local ring is a **discrete valuation ring**.

$$f=u\,t_p^{\,n},\qquad u\in\mathcal{O}_{X,p}^{\times},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}$$

*Introduced:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md)

### Order at a point

That exponent $n$: how many times the uniformizer divides $f$. Positive is a zero,
negative is a pole, zero means finite and nonvanishing.

$$\operatorname{ord}_p(f)=n\ \text{where}\ f=u\,t_p^{\,n}$$

*Introduced:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md)

### Divisor

A ledger with one integer per point, almost all zero. They form a free abelian
group; the **degree** is the running total.

$$D=\sum_{p}n_p[p]\in\operatorname{Div}(X),\qquad \deg D=\sum_p n_p$$

$D$ is **effective** ($D\ge0$) when every $n_p\ge0$; an effective divisor has
$\deg\ge 0$, which is the workhorse of almost every argument in Module 4.

*Introduced:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md)

### Principal divisor and linear equivalence

The divisor of a function is all its zeros minus all its poles. Two divisors are
linearly equivalent when they differ by one of these.

$$\operatorname{div}(f)=\sum_p\operatorname{ord}_p(f)\,[p],\qquad D\sim D'\iff D-D'=\operatorname{div}(f)$$

*Introduced:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md)

### Riemann–Roch space

The functions that live within the pole budget $D$ sets — a finite-dimensional
$k$-vector space depending only on the class of $D$.

$$L(D)=\{f\in k(X)^{*}:\operatorname{div}(f)+D\ge0\}\cup\{0\},\qquad \ell(D)=\dim_k L(D)$$

Coefficient-wise the condition reads $\operatorname{ord}_p(f)\ge-n_p$ at every $p$.

*Introduced:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md)

### Line bundle (invertible sheaf)

The pole budget sheafified: a sheaf of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules that is locally free
of rank $1$ — a one-dimensional space attached to each point, twisting as you move.
Its global sections are $L(D)$.

$$\mathcal{O}_X(D)(U)=\{f:(\operatorname{div}(f)+D)|_U\ge0\}\cup\{0\},\qquad \Gamma\big(X,\mathcal{O}_X(D)\big)=L(D)$$

*Introduced:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md)

### Picard group

Line bundles up to isomorphism, multiplied by tensor product. On a smooth curve
this is exactly the group of divisor classes, and degree descends to it.

$$\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\operatorname{Div}(X)/\!\sim,\qquad \mathcal{O}_X(D)\otimes\mathcal{O}_X(D')\cong\mathcal{O}_X(D+D')$$

*Introduced:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md)

### Canonical divisor

The zeros-minus-poles divisor of any nonzero rational differential. Two
differentials differ by a function, so $K$ is well-defined **as a class**, not as a
divisor.

$$K=\operatorname{div}(\omega),\qquad \deg K=2g-2$$

*Introduced:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-riemann-roch.md)

### Genus

The number of everywhere-regular differentials — and, over $\mathbb{C}$, the number
of handles on the curve as a surface. It is the exact amount by which the naive
section count overcounts.

$$g=\ell(K)$$

*Introduced:* [4.5](lessons/04-05-riemann-roch.md)

## Formulas and rules

### The affine Nullstellensatz

Over $k=\bar k$ only. Three statements, one theorem.

| Form | Statement | Use it to |
|---|---|---|
| **Weak** | every maximal ideal of $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is $\mathfrak{m}_p$; equivalently $J\subsetneq R$ proper $\Rightarrow V(J)\neq\varnothing$ | turn points into maximal ideals; certify a system is solvable |
| **Strong** | $I(V(J))=\sqrt J$ | decide whether $f$ vanishes on $V(J)$: ask whether some $f^m\in J$ |
| **Bijection** | $\{\text{radical ideals}\}\leftrightarrow\{\text{varieties}\}$, restricting to $\{\text{prime}\}\leftrightarrow\{\text{irreducible}\}$ and $\{\text{maximal}\}\leftrightarrow\{\text{points}\}$ | decide when two ideals cut out the same shape: compare radicals |
| **Certificate** | $g_1=\cdots=g_r=0$ has no solution $\iff$ $\sum_i h_ig_i=1$ for some $h_i$ | prove inconsistency algebraically (the engine of the Rabinowitsch trick) |

Over $\mathbb{R}$ all of this fails at once: $(x^2+1)$ is proper — even maximal —
yet $V(x^2+1)=\varnothing$, so $I(V(J))=(1)\neq\sqrt J$.

*From* [1.5](lessons/01-05-nullstellensatz.md), *previewed in* [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md)

### The homogeneous Nullstellensatz

Same theorem, one glitch. For a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq S=k[x_0,\dots,x_n]$:

$$\text{(1)}\quad V_{+}(I)=\varnothing\iff\sqrt I\supseteq\mathfrak{m}_0 \qquad\qquad \text{(2)}\quad V_{+}(I)\neq\varnothing\ \Rightarrow\ I\big(V_{+}(I)\big)=\sqrt I$$

**Always check (1) before invoking (2).** Proof runs through the affine cone
$V(I)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$, a union of lines through the origin; the projective
points are those lines other than $\{0\}$.

*From* [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md)

### Operations on $V$ and $I$

| Geometry | Algebra |
|---|---|
| $V(S)$ | $=V(\langle S\rangle)$ — only the generated ideal matters |
| $S\subseteq T$ | $\Rightarrow V(S)\supseteq V(T)$; and $X\subseteq Y\Rightarrow I(X)\supseteq I(Y)$ |
| $V(J)\cup V(J')$ | $=V(JJ')=V(J\cap J')$ |
| $\bigcap_\alpha V(J_\alpha)$ | $=V\big(\sum_\alpha J_\alpha\big)$ |
| $I(X\cup Y)$ | $=I(X)\cap I(Y)$ |
| $V(I(X))$ | $=\bar X$, the Zariski closure; $=X$ when $X$ is already closed |
| $I(V(J))$ | $\supseteq\sqrt J$ over any field; $=\sqrt J$ over $\bar k$ |
| $V(0)=\mathbb{A}^n$, $V(1)=\varnothing$ | $I(\varnothing)=(1)$, $I(\mathbb{A}^n)=(0)$ |

*From* [1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md)

### Computing a radical by sandwich

The move that solves nearly every radical exercise. To show $\sqrt J=\mathfrak{a}$:

1. **Up:** find a power. If $f^m\in J$ then $f\in\sqrt J$, so $\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\sqrt J$ whenever each generator of $\mathfrak{a}$ has a power in $J$.
2. **Down:** find a radical roof. If $J\subseteq\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{a}$ is radical, then $\sqrt J\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{a}$.
3. Radical roofs are cheap: **prime** ideals are radical, **maximal** ideals are radical, and an intersection of radical ideals is radical.

Worked shapes: $\sqrt{(x^2)}=(x)$, $\sqrt{(x^2,y)}=(x,y)$, $\sqrt{(x^2y,xy^2)}=(xy)=(x)\cap(y)$,
$\sqrt{(x^3,xy^2)}=(x)$.

*From* [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md)

### Noetherian toolkit

- **Three faces.** ACC $\iff$ every nonempty family of ideals has a maximal element $\iff$ every ideal is finitely generated.
- **Hilbert Basis Theorem.** $R$ Noetherian $\Rightarrow R[x]$ Noetherian. By induction, $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is Noetherian for every field $k$.
- **Inheritance.** Quotients $R/I$ of Noetherian rings are Noetherian — so every coordinate ring is.
- **Geometric payoff.** Every variety $V(S)$, however presented, equals $V(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ for finitely many $f_i$; descending chains of varieties stabilize; every variety is a finite union of irreducible components.
- **Non-example.** $k[x_1,x_2,x_3,\dots]$ is not Noetherian: $(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq\cdots$ never stops.
- The theorem is **non-constructive** — it proves generators exist without producing them. Producing them is what Gröbner bases are for.

*From* [1.3](lessons/01-03-noetherian-hilbert-basis.md)

### Projective bookkeeping

| Job | Recipe |
|---|---|
| dehomogenize into chart $U_0$ | set $x_0=1$; affine coordinates are $x_i/x_0$ |
| homogenize an affine $f$ of degree $d$ | $f^{h}=x_0^{\,d}f(x_1/x_0,\dots,x_n/x_0)$ |
| find the points at infinity of $\overline X$ | set $x_0=0$ in the homogenized equations and solve |
| count points over $\mathbb{F}_q$ | $\lvert\mathbb{P}^n(\mathbb{F}_q)\rvert=1+q+\cdots+q^n=\dfrac{q^{n+1}-1}{q-1}$ |
| two lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$ | always meet in exactly one point; same slope $\Rightarrow$ that point is at infinity |

Homogenizing generators one at a time can give something strictly **larger** than
the closure (the twisted cubic is the standard trap); for a single hypersurface it
is always safe.

*From* [2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md)

### Dimension: three computations and the standard values

$$\dim X=\dim k[X]=\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(X)$$

In practice: **compute the transcendence degree** (eliminate variables using the
relations until you recognize $k(t_1,\dots,t_d)$), then exhibit a tower or a prime
chain of that length as a check.

| Variety | Dimension |
|---|---|
| $\mathbb{A}^n$, $\mathbb{P}^n$ | $n$ |
| a point, a finite set | $0$ |
| irreducible hypersurface $V(f)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^n$ | $n-1$ |
| any irreducible plane curve | $1$ |
| twisted cubic in $\mathbb{A}^3$ | $1$ — the ambient $n$ is irrelevant |
| Segre variety $\Sigma_{m,n}$ | $m+n$ |
| Veronese image $\nu_d(\mathbb{P}^n)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^N$ | $n$ |
| $\mathcal{O}_{X,p}$ for any $p\in X$ | $\dim X$ — dimension is local |

**Dimension is a birational invariant** (it depends only on $k(X)$), and
$Y\subsetneq X$ closed irreducible $\Rightarrow\dim Y<\dim X$.

*From* [3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md)

### The localization catalogue

One construction, three jobs — know which one a lesson means.

| Invert | Ring | Geometric meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $S=R\setminus\{0\}$ ($R$ a domain) | $\operatorname{Frac}(R)=k(X)$ | all rational functions |
| $S=\{1,f,f^2,\dots\}$ | $R_f=R[1/f]$ | functions on the basic open $D(f)$ |
| $S=R\setminus\mathfrak{p}$ | $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$, local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ | functions near the subvariety $V(\mathfrak{p})$; at $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}_p$, functions at the point $p$ |

Primes of $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ correspond to primes of $R$ **contained in**
$\mathfrak{p}$ — localizing forgets every other point, including whole components of
a reducible $X$ that miss $p$.

*From* [3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md)

### Smoothness test

$$\dim_k T_pX=n-\operatorname{rank}J_p,\qquad p\ \text{smooth}\iff\operatorname{rank}J_p=n-\dim X$$

| Situation | Test |
|---|---|
| hypersurface $V(f)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^n$ | $p$ singular $\iff f(p)=0$ **and** every $\partial f/\partial x_j(p)=0$ |
| higher codimension | compare $\operatorname{rank}J_p$ to $n-\dim X$ — not to $0$ |
| intrinsic form | $p$ smooth $\iff\mathcal{O}_{X,p}$ is a regular local ring |
| whole variety | $\operatorname{Sing}(X)$ is closed (all $(n-d)\times(n-d)$ minors vanish) and proper — $X$ is smooth on a dense open |

*From* [3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md), [3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md)

### Classifying a plane-curve singularity

Translate $p$ to the origin, expand $f=f_m+f_{m+1}+\cdots$, and factor the lowest
form $f_m$ over $k=\bar k$.

| $f_m$ | Verdict |
|---|---|
| $f_1\neq0$ (so $m=1$) | **smooth**; $C_pX=T_pX=V(f_1)$, a hyperplane |
| $m=2$, two distinct linear factors | **node** — two branches crossing |
| $m=2$, a repeated linear factor | **cusp** — one pinched branch |
| $f$ already homogeneous | $C_pX=X$: the variety is its own cone (e.g. a quadric cone at its vertex) |

Factor **over $\bar k$** before deciding: $x^2+y^2=(y-ix)(y+ix)$ is a node, not a
point. The top-degree terms are irrelevant — $y^2-x^{100}$ still has a cusp.

*From* [3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md)

### Sheaves: what to check, and what the standard values are

To verify a sheaf: (i) restriction is functorial, (ii) **locality** — sections
agreeing on a cover are equal, (iii) **gluing** — sections agreeing on overlaps
come from a global one. A defining property that is checkable near each point
gives a sheaf; a global constraint does not (constant, bounded — both fail
gluing, and disconnected opens are the sharpest test since compatibility there is
vacuous).

| Sheaf evaluated at | Value |
|---|---|
| $\mathcal{O}_X(X)$, $X$ affine | $k[X]$ — global sections are the coordinate ring |
| $\mathcal{O}_X(D(g))$, $X$ affine | $k[X]_g$ — poles allowed exactly along $g=0$ |
| stalk $\mathcal{O}_{X,p}$ | $k[X]_{\mathfrak{m}_p}$, the local ring of germs |
| $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(\mathbb{P}^1)$ | $k$ — **constants only** |
| $\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}R}(D(f))$ | $R_f$; global sections $=R$; stalk at $\mathfrak{p}$ is $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$ |

The projective collapse is the algebraic Liouville theorem: every global regular
function on an irreducible projective variety is constant. It is exactly why the
geometry has to live in the *sheaf* and in line bundles.

*From* [3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md), [3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md), [2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md)

### Spec: the topology in one block

$$V(I)=\{\mathfrak{p}\supseteq I\},\qquad D(f)=\{\mathfrak{p}:f\notin\mathfrak{p}\},\qquad D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg),\qquad \overline{\{\mathfrak{p}\}}=V(\mathfrak{p})$$

- The $D(f)$ are a **basis**; $V(I)\cup V(J)=V(I\cap J)=V(IJ)$ and $\bigcap_\alpha V(I_\alpha)=V(\sum_\alpha I_\alpha)$.
- $D(f)=\varnothing\iff f$ is **nilpotent** (the nilradical is the intersection of all primes).
- Closed points $=$ maximal ideals. $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is $T_0$ but essentially never Hausdorff: a generic point lies in every nonempty open.
- **Functoriality is contravariant:** $\varphi:R\to S$ gives $\operatorname{Spec}S\to\operatorname{Spec}R$, $\mathfrak{q}\mapsto\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{q})$.
- A quotient $R\twoheadrightarrow R/I$ gives a homeomorphism $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)\cong V(I)$: adding relations $=$ cutting out a closed subscheme.
- Landmark spectra: $\operatorname{Spec}k[x]=\{(0)\}\cup\{(x-a)\}$; $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}=\{(0)\}\cup\{(p)\}$, the arithmetic line; $\operatorname{Spec}k[x]/(x^2)$ is one point whose ring still remembers a doubled point.

*From* [4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md)

### Building $\mathbb{P}^1$ by gluing, and what the glue decides

Two charts $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ and $\operatorname{Spec}k[u]$, glued along
$D(t)\cong D(u)$:

| Transition | Result | Global sections |
|---|---|---|
| $t=1/u$ (orientation reversing) | $\mathbb{P}^1$ | $k$ — a polynomial in $t$ that is also a polynomial in $1/t$ is constant |
| $t=u$ (identity) | the line with **two origins**, non-separated | $k[t]$ |

$\mathbb{P}^1$ is therefore **not affine**: an affine scheme is recovered from its
global ring, and $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is a single point. Each chart *is* affine —
the failure is global, not local.

*From* [4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md)

### Valuation rules

For $v=\operatorname{ord}_p$ on a smooth curve point:

$$v(fg)=v(f)+v(g),\qquad v(f+g)\ge\min\big(v(f),v(g)\big),\qquad v(f)\neq v(g)\Rightarrow v(f+g)=\min\big(v(f),v(g)\big)$$

The worse pole wins whenever the orders differ; only equal orders can cancel.

*From* [4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md)

### Divisors on $\mathbb{P}^1$ — the computational base case

Uniformizers: $t-a$ at a finite point $a$, and $s=1/t$ at $\infty$. **Always
convert to $s$ and inspect $s=0$** — a forgotten $[\infty]$ is the error in almost
every failed degree check.

| Function | Divisor |
|---|---|
| $t$ | $[0]-[\infty]$ |
| $t-a$ | $[a]-[\infty]$ |
| $\dfrac{t-a}{t-b}$ $(a\neq b)$ | $[a]-[b]$ — so any two points of $\mathbb{P}^1$ are linearly equivalent |
| $f=p(t)/q(t)$ in lowest terms, $\deg p=m$, $\deg q=n$ | finite zeros total $m$, finite poles total $n$, and $\operatorname{ord}_\infty(f)=n-m$ |

Consequences: $\deg\operatorname{div}(f)=0$ on any smooth **projective** curve;
$D\sim(\deg D)[\infty]$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$; hence $D\sim D'\iff\deg D=\deg D'$ and
$\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}^1)\cong\mathbb{Z}$. A degree-$d$ polynomial has a pole
of order $d$ at infinity — $t^2$ is not pole-free.

*From* [4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md)

### Counting sections

$$\ell(D)=0\ \text{whenever}\ \deg D<0,\qquad L(0)=k\ \text{so}\ \ell(0)=1,\qquad D\sim D'\Rightarrow \ell(D)=\ell(D')$$

The first line is one argument used three times: a nonzero $f\in L(D)$ makes
$\operatorname{div}(f)+D$ **effective**, and an effective divisor has degree $\ge0$,
while $\deg\operatorname{div}(f)=0$ forces $\deg D\ge 0$.

On $\mathbb{P}^1$: $L(n[\infty])=\operatorname{span}_k\{1,t,\dots,t^n\}$ so
$\ell(n[\infty])=n+1$, and $L([0]+[\infty])=\operatorname{span}_k\{t^{-1},1,t\}$ so
$\ell=3$. The bundle of degree $n$ is $\mathcal{O}(n)$, with
$\dim\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O}(n))=n+1$ for $n\ge0$.

*From* [4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md)

### Riemann–Roch

$$\ell(D)-\ell(K-D)=\deg D+1-g$$

| Consequence | Statement |
|---|---|
| forced by $D=0$ | $\ell(K)=g$ |
| forced by $D=K$ | $\deg K=2g-2$ |
| large degree | $\deg D>2g-2\Rightarrow\ell(K-D)=0$ and $\ell(D)=\deg D+1-g$ **exactly** |
| Riemann's inequality | $\ell(D)\ge\deg D+1-g$ always |
| smooth plane curve of degree $d$ | $g=\binom{d-1}{2}=\dfrac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}$ — line/conic $0$, cubic $1$, quartic $3$ |
| genus $0$ | $\ell(D)=\deg D+1$ for $\deg D\ge0$; $K=-2[\infty]$ on $\mathbb{P}^1$ |
| genus $1$ | $K\sim0$; $\deg D=3\Rightarrow\ell(D)=3$, and $L(3[O])=\operatorname{span}\{1,x,y\}$ embeds the curve as a plane cubic |
| any $g\ge1$ | $\ell([p])=1$: no function with a single simple pole, so $X\not\cong\mathbb{P}^1$ |

The interesting range is $0\le\deg D\le 2g-2$, where the correction term
$\ell(K-D)$ is genuinely alive and the formula stays a difference of two unknowns.

*From* [4.5](lessons/04-05-riemann-roch.md)

## Pitfalls

### The $V$–$I$ dictionary

- $I(V(J))=J$ is false in general — it recovers $\sqrt J$, so $J\subsetneq I(V(J))$ exactly when $J$ is not radical. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md), [1.5](lessons/01-05-nullstellensatz.md))*
- $\sqrt J$ is not a square root — it is about *powers landing in $J$*, at any exponent. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md))*
- $V$ is not injective, and that is not a defect: restrict to radical ideals and it becomes a bijection. Radical ideals are the right objects. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md))*
- $V$ sees zero-*sets*, never multiplicities: $V(x^2)=V(x)$. Recovering that lost data is the entire reason for $\operatorname{Spec}$. *([1.5](lessons/01-05-nullstellensatz.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md))*
- Different generating sets give the same variety — $V$ only ever sees the generated ideal. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md))*
- $\mathbb{A}^n$ is not the vector space $k^n$; treating the origin as special breaks the moment a morphism relocates it. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md))*
- Union $\leftrightarrow$ **product/intersection** of ideals; intersection $\leftrightarrow$ **sum**. Swapping them is the most common algebra-side slip. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md))*

### Hypotheses you will be tempted to drop

- Everything needs $k=\bar k$. Over $\mathbb{R}$, $(x^2+1)$ is proper with empty zero set, and it is a maximal ideal that is not $(x-a)$ — the whole "points $\leftrightarrow$ maximal ideals" bijection dies. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-affine-dictionary.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-ideals-radicals.md), [1.5](lessons/01-05-nullstellensatz.md), [1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md))*
- Function fields, birational equivalence, and dimension all require **irreducible**: on a reducible $X$, $k[X]$ has zero-divisors and $\operatorname{Frac}$ is not a field. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md))*
- $\deg\operatorname{div}(f)=0$ needs both smooth **and** projective. On $\mathbb{A}^1$ the function $t$ has degree $1$ — its pole escaped to an infinity the space doesn't contain. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md))*
- "Point $=$ maximal ideal" is a Module 1–3 convention. In $\operatorname{Spec}R$ a point is *any* prime; silently dropping the non-maximal ones is the standard beginner error. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md))*
- "Prime $=$ point" is also wrong: $(0)$ is prime and corresponds to the whole space. Maximal is the special case of prime that lands on one point. *([1.5](lessons/01-05-nullstellensatz.md))*

### Zariski intuition

- It is **not Hausdorff** — disjoint neighborhoods do not exist, nonempty opens are enormous and always overlap, and "closed = contains its limit points" is not a useful picture. Closed means *cut out by equations*. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md))*
- Irreducible is strictly stronger than connected: $V(xy)$ is connected and reducible. Connected forbids a *disjoint* clopen split; irreducible forbids *any* proper closed cover. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md))*
- Say "quasi-compact", not "compact" — finite subcovers exist, but the separation that "compact" usually implies is absent. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-zariski-topology-irreducibility.md))*
- Noetherian forbids infinite *ascending* chains of ideals only. $k[x]$ has infinitely many ideals, and $(x)\supsetneq(x^2)\supsetneq\cdots$ never stabilizes. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-noetherian-hilbert-basis.md))*
- The Hilbert Basis Theorem promises a finite generating set exists; it does not hand you one, and "$V(S)=V(f_1,\dots,f_r)$" does not mean $S$ was finite. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-noetherian-hilbert-basis.md))*

### Maps

- A tuple of polynomials is a morphism only after you check $\phi(X)\subseteq Y$. *([1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md))*
- The pullback runs **backwards**: $\phi:X\to Y$ gives $\phi^{*}:k[Y]\to k[X]$. Arrows pointing the same way is a category-theory sign error. Same for $\operatorname{Spec}$. *([1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md), [4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md))*
- **Bijective is not isomorphic.** Unlike groups or vector spaces, a bijective morphism of varieties need not invert — check the map on *rings*. The cuspidal cubic is the standing counterexample. *([1.6](lessons/01-06-coordinate-ring-polynomial-maps.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md))*
- Birational is strictly coarser than isomorphic; they coincide only when the bad locus is empty. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md))*
- Dominant means *dense* image, not surjective — and you may only compose rational maps when the inner one is dominant. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md))*
- A rational map is not a function on $X$; always ask for the domain of definition, and simplify before declaring a point bad. *([2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md))*
- A projective map needs forms of **one common degree** with **no common zero**, and it need only have that form locally — different charts may need different tuples. *([2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md))*
- There are no nonconstant maps from a projective variety to $\mathbb{A}^1$: global regular functions are constant. Maps out of projective space go to projective space. *([2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md), [3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md))*
- $\mathbb{P}^m\times\mathbb{P}^n$ does **not** carry the product Zariski topology — it carries the strictly finer one pulled back from the Segre embedding. *([2.5](lessons/02-05-morphisms-projective-varieties.md))*

### Rational functions and local rings

- "$g\neq0$" means *not identically zero*, not *nowhere zero* — legal denominators are allowed to vanish at points, and those are the candidate poles. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md))*
- Never trust a single representative to report the domain: on $V(xy-1)$ the function $1/x$ equals $y$ and has no pole at all. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-regular-rational-maps.md))*
- A **pole** (value diverges) and an **indeterminacy** (bounded but multivalued, as $x/y$ at a node) are different failures — both lie outside the domain, but only one is a pole. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-rational-functions-function-field.md))*
- Localizing **grows** the ring, $R\subseteq R_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq k(X)$; what shrinks is the set of primes. *([3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md))*
- $\mathfrak{m}_p\subseteq k[X]$ and its extension $\mathfrak{m}_p\mathcal{O}_{X,p}$ are different objects: one maximal ideal among many, versus *the* maximal ideal. *([3.2](lessons/03-02-local-rings-localization.md))*
- $D(g)\subseteq D(h)$ means $\sqrt{(g)}\subseteq\sqrt{(h)}$, not $g\mid h$. *([3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md))*

### Projective coordinates

- Homogeneous coordinates are not numbers: $[1:2:3]=[2:4:6]$. Only ratios and "$x_i=0$?" survive. *([2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md))*
- $[0:\cdots:0]$ is not a point — which is exactly why the irrelevant ideal cuts out nothing and why the homogeneous Nullstellensatz needs its exception clause. *([2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md))*
- "At infinity" is chart-dependent, not intrinsic: $[0:2:5]$ is a perfectly finite point of $U_1$. *([2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md))*
- $\mathbb{P}^n$ has dimension $n$, not $n+1$ — collapsing lines to points spends a dimension. *([2.3](lessons/02-03-projective-space.md))*
- Homogenize the whole **ideal**; doing it generator-by-generator can add spurious components at infinity (twisted cubic). *([2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md))*
- Check $V_{+}(I)\neq\varnothing$ before using $I(V_{+}(I))=\sqrt I$: for $I=(x_0^2,x_1^2)$, $\sqrt I=\mathfrak{m}_0$ but $I(V_{+}(I))=S$. *([2.4](lessons/02-04-projective-varieties-homogeneous-nullstellensatz.md))*

### Dimension, tangent spaces, smoothness

- Dimension is the number of $\subsetneq$ **steps**, not of terms in the tower. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md))*
- The ambient $n$ is irrelevant — the twisted cubic in $\mathbb{A}^3$ is a curve. Only the function field matters. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md))*
- More generators does not mean more dimension; transcendence degree counts only the *independent* ones. And Krull dimension is not vector-space dimension. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-dimension.md))*
- $T_pX$ is a vector space through the **origin**; the tangent line you would draw is the translate $p+T_pX$. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md))*
- Feed the Jacobian generators of $I(X)$ — using $(f^2)$ instead of $(f)$ changes the ring and gives the wrong tangent space. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md))*
- A big tangent space means *too much* tangent, not none: $\dim_k T_pX\ge\dim_pX$ never fails, and singular is where it is strict. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md))*
- "The Jacobian vanishes" is not the test — compare $\operatorname{rank}J_p$ to $n-\dim X$. On a hypersurface one nonzero partial suffices. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md))*
- The tangent cone comes from the **lowest**-degree form, never the top one, and $C_pX=T_pX$ only at smooth points. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md))*
- A repeated linear factor is a cusp, distinct factors are a node — and factor over $\bar k$ first, since $x^2+y^2$ splits. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-smoothness-singularities-tangent-cone.md))*
- Smoothness is a property of a **point**, not a curve: the cuspidal cubic is singular at $0$ and smooth everywhere else. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-tangent-space.md))*

### Sheaves and schemes

- The "agree on overlaps" hypothesis is the entire content of gluing — and when the overlap is empty it is vacuous, which is why disconnected opens break the constant presheaf. *([3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md))*
- Locality and gluing are independent axioms: constants satisfy the first and fail the second. *([3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md))*
- "Isomorphism on every stalk $\Rightarrow$ isomorphism" holds for **sheaves only**; a presheaf and its sheafification share all stalks. *([3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md))*
- A germ remembers everything in an arbitrarily small neighborhood, not just the value at the point. *([3.5](lessons/03-05-sheaves-first-treatment.md))*
- The structure sheaf is not the coordinate ring — $k[X]$ is only $\mathcal{O}_X(X)$. On a projective variety that global ring is $k$, while the sheaf is rich. *([3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md))*
- A section is a function, not a fraction: its presentation as $f/g$ may differ from point to point. *([3.6](lessons/03-06-structure-sheaf.md))*
- $\mathcal{O}(U)=R_f$ is guaranteed on **basic** opens only; on a general open a section is a compatible family. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md))*
- A morphism of schemes must be **local** on stalks; drop that and $\operatorname{Spec}$ stops being an anti-equivalence. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md))*
- $\mathcal{O}(X)=k$ does not make $X$ a point ($\mathbb{P}^1$), and matching global sections does not make two schemes isomorphic (the line with two origins). The glue matters. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-structure-sheaf-gluing-schemes.md))*
- "Vanishes at $\mathfrak{p}$" is *defined* as $f\in\mathfrak{p}$ — there is no numerical evaluation. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-spec-of-a-ring.md))*

### Divisors and Riemann–Roch

- The pole at infinity is real: a degree-$d$ polynomial on $\mathbb{P}^1$ has a pole of order $d$ at $\infty$. A degree check that fails is almost always a forgotten $[\infty]$. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md))*
- Measure order with the **uniformizer**, not the coordinate: at $\infty$ use $s=1/t$, so $\operatorname{ord}_\infty(t)=-1$. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md))*
- $\operatorname{ord}_p$ needs smoothness — at a node the local ring is not a DVR and order is not well-defined. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md))*
- Principal is stronger than degree $0$: on an elliptic curve $[p]-[q]$ is principal only when $p=q$. They coincide on $\mathbb{P}^1$, which is what makes $\mathbb{P}^1$ special. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-divisors-on-a-curve.md))*
- $\ell(D)=\deg D+1$ is a genus-$0$ accident; in general only $\ell(D)\ge\deg D+1-g$ holds, with equality guaranteed once $\deg D>2g-2$. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md), [4.5](lessons/04-05-riemann-roch.md))*
- A section of a line bundle is a function only after trivializing; its zeros move with the section, and only their divisor **class** is fixed. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-line-bundles-picard.md))*
- $K$ is a class, not a divisor. Every statement about it — $\deg K=2g-2$, $\ell(K)=g$, $K-D$ — is a statement about the class. *([4.5](lessons/04-05-riemann-roch.md))*
- Genus is a birational invariant; the degree of a plane model is not. Do not confuse $d$ with $g$. *([4.5](lessons/04-05-riemann-roch.md))*
