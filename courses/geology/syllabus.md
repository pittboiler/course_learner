# Geology — Syllabus

> Earth & Space · Tier 0 · ~18 lessons · Prereqs: none · Roadmap id: `geology`

## Goal

Read the Earth the way a field geologist does: name what a rock is made of and how it formed, explain why continents drift and mountains rise, and reconstruct a landscape's history from the story written in its layers. You will finish able to date rocks, decode plate boundaries, and estimate the tempo of geologic change — without any quantitative geophysics (that's `geophysics`) or specialist-depth mineralogy and petrology.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Identify a mineral from its physical properties (hardness, cleavage, luster) and explain why those properties follow from its internal structure
- [ ] Classify any rock as igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic and trace its path around the rock cycle
- [ ] Explain the evidence that convinced geologists continents move, and why it took a driving mechanism to make it stick
- [ ] Predict the landforms, earthquakes, and volcanoes expected at a divergent, convergent, or transform boundary
- [ ] Estimate the depth and rupture length of an earthquake from its magnitude, and say why that magnitude scale is logarithmic
- [ ] Explain why some volcanoes erupt gently and others explode, from magma composition alone
- [ ] Read a river, glacier, or desert landscape and infer the process that shaped it
- [ ] Order a set of rock layers and cross-cutting features in time using relative-dating principles
- [ ] Compute the age of a rock from a parent/daughter isotope ratio and a half-life
- [ ] Sketch Earth's internal layers and explain how differentiation produced them
- [ ] Reconstruct the environment that deposited a sedimentary sequence from its stratigraphy
- [ ] Place a major event in Earth history on the geologic timescale and connect it to a natural resource we exploit today

## Modules

### Module 1: Earth Materials & the Rock Cycle

From atoms to outcrops: what minerals are, why they behave as they do, and the three ways rock is made and remade.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What a Mineral Is | Define a mineral and predict its properties from its crystal structure | Crystal lattice, composition, silicate tetrahedron, physical properties |
| 1.2 | Identifying Minerals | Identify common minerals by hardness, cleavage, luster, and streak | Mohs hardness, cleavage vs. fracture, the rock-forming minerals |
| 1.3 | Igneous Rocks | Read an igneous rock's texture and composition to tell where it cooled | Magma vs. lava, intrusive/extrusive, felsic-to-mafic, Bowen's series |
| 1.4 | Sedimentary Rocks | Distinguish clastic, chemical, and organic sediments and their environments | Weathering to lithification, grain size, depositional environment |
| 1.5 | Metamorphic Rocks & the Rock Cycle | Explain how heat and pressure transform rock, and close the cycle | Foliation, grade, protolith, the rock cycle as an energy diagram |

**Boss problem 1:** Given a field description — "fine-grained dark rock with visible gas holes, overlying a layer of rounded pebbles cemented in sand, both cut by a band of shiny wavy-layered rock" — name each rock, state how each formed, and reconstruct the sequence of events that produced this outcrop.

### Module 2: Plate Tectonics & the Dynamic Earth

The unifying theory: the evidence for moving plates, what happens at their edges, and the engine underneath.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The Evidence for Drift | Marshal the fit-of-continents, fossil, and paleomagnetic evidence for moving plates | Continental fit, fossil/rock matches, seafloor magnetic stripes, apparent polar wander |
| 2.2 | Plate Boundaries | Predict landforms and hazards at divergent, convergent, and transform boundaries | Ridges, subduction, collision, transform faults, boundary triads |
| 2.3 | The Driving Mechanism | Explain what actually moves plates and why the theory needed it | Mantle convection, ridge push, slab pull, the lithosphere/asthenosphere pair |
| 2.4 | Earthquakes | Locate an earthquake and interpret its magnitude and hazard | Elastic rebound, P/S waves, epicenter location, the logarithmic magnitude scale |
| 2.5 | Volcanoes | Explain why magma composition decides whether a volcano oozes or explodes | Viscosity and gas, shield vs. stratovolcano, hotspots, volcanic hazards |

**Boss problem 2:** A chain of islands runs in a straight line; the ages of their volcanic rock increase steadily with distance from an active volcano at one end. Explain what makes this chain, use two of the ages to estimate the plate's speed in cm/yr, and predict where the next island will form.

### Module 3: Surface Processes & Geologic Time

How wind, water, and ice sculpt the land — and how geologists tell time, from stacking principles to radioactive clocks.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Weathering & Erosion | Distinguish how rock breaks down chemically vs. physically and how debris moves | Mechanical vs. chemical weathering, soil, mass wasting, erosion vs. transport |
| 3.2 | Rivers, Glaciers & Deserts | Read a landscape and name the surface process that shaped it | Stream profiles, glacial valleys and till, aeolian dunes, base level |
| 3.3 | Relative Dating | Order events in rock using the classic stratigraphic principles | Superposition, original horizontality, cross-cutting, unconformities |
| 3.4 | Radiometric Dating | Compute an absolute age from a parent/daughter ratio and half-life | Radioactive decay, half-life, isotope systems, closure temperature |
| 3.5 | The Geologic Timescale | Place events on the timescale and explain how it was built and calibrated | Eons/eras/periods, index fossils, calibrating relative time with absolute dates |

**Boss problem 3:** A volcanic ash bed contains a mineral whose potassium-40 has decayed so that 12.5% of the original remains (half-life 1.25 billion years). The ash sits above tilted, eroded sedimentary layers and below flat-lying ones. Give the ash's age, name the feature between the two sedimentary sets, and state what you can and cannot say about the ages of the layers it separates.

### Module 4: Earth's Interior & History

Inward and backward: the layered planet beneath our feet, and the four-billion-year story recorded in its rocks and resources.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Earth's Internal Structure & Differentiation | Sketch Earth's layers and explain how a molten young planet sorted itself | Crust/mantle/core, differentiation, compositional vs. mechanical layers, evidence from waves and meteorites |
| 4.2 | Reading the Rock Record | Reconstruct a past environment from a stratigraphic sequence | Facies, transgression/regression, correlation, Walther's law in plain terms |
| 4.3 | Earth History & Natural Resources | Trace key events in Earth history and connect them to resources we use | Early Earth, the oxygen revolution, mass extinctions, fossil fuels, ores, groundwater |

**Boss problem 4:** A drill core passes downward through coal, then shale with marine shells, then sandstone with ripple marks, then granite. Reconstruct the sequence of environments this site passed through over time, say which layer is oldest and why, and name one natural resource in the core and the past conditions that concentrated it.

## Sources of truth

- Marshak, *Essentials of Geology* — organization, terminology, and the "big picture" framing.
- Grotzinger & Jordan, *Understanding Earth* — process-based explanations and figure conventions.
- U.S. Geological Survey public materials — hazard definitions, magnitude/intensity scales, and current nomenclature.
- International Commission on Stratigraphy timescale — period names, boundaries, and ages.
