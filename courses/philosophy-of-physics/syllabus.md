# Philosophy of Physics — Syllabus

> Philosophy · Tier 2 · ~25 lessons · Prereqs: [`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md), [`quantum-mechanics`](../quantum-mechanics/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `philosophy-of-physics`

## Goal

This course owns the philosophy of physics for the whole library: what our best physical theories say the world is like, where they fail to say it clearly, and what each proposed repair costs. Jacob has built the physics already. [`quantum-mechanics`](../quantum-mechanics/syllabus.md), [`relativity`](../relativity/syllabus.md), [`stat-mech`](../stat-mech/syllabus.md), [`qft`](../qft/syllabus.md), [`analytical-mechanics`](../analytical-mechanics/syllabus.md) and [`dynamical-systems`](../dynamical-systems/syllabus.md) are cited lesson by lesson and never re-taught. This course asks what that physics means. It runs through six questions. Why does a theory that evolves linearly predict single outcomes? What do the realist repairs (Bohm, GRW, many-worlds) each give up? What exactly does Bell's theorem rule out? Is spacetime a thing or a system of relations? Why does entropy rise toward the future when the laws do not care about direction, and what are the probabilities of statistical mechanics? And what do symmetries, gauge freedom and effective field theories tell us about which structure is real? You will finish able to state the measurement problem as a trilemma and place every interpretation on it, derive the CHSH bound and say which premise each interpretation denies, reconstruct the Leibniz shift arguments and the hole argument, compute the Boltzmann counting that makes the second law typical, and say what the past hypothesis does and does not explain. The course is verdict-neutral across interpretations of quantum mechanics, between substantivalism and relationalism, and between the typicality, ergodic and epistemic readings of statistical probability. It deliberately skips the general theory of scientific realism, underdetermination, explanation and laws ([`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md), cited), the metaphysics of time and of free will ([`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md) Modules 5 and 6), quantum gravity and the interpretation of cosmology beyond the past hypothesis, the axiomatic (algebraic) quantum field theory programme beyond naming it, and quantum logic.

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md): every part declares its kind, and every solution is a rubric (must-hit moves, wrong turns) plus a model answer. Here that plays out in four ways:

- **Formal parts are common and graded as math, but only where the math is the philosophy.** The CHSH bound from factorizability, the reduced density matrix that looks like a proper mixture, branch counting against Born weights, GRW collapse rates, Reichenbach's ε and the one-way speeds it implies, the hole diffeomorphism, microstate counting and typicality, Norton's dome, predictability horizons, and gauge invariance of the Aharonov–Bohm phase. Every number in a lesson or a key is computed twice, once by hand and once in closed form, before it ships.
- **Exegetical parts are strict.** What the measurement-problem trilemma's three claims are, which of parameter and outcome independence Bohmian mechanics violates, what Leibniz's static shift argument needs from the PSR, what the past hypothesis asserts, what the Earman–Norton dilemma says a substantivalist must accept. These have right answers.
- **Evaluative parts are verdict-neutral.** No rubric rewards preferring Bohm, GRW, many-worlds, QBism or Copenhagen, concluding that nature is nonlocal, that spacetime is a substance, that the past hypothesis needs explaining, or that gauge potentials are unreal. Lessons may report where specialists sit (for example, that the Deutsch–Wallace derivation is contested among philosophers of physics) but never argue for a side in the lesson's voice. Must-hit moves are ones every side has to make: say which premise of the problem the view denies, what it posits instead, and what that costs.
- **Archetypes.** Find-the-crux and apply-to-a-hard-case are the workhorses (Wigner's friend, the rotating globes, the hole, the melting ice cube run backward). Counterexample carries determinism (the dome, space invaders). **Diagnose** uses invented popular-science paragraphs that claim decoherence solved the measurement problem or that Bell proved the Moon isn't there when nobody looks. Close reading uses public-domain texts only: Galileo's ship in the *Dialogue* (Salusbury, 1661); Newton's Scholium to the Definitions in the *Principia* (Motte, 1729); the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence (Clarke's 1717 edition); Mach, *The Science of Mechanics* (McCormack translation); Poincaré, *Science and Hypothesis* (English translation, 1905); Einstein's 1905 relativity paper in the Perrett–Jeffery translation (1923). Modern authors (Bohr, Bell, Bohm, Everett, Albert, Maudlin, Wallace, Earman, Norton, Brown, Healey) are paraphrased and cited, never quoted at length.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] State the measurement problem as three claims that cannot all be true, and place Copenhagen, QBism, relational quantum mechanics, Bohmian mechanics, GRW and many-worlds by the claim each denies
- [ ] Compute the reduced density matrix of a system after decoherence, and say precisely why an improper mixture does not by itself deliver a single outcome
- [ ] State the Bohmian guidance equation's role, the GRW collapse parameters, and the Everettian branching picture, and compute how fast GRW collapses a pointer and how branch counting diverges from Born weights
- [ ] Explain the incoherence and quantitative problems of probability in many-worlds, and reconstruct the decision-theoretic and self-locating answers with the strongest objection to each
- [ ] Derive the CHSH bound from local factorizability, compute a quantum violation for given measurement angles, and say which premise Bohm, GRW, many-worlds, QBism, superdeterminism and retrocausal models each deny
- [ ] Separate nonlocal correlation from signalling, parameter independence from outcome independence, and say where each interpretation's nonlocality strains against relativity
- [ ] Reconstruct Leibniz's static and kinematic shift arguments and Newton's bucket and globes, and say what neo-Newtonian (Galilean) spacetime concedes to each side
- [ ] Compute the synchronization Reichenbach's ε allows and the one-way light speeds it implies, state what Malament's theorem claims against conventionalism, and reconstruct the Rietdijk–Putnam argument
- [ ] Construct the hole diffeomorphism, state the Earman–Norton dilemma, and compare Leibniz equivalence, sophisticated substantivalism and metric essentialism as replies
- [ ] Give a precise definition of determinism for a physical theory, show Norton's dome violates it in Newtonian mechanics, and compute a predictability horizon to separate determinism from predictability
- [ ] Compute the Boltzmann entropy change and the typicality of equilibrium for a gas, state Loschmidt's and Zermelo's objections, and say what the past hypothesis adds, what it costs, and what Boltzmann brains do to the fluctuation alternative
- [ ] Compare the ergodic, typicality and maximum-entropy accounts of probability in statistical mechanics, and say whether a deterministic world can contain objective chances
- [ ] Say when a symmetry marks surplus structure and when it has direct empirical significance, and run the potentials, fields and holonomy interpretations on the Aharonov–Bohm effect
- [ ] Say what the Unruh effect and the no-go theorems for localizable particles do to a particle ontology, and state the realist and anti-realist readings of effective field theory

## Modules

### Module 1: The measurement problem

Why a theory with linear dynamics and a separate collapse rule is not yet a coherent description of the world, why decoherence does not close the gap, and the family of views that deny the problem is posed correctly.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | The measurement problem as a trilemma | State the three claims that cannot all hold, and run them on Schrödinger's cat and Wigner's friend | the collapse postulate as a primitive (from [`quantum-mechanics`](../quantum-mechanics/syllabus.md) 1.5), von Neumann's measurement chain, Maudlin's trilemma (the wavefunction is complete, it always evolves linearly, measurements have single outcomes), what "measurement" would have to mean, Bell's "against measurement" complaint paraphrased, Wigner's friend |
| 1.2 | Decoherence and what it does not do | Compute a decohered reduced state and say exactly which problem decoherence solves and which it leaves | system–apparatus–environment entanglement, the partial trace (from [`quantum-computing`](../quantum-computing/syllabus.md) 2.2, 5.1), proper vs improper mixtures (d'Espagnat), the preferred-basis problem as the one decoherence addresses, the "and-or" problem that survives it, **diagnose** problems |
| 1.3 | Copenhagen, QBism, and relational views | State Bohr's view, the textbook view, and two contemporary heirs at full strength, and say which claim of the trilemma each rejects or reframes | Bohr's complementarity and the necessity of classical concepts, the Heisenberg cut, the textbook (Dirac–von Neumann) collapse view as distinct from Bohr's (Howard), ψ-ontic vs ψ-epistemic (Harrigan–Spekkens; the PBR theorem named), QBism (Fuchs, Schack, Mermin) and quantum states as an agent's credences (credences from [`epistemology`](../epistemology/syllabus.md) 5.1), Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics, the objection that each changes the subject |

**Boss problem 1:** (a) Formal: a spin in the state √(1/3)|up⟩ + √(2/3)|down⟩ interacts with an environment, ending in √(1/3)|up⟩|E-up⟩ + √(2/3)|down⟩|E-down⟩. Compute the spin's reduced density matrix when the environment states overlap with inner product γ, then when γ = 0, and show the second is identical to the density matrix of a 1/3–2/3 proper mixture. (b) Exegetical: state the three claims of the measurement-problem trilemma, and say which of them decoherence alone requires one to deny (none), with the reason. (c) Diagnose: an invented magazine paragraph says that "since the 1980s we know the cat is never both alive and dead, because decoherence destroys the superposition in a trillionth of a second." Name the conflation (improper for proper mixture, or suppression of interference for selection of an outcome) and the words that commit it. (d) In 300–500 words: does QBism or relational quantum mechanics solve the measurement problem, dissolve it, or decline to address it? Any verdict, if the answer runs the chosen view on Wigner's friend, says what it takes a quantum state to describe, and states the strongest objection to the view in its defenders' own terms. Part (a) is formal; (b)–(c) are exegetical; (d) is evaluative.

### Module 2: The realist solutions

Three ways to take the wavefunction as describing reality and still get single outcomes, or the appearance of them: add particles, change the dynamics, or take every branch as real.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Bohmian mechanics | State the theory, show how it recovers Born statistics, and name what it posits that no experiment can see | particle positions as the primitive ontology, the guidance equation, quantum equilibrium and equivariance (typicality justification, Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì, returning in 5.6), contextuality of spin, empty wave packets, the "surrealistic trajectories" objection, a preferred foliation for many particles (returning in 3.3) |
| 2.2 | Collapse theories: GRW and CSL | Compute how fast GRW collapses a single particle and a pointer, and state the tails problem and the primitive ontology question | modified dynamics, the GRW rate (about 10⁻¹⁶ per second per particle) and localization width (about 10⁻⁷ m), amplification by particle number, continuous spontaneous localization named, the tails problem, mass-density vs flash ontologies, empirical testability as the one interpretation that differs from standard predictions |
| 2.3 | Many-worlds I: branching | State Everett's relative-state idea and the modern decoherence-based account of what a branch is | Everett's relative states, the preferred-basis problem revisited (from 1.2), branches as emergent, quasi-classical patterns (Wallace; Dennett's real patterns), wavefunction realism vs spacetime-state realism, what "world" means here and how it differs from the cosmological multiverse ([`philosophy-of-religion`](../philosophy-of-religion/syllabus.md) 3.2) |
| 2.4 | Many-worlds II: the probability problem | Say why probability is hard when every outcome occurs, and assess the decision-theoretic and self-locating answers | the incoherence problem and the quantitative problem, branch counting vs Born weights, the Deutsch–Wallace theorem as a representation theorem (template from [`decision-theory`](../decision-theory/syllabus.md) 1.3, 2.1–2.2), self-locating uncertainty (Vaidman; Sebens and Carroll), the evidential problem (Greaves and Myrvold; confirmation from [`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md) 1.3), interpretations as empirically equivalent rivals ([`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md) 2.2) |

**Boss problem 2:** (a) Formal: take the GRW localization rate as 10⁻¹⁶ per second per nucleon. Compute the mean time before a single isolated nucleon undergoes a collapse, in years, and the mean time before a pointer of 10²³ nucleons in a superposition of two well-separated positions undergoes one, and say what the pair of numbers is designed to achieve. (b) Formal: a spin with Born probability 1/10 for up is measured three times in succession on fresh copies. Count the branches and give the fraction in which every result is down, then give the Born weight of that same outcome, and say which number an Everettian must justify using and why counting is not obviously wrong. (c) Exegetical: say how Bohmian mechanics secures Born-rule statistics (what quantum equilibrium is and what equivariance guarantees), and what an observer in a Bohmian world could never learn about the particle positions. (d) In 300–500 words: Bohmian mechanics and many-worlds agree on every prediction, while GRW differs in principle. Is this a case of underdetermination in the sense of [`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md) 2.2, and if so, what should one believe about the world? Any verdict, if the answer states at least one non-empirical virtue each side claims (ontological clarity, fidelity to the formalism, locality of ontology), engages the probability problem of 2.4, and says whether GRW's testability changes the dialectic. Parts (a)–(b) are formal; (c) is exegetical; (d) is evaluative.

### Module 3: Bell's theorem and nonlocality

What goes into Bell's theorem, what its experimental violation forces each interpretation to give up, and whether what it forces can live with relativity.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | EPR and Bell's theorem: the premises | Reconstruct the EPR argument and derive the CHSH bound, naming every premise used | the EPR criterion of reality paraphrased, hidden variables λ, Bell's local causality and factorizability, measurement independence (statistical independence), definite single outcomes as a silent premise, the deterministic case by enumerating strategies, the quantum value assumed from [`quantum-mechanics`](../quantum-mechanics/syllabus.md) 5.3, Fine's theorem on joint distributions named |
| 3.2 | What Bell rules out | Say which premise each interpretation denies, and whether "local realism" is the right name for what failed | Jarrett's (and Shimony's) split of factorizability into parameter independence and outcome independence, Bohm violates the first and GRW the second, many-worlds and the denial of single outcomes, QBism's local reading, superdeterminism, retrocausal models (Price, Wharton), the dispute over "local realism" as a label (Maudlin against the operationalist reading), loopholes and the 2015 experiments as settled physics (cited to [`quantum-computing`](../quantum-computing/syllabus.md) 2.6), Kochen–Specker contextuality named |
| 3.3 | Nonlocality and relativity | Separate nonlocal correlation from signalling and assess whether each interpretation's nonlocality is compatible with relativity | no-signalling from flat marginals (from [`quantum-mechanics`](../quantum-mechanics/syllabus.md) 5.3) and no-cloning ([`quantum-computing`](../quantum-computing/syllabus.md) 2.3), frame-dependence of which measurement is first, "peaceful coexistence" (Shimony), Bohm's preferred foliation, relativistic flash GRW (Tumulka), whether Bell correlations are causal (counterfactual causation from [`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md) 4.2), Maudlin's taxonomy of what relativity forbids |

**Boss problem 3:** (a) Formal: define S = E(a,b) − E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) + E(a′,b′), where each E is a correlation of ±1 outcomes. Prove |S| ≤ 2 for any deterministic local assignment, then extend to any mixture of such assignments, and name each step that uses factorizability or measurement independence. (b) Formal: for the singlet, E(x,y) = −cos(x − y). With a = 0°, a′ = 90°, b = 30°, b′ = 120°, compute S exactly and compare it with 2 and with 2√2. (c) Exegetical: for Bohmian mechanics, GRW and many-worlds, say which of parameter independence, outcome independence, measurement independence and single outcomes each gives up, and in one sentence each, what it posits in its place. (d) In 300–500 words: an invented science-news headline reads "Bell experiments prove the universe is not locally real." Is "nature is nonlocal" a better summary, a worse one, or neither? Any verdict, if the answer states what each summary takes Bell to have refuted, treats superdeterminism and many-worlds as exits at the strength their defenders give them, and says what no-signalling does and does not show about compatibility with relativity. Parts (a)–(b) are formal; (c) is exegetical; (d) is evaluative.
