# The New Testament · Lesson 6.5: Revelation

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 6: The other letters, Revelation, and the canon · Builds on: [1.3 Under Rome](01-03-under-rome.md), [6.4 Peter, Jude, and the letters of John](06-04-peter-jude-and-the-letters-of-john.md) · Unlocks: [6.6 The formation of the canon](06-06-the-formation-of-the-canon.md)

## Why this matters

Most people meet Revelation through 666 and predictions of the end, which turns it into a codebook for the news. Its first hearers met a letter to seven small churches in Roman Asia, asking whether they would keep faith or accommodate. This lesson reads it that way, works the gematria behind 666 and its variant 616, sorts the four ways the book has been read, and marks what the Church actually teaches about it: less than the prophecy charts suggest.

## The idea

The book tells you its genre three times in its first four verses. It is an *apokalypsis*, an "unveiling" (1:1). It is a "prophecy" to be read aloud in church (1:3). And it is a letter: "John to the seven churches which are in Asia. Grace be unto you and peace" (1:4), the same opening Paul uses ([4.2](04-02-pauls-life-and-letters.md)).

[Apocalyptic](../reference.md#apocalyptic-and-the-kingdom-of-god) is the genre of Daniel. A seer is shown heaven's view of history in symbols: beasts for empires, horns for kings, numbers for spans of time. [`old-testament`](../../old-testament/syllabus.md) 6.3 owns the genre. The symbols were a code the first readers could break, not riddles for a later age: "let him count".

Picture a Christian in Pergamum near the end of the first century. The city has a temple to the emperor, and the guild banquets serve meat offered to a god. The pressure comes from dinners, business and civic loyalty, not the arena. Revelation unveils that world: the friendly city is Babylon (17:5), the imperial power is a beast (13:1), and the only throne that matters is in heaven (4:2).

## Source

Revelation 13:17–18 (Douay–Rheims, Challoner):

> And that no man might buy or sell, but he that hath the character, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name. Here is wisdom. He that hath understanding, let him count the number of the beast. For it is the number of a man: and the number of him is six hundred sixty-six.

Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* V.30.1 (c. 180; ANF vol. 1), on a variant reading he already knew:

> I do not know how it is that some have erred following the ordinary mode of speech, and have vitiated the middle number in the name, deducting the amount of fifty from it, so that instead of six decads they will have it that there is but one.

"The number of a man" (*arithmos anthrōpou*) is best rendered "a human number": a count anyone can do. Greek and Hebrew used letters as numbers, so every name had a sum; adding it up was a known game, *gematria* in Hebrew, *isopsephy* in Greek. Irenaeus's "six decads" is the 60 in 666; the copies he complains about read 616.

## The argument

1. **The throne comes before the beast (chapters 4–5).** Before any plague comes a heavenly liturgy: the living creatures sing the Sanctus of Isaiah 6:3 (4:8), the elders cast down their crowns, and no one can open the sealed scroll of history. An elder announces "the lion of the tribe of Juda". John turns and sees "a Lamb standing, as it were slain" (5:5–6). The Lamb is then worshipped with "him that sitteth on the throne" by every creature (5:13). Yet the angel twice refuses John's worship: "See thou do it not... Adore God" (19:10; 22:9). Richard Bauckham (*The Climax of Prophecy*, 1993) reads the pairing as placing Jesus inside the worship owed to the one God; the doctrine is read in [`trinity-and-god` 2.2](../../trinity-and-god/lessons/02-02-the-father-and-the-son-in-the-new-testament.md). *In words:* the lion wins by being the slain lamb.
2. **The [letters to the seven churches](../reference.md#the-letters-to-the-seven-churches) (chapters 2–3)** follow one form. Address to the church's "angel"; a title of Christ from chapter 1; "I know thy works"; praise and blame; a call to "do penance" (repent), "He that hath an ear...", and a promise "to him that overcometh". The problems are specific: conflict with local synagogues (2:9; 3:9); tolerance of eating "things sacrificed to idols" (2:14, 20); Laodicea's "I am rich" (3:17). One martyr is named, Antipas (2:13). *In words:* the main danger is accommodation; violence is real but local.
3. **[Gematria and the number of the beast](../reference.md#gematria-and-the-number-of-the-beast).** The most widely held solution is Nero Caesar written in Hebrew letters, *nrwn qsr*. That spelling is attested in an Aramaic document from Wadi Murabba'at, on the authorities cited by Bauckham. With nun $= 50$, resh $= 200$, waw $= 6$, qoph $= 100$ and samekh $= 60$:
$$\underbrace{50 + 200 + 6 + 50}_{n\,r\,w\,n} + \underbrace{100 + 60 + 200}_{q\,s\,r} = 306 + 360 = 666.$$
Now drop the final nun, spelling the name as the Latin *Nero* rather than the Greek *Nerōn*:
$$50 + 200 + 6 + 100 + 60 + 200 = 616.$$
That is exactly Irenaeus's "deducting ... fifty". The 616 reading stands in Codex Ephraemi (fifth century) and in P115 (P.Oxy. 4499), a third-century papyrus that is among the oldest witnesses to the chapter. *In words:* one hypothesis explains both readings, and this is the strongest evidence that the Nero solution is right.
4. **Why Nero fits beyond the sum.** Isopsephy was a Roman street game in Nero's own day. Suetonius (*Nero* 39) reports a Greek lampoon that "Nero" has the same value as "killed his own mother". In Greek, $Ner\bar{o}n = 50+5+100+800+50 = 1005$, and *idian mētera apekteine* $= 75 + 454 + 476 = 1005$. Suetonius also says some people long expected Nero to "shortly return" (*Nero* 57). Revelation's beast has a head "slain to death" and healed (13:3). It "was, and is not, and shall come up" (17:8). It is "the eighth, and is of the seven" (17:11). *In words:* the beast is the imperial power, and Nero, the persecutor rumoured to be coming back, is its face.
5. **[The date of Revelation](../reference.md#the-date-of-revelation).** Irenaeus says the vision was seen "towards the end of Domitian's reign" (V.30.3, ANF), which means about 95; Domitian reigned 81–96. Eusebius repeats this (*Church History* III.18). Most scholars accept a date in the 90s, Raymond Brown and Adela Yarbro Collins among them. The name "Babylon" (17:5) for Rome points after 70, since it was the Babylonians who destroyed the first Temple (compare "Babylon" in 1 Peter 5:13, [6.4](06-04-peter-jude-and-the-letters-of-john.md)). A minority, J. A. T. Robinson (*Redating the New Testament*, 1976) among them, dates the book to the late 60s. They point to the Temple still standing to be measured (11:1–2) and to the count of kings in 17:10. The count depends on which emperor starts it and whether the brief reigns of 68–69 count. Leonard Thompson (1990) and others doubt any empire-wide Domitianic persecution, which fits the letters and [`church-history` 1.3](../../church-history/lessons/01-03-persecution-and-martyrdom.md)'s "local and sporadic" phase. *In words:* the 90s are the majority date, and a majority is not a proof.
6. **Which John?** The author calls himself John, on Patmos "for the word of God" (1:9), and never claims to be an apostle. Dionysius of Alexandria (third century, in Eusebius VII.25) found the Gospel's Greek elegant and the Apocalypse's full of "barbarous idioms, and, in some places, solecisms", and concluded that another John wrote it, while refusing to reject the book. Most modern scholars agree; the question stays disputed, and [6.6](06-06-the-formation-of-the-canon.md) follows the book's slow reception in the East.
7. **Revelation 12.** A woman clothed with the sun gives birth to a son "who was to rule all nations" while a dragon waits to devour him. Readings: Israel bearing the Messiah; the Church in labour through persecution; Mary. The Douay's own note gives the first Catholic sense as "the church of God" and adds that it "may also, by allusion, be applied to our blessed Lady". The Marian reading as doctrine belongs to [`ecclesiology-and-mariology`](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/syllabus.md) 6.1.

**Mark the levels.** That the Apocalypse is sacred and canonical is **defined dogma** (rung 1; Trent, [the canon as defined](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/03-03-the-canon-as-defined.md)), and so is its inspiration. Trent's list titles it "the Apocalypse of John the apostle". How far such titles bind a view of authorship is **freely disputed** (rung 5), as with the letters in [4.2](04-02-pauls-life-and-letters.md). Christ's return in glory to judge is **creedal dogma** (rung 1). The rejection of a visible earthly reign of Christ before the last judgment is **authentic ordinary magisterium** (at least rung 3): CCC 676 rejects "even modified forms" of millenarianism, citing a Holy Office decree of 1944 which judged that mitigated millenarianism cannot safely be taught. That doctrine belongs to [`creation-grace-last-things`](../../creation-grace-last-things/syllabus.md) 7.2. The Nero solution, the date, the author's identity and the choice among the reading schools are **hypotheses with no magisterial standing** ([church teaching vs hypothesis](../reference.md#church-teaching-vs-hypothesis)). The gematria is **arithmetic**, and anyone can check it.

## Map of positions

[The four reading schools](../reference.md#the-four-reading-schools) differ on *when* the visions refer:

| School | The visions refer to | The beast of 13 | The thousand years of 20:1–6 | Classic exponents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preterist** | the author's own time | Nero, or Rome's imperial cult | usually symbolic, after Rome's fall | the Jesuit Luis de Alcázar (early 1600s); in "contemporary-historical" form, most critical scholarship |
| **Historicist** | church history in sequence, from John to the end | a later power, often the papacy in Reformation polemic | an identifiable era of church history | Joachim of Fiore; many Reformers; Isaac Newton |
| **Futurist** | mostly (chs. 4–22) events still to come | a future Antichrist | a future reign on earth (premillennialism) | Irenaeus on the Antichrist; the Jesuit Francisco Ribera (late 1500s); modern dispensationalism |
| **Idealist** | no one period; the permanent struggle of Church and world | every persecuting power | the Church's age now, Satan "bound" by the cross | Tyconius, Augustine (*City of God* XX.7), the Douay notes |

Most Catholic exegetes now combine two: the book is read first as addressed to first-century Asia (preterist in method), then its symbols as true of every age (idealist in application).

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (clean): Augustine on 20:1–6.** Augustine once held a spiritual Sabbath-millennium himself (*City of God* XX.7). He then ties the binding of Satan to binding "the strong man" (Mark 3:27): Satan is bound now, unable to hold those who are to be freed. The "first resurrection" is the soul's rising in this life. On the second of his two options, the thousand years is "the whole duration of this world", since $1000 = 10^3$, the cube of ten, is a number of fullness. The visions refer to the Church's present age, with no date in view: the idealist mark.

**Example 2 (hard): the gematria has limits.** The Nero solution strains in three places. First, the book was written in Greek for Greek-speaking churches, yet the solution needs Hebrew letters. Greek *Nerōn Kaisar* sums to $50+5+100+800+50+20+1+10+200+1+100 = 1337$. Second, a sum does not pick out a name. Irenaeus himself found three Greek names worth 666: *Euanthas* $= 5+400+1+50+9+1+200$, *Lateinos* $= 30+1+300+5+10+50+70+200$ and *Teitan* $= 300+5+10+300+1+50$. Third, Bauckham notes that *thērion*, "beast", transliterated into Hebrew as *trywn*, gives $400+200+10+6+50 = 666$. He also notes that 666 is a doubly triangular number, $1+2+\dots+36 = \tfrac{36\cdot 37}{2}$, with $36 = \tfrac{8\cdot 9}{2}$. So the number may be both a name and a pattern. Replies: the author thinks in Hebrew idiom (Dionysius's "barbarous idioms"), and only Nero explains 616. The honest report: the best explanation on offer, still a hypothesis.

## Watch out

- **A hypothesis promoted to teaching.** "The Church teaches that 666 is Nero", or that Revelation dates from 95, or that it was written by the apostle. No act fixes any of these. The same goes in reverse: a futurist chart is not "the Catholic reading" either.
- **Teaching demoted to hypothesis.** The book's canonicity is defined. Its slow reception in the East (6.6) is history, and it does not reopen the definition.
- **"Preterist means it was all fulfilled in the first century."** Full preterism denies a future return; Catholic preterist readings keep the parousia (creedal dogma) and read only the beast and Babylon as first-century.
- **Quoting Irenaeus for 666 as though he denied 616 existed.** He attests the variant and blames copyists. He is our earliest witness that it existed.

## One-liner

> Revelation is a letter to churches tempted to make peace with Rome: count the beast's name and you find an emperor, and look up and you find a slain Lamb on the throne.

## Problems

**P1 (🟢)** Greek letter values: alpha 1, epsilon 5, eta 8, iota 10, lambda 30, nu 50, xi 60, omicron 70, sigma 200, tau 300, upsilon 400, chi 600, and the numeral stigma 6.

(a) *(Formal)* In Greek numerals 666 is written chi-xi-stigma. Irenaeus says 616 arose when the letter for sixty was mistaken for iota. Write 616 in Greek numerals and show that the sum works.
(b) *(Formal)* The Sibylline Oracles give the name *Iēsous* (iota-eta-sigma-omicron-upsilon-sigma) a number. Compute it. Then compute Irenaeus's candidate *Lateinos* (lambda-alpha-tau-epsilon-iota-nu-omicron-sigma).
(c) *(Exegetical)* Irenaeus blames copyists for 616. Give the rival explanation from this lesson in the form of a sum, and say in **two sentences** why it counts as evidence for the Nero solution.

**P2 (🟡)** *(Exegetical)* Four **invented** readings of Revelation 20:1–6 appear in an invented study-group handout. For each, name the reading school, then state the level of doctrinal authority that bears on it. **One line each.**

1. "The thousand years ran from Constantine's conversion to the rise of the Ottoman Turks, and the loosing of Satan is that later era."
2. "After his return Christ will reign visibly in Jerusalem for a literal thousand years before the last judgment."
3. "The martyrs are Christians beheaded under Nero and Domitian, and the vision promised the churches of Asia that their dead would be vindicated."
4. "Satan was bound at the cross; the saints reign with Christ now, in every age, and the thousand years is the whole time of the Church."

**P3 (🔴)** Revelation 2:12–16 (Douay–Rheims):

> And to the angel of the church of Pergamus write: These things saith he that hath the sharp two-edged sword: I know where thou dwellest, where the seat of Satan is. And thou holdest fast my name and hast not denied my faith. Even in those days when Antipas was my faithful witness, who was slain among you, where Satan dwelleth. But I have against thee a few things: because thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat and to commit fornication. So hast thou also them that hold the doctrine of the Nicolaites. In like manner do penance. If not, I will come to thee quickly and will fight against them with the sword of my mouth.

(a) *(Exegetical)* Name the words that show pressure from **outside** the church and the words that show a problem **inside** it. Which does the letter treat as the thing needing repentance? **Three sentences.**
(b) *(Evaluative)* Does this letter help the Domitianic date, the Neronian date, or neither? **100 words or fewer**, any verdict.

<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Formal (a) · Formal (b) · Exegetical (c))*

**Must hit, strict (a):** chi-iota-stigma: $600 + 10 + 6 = 616$, against chi-xi-stigma $600 + 60 + 6 = 666$. The difference is $60 - 10 = 50$, Irenaeus's "deducting the amount of fifty".
**Must hit, strict (b):** *Iēsous* $= 10 + 8 + 200 + 70 + 400 + 200 = 888$. *Lateinos* $= 30 + 1 + 300 + 5 + 10 + 50 + 70 + 200 = 666$.
**Must hit, strict (c):** Hebrew *nrw qsr*, the Latin form *Nero* without the final nun: $50 + 200 + 6 + 100 + 60 + 200 = 616$, beside *nrwn qsr* $= 666$. The point is that one identification explains **both** readings. No rival candidate does, and that raises the Nero solution's probability above a single sum that many names could match.
**Wrong turns:** using eta $= 8$ for the epsilon in *Lateinos* (it is epsilon, $5$). Saying Irenaeus denied that the 616 reading existed, when he reports it. Treating the match as proof, when Irenaeus himself shows that a sum fits many names.
**Model answer (c):** Spelled as the Latin *Nero*, Nero Caesar in Hebrew letters gives $50+200+6+100+60+200 = 616$, and spelled as the Greek *Nerōn* it gives 666. A hypothesis that predicts both readings in the manuscripts explains more than one that fits only 666, which is why the variant counts in the Nero solution's favour.

**P2** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:**
1. **Historicist.** No magisterial act on it: freely disputed (rung 5).
2. **Futurist**, in premillennial form. This is the mitigated millenarianism that CCC 676, citing the Holy Office decree of 1944, says the Church rejects: **authentic ordinary magisterium, at least rung 3**. Denying it is not heresy as such, but it cannot safely be taught.
3. **Preterist.** No magisterial standing either way (rung 5), provided it keeps a future parousia, which is creedal dogma.
4. **Idealist** (the Augustinian reading). The meaning of the thousand years is not defined, so rung 5. The reading agrees with CCC 676's exclusion of an earthly reign. Credit calling it the common Catholic reading since Augustine, provided it is not called defined.

**Wrong turns:** calling (2) heresy, which overstates the level. Calling (4) "the Church's defined interpretation", which also overstates it. Classing (3) as historicist because it names emperors: historicism runs a chain of eras down to the end, while (3) stops at the author's own day.
**Model answer:** (1) Historicist, freely disputed. (2) Futurist and premillennial, rejected as mitigated millenarianism by the ordinary magisterium (CCC 676; Holy Office 1944), not defined. (3) Preterist, freely disputed, so long as the return of Christ is kept. (4) Idealist in Augustine's line, freely disputed, and consistent with CCC 676.

**P3** *(Exegetical (a) · Evaluative (b))*

**Must hit, strict (a):** outside: "where the seat of Satan is" and "where Satan dwelleth", with the martyr Antipas "slain among you". Commentators propose several referents for the "seat" (literally *thronos*, "throne"), such as the imperial cult or the great altar of Zeus, but the text itself names none. Inside: "the doctrine of Balaam", eating (idol food, by comparison with 2:20) and "fornication", and "the Nicolaites". Repentance ("do penance") is demanded for the **inside** problem. The church is praised for its conduct under outside pressure ("hast not denied my faith").
**Must hit, any verdict (b):** note that the letter names **one** martyr, in the past ("in those days"), and at one place. It gives no picture of empire-wide persecution in either reign, and it gives no emperor. Say what that does to arguments for either date that rest on persecution. Any verdict passes, including "neither", if the evidence is placed.
**Wrong turns:** reading Antipas as proof of a general Domitianic persecution. Treating "seat of Satan" as a fixed identification. Missing that the rebuke is about accommodation, not courage.
**Model answer (b), one of several:** Neither, strictly. Antipas is a single martyr from an earlier moment, "in those days", and the present danger is members eating idol food. That fits the local, sporadic hostility of [`church-history` 1.3](../../church-history/lessons/01-03-persecution-and-martyrdom.md), which could hold under Nero in Asia or under Domitian. What it does undercut is any argument for the 90s that needs a great Domitianic persecution. The case for that date must rest on Irenaeus and "Babylon", not on this letter.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [6.3](06-03-james-faith-and-works.md) (James: faith and works):** *(Exegetical.)* Two New Testament writers tell the same story of Rahab, the Canaanite woman of Jericho who hid Joshua's spies (Joshua 2), and credit it to different things (Douay–Rheims):

| Hebrews 11:31 | James 2:25–26 |
|---|---|
| By faith Rahab the harlot perished not with the unbelievers, receiving the spies with peace. | And in like manner also Rahab the harlot, was not she justified by works, receiving the messengers and sending them out another way? For even as the body without the spirit is dead: so also faith without works is dead. |

Hebrews opens with *pistei*, "by faith"; James asks *ouk ex ergōn edikaiōthē*, "was she not justified from works?" Before she acts, Rahab tells the spies: "the Lord your God he is God in heaven above, and in the earth beneath" (Joshua 2:11). (a) Can both writers be right about the same act? Answer from James's own argument, **two sentences**. (b) James 2:19 says the demons also believe there is one God. What separates Rahab's faith from theirs, and what kind of "work" is hers? Why could Paul's phrase "works of the law" not describe it? **Two sentences.**

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Must hit, strict (a):** **yes.** For James, faith and works are not rival causes. Faith "cooperates" with works and is completed by them (2:22), and 2:26 makes them body and spirit of one living thing. So Hebrews can name the act's root (**faith**) and James the faith's **deed**, and the act is the same. Credit the extra point that *dikaioō* in James can mean shown or judged righteous ([6.3](06-03-james-faith-and-works.md)), not the initial verdict Paul means.
**Must hit, strict (b):** her confession has the same content as the demons' creed, but she **acts on it at risk to herself**: she shelters the messengers and sends them away safely. The demons only "believe and tremble". Her work is **hospitality and protection**, done by a Gentile outside the covenant, with no circumcision, food laws or Torah observance in view. That is why it is not *erga nomou*, the works of the law that Paul excludes.
**Wrong turns:** declaring that Hebrews and James contradict each other because one says "faith" and the other "works". That is the same verbal error 6.3 took apart for Romans and James. Reading Rahab as a second Abraham chosen for piety, when James's pairing gives a patriarch and a Canaanite prostitute, which makes the point that the "work" is obedient mercy, not ritual law. Claiming the Church has defined how the two verses fit: it has defined the canonicity of both books, not the exegesis.
**Model answer:** (a) Yes: for James faith works together with works and is dead without them, so Hebrews names the faith behind Rahab's act and James the deed that made that faith alive, and "justified" in James can mean shown to be righteous. (b) Rahab confessed the same one God the demons believe in, but she staked her life on it by hiding and freeing the messengers, which is exactly the living faith James asks for. Her work was mercy and hospitality done by a Canaanite outside the Law, not circumcision or Torah observance, so Paul's "works of the law" cannot name it. 1 Clement 12 (ANF) says she was saved "on account of her faith and hospitality".

</details>
