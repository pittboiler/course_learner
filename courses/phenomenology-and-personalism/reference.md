# Phenomenology & Personalism · Reference Card

> Open book. This card is meant to be open while you work — including during
> quizzes. Nothing here is something you're expected to recall cold.

The course follows one method from its birth to its moral payoff. Brentano makes directedness the mark of the mental; Husserl turns description of what is given, as it is given, into a discipline with the epoché and the reductions, and ends with the lifeworld the sciences forgot. Kierkegaard and Heidegger turn description from consciousness to existence: the single individual, Dasein, das Man, care, death. Sartre, Marcel and Levinas ask what another person is, and answer with a look, a presence and a face. Scheler and Stein describe values, sympathy, empathy and the person, and Stein carries the method to Aquinas. Wojtyła reads the person off action and tries to join the description to Thomist metaphysics. The card is **verdict-neutral**: each entry states what a thinker held, in his or her own terms of art (with the original term), and the main critic's reply; personalism and its critics get the same treatment, and nothing here marks doctrinal authority. Most of these thinkers wrote after 1930 and are paraphrased; short quoted terms only. The recurring trap is reading a term of art in its everyday sense ([terms of art](#terms-of-art)). Use the card mid-problem to look up a term, a position, an argument's premises, who said it where, and which premise a reply denies; problems ask you to *use* these, not recite them.

### Intentionality

Consciousness is always consciousness *of* something: fear of the dog, belief that it is friendly, a wish that it were gone. A stone points at nothing.

**Brentano** (*Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint*, 1874, Book II ch. 1 §5): directedness is the *mark of the mental*, the feature that separates mental from physical phenomena, and the object is said to "in-exist" in the act ([intentional inexistence](#intentional-inexistence)). **Husserl** (*Logical Investigations* V, 1901) keeps the directedness and drops the "in": directedness is a descriptive character of the act, and the intentional object is the object itself, existing or not ([act, content, object](#act-content-object)). Two differences follow. Husserl admits non-intentional experiences (sensations, a burn's pain before an act localizes it as "the pain in my finger"); Brentano did not. For a dim longing with no conscious goal Husserl offers two descriptions (V §15): bare sensations, or an intention aimed at an indeterminate "something", where the indeterminacy belongs to the aim.

**Everyone downstream uses it.** Heidegger rebuilds it as [being-in-the-world](#being-in-the-world); Sartre makes it the emptiness of the [for-itself](#in-itself-and-for-itself); Scheler extends it to [feeling values](#feeling-and-preferring); Stein to [empathy](#empathy); Wojtyła to [action](#mirroring-and-reflexive-functions); Levinas claims the face [overflows](#idea-of-the-infinite) it.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md). Brentano's thesis as a live claim about minds (are pains and moods intentional?): [philosophy-of-mind syllabus](../philosophy-of-mind/syllabus.md), 4.1.

### Intentional inexistence

Brentano's phrase for an act's containing an object: *in*-existence is existence-*in*, not non-existence.

The 1874 text says every mental phenomenon has the "intentional (or mental) inexistence of an object", which Brentano also calls relation to a content, direction toward an object ("by which a reality is not to be understood"), or immanent objectivity, flagging his own terms as ambiguous. Three basic classes: **presentation** (*Vorstellung*), **judgment** (accepting or rejecting what is presented), **love and hate** (feeling, desire, will); judging and loving are built on presenting. Against Hamilton's objectless feelings: pleasure at a harmonious sound is at least pleasure in the hearing. Brentano's footnote runs the lineage from Aristotle through Augustine and Anselm to Aquinas, for whom the thing understood is "intentionally" in the one who understands.

**Two readings of 1874.** *Immanent:* each act has an object lodged inside it, existing whether or not anything outside answers to it. *Directedness:* the act is aimed, and the parenthesis warns against reifying the target. Brentano later called intentionality a **quasi-relation**.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)

### Psychologism

The view that the theoretical foundations of logic lie in psychology, so that logical laws are laws of how minds run.

Husserl's *Philosophy of Arithmetic* (1891) sought a psychological foundation for number; Frege's 1894 review charged it with psychologism. Many commentators read Husserl's change of mind as a response to Frege; J. N. Mohanty (1982) found the seeds in Husserl's own development. The *Prolegomena* (1900) confesses the conversion (with a line from Goethe about being strictest against errors just laid aside). The refutation is the [argument against psychologism](#argument-against-psychologism). The neo-Kantians were already anti-psychologist ([neo-Kantianism](#neo-kantianism)); Husserl got there by description, not transcendental deduction.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md). Frege's side (sense and reference): [philosophy-of-language-and-logic syllabus](../philosophy-of-language-and-logic/syllabus.md).

### Neo-Kantianism

The backdrop of German philosophy around 1900; named, not taught.

**Marburg school** (Hermann Cohen, Paul Natorp): logic and the exact sciences. **Southwest or Baden school** (Wilhelm Windelband, Heinrich Rickert): validity, value, the historical sciences. Both held that validity is not a psychological fact. Natorp is the target of Husserl's 1901 refusal of a pure ego ([transcendental ego](#transcendental-ego)). Kant himself: [modern-philosophy 5.1](../modern-philosophy/lessons/05-01-the-critical-question.md).

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)

### Act, content, object

The three things to keep apart in any experience: the act (the experiencing), its content (what fixes what it is about), and its object (what it is about).

- **Act:** a real (*reell*) event, whose parts are sensations and an intentional character. These are lived through, not intended: "I do not see colour-sensations but coloured things" (LU V §11, translation ours, from the 1901 German).
- **Content:** in Husserl, what fixes aboutness; analysed in [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md) as the act's [matter](#act-quality-and-matter).
- **Object:** the thing itself, whether it exists, is fictional or is impossible. Imagining Jupiter, no Jupiter is found among the act's parts; the act would be described identically if Jupiter were real. "Merely intentional" means the intention exists and the object does not.

**Twardowski's rival** (*On the Content and Object of Presentations*, 1894): there are no objectless presentations, only presentations whose object does not exist. His *content* is the immanent object, a mental part of the act; the *object* is what the presentation is about, round squares included. This is the pressure point of [directedness without immanent objects](#directedness-without-immanent-objects).

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md), [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)

### Image theory

The view that the mind reaches things through inner pictures that resemble them; Husserl's target in LU V, appendix to §§11 and 20.

A resemblance between an inner picture and an outer thing is nothing *for* a consciousness that has only the picture. Something becomes an image only when an act uses it to mean something else, so the image presupposes intentionality and cannot explain it. Conclusion: the intentional object *is* the actual (and, where applicable, external) object; distinguishing the two is absurd. Aimed at the theory of ideas: Locke's idea as the mind's immediate object ([modern-philosophy 3.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/03-02-locke-qualities-substance-and-essence.md)), Hume's perceptions ([modern-philosophy 4.1](../modern-philosophy/lessons/04-01-impressions-ideas-and-the-fork.md)). Reid attacked the same way of ideas from common sense ([modern-philosophy 4.5](../modern-philosophy/lessons/04-05-reid-common-sense-against-the-way-of-ideas.md)); Husserl attacks it by description. *Ideas* I §43 repeats the point for perception: no picture or sign stands in the thing's place.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)

### Act quality and matter

What kind of act it is, and what it is about *as what*.

LU V §20 (1901). **Quality** (*Qualität*): believing, asserting, wishing, asking, imagining. **Matter** (*Materie*): "that peculiarity in the act's phenomenological content which determines *as what* the act apprehends its object" (translation ours), the features, forms and relations it ascribes. Husserl's series: imagining, asserting, asking and wishing that there are intelligent beings on Mars (same matter, four qualities). Same matter, same object; **not conversely**: "the equilateral triangle" and "the equiangular triangle" share an object and differ in matter. Matter is what [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)'s "content" becomes; what a *shared* matter is becomes the [noema](#noesis-and-noema) dispute.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)

### Empty and fulfilled intention

The difference between meaning a thing and having it before you.

LU VI. A **signitive** (empty) intention means its object in words (a friend's text about the blue mug). **Fulfilment** (*Erfüllung*, §8): intuition gives the very object meant, *as* it was meant, adding **fullness** (*Fülle*, §21), not a new object. **Disappointment** (*Enttäuschung*, §12): a positive experience of conflict (the mug is red), not a mere absence. **Evidence** (*Evidenz*, §39): the lived agreement of meant and given; **truth** is that agreement. An empty intention is not vague: its matter is fully determinate; it lacks fullness, not content.

**Reused.** Scientific confirmation as fulfilment by readings in the lifeworld ([1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md)); Stein's second level of empathy fills the first grasp, but only with more empathy, never primordially ([4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md)). Every perception of a thing mixes fulfilled and empty intentions ([profiles and horizons](#profiles-and-horizons)).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md). Fulfilment as one answer to the Sellarsian dilemma: [epistemology 2.2](../epistemology/lessons/02-02-foundationalism.md).

### Categorial intuition

Seeing that the paper is white: the state of affairs, not only the paper and the white, is given, in a non-sensuous act founded on perception.

LU VI §§40-45. "Being" is no real part of the object: I can see the colour, not the being-coloured (§43), and Husserl cites Kant's "being is not a real predicate" ([modern-philosophy 6.3](../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-03-the-critique-of-the-proofs-of-god.md)) for exactly this, extending it from existential to predicative being. Nor does reflection on my judging supply the *is* (§44, against Locke): reflection gives the concept *judgment*. So "intuition" is widened to any act that gives its object itself. Kant drew the opposite conclusion: form comes from the understanding's spontaneity ([modern-philosophy 5.3](../modern-philosophy/lessons/05-03-concepts-and-the-categories.md)).

**Critics.** *Kantian:* the judgment is correct given what I see, but its form is contributed, not given. *Schlick* (*Allgemeine Erkenntnislehre*, 1918): intuiting is acquaintance, and calling it knowing conflates the two (a broader charge against intuition as knowledge). **Reply:** for Husserl knowledge just is a synthesis of meaning and given, and categorial acts are founded on ordinary seeing, so the claim is modest. Skeleton: [argument for categorial intuition](#argument-for-categorial-intuition).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)

### Profiles and horizons

A thing is always seen from one side, with the rest co-meant.

*Ideas* I §41 (walking round a table): the perception changes continuously while the table stays one and the same; the thing appears in **profiles** (*Abschattungen*). The profile is a lived experience; the profiled thing is spatial. Unseen sides, inside and underside are co-intended emptily (**inner horizon**); the room, kitchen and world around are the **outer horizon**. No perception exhausts the thing. *Ideas* I §43: this is no defect of our senses; it is an error of principle to think perception falls short of the thing, and absurd to think even God would perceive a spatial thing without appearances or that a picture or sign stands in its place. We see the thing itself, one-sidedly.

**Reused.** Profiles in harmonious series are premise 1 of the [route to transcendental idealism](#route-to-transcendental-idealism); they survive the [epoché](#epoche) as described, not relied on ([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md), [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

### Noesis and noema

Two sides of every act: the experiencing, and the experienced exactly as experienced.

*Ideas* I §§88-89 (1913). **Noesis:** the act's real components. **Noema:** the perceived *as such*, the sense (*Sinn*). The tree plain and simple can burn down; the sense of the perception cannot, having no chemical elements or real properties (§89, paraphrase of the course's translation). *Ideas* I introduces the noema only after the reduction.

**Readings divide.** *Føllesdal* (1969): the noema is a Fregean sense, an abstract meaning *through which* the act reaches its object. *Sokolowski and Drummond:* the noema is the object itself considered as intended, one thing from two perspectives, not a second entity. The text supports parts of both; the dispute is live.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md), [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

### Inner time-consciousness

Why you hear a melody and not one note at a time; named, not taught.

At the third note, the first two are not remembered but held as just-past (**retention**), around a **primal impression** of the present note, with the next anticipated (**protention**). Retention belongs to the present act; remembering is a new act that brings a past back. Lectures of 1905, published 1928, edited by Heidegger.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)

### To the things themselves

The method's motto: cash empty meanings in experience.

LU II, Introduction §2 (1901 first edition, not a 1913 addition): not content with "mere words", a merely symbolic understanding, we want to go back to the "things themselves" (*Sachen selbst*). The context is clarifying logical concepts by fulfilling intuition. A *Sache* is the matter at issue as given, not Kant's thing in itself ([modern-philosophy 6.1](../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-01-transcendental-idealism-and-the-thing-in-itself.md)); *Ideas* I §43 denies there is a hidden thing in itself that perception fails to reach. Whether that answers Kant or changes the subject is what Kantians dispute.

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)

### Natural attitude

The everyday stance in which the world is simply there.

*Ideas* I §§27-30. I am aware of a world endlessly spread out in space and time, things and people simply on hand (*vorhanden*), with a co-meant surround and a horizon that never runs out; it comes already as a world of values and goods, and others find the same world from their own places (§29). Its **general thesis** (*Generalthesis*, §30): the world is taken as existing, always already there. It is not a judgment but a standing character every experience carries; doubting one thing never touches it (a hallucination is struck *out of* a world that stays there). The sciences live inside it. The [epoché](#epoche) suspends exactly this thesis. In [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md) the [lifeworld](#lifeworld) is this world studied as ground rather than bracketed.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md)

### Epoche

*Epoché*: the general thesis put out of action. The belief stays; we make no use of it.

*Ideas* I §§31-32. Husserl starts from Descartes's attempt at universal doubt only to isolate one component: trying to doubt requires putting being out of play, but doubt adds a posited not-being, so Descartes's attempt is really universal *negation*. The epoché keeps only the suspension: **not negation, not doubt, not conjecture, not supposing** (*Annahme*, §31). It is compatible with conviction unshaken because evident. Images: brackets (*einklammern*), a switch thrown in a circuit (*ausschalten*), "out of action" (*außer Aktion*). §31: I do not negate the world like a sophist or doubt its existence like a sceptic; what is closed off is *judgment about* spatio-temporal existence. §32: the suspension is targeted at the general thesis and every science of the natural world; Husserl contests none of them, takes none of their propositions as premises, and distinguishes the epoché from the positivist demand for theory-free science. Nothing is deleted: the perceived tree is still there *as perceived*.

**Epoché vs Cartesian doubt** is a named crux (see [the rival accounts at a glance](#the-rival-accounts-at-a-glance)). In [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md) the Husserlian objection to Wojtyła's [suppositum](#cosmological-and-personalist-approaches) asks whether a "through-the-phenomenal" subject survives the epoché.

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md). The sceptics' *epochē* (which the word borrows): [ancient-medieval-philosophy 4.3](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/lessons/04-03-the-skeptics.md); Descartes's doubt: [modern-philosophy 1.1](../modern-philosophy/lessons/01-01-the-method-of-doubt.md).

### Principle of all principles

What shows up in person counts as evidence, as far as it shows up and no further.

*Ideas* I §24: every originarily giving intuition is a legitimate source (*Rechtsquelle*) of cognition; whatever presents itself originarily in intuition is to be taken as what it gives itself as, but only within the limits in which it gives itself. The last clause is a restriction, not a licence. A foundationalism of intuition: compare the modest foundationalist's prima facie justified basic beliefs ([epistemology 2.2](../epistemology/lessons/02-02-foundationalism.md)).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

### Eidetic variation

Vary an example in imagination; what cannot change without losing the kind is the essence.

The **eidetic reduction** leads from fact to essence (*eidos*) (*Ideas* I Introduction). An essence is exemplified in perception and equally in "mere fantasy" (§4 lists melodies and social events); the geometer works more in imagination than on the drawing (§70), hence fiction as the "vital element" (*Lebenselement*) of phenomenology. *Ideas* I names the reduction and grounds it in free fantasy; the worked-up method of **free variation** belongs to later manuscripts. Stein uses it for the [personal core](#personal-core) (Caesar in a village).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Transcendental reduction

From real psychological events to "irreal" phenomena: consciousness as that in which the world has its sense.

*Ideas* I Introduction and §33. Empirical psychology is a science of facts and realities; phenomenology departs from it twice, by the eidetic reduction (fact to essence) and the transcendental reduction (real to irreal). The epoché strips experiences of their place in the real world, leaving pure consciousness as the **phenomenological residuum**. *Ideas* I's phenomenology needs both. "Reduction" means *leading back*, not reducing to something smaller. It leads, in §§49 and 55, to transcendental idealism, which Husserl says is not Berkeleyan ([route to transcendental idealism](#route-to-transcendental-idealism)).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

### Transcendental ego

The identical "I" whose regard passes through every act without being part of any.

**1901:** LU V §8 cannot find a pure ego as a necessary centre of relation (against Natorp). **1913:** *Ideas* I §57 admits it: the empirical human being is bracketed; what survives is a pure ego, a "transcendence in immanence". Husserl cites Kant's "I think" that must be able to accompany all my representations. The difference is method: Kant's "I think" is a thought, a condition reached by argument ([modern-philosophy 5.4](../modern-philosophy/lessons/05-04-the-transcendental-deduction.md)); Husserl's pure ego is a datum exactly as far as it is evidently co-given with each act, every further doctrine bracketed. **1931:** the *Cartesian Meditations* (Sorbonne lectures, February 1929; French translation by Gabrielle Peiffer and Emmanuel Levinas, 1931; no German edition in Husserl's lifetime) restage Meditation II ([modern-philosophy 1.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/01-02-the-cogito-and-the-wax.md)) and fault Descartes for making the ego a thinking substance, a small piece of the world. The transcendental ego is not in the world; it is where the world's sense is given.

**Rejected.** *Heidegger* ([2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md)): Husserl brackets the world to reach the ego; Heidegger asks what way of being that ego has. *Sartre*, *The Transcendence of the Ego* (1936): there is no ego inside consciousness; the ego is an object in the world, constituted by reflection ([3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md), [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)

### Lifeworld

*Lebenswelt*: the world as pre-given in experience before any theory; the ground on which science is built and checked.

*Crisis* §9 (1936). Tables seen from one side, baths that feel warm, plates round "enough", days measured by the sun. Pre-given: always already there as the ground of any project, science included. It is the world of the [natural attitude](#natural-attitude), studied as the soil of meaning rather than bracketed. Science's results flow back into it. Dermot Moran notes the *Crisis* characterizes it in ways that do not fully cohere. It is **not** everyday opinion, folk physics or a worldview; it is the ground of evidence on which any of those is formed and checked.

**Context.** Lectures in Vienna (May 1935) and Prague (1935); Husserl, barred from official academic activity for his Jewish ancestry, published Parts I-II in *Philosophia* (Belgrade, 1936); the whole text appeared in 1954 (Husserliana VI, ed. Walter Biemel). He died 27 April 1938. The book is paraphrased only. Skeleton: [crisis argument](#crisis-argument). Rival description of the pre-theoretical world: Heidegger's [ready-to-hand](#ready-to-hand-and-present-at-hand).

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md)

### Idealization and limit shapes

Exactness is not found in things; it is achieved by a method.

*Crisis* §9. In the lifeworld, straightness and roundness come in degrees and tolerances. Measuring arts keep perfecting them (a truer straightedge, a finer lathe), and each improvement points toward a limit no improvement reaches: the ideal line, plane, circle, **limit shapes** (*Limesgestalten*). Pure geometry is their science. Not **abstraction**: abstraction from tabletops gives vague roundness; the circle is *constructed* as the limit of a series of perfectings, an achievement with a history ("The Origin of Geometry": ideal sense handed down in writing, reactivated or sedimented).

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md)

### Mathematization of nature

Galileo's step: nature as a whole is a mathematical manifold.

*Crisis* §9. Galileo inherited geometry as a finished tradition and applied it to all of nature. Shapes and motions are mathematized directly; sensory *plena* (colour, sound, warmth) only indirectly, by correlation with shapes and motions, which is the root of their demotion to "merely subjective" (Locke's version: [modern-philosophy 3.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/03-02-locke-qualities-substance-and-essence.md); Berkeley's objection, which *Ideas* I §40 invokes: [modern-philosophy 3.4](../modern-philosophy/lessons/03-04-berkeley-immaterialism.md)). Then algebra, formulae and technique: the method runs without anyone reactivating its sense. Husserl's "Galileo" is the type of a tradition.

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md)

### Garb of ideas

*Ideenkleid*: the mathematical idealities thrown over the lifeworld, leading us to take for true being what is a method.

*Crisis* §9. The constructed world of idealities is slipped in for the only world actually given in perception. Galileo is a genius who both discovers and conceals. The earlier version of the point: *Ideas* I §52 (1913) calls it mythology to set up physical nature, a higher-level correlate worked out from the very thing the physicist weighs and heats, as an unknown world of things in themselves.

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md)

### Objectivism

Husserl's name for the self-interpretation on which the method's product is reality and the lifeworld mere subjective appearance.

The target of the *Crisis*, not physics' results. It inverts the order of founding, treating what is founded as the foundation. The cure is reflection on origins, not less physics. The complaint is the reverse of relativism: Husserl wants science's sense secured by tracing it to evidence anyone can check. Sellars's *scientia mensura* (1956) states the view Husserl rejects.

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md). The live realism debate: [philosophy-of-science syllabus](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md) Module 5; [philosophy-of-physics syllabus](../philosophy-of-physics/syllabus.md).

### Argument against psychologism

*Prolegomena* (1900) §§21-23, 36.

1. Psychologism: the foundations of logic lie in psychology, a science of mental fact.
2. Psychology's laws are vague generalizations, justified by induction, at best probable.
3. Logical laws are exact and known a priori.
4. No logical law implies a matter of fact, not even that judgments exist.
5. **C:** Logical laws are not psychological laws.

Rider (§36): relativizing truth to a species's constitution makes one proposition true for us and false for others, against the sense of "true"; truth is identically one whoever grasps it. The calculating machine (§22) separates obeying a law from being explained by it.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)

### Directedness without immanent objects

LU V §11 and the appendix to §§11 and 20.

6. An act's real parts are its sensations and intentional character; its object is not among them.
7. The act is described identically whether its object exists, is fictional or is impossible.
8. An inner image represents nothing by resemblance alone; only an act that means the thing through it makes it an image.
9. **C:** Directedness is a descriptive character of the act, and its intentional object is the object itself.

**Pressure point: 9.** It makes "of" look like a relation with one end missing (Twardowski: what is the Jupiter-presenting *about*, if it "is nothing"?). **Reply:** aboutness needs no second term; what an act is of is fixed by its matter. **Rejoinder:** that relocates the problem, since sameness of object across acts must lie in their contents, which is the [noema](#noesis-and-noema) debate.

*Lessons:* [1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)

### Argument for categorial intuition

LU VI §§40-45.

1. A meaning-intention is fulfilled when an intuition gives the same object as it was meant (§8).
2. When I see that this paper is white and say so, the whole meaning is fulfilled, form included (§40).
3. Sense perception cannot fulfil the form: I see the colour, not the being-coloured (§43).
4. Reflection on my own judging does not supply it either (§44).
5. **C:** Some non-sensuous act, founded on sense perception, gives the state of affairs (§45).

**Pressure point: 2.** The Kantian denies the form is *given* (see [categorial intuition](#categorial-intuition)).

*Lessons:* [1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)

### Route to transcendental idealism

*Ideas* I §§49, 55; the argument the Göttingen students fought over.

1. A real thing is given only through profiles in experiences that must keep agreeing; its existence is the correlate of ordered manifolds of experience (§§41-49).
2. Nothing in the essence of experience guarantees the agreement; experience could dissolve into irreconcilable conflict, so that there is no world (§49).
3. Then the stream of experience would be modified, not touched in its own existence (§49).
4. **C1:** Consciousness is absolute being: it *nulla re indiget ad existendum* (needs no thing to exist), while the world depends on actual consciousness (§49).
5. **C2:** Every real unity is a "unity of sense", presupposing sense-giving consciousness; an absolute reality is worth as much as a round square (§55).

Husserl adds at once: no Berkeleyan idealism; nothing is subtracted from the world's being, only a philosopher's absolutizing of it. **Pressure point: 3 to 4-5** ([Goettingen realist objection](#goettingen-realist-objection)).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

### Goettingen realist objection

The epoché promised to withhold every judgment about existence; *Ideas* I §§49 and 55 then issue one.

The Göttingen circle (Adolf Reinach, Hedwig Conrad-Martius, Edith Stein, later Roman Ingarden) had read the *Logical Investigations* as realist: phenomenology went to the things and described them. Against *Ideas* I: steps 4-5 of the [route to transcendental idealism](#route-to-transcendental-idealism) say the world's being depends on consciousness, a judgment the epoché forbade; and step 3 at most shows experience could go on without a world, not that the world's being *consists in* being experienced. **Husserl's reply in outline:** constituting is sense-giving, not creating; step 5 is a claim about what "real" means, read off the evidence; the natural attitude's realism is left whole. Stein carries the realist side into [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md) and [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md).

*Lessons:* [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Crisis argument

*Crisis* §9 (paraphrased) with *Ideas* I §52 as precursor.

1. Every exact concept of physics gets its sense from measuring practices on things in the lifeworld.
2. Exactness is reached by [idealization](#idealization-and-limit-shapes): limit shapes replace the open-ended perfecting of practice.
3. Idealization is a method, confirmed only by returning to lifeworld experience (readings, coincidences, outcomes: [fulfilment](#empty-and-fulfilled-intention)).
4. Mathematization, formalization and technique let the method run without reactivating 1-3.
5. Once 1-3 are forgotten, the method's product is taken for true being, the lifeworld for mere appearance.
6. **C:** Science's self-interpretation inverts the order of founding; the remedy is reflection on the lifeworld, not revision of physics' results.

**Pressure point: 4-5 read as history.** Ernan McMullin ("Galilean Idealization", 1985): Galileo idealized deliberately and controllably, and in *Two New Sciences* (1638) concedes that medium resistance and the earth's curvature spoil his parabolas. **Reply:** "Galileo" is a type, and the forgetting sits in philosophy's interpretation of science (Descartes, the primary-secondary split), not lab practice. That saves the argument by narrowing it; whether the narrower charge is still a *crisis* is open.

*Lessons:* [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md). Negative absolute temperature (warmth vs temperature): [stat-mech 1.4](../stat-mech/lessons/01-04-temperature-pressure-chemical-potential.md).

### Pseudonymity

Kierkegaard's books are signed by invented authors, and he asked that their words be attributed to them, not to him.

*Either/Or* (1843): "edited" by Victor Eremita, papers of the aesthete A and letters of Judge William. *Fear and Trembling* (1843): Johannes de silentio. *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844): Vigilius Haufniensis. *Stages on Life's Way* (1845): compiled by Hilarius Bookbinder. *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846): Johannes Climacus. *The Sickness unto Death* (1849): Anti-Climacus. The request is in a signed note at the end of the *Postscript*. The point is **indirect communication**. De silentio says repeatedly he cannot make the movement of faith he describes, and Climacus says he is not a Christian: read them as witnesses, not spokesmen. Quoting de silentio as "Kierkegaard's view" erases the distance the authorship keeps.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md)

### Stages of existence

Three ways of living, each displayed from inside; passages between them are chosen, not automatic.

- **Aesthetic:** life organized around the interesting and the immediate (pleasure, mood, possibility kept open); A's essays and the "Seducer's Diary". Its secret is boredom, and beneath that despair.
- **Ethical:** Judge William's demand to *choose*, and to choose oneself: take up one's history as one's own under universal duties (marriage, work, promises) that make a life continuous.
- **Religious:** a relation to God the ethical cannot contain. Climacus: **Religiousness A** (immanent: resignation, consciousness of guilt) and **Religiousness B** (Christianity, the paradox of the god in time).

No stage leads to the next by logic; the aesthete can stay an aesthete. "Three stages" as a ladder is an oversimplification: they are voices.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md)

### Anxiety Kierkegaard

Kierkegaard's anxiety (*Angest*): no definite object, unlike fear; the relation to one's own possibility, the bare *I can*.

Vigilius Haufniensis, *The Concept of Anxiety* (1844). Image: the "dizziness of freedom", vertigo before one's own open future. Anxiety attracts and repels at once; it is the psychological state out of which, on his account, sin comes, though it does not explain the fall, which happens as a leap. **Heidegger's footnote** to SZ §40 names Kierkegaard as having gone furthest in analysing anxiety, within the theological context of a "psychological" exposition of original sin; the same footnote names Augustine and Luther. Heidegger keeps the formal shape (no entity as object; discloses freedom and possibility) and sets the theological frame aside ([anxiety and fear](#anxiety-and-fear)). Sartre's anguish reworks it again ([anguish and abandonment](#anguish-and-abandonment)).

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md), [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Despair

*Fortvivlelse*: a misrelation in the self's relation to itself; a structure, not a mood, and possible without knowing it.

Anti-Climacus, *The Sickness unto Death* (1849). The self is a relation that relates itself to itself: a synthesis of finite and infinite, necessity and possibility, existing only in taking up that synthesis, and grounded in the power that established it. Forms: not knowing one has a self; **weakness** (not willing to be oneself); **defiance** (willing to be oneself on one's own terms). Cure: a self that wills to be itself while resting transparently in the power that established it.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md)

### Truth as subjectivity

Not *what* is believed but *how*: where an existing person's life is at stake, truth is passionate inwardness held fast under objective uncertainty.

Johannes Climacus, *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846). Whether this makes faith a leap beyond reason is assessed as fideism elsewhere: [philosophy-of-religion 5.4](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/05-04-fideism.md). Anti-Climacus's suspicion of defending Christianity at all: [apologetics-foundations 1.1](../apologetics-foundations/lessons/01-01-what-apologetics-is-and-what-it-isnt.md).

### Teleological suspension of the ethical

If the ethical (the universal) is highest, Abraham is a murderer; so either the single individual can stand higher than the universal, or there has never been faith.

*Fear and Trembling* (1843), Problema I. "The ethical" is a term of art: *det Almene*, close to Hegel's ethical life ([history-of-political-thought 6.1](../history-of-political-thought/lessons/06-01-hegel-freedom-realized-in-the-state.md)); *ophæve* is *aufheben* ([modern-philosophy 6.4](../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-04-hegel-dialectic-and-spirit.md)); the duty that holds for everyone echoes Kant's universal law ([ethics 2.2](../ethics/lessons/02-02-the-formula-of-universal-law.md)). Suspension does not exempt the believer from morality; it means the individual's highest relation is not to the universal. Skeleton: [Problema I dilemma](#problema-i-dilemma).

**Kant's objection** (*The Conflict of the Faculties*, 1798, Ak. VII 63n): Abraham should have answered that he is certain he must not kill his good son, and can never be certain the voice is God's; moral knowledge outranks claimed revelation. **Replies:** de silentio concedes the paradox is easily confused with *Anfægtelse* and demands marks to tell them apart (a warning against cheap faith); on a reading such as C. Stephen Evans's, what is suspended is social ethics (*Sittlichkeit*), not God-given morality. Whether that survives Kant's point about certainty is open. Sartre reads Abraham's voice in [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md).

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md)

### Knight of faith

Renounces the finite, like the knight of resignation, then receives it back "by virtue of the absurd".

*Fear and Trembling*, "Preliminary Expectoration". A youth loves a princess he can never have. The **knight of infinite resignation** gives her up wholly by his own strength, keeps the love as eternal, and finds peace in the pain; de silentio calls this a purely philosophical movement he can make. The **knight of faith** makes the same movement and one more: he believes he will have her in this life, since for God all things are possible. Outwardly he may look entirely ordinary. Faith is not the young girl's confident wish either, which never looks the impossibility in the eye.

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md)

### Problema I dilemma

*Fear and Trembling*, Problema I, in paraphrase.

1. Suppose the ethical is the universal and the highest: my task is to express myself in duties that hold for anyone; singularity asserted against them is sin or *Anfægtelse*.
2. The tragic hero stays inside the ethical: one ethical duty yields to a higher one, and everyone understands him.
3. Abraham has no higher ethical *telos*: he acts for God's sake and, identically, his own.
4. So if 1 holds, Abraham is a murderer, not the father of faith.
5. Unless faith is the paradox that the single individual as such is higher than the universal, in an absolute relation to the absolute.
6. **C:** Either there is a teleological suspension of the ethical, or Abraham is lost and there has never been faith.

De silentio claims to understand only that the second horn follows from 1. **Pressure point: 1 applied to a voice** (Kant; see [teleological suspension](#teleological-suspension-of-the-ethical)).

*Lessons:* [2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md)

### Question of Being

Not "which things exist?" but what it means *to be* at all.

SZ §§2-4. Asked of Dasein, the being that already understands Being vaguely and pre-ontologically, using "is" competently. First rule: Being is not itself a being ([ontological difference](#ontological-difference)). Method (§7): phenomenology names no subject matter, only a way of access, to let what shows itself be seen from itself as it shows itself, the old maxim "to the things themselves" ([1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md)); describing Dasein is interpretation (hermeneutics).

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md)

### Ontological difference

Being is not itself a being: you cannot explain Being by tracing beings back to another, bigger being.

SZ §2 states the distinction. The **phrase** "ontological difference" is readers' (and Heidegger's elsewhere), and does not occur in *Being and Time*. Levinas's later charge that ontology subordinates the other to Being: [ethics as first philosophy](#ethics-as-first-philosophy).

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md)

### Dasein

The entity for which, in its being, its own being is at issue: us, named by our way of being.

Ordinary German *Dasein* means "existence"; literally "being-there". SZ §4, restated in §9. Two features (§9): **existence** (*Existenz*), a term of art reserved for Dasein, to be its possibilities, its characteristics being "possible ways to be", not properties, its essence lying in its having-to-be; and **mineness** (*Jemeinigkeit*), in each case mine. Traditional *existentia* (actual occurrence) is renamed presence-at-hand, so "tables exist" in Heidegger's sense is a category mistake. Not "consciousness", "the subject" or "the human organism": swapping those in smuggles back what the analysis denies. Sartre takes over "existence" with a different meaning ([existence precedes essence](#existence-precedes-essence)).

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md)

### Being-in-the-world

Dasein's basic state: one unitary phenomenon, not a subject plus a world plus a relation.

SZ §12: the "in" is not water in a glass or a dress in a cupboard; Heidegger traces it to an old root meaning to dwell, to be familiar with. Against the worldless subject of Descartes (§§19-21), whose *sum* goes undetermined (§6); §43 reverses the cogito: first "I am", in the sense I am in a world. The break with Husserl: Husserl brackets the world to reach the [transcendental ego](#transcendental-ego); Heidegger asks what kind of being that ego has, and answers that the one who brackets is already in a world. Skeleton: [being-in-the-world argument](#being-in-the-world-argument). Descartes's worldless subject: [modern-philosophy 1.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/01-02-the-cogito-and-the-wax.md).

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md), [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Ready-to-hand and present-at-hand

Two ways things are: equipment in use, and mere objects with properties.

**Readiness-to-hand** (*Zuhandenheit*, §15): equipment (*Zeug*) is "in order to" (*um zu*), belongs to a referential whole (pen, ink, paper, desk, lamp, room), and **withdraws** in use; concern attends to the work. Using has its own sight, **circumspection** (*Umsicht*). The less we stare at the hammer and the more we use it, the more it is unveiled as what it is. **Presence-at-hand** (*Vorhandenheit*): mere occurrence with properties, shown in breakdown or bare looking (§§13, 16). §69: "the hammer is too heavy" (in use) vs "the hammer has weight" (a property). Not layering: the ready-to-hand is not a present-at-hand object with use-value added (§15 questions what "value-laden things" even means). Compare Husserl's [lifeworld](#lifeworld), a world of perception rather than use.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md)

### Broken equipment

When equipment fails, presence-at-hand and the world itself light up.

SZ §16. Unusable: **conspicuousness** (*Auffälligkeit*). Missing: what remains becomes **obtrusive** (*Aufdringlichkeit*). In the way: the blocked business becomes **obstinate** (*Aufsässigkeit*). Presence-at-hand *announces itself* but stays bound to readiness-to-hand. The reference lived in (from *this* to *that*) is broken and becomes explicit, and the whole workshop lights up as what we were always already in. The chain ends (§18) in a **for-the-sake-of-which**: hammering is for fastening, fastening for protection against bad weather, that for the sake of Dasein's sheltering, a possibility of Dasein, not another tool.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md)

### Being-in-the-world argument

SZ §§2-21, 43.

1. The meaning of Being must be asked of a being that already understands Being: Dasein (§§2-4).
2. Dasein's being is existence: its characteristics are ways to be, not properties (§9).
3. Phenomenology must let Dasein show itself in its average everydayness (§§7, 9).
4. In everyday dealings, entities show up first as equipment: ready-to-hand, withdrawn, in a referential whole, seen by circumspection (§15).
5. Presence-at-hand shows itself when dealings are disturbed, or when we hold back and merely look (§§13, 16).
6. The referential whole ends in a for-the-sake-of-which that is a possibility of Dasein; this whole is the world (§18).
7. **C:** Dasein is being-in-the-world; the worldless subject facing present-at-hand objects takes a derivative mode for the basic one (§§19-21, 43).

**Pressure point: 5 with 4** (is mindful regard derivative?). Hubert Dreyfus (*Being-in-the-World*; APA presidential address "Overcoming the Myth of the Mental", 2005) reads 4 as absorbed, non-conceptual skilful coping. John McDowell ("What Myth?", *Inquiry*, 2007): conceptual capacities are at work even in absorbed activity, so there is no mindless layer for theory to derive from. Dreyfus: expert coping shows no detached "I". **Heideggerian third move:** §15 says use "is not blind"; grant that coping is intelligent and deny its intelligence is the staring of premise 5. Whether circumspection is conceptual is live.

*Lessons:* [2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md). The computational theory of mind Dreyfus attacked, and extended mind: [philosophy-of-mind syllabus](../philosophy-of-mind/syllabus.md) 6.1, 4.3.

### Das Man

"The one", usually translated "the they": the anonymous everyone-and-no-one, me included, who lives everyday life.

SZ §27, from the pronoun *man* ("one doesn't do that"). Not a group of others set against me: we read and judge as *one* does, and even withdraw from the "great mass" as one withdraws. Everyone is the other, and no one is himself. Ways of being: **distantiality** (*Abständigkeit*, which §27 says belongs to being-with), **averageness**, **levelling down**, **publicness**, and **disburdening** of decision. §27: das Man is an existential, an original phenomenon of Dasein's positive constitution (and also a "dictatorship"); authentic selfhood is an existentiell modification of it, not a detached state. Its talk of death, **"one dies"** (*man stirbt*, §51), makes death a public event befalling no one in particular and "not yet" ([being-toward-death](#being-toward-death)).

**Readings divide** (*Inquiry* 1994-95). Frederick Olafson: a distorted mode of being-with. Taylor Carman: the shared social norms that make everyday intelligibility possible. Dreyfus (1995): both grant that Heidegger's characterizations are inconsistent. **Textual reply:** §27 distinguishes das Man (the public norms, which never go away) from the **Man-self** (the self that let them choose for it). Sartre's foil: his "crew" image for *Mitsein* against §27's distantiality ([3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md). Social facts, which are external to me as das Man is not: [social-theory 1.1](../social-theory/lessons/01-01-individuals-and-social-facts.md); Tocqueville on the majority's tyranny over opinion: [history-of-political-thought 6.3](../history-of-political-thought/lessons/06-03-tocqueville-tyranny-of-the-majority-and-soft-despotism.md).

### Idle talk, curiosity and ambiguity

The three marks of the they's disclosure of the world.

SZ §§35-37. **Idle talk** (*Gerede*): understanding everything without having made the matter one's own; §35 says the term is not meant disparagingly. **Curiosity** (*Neugier*): seeing only in order to have seen, leaping from the new to the newer. **Ambiguity** (*Zweideutigkeit*): no longer telling what was really understood from what only looks it. Together they make up [fallenness](#fallenness).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Fallenness

*Verfallen*: Dasein absorbed in its concerns and lost in the public way of seeing things.

SZ §38: the title expresses **no negative evaluation**; it is not a fall from a purer primal state (*Urstand*), not a defect a more advanced culture might cure, and makes no ontic claim about the corruption of human nature, leaving undecided whether we are in a state of sin, integrity or grace (though faith, speaking conceptually of Dasein, must come back to these structures). SEP notes the account draws on the Christian tradition and Kierkegaard. Fallen everydayness is a flight into the they's at-homeness from the uncanny ([anxiety and fear](#anxiety-and-fear)). One of care's three moments ([care](#care)).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Attunement

*Befindlichkeit*, "how one finds oneself": mood as a basic way Dasein is disclosed.

SZ §29, from *sich befinden*. A mood is not an inner state colouring neutral data; it comes neither from outside nor from inside but rises out of being-in-the-world itself, and discloses the bare "that it is and has to be" ([thrownness](#thrownness-and-projection)), whence and whither dark. Fear and anxiety are attunements. Scheler's rival: [intentional feeling](#feeling-and-preferring) grasps values.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Thrownness and projection

Delivered over to *that* I am; always already pressing into possibilities. "Thrown projection".

**Thrownness** (*Geworfenheit*, §29): the facticity of being delivered over to one's "that it is". **Projection** (*Entwurf*, §31): understanding as pressing ahead into ways one can be, not drawing up plans. Sartre's pair [facticity and transcendence](#facticity-and-transcendence) reworks it; Stein's late "security of being" is contrasted with thrownness ([finite and eternal being](#finite-and-eternal-being)).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Anxiety and fear

Fear is of something in the world; anxiety is in the face of being-in-the-world itself.

**Fear** (SZ §30): of a definite harmful entity within the world, approaching from some region, which might yet pass by; about oneself. **Anxiety** (*Angst*, §40): that in the face of which anxiety is anxious is being-in-the-world as such; nothing within the world is relevant, tools, plans and others sink into insignificance, and afterwards everyday speech says, truly, "it was really nothing". It is *unheimlich*, **not-at-home**; it **individualizes** Dasein before its ownmost potentiality-for-being. Fear is anxiety fallen into the world and hidden from itself. In [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md) anxiety is the mood that holds open the threat of death as ownmost possibility (§§50, 53), distinct from fear of demise. Source: [Kierkegaard](#anxiety-kierkegaard) (the §40 footnote). Skeleton: [care argument](#care-argument).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md)

### Care

*Sorge*: the being of Dasein, the unity of existence, facticity and fallenness.

SZ §41: being-ahead-of-itself-already-in-(the-world) as being-alongside (entities encountered within the world). Three moments, not three faculties or pieces: **existence** (ahead of itself, projection), **facticity** (already in a world, thrownness, attunement), **fallenness** (alongside entities, absorbed in das Man). Purely ontological: excludes worry and carefreeness, and gives practice no primacy over theory (contemplation is a mode of care). Forms: **concern** (*Besorgen*) for things, **solicitude** (*Fürsorge*) for others. Being ahead of itself raises the problem of grasping Dasein as a whole (§46); temporality is the meaning of care (§65).

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md), [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md)

### Care argument

SZ §§40-41: the unity of Dasein's being.

1. Fallen absorption in the they is a flight, and a flight is always in the face of something threatening.
2. What falling flees is not an entity within the world (falling turns *toward* entities).
3. Anxiety is the attunement whose in-the-face-of-which and about-which are both being-in-the-world itself.
4. Anxiety strips away the they's interpretation and individualizes Dasein before its ownmost potentiality-for-being.
5. What anxiety discloses at once is Dasein as thrown, projecting, and fled into the world.
6. These belong together originally, not as pieces of a composite.
7. **C:** The being of Dasein is care.

**Pressure point: the status of the they**, which 1 and 4 need (Olafson vs Carman; see [das Man](#das-man)). If das Man is a distortion it is no existential; if it is the norms of intelligibility, it is unclear what anxiety strips away.

*Lessons:* [2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md)

### Being-toward-death

*Sein zum Tode*: death not as the event of demise but as a possibility Dasein always is toward.

SZ §§46-53. **The problem of the whole** (§§46-47): care is always ahead of itself, so something is always outstanding; others' deaths will not do, since no one can take another's dying from him (§47), though one can die *for* another "in a definite matter". **Death's characters** (§52, the full formula; §53 restates them): Dasein's **ownmost**, **non-relational** (*unbezüglich*), **certain and as such indefinite**, **not to be outstripped** possibility. **The they's evasion** (§51): "one dies" makes death a public event befalling no one in particular and "not yet", and even "consoles" the dying. **Anticipation** (*Vorlaufen*, §53): not brooding, not expecting, not actualizing; holding death open *as possibility*. Because it is non-relational, anticipation **individualizes** (*vereinzelt*): being-alongside and being-with "fail" (*versagt*) when the ownmost ability-to-be is at issue, yet are not cut off from authentic selfhood. Individualizing is a disclosure, not a making. §53 also quotes Nietzsche on growing too old for one's victories ([modern-philosophy 6.5](../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-05-nietzsche-perspectivism-and-genealogy.md)).

**The argument in outline** (the lesson gives the steps; the standard-form reconstruction is left to you): care requires a relation to the end; others' deaths cannot supply it; so death is a possibility with the §52 characters; "one dies" conceals them; anticipation holds them open and so individualizes. **Pressure point: from non-substitutability to individualizing.** Sartre (*Being and Nothingness*, 1943, Part Four ch. 1): no one can love for me either; death is not my possibility but the end of all my possibilities, an absurd fact that hands my life's meaning to others. **Reply:** §47 concedes substitution within tasks; the claim is that death is the one possibility concerning being-in-the-world as such and not to be outstripped. Epicurus (death is nothing to us): [ancient-medieval-philosophy 4.1](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/lessons/04-01-epicurus-atoms-the-swerve-and-death.md); Heidegger agrees it is never experienced and denies it is nothing.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)

### Authenticity

*Eigentlichkeit*, from *eigen*, "own": owning one's existence as one's own possibilities.

SZ §9: Dasein can be authentic because it can be "its own". **Not sincerity** or fidelity to an inner true self (there is none; Dasein is its possibilities). **Inauthenticity is not lesser being** or a moral verdict (§9): it can mark Dasein at its busiest and most engaged, and an authentic choice can be the very same choice, made as one's own. §27: authentic selfhood is an existentiell modification of [das Man](#das-man). Made possible by anticipation of death and the call of conscience ([resoluteness](#resoluteness)). **Contrast Sartre:** authenticity barely developed, an escape from [bad faith](#bad-faith) by recognizing freedom ([3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)); Beauvoir's "authentic love" as mutual recognition of two freedoms ([3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)). Wojtyła's [authentic attitudes](#authentic-and-inauthentic-attitudes) are a different notion.

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md), [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)

### Resoluteness

*Entschlossenheit*: hearing the call of conscience and taking over one's ownmost ability-to-be; what is resolved upon is fixed only by the resolution itself.

The call of conscience (§56) says nothing about the world; it calls the self out of das Man. Resoluteness (§60), built on *erschlossen*, "disclosed": no content is fixed in advance. It does not isolate: authentic being-with springs from it, and it frees others for their own possibilities (§60). United with anticipation as **anticipatory resoluteness** (§62); their meaning is temporality (§65). The 1933 debate turns on the empty content: Löwith's charge ([Heidegger and 1933](#heidegger-and-1933)).

*Lessons:* [2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md)

### Heidegger and 1933

Facts first, as the lesson states them (checked against the SEP and IEP entries); then the disputed readings.

**The record.**

- Elected rector of the University of Freiburg on 21 April 1933.
- Joined the NSDAP on 1 May 1933 (IEP gives 3 May); member until 1945.
- Rectoral address, "The Self-Assertion of the German University", 27 May 1933; as rector oversaw the university's alignment (*Gleichschaltung*) with the regime.
- Resigned the rectorate in April 1934.
- After the war: denazification hearings; barred from teaching by the French military government; the ban was lifted between 1949 and 1951 (sources date it differently); retired as emeritus.
- *Black Notebooks* (private notebooks from 1931 on) began appearing in 2014 in the collected works, ed. Peter Trawny; they contain antisemitic passages, including talk of "world Judaism" (*Weltjudentum*).

**The readings** (disputed).

### Existence precedes essence

There is no human nature fixed in advance: a human being first exists, then defines himself by what he does.

Sartre, *Existentialism Is a Humanism* (lecture, Paris, 29 October 1945; published 1946). The paper-knife is made from a concept, so its essence comes first; if God made us, we would be paper-knives too. Sartre's atheist premise: no designer, so no human nature. The thesis covers **human beings only**; it does not say anything is possible (facticity is real) and is not a claim about causal indeterminism ([metaphysics 6.4](../metaphysics/lessons/06-04-libertarianism-and-its-critics.md)).

**Its source and its critic.** SZ §9: the "essence" of Dasein lies in its existence, its characters being possible ways to be, not properties ([Dasein](#dasein)). Heidegger puts "essence" in scare quotes and asserts no order; Sartre adds an order in time and a chooser. Heidegger's "Letter on 'Humanism'" (written December 1946 to Jean Beaufret, published 1947): reversing a metaphysical statement leaves it metaphysical, so Sartre stays inside metaphysics and its forgetting of Being. **Crux with Wojtyła** ([5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)): the self determines itself in acting, but within a nature ([self-determination](#self-determination)). Opposite of the natural-law view: [ethics 4.4](../ethics/lessons/04-04-natural-law-aquinas.md); "existential Thomism" is a different thing ([thomistic-synthesis 6.3](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/06-03-strict-observance-and-existential-thomism.md)).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)

### Anguish and abandonment

Sartre's three moods of freedom, terms of art: anguish, abandonment, despair.

*Existentialism Is a Humanism*. **Anguish** (*angoisse*): in choosing for myself I set up an image of what a person should be, for everyone, with nothing outside me to guarantee it. *Being and Nothingness* Part One ch. 1 separates it from fear: on a path above a precipice, fear is of falling; anguish is vertigo before *myself*, since only a choice I must keep making stops me from jumping. **Abandonment** (*délaissement*): no God, no a priori values, so no excuses; "condemned to be free". **Despair:** count only on what depends on my will, acting without hope that the world will cooperate. **The student:** during the Occupation, his brother killed in 1940, torn between joining the Free French in England and staying as his mother's one consolation; charity, Kantian respect and "follow your feelings" each pull both ways; even choosing Sartre as adviser was a choice. Sartre's answer, paraphrased: you are free, so choose, that is, invent.

Compare Kierkegaard's [anxiety](#anxiety-kierkegaard) and Heidegger's [anxiety and fear](#anxiety-and-fear).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)

### In-itself and for-itself

The full, self-identical being of things vs consciousness, a "nothingness" never identical with what it is.

*Being and Nothingness* (1943). **Being-in-itself** (*être-en-soi*): full, opaque, simply what it is (an inkwell is an inkwell). **Being-for-itself** (*être-pour-soi*): consciousness, which, being always consciousness *of* something ([intentionality](#intentionality)), never coincides with itself; a gap, beyond what it is toward what it is not yet. *The Transcendence of the Ego* (1936) had removed the one thing that might fill the gap: no "I" inside consciousness; the ego is an object in the world built up by reflection ([transcendental ego](#transcendental-ego)). [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md) adds **being-for-others** as a third region beside these two ([being-for-others](#being-for-others)).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)

### Facticity and transcendence

The two sides of the for-itself: the given, and the going-beyond it.

**Facticity:** body, past, place, the facts about me. **Transcendence:** going beyond the given toward chosen possibilities. Facts resist but never decide: my past is mine, but I am not it as the inkwell is its ink. **The crag** (*Being and Nothingness* Part Four ch. 1): an obstacle only to someone who has chosen to climb it, a pleasant view to someone out walking; what facticity *means* comes from the project. Playing one side against the other is [bad faith](#bad-faith). Reworks Heidegger's [thrownness and projection](#thrownness-and-projection). The critic: Merleau-Ponty on situated freedom ([bad faith argument](#bad-faith-argument)).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)

### Bad faith

*Mauvaise foi*: lying to oneself about one's freedom by reading transcendence as facticity or facticity as transcendence.

*Being and Nothingness* Part One ch. 2. A lie to another needs two minds; how can one consciousness be deceiver and deceived? Sartre rejects Freud's censor (it must know what it represses, so the problem moves inside it). Answer: bad faith is a kind of **faith**, deciding in advance to be satisfied by evidence that would never persuade it. Cases, paraphrased:

- **Transcendence read as facticity** ("I'm just like this"): the **waiter**, a little too quick and precise, *playing at being* a waiter as the inkwell is an inkwell, though he could walk out tomorrow.
- **Facticity read as transcendence** ("that wasn't me"): the **woman on the date** leaves her hand in her companion's as if it were a thing, while talking of her life as pure mind, and so avoids deciding; the **man denying the pattern of his past** love affairs with men.
- **The champion of sincerity** demands that man declare himself that kind of man once and for all, asking him to *be* his past as a thing is what it is. Sincerity as an ideal of being what one is is itself bad faith.

Not lying or hypocrisy: it can coexist with complete sincerity. The exit, an undeveloped "authenticity" in a footnote, takes up facticity and transcendence together. In [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md), indifference to the look is a bad-faith flight from it. Skeleton: [bad faith argument](#bad-faith-argument).

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)

### Bad faith argument

*Being and Nothingness* Part One, in paraphrase.

1. The for-itself is consciousness of something and identical with no object it is conscious of, including its past and body.
2. So no fact about me fixes what I will do; I am always beyond facticity toward chosen possibilities.
3. The for-itself is pre-reflectively aware of this; that awareness is anguish.
4. To flee anguish, consciousness disguises one side of itself as the other.
5. Since one consciousness both lies and is lied to, the flight works only as faith, a resolve to accept unpersuasive evidence.
6. Sincerity, as the demand to be what one is, treats the for-itself as an in-itself: the same error.
7. **C:** Bad faith is a standing possibility of every consciousness; the escape is to take up facticity and transcendence together.

**Pressure point: 2, the totality of freedom.** Maurice Merleau-Ponty, *Phenomenology of Perception* (1945), closing chapter: freedom is always situated; habit, class, bodily style are sedimented and make some choices likely and others nearly impossible; a freedom present everywhere would make no difference anywhere. **Reply:** the crag (Part Four ch. 1): facticity resists, but its meaning comes from the project. Sartre's later "freedom-in-situation" reads either as concession or as refinement.

*Lessons:* [3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md)

### The look

*Le regard*: my being-seen, lived in shame or pride, not perceived; it needs no eyes.

*Being and Nothingness* Part Three, ch. 1, "The Look" (paraphrased; Sartre is copyrighted). **The keyhole:** a jealous man peering through a keyhole is wholly absorbed, pre-reflective, no "I" in the picture; footsteps in the hall, and all at once he *is* a voyeur, a self that arrived in **shame**. Shame shows three things: I am this object; I cannot see this object (it exists in the other's freedom: [being-for-others](#being-for-others)); the other is a **subject**, given without being perceived. A rustle of branches, a step then silence, a moving curtain can carry the look; when I am really looked at, the other's eyes recede. **The park:** a man crossing the lawn is seen as object; the world flows away from me toward him, but it is only *probable* he sees the lawn. **The false alarm:** if the hall is empty, I was wrong that someone is *there*, not that I am a being who can be seen. **Zahavi's reply:** that saves certainty only by making the looker an anonymous "someone", close to the a priori being-with Sartre faulted in Heidegger.

Shame here is **pre-moral** (at being an object at all; pride has the same structure). No proof of other minds: every proof (analogy, Husserl's constitution) founders on the reef of solipsism ([argument from analogy](#argument-from-analogy)). *Huis clos* (*No Exit*, 1944): "Hell is other people".

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md), [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md). The window scene and inferred minds: [modern-philosophy 1.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/01-02-the-cogito-and-the-wax.md).

### Being-for-others

*Être-pour-autrui*: the dimension of my being that exists for another freedom, an object-self I am but cannot see.

*Being and Nothingness* Part Three. A third region beside [in-itself and for-itself](#in-itself-and-for-itself): it gives me an outside. **Against Heidegger:** *Mitsein* fits a rowing crew, side by side, never face to face (Part Three ch. 1, "Husserl, Hegel, Heidegger"); for Sartre being-for-others precedes and founds being-with, and a felt "we" is derived, often arising when a third looks at us together (ch. 3). **But** SZ §27's opening ([das Man](#das-man)) already has rivalry (catching up, keeping down) as distantiality that "belongs to being-with", so it presupposes the structure Sartre calls derived. **Conflict** is the original meaning of being-for-others (Part Three ch. 3): [conflict argument](#conflict-argument). The master-bondsman struggle Sartre reworks: [modern-philosophy 6.4](../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-04-hegel-dialectic-and-spirit.md).

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)

### Conflict argument

*Being and Nothingness* Part Three, ch. 3, "Concrete Relations with Others".

1. I am a for-itself, not pre-reflectively an object to myself.
2. Under the look I acquire an object-self that is really mine, whose meaning another freedom fixes.
3. I can neither know nor control it, since it lives in his freedom.
4. I can grasp the other as subject (being looked at) or as object (looking at him), never both at once.
5. So my projects toward him either assimilate his freedom while it stays free (love; masochism) or make him an object (indifference, desire, sadism, hate).
6. The first is self-contradictory (I want his freedom to choose me as no free choice could); the second destroys the freedom that holds my being.
7. **C:** Conflict is the original meaning of being-for-others; love fails by its structure.

**Pressure point: 4.** Simone de Beauvoir, *The Ethics of Ambiguity* (1947): to will oneself free is to will others free; *The Second Sex* (1949): authentic love as mutual recognition of two freedoms. If subjects can meet as subjects, 5 is a false dilemma. **Reply:** Part Three describes relations before any change of fundamental project; Sartre's footnote allows an ethics of deliverance only after a "radical conversion". **Rejoinder:** a premise stated as ontology cannot be relaxed by an ethics. Marcel's and Levinas's answers: [presence argument](#presence-argument), [first philosophy argument](#first-philosophy-argument).

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)

### Broken world

Marcel's diagnosis: modern life confuses a person with her functions, and the need to *be* withers.

"On the Ontological Mystery" (published 1933 with the play *Le Monde cassé*, "The Broken World"). The metro clerk, treated by commuters as a token dispenser, whose life is organized as a timetable of functions, and who may come to see herself so (paraphrased). What withers is the *exigence ontologique*. Close kin to [das Man](#das-man), though Marcel diagnoses an age where Heidegger describes a structure of Dasein.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md)

### Problem and mystery

A problem lies wholly before me and anyone competent could take my place; a mystery involves the questioner, so the line between in-me and before-me fails.

Marcel. A *problème* is solved by technique, inquirer interchangeable. A *mystère* (the union of body and soul, evil, love, freedom) is a problem that "encroaches upon its own data". Not "the unknowable" or an unsolved puzzle: the unknown is a problem not yet solved; a mystery is a region where the problem posture is the wrong posture. The cause of a rare disease may be baffling and still a problem; "what do I owe my brother?", asked by his brother, is a mystery. **Degradation:** a mystery handled by technique becomes a problem, and you answer a different question. Not anti-science: problems are real and should be solved by technique; a marriage contains problems (money, logistics) inside a mystery. Skeleton: [presence argument](#presence-argument).

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md)

### Primary and secondary reflection

Primary reflection analyses experience into objects and functions; secondary reflection reflects on that analysis and recovers the unity it broke.

Marcel. Secondary reflection is recuperative, not anti-analytic: it argues, it just does not compute. Marcel distrusted formal proofs of God as the work of primary reflection, and offered his descriptions as phenomenology anyone can check.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md)

### Being and having

*Être et avoir* (1935): having is external, inventoried, disposable, and possesses the haver; being is participation.

What I have can be listed, disposed of, lost; but possessions possess, and an opinion held as a possession is defended like property. **My body** is the borderline: primary reflection treats it as an instrument I have, yet a body I merely had would be *a* body, not *my* body. Incarnation is neither ownership nor identity with an object. Parallel to problem and mystery: having is being degraded into possession. Contrast Wojtyła's [self-possession](#self-possession-and-self-governance), a structure of the acting person.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md)

### Availability

*Disponibilité*: being at the other's disposal, open, able to make room for her and be affected; its opposite is self-encumbrance, not a full schedule.

Marcel. *Indisponibilité*: encumbered with myself (pride, anxiety over what I have, my own agenda). Someone can listen attentively and still be unavailable, taking my words as data for her purposes; someone else can say almost nothing and be wholly **present**. *Présence* is the other given as a *thou*, not a case or my idea of her; it cannot be extracted or demanded, only given and received. A doctor with an open diary can be unavailable; a nurse with two minutes can be present. Marcel stands nearer Martin Buber (*I and Thou*, 1923: I-It vs I-Thou) than Sartre.

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md), [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### Creative fidelity

Fidelity is to the person, not to my feelings on the day of the vow nor to bare behaviour; kept by renewed availability, it creates.

How can I commit feelings I do not yet have? If the vow binds only behaviour, keeping it against the grain is **constancy**, which can become a lie to myself and the other. Fidelity is *créatrice*: it makes something that did not exist at the vow. *Du refus à l'invocation* (1940; English *Creative Fidelity*). **Hope** (*Homo Viator*, 1945): not optimism about an outcome but a refusal of despair, constituted through and for a "we". Marcel held unconditional fidelity and hope point toward an absolute Thou. The fideism lesson's appeal to marriage and vocation as unconditional commitments under uncertainty has this structure ([philosophy-of-religion 5.4](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/05-04-fideism.md)).

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md)

### Presence argument

Reconstructed from "On the Ontological Mystery" and *Being and Having* (paraphrase).

1. A problem's data lie wholly before the inquirer; any competent inquirer could take it up.
2. Some questions involve the questioner (my body, my love, evil that strikes me).
3. Treating such a question by technique changes it into another question.
4. The other, as I actually meet her, is such a reality: what she is to me depends partly on whether I am available.
5. An object is what can stand wholly before me; what cannot is given, if at all, as a presence.
6. **C1:** The other is fully given only as presence, to an available self; handling her as a case is a degradation.
7. A commitment to a presence cannot be to my present feelings or to bare behaviour.
8. **C2:** Fidelity is creative.

**Pressure point: 4-5.** The Sartrean: there is no third mode between objectifying and being objectified; "communion" is the project of love, which fails. **Reply:** Sartre's cases (shame, suspicion) are cases of unavailability, and shame presupposes I was already exposed to the other before any contest. **Second charge** (critics who read Marcel as mysticism): "mystery" shields claims from argument. **Reply:** premise 1 gives a test, interchangeability of the inquirer. Levinas's objection to the reciprocity of Marcel's "we": [asymmetry](#asymmetry).

*Lessons:* [3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md)

### Totality and infinity

Totality is the Same turning everything into cases of a whole; infinity is what overflows its idea, met in the face.

Levinas, *Totality and Infinity* (1961). To know is to grasp through a third term (concept, horizon, meaning, being) in which a thing shows up as a case; knowledge makes the strange familiar, the **Same** (*le Même*). Pushed to the limit it yields a totality with nothing outside. Preface: **war** is that order made visible, individuals counting only as forces in a whole. Levinas finds the habit from Plato to Heidegger. What escapes it is [the face](#the-face); its structure is the [idea of the infinite](#idea-of-the-infinite).

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### The face

*Le visage*: the other's self-expression, exceeding every idea of her; not the front of the head.

*Totality and Infinity* Section III, "Exteriority and the Face". Three marks: **expression, not form** (she is present in her own expression, can correct my reading; even silent, the face addresses me); **destitution and height** (naked and exposed, the stranger, the widow, the orphan; yet addressing me from above, as a teacher does); **command** (its "first word" forbids murder; the other is the one being I can *want* to kill, since annihilation is the only way to make her wholly mine; the face resists ethically, not by force). A voice, a hand, a shoulder can present a face; a perfect photograph does not, since an image fixes expression into form. Reading "face" as facial appearance is the standard misreading.

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### Idea of the infinite

The one idea whose object overflows it, not built from the finite. Levinas keeps the structure and drops the proof of God.

Descartes, Meditation III ([modern-philosophy 1.3](../modern-philosophy/lessons/01-03-god-clear-and-distinct-ideas-and-the-circle.md)): it is of the nature of the infinite not to be comprehended by the finite (Veitch, 1901). Levinas: in the face, I think more than I can think. The relation is **desire**, which unlike need is not satisfied but deepened. Premise 4 of the [first philosophy argument](#first-philosophy-argument).

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### Ethics as first philosophy

Ethics, the calling into question of my freedom by the other, precedes and conditions knowing and being.

Levinas. "Ethics" is a term of art: not a moral theory, no rules, but the relation in which the other's presence questions my spontaneity; I am responsible before I choose to be. A claim about **order**, not importance or a ranking of fields. **Against Heidegger** ("Is Ontology Fundamental?", 1951): Heidegger makes every relation to a being pass through the understanding of being; but I do not first understand the person I speak to and then address her. An ontology putting being before the existent person is a **"philosophy of power"** (cf. [ontological difference](#ontological-difference)). 1933 ([Heidegger and 1933](#heidegger-and-1933)) is the background of the charge, not its premise. Skeleton: [first philosophy argument](#first-philosophy-argument).

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### Asymmetry

I am responsible for the other without waiting for her to reciprocate; that is her affair.

Levinas, interviews published as *Ethics and Infinity* (1982). **First-personal:** it does not say others owe me nothing or that she has rights against me and I none against her; it says I cannot make my responsibility conditional on hers. **Equality returns with the third**, where I count as one among others; it is not the starting point. Against the reciprocity of Marcel's "we" ([creative fidelity](#creative-fidelity)) and against Kant's symmetrical dignity ([ethics 2.3](../ethics/lessons/02-03-humanity-autonomy-and-the-lie.md)).

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### The third party

*Le tiers*: the other's other, who forces me to compare incomparables; that comparison is justice.

Levinas. The other is never alone: in *Totality and Infinity* the third already looks at me through the other's eyes (Sec. III). With the third I must ask what each is owed: measure, law, institutions, the state. In *Otherwise than Being* (1974), the third is also where I learn I am an other for others, one among others. Justice is necessary, and remains answerable to the face it was invented to serve.

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### First philosophy argument

Levinas, *Totality and Infinity* and "Is Ontology Fundamental?" (paraphrase).

1. To comprehend a thing is to grasp it through a third term in which it appears as a case.
2. Ontology, Heidegger's included, makes every relation to a being pass through the understanding of being.
3. But my relation to another person is first one of address, and address is not comprehension.
4. What expresses itself exceeds every idea I form of it, as the infinite exceeds the idea of the infinite.
5. What exceeds my grasp cannot be possessed; I can only try to annihilate it, and the face forbids that, ethically.
6. That prohibition calls my freedom into question before I choose: this is "ethics".
7. **C:** Ethics precedes and conditions comprehension; ethics is first philosophy.

**Pressure point: 3-4.** Jacques Derrida, "Violence and Metaphysics" (1964): to identify the other as *other* and say so, Levinas must use the language of being and the Same, bringing back the totality; Husserl's *alter ego* (*Cartesian Meditations* V) respects otherness better, never claiming access to the other's own experience. **Reply:** *Otherwise than Being* (1974): the **Saying** (exposure of address) vs the **Said** (thematizable content); every Said betrays the Saying, so philosophy must keep unsaying. Whether that escapes the circle is argued.

*Lessons:* [3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)

### Material a priori

Essential connections given in intuition can hold among contents, so "a priori" does not mean "formal".

Scheler (with Husserl). That a colour cannot exist without extension is a content truth, not a formal one. Kant equated a priori with formal (whatever is necessary comes from reason's form, so any content is empirical); Scheler calls the identification a fundamental error of Kant's teaching (*Formalismus* Part I, ch. II). Values, with their ranks, are such contents. Kant's dilemma (formal and a priori, or material and hedonist): [ethics 2.2](../ethics/lessons/02-02-the-formula-of-universal-law.md).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md)

### Values and goods

A value is a quality given in feeling, independent of its bearer; a good is a "value-thing".

*Formalismus* Part I, ch. I.1. Like a colour, a value is given independently of the thing that bears it. A good (*Wertding*) is a thing that bears it: when a painting's colours fade, the thing survives but the good is destroyed. Goods come and go; the values and their order do not. A value is not whatever someone values: valuing (*Werthaltung*) can be blind or deluded, and the value is what it misses. Not the same as Wojtyła's "the person is a good" ([personalistic norm](#personalistic-norm)), where "good" is a being, not a Schelerian value-thing.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md)

### Feeling and preferring

Intentional feeling grasps a value-quality directly; preferring gives one value as higher; neither is choosing.

**Feeling** (*Fühlen*) vs **feeling-states** (*Gefühle*), *Formalismus* Part II, V.2. A pain can be suffered, endured or even enjoyed, and a state is linked to its cause only afterwards, by thought. Feeling the beauty of snowy mountains at sunset reaches its object directly: we feel a determinate value-quality, not *about* something. That is Husserl's intentionality ([intentionality](#intentionality)) extended to emotional life (LU V §15 had already separated feeling-sensations from feeling-acts). Anger wells up and grasps nothing; some evil must already be felt for it to arise. **Preferring** (*Vorziehen*): built on feeling, the act in which one value is given as higher. Not choosing: we choose between actions but prefer fine weather to foul. The rank is never deduced (Part I, II.B.3); it is grasped anew in preferring with an intuitive evidence (*Vorzugsevidenz*). Source passage (Part II, V.2, after Pascal's "the heart has its reasons"): a kind of experience closed to the understanding as colour is to hearing, giving objective objects and an order among them as exact as logic. Contrast Heidegger's [attunement](#attunement), which discloses thrownness, not values.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md)

### Hierarchy of values

Four modalities, lowest to highest: sensible, vital, spiritual, holy.

*Formalismus* Part I (II.B.5).

| Modality | Value pair | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sensible | pleasant / unpleasant | The **useful** is no rung: a consecutive value founded on the pleasant |
| Vital | noble / vulgar | Health, vigour, the felt rise and decline of life; welfare |
| Spiritual | beautiful / ugly; right / wrong; the value of pure knowledge of truth | Culture values; "right" is distinct from conformity to law; truth itself is not a value, knowing it is |
| Holy | holy / unholy | Given only in objects intended as absolute; consecutive values in cult and sacrament |

**Moral good is not a rung.** It appears "on the back" of realizing a higher value over a lower one, so it can never be what an act aims at. Four ranks, not five: SEP's list with "utility" as a rank is not supported by the 1913 text. Height is read off by the [criteria of value height](#criteria-of-value-height). Love moves along this order ([love as movement toward higher value](#love-as-movement-toward-higher-value)); a person's loves rank it rightly or wrongly ([ordo amoris](#ordo-amoris)); [ressentiment](#ressentiment) falsifies it. Wojtyła finds here a moral value that appears only in realizing other values ([Wojtyla on Scheler](#wojtyla-on-scheler)).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md)

### Criteria of value height

Five marks that seem to go with a higher value, offered as signs and perhaps reducible to one.

*Formalismus* Part I, II.B.3 (1913, pp. 491-500). A value seems higher the more it: **lasts** (duration); is **less extended and divisible**; is **founding, not founded** (the founding value is the higher: useful founded on pleasant, pleasant on a vital value such as health); gives **deeper satisfaction** in feeling it; is **less relative** to the kind of being that feels it (the pleasant exists only for sensing beings, the noble only for living ones). They are signs, not a deduction: rank is given in preferring. Nicolai Hartmann's rival law (*Ethik*, 1926): the higher values are the weaker, the lower the stronger, so height and strength diverge.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md)

### Scheler against formalism

Grant Kant that an ethics of goods or purposes fails and that "good" cannot be the will's content; deny that a priori means formal; then every ought rests on values, not the reverse.

**Granted:** goods and outcomes change, so a morality pegged to them would be empirical and relative; "good" appears on the back of the act. **Denied:** a priori = formal ([material a priori](#material-a-priori)). **Inversion:** "every ought is founded on values, whereas values are in no way founded on an ideal ought" (Part II, IV.2a, translation ours). Kant's *Critique of Practical Reason* (Analytic, ch. 2) holds the opposite, the paradox of method: good and evil are determined after and by means of the law. Each side calls the other's order the root error. Scheler also faults Kant for excluding love, which cannot be commanded, from moral worth ([ethics 2.3](../ethics/lessons/02-03-humanity-autonomy-and-the-lie.md)). Wojtyła answers both "apriorisms" ([5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md); [ideal ought and duty ought](#ideal-ought-and-duty-ought)). Skeleton: [formalism argument](#formalism-argument).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md). Kant's good will and Universal Law: [ethics 2.1](../ethics/lessons/02-01-the-good-will-and-acting-from-duty.md), [2.2](../ethics/lessons/02-02-the-formula-of-universal-law.md).

### Formalism argument

*Formalismus* Parts I-II (1913-16).

1. Kant: a material ethics must take its content from goods or purposes, so it is a posteriori and heteronomous; only a formal law is a priori.
2. "A priori" means given as an essential connection in intuition, not "contributed by reason's form".
3. Values are such contents: qualities distinct from bearers, given in intentional feeling, with relations of higher and lower given in preferring.
4. These relations hold whatever goods exist and whatever the organization of the feeling being.
5. Every ought rests on a value, not the reverse (Part II, IV.2a).
6. **C:** A non-formal ethics can be a priori and non-hedonistic; Kant's ethics of law inverts value and ought.

**Pressure point: 3, the evidence of feeling.** J. L. Mackie, *Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong* (1977), ch. 1: rankings vary, and ways of life explain the variation better than perception does; a special organ of value-perception is queer ([ethics 6.3](../ethics/lessons/06-03-error-theory.md)). **Scheler's built-in reply:** ages differ in their *dominant* ranking, not the order; error is value-blindness and value-delusion, as optical illusion is explained without denying sight; love widens what a person can feel. **Rejoinder:** a theory that redescribes every dissent as blindness is unfalsifiable. Moral intuition and disagreement: [ethics 6.6](../ethics/lessons/06-06-moral-knowledge-and-disagreement.md).

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md)

### Ressentiment

A lasting self-poisoning attitude, born of repressed revenge, envy and spite under a sense of impotence, that ends in value-delusions.

Scheler's 1912 essay, revised as "Das Ressentiment im Aufbau der Moralen" in *Vom Umsturz der Werte* (1923 printing; its phenomenology-and-sociology section was newly written for that collection). **Value-delusion** (*Werttäuschung*): the true values are still felt but overlaid, shining through "transparently"; not blindness, not error, not lying. **Sour grapes is only a precursor:** the fox says the grapes are sour, not that sweetness is bad; ressentiment proper falsifies the values themselves. **With Nietzsche** (*Genealogy* I; [modern-philosophy 6.5](../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-05-nietzsche-perspectivism-and-genealogy.md)): Scheler accepts the mechanism and calls it the most profound modern discovery about moral judgment. **Against Nietzsche:** Christian love is not the "flower of ressentiment" (Scheler cites GM I §8). It does reverse the Greek direction (the noble stoops to the lowly), but from fullness and inner security, not impotence. Scheler locates ressentiment instead at the root of modern **bourgeois morality** and in a modern "love of humanity" that loves mankind to set it against something hated. Not ordinary resentment or envy.

*Lessons:* [4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md)

### Forms of fellow-feeling

Four different things "I feel your pain" can mean, built on vicarious understanding: two give the other as other, two do not.

Scheler, *Wesen und Formen der Sympathie* (1923; the 1913 book "more than doubled"; quoted from the unchanged 1926 printing), A.II.

| Form | German | What happens | Other given as other? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feeling-with-one-another | *Miteinanderfühlen* | One and the same feeling, neither's an object for the other: father and mother beside their dead child | Yes |
| Fellow-feeling | *Mitgefühl an etwas* | A second, reactive feeling directed at the other's (their friend C's pity); needs phenomenal ego-distance | Yes |
| Emotional contagion | *Gefühlsansteckung* | State to state, no knowledge of the other's feeling, involuntary, snowballing (the bar, a crowd) | No |
| Identification | *Einsfühlung* | Limit case of contagion: the other's whole self taken as mine; idiopathic (I absorb him) or heteropathic (I am absorbed); new in 1923 | No |

**The base: *Nachfühlen*** (vicarious feeling), understanding another's feeling at a distance, possible without sharing it: the cruel man grasps his victim's pain exactly and enjoys its rise (cruelty is vicarious feeling joined to the wrong response). Bodily pain cannot be co-felt: no "co-pain", only pity *at* another's pain. Scheler against Nietzsche (*Antichrist* 7): Nietzsche confuses pity with contagion. Founding laws (A.VI): identification founds vicarious feeling, which founds fellow-feeling, which founds love of humanity, then acosmistic love of persons and God. Every fellow-feeling is founded in love, never conversely.

**Neighbours.** Stein: *Mitfühlen* is my own primordial feeling about what moves the other, built on empathy; contagion (*Gefühlsübertragung*) grasps nothing of the other ([empathy](#empathy)). Wojtyła's *sympatia* is not Scheler's *Mitgefühl* ([sympathy and friendship](#sympathy-and-friendship)). The same English word "empathy" covers Lipps's *Einfühlung*, Scheler's *Nachfühlen* and Stein's *Einfühlung*.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Love as movement toward higher value

Love is not a feeling but a spontaneous act and movement, in which an object's higher value first appears.

Scheler, *Sympathie* (1926 printing, p. 176): love is the intentional movement in which, starting from a given value of an object, the appearance of its higher value is realized. Love is a **movement, not a function**: feeling is receptive and reactive and only for feeling beings; we love beauty, knowledge and God. It neither **creates** the higher value (that would be illusion, which comes from being stuck in one's own interests) nor **demands** that the beloved improve. It is not the most intense fellow-feeling; it founds fellow-feeling (A.XI). Love cannot be commanded. Wojtyła's rival account of love as a virtue of the will: [love as experience and love as virtue](#love-as-experience-and-love-as-virtue).

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Ordo amoris

The ordering of a person's loves, rightly or wrongly ranked against the hierarchy of values.

Scheler, unfinished essay published posthumously in 1933.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md)

### Person as act-center

The person is the concrete, essential unity of acts of different kinds, prior to all their differences; never an object.

Scheler, *Formalismus* (*Jahrbuch* II, 1916, pp. 255-60): the person is "the concrete, itself essential unity of being of acts of different essence" (translation ours, pp. 255-56). The person is what *does* perceiving, willing, feeling, loving. **Never an object** ("b) The being of the person is never object", pp. 259-60): the ego (*Ich*) is given in inner perception and so is an object; the person is given only in performing, or co-, re-, pre-performing (*Mit-, Nach-, Vorvollzug*), acts. Another person is given cognitively in understanding, morally in following, and as this irreplaceable individual only in love. **Answer to Hume's bundle** ([modern-philosophy 4.4](../modern-philosophy/lessons/04-04-the-self-belief-and-the-passions.md)): inner perception finds only the ego and its states; the person was the one looking. A claim about **givenness**, not a moral rule: reading it as "never treat a person as a thing" misreads *Gegenstand*. Skeleton: [person-is-never-an-object argument](#person-is-never-an-object-argument).

**Wojtyła's target** ([5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)): experience without [efficacy](#efficacy) and no persisting subject. The sympathy book's own phrase is "unity-substance" (*Einheitssubstanz*) of all acts (B.III). Levinas's other, not a theme of knowledge, is a parallel claim on different grounds ([3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md)).

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)

### Person-is-never-an-object argument

*Formalismus* (1916), pp. 246-60.

1. An act exists only in being performed; reflection can accompany it but does not make it an object.
2. The ego is still an object, given in inner perception with its states.
3. The person is the concrete unity of acts of every kind, prior to all their differences.
4. A unity of acts is given only as acts are given: in performing (for another person: co-, re-, pre-performing) them.
5. **C:** The person, one's own or another's, is never an object.

**Pressure point: 4, and the slide from "not an object" to "nothing but acts".** Wojtyła's habilitation: a person given only in performance of experienced acts is a centre of *experience*, with no grasp of the self as efficient cause and no subject persisting when not acting (sleep). **Reply:** "unity-substance" (*Einheitssubstanz*); the denial of objecthood blocks reifying the person as a thing, not its reality and endurance. Whether "substance" is more than a word is Module 5's question.

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md)

### Projection theory

Theodor Lipps (1851-1914): a seen gesture rouses me to inner imitation, which calls up the matching feeling, felt "in" the other; in full empathy I am one with the acrobat I watch.

**Scheler against Lipps:** we understand a dog's wagging joy that we cannot imitate, and imitation explains contagion, the *opposite* of understanding. **Stein against Lipps** (*Zum Problem der Einfühlung*, Part II §3): imitation gives me *my own* experience, roused by his gesture, never his; and I am "with" the acrobat, not one with him; Lipps confused self-forgetful absorption in an object with dissolving into it. Scheler cites Stein's acrobat critique. **Adam Smith's procedure** (§3): I push the other aside, surround myself with his situation and hand him the result; Stein calls it a substitute (*Surrogat*) for empathy, not experience. Live heir: Karsten Stueber's neo-Lippsian simulation theory (*Rediscovering Empathy*, 2006).

*Lessons:* [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md)

### Empathy

*Einfühlung* (Stein): an experiencing act *sui generis*, primordial as act, non-primordial in content, in which another's experience is given to me as his.

Edith Stein, *Zum Problem der Einfühlung* (doctoral examination at Freiburg under Husserl, 3 August 1916; printed Halle 1917 as Parts II-IV of a longer dissertation, Part I unprinted), Part II §2. Stein's opening case: a friend tells me his brother has died. **Not outer perception:** no change of angle brings the pain into view, as turning a die brings its hidden face. **Yet present:** neither remembered nor imagined. **A presentification** (*Vergegenwärtigung*) like memory, expectation, fantasy: present act, non-primordial content ([primordial and non-primordial](#primordial-and-non-primordial)); unlike memory, its subject is another I, joined to mine by no continuous stream. The sadness is "read off" the face, not inferred. Runs through [three levels](#three-levels-of-empathy); shows itself in the [lived body](#lived-body). **Not sympathy:** I can empathize a rival's glee without sharing it; sharing (*Mitfühlen*) is a further primordial act over what he rejoices at. **Can deceive:** crediting a bored listener with my delight in Beethoven; one look at his face, itself empathy, corrects me. Errors are unmasked by further acts of the same kind. Stein separates what empathy is from whether it is valid, and defers validity. **Against Scheler** (§6): she rejects his undifferentiated stream of experience prior to self and other; every experience belongs essentially to an I. She glosses Scheler's *Nachfühlen* as her *Einfühlung* ([4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md)).

**Husserl's later account** (*Cartesian Meditations* V, French 1931): pairing and appresentation, an "analogizing" grasp that is no inference by analogy. Stein, his assistant October 1916 to February 1918, worked on the *Ideas* II manuscripts and left her results unrevised. Skeleton: [empathy argument](#empathy-argument). Cited forward from [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md) (the other as the Göttingen realists' problem).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

### Primordial and non-primordial

*Originär* / *nicht-originär*: every present act is primordial as act; memory, fantasy, expectation and empathy give their content non-primordially.

Stein. Seeing the face of the die turned to me gives its content primordially; I remember *now* a joy given as *once* alive. Empathized content is primordial **for the other**, never for me. "Non-primordial" names a mode of givenness, not second-hand, inferred or less real: the content is given as present, not past or fantasized. Compare Husserl's [fulfilment](#empty-and-fulfilled-intention) and the hidden sides of a thing ([profiles and horizons](#profiles-and-horizons)).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md)

### Three levels of empathy

1 emergence (*Auftauchen*), 2 fulfilling explication (*erfüllende Explikation*), 3 comprehensive objectification (*zusammenfassende Vergegenständlichung*).

Stein, Part II §2c (the same levels hold for memory, expectation, fantasy). **1:** the experience arises before me all at once and stands over against me as an object (sadness read off a face). **2:** I follow out its implied tendencies; it draws me in, so that I am turned not toward it but, in it, toward *its* object, "with its subject, in its place". The filling is more empathy, never his pain lived as mine; not fusion (that is Lipps's error). **3:** the clarified experience faces me again as an object, as his. **Modes, not compulsory steps:** one often stops at a lower level. **Pressure point** (simulation theorists): "in his place" at level 2 sounds like re-enactment, so Lipps returns; Stein's defender separates being *with* the other from Smith's substitution ([projection theory](#projection-theory)).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md)

### Argument from analogy

Inference to other minds from bodily likeness: I know from my own case what links bodily changes and experiences, and infer the same link in others.

Stein names J. S. Mill (Part II §5d). **Stein's charge:** it ignores the phenomenon, as if we saw soulless, lifeless bodies; it presupposes the other already given as an I and his expression as expression; it yields only probable knowledge, not experience. Analogical inferences happen, but rest on empathy. **Sartre** rejects every proof of others, analogy and Husserl's constitution alike: all founder on the reef of solipsism ([the look](#the-look), [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)). **Husserl's** analogizing appresentation is explicitly not an inference ([empathy](#empathy)). Descartes's hats and cloaks are the picture all three reject ([modern-philosophy 1.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/01-02-the-cogito-and-the-wax.md)). Plantinga's parity between this argument and belief in God: [philosophy-of-religion 5.2](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/05-02-reformed-epistemology.md).

*Lessons:* [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md)

### Empathy argument

Stein, *Zum Problem der Einfühlung*, Part II.

1. Empathy is not outer perception.
2. Yet its object is present, here and now, itself.
3. Like memory, expectation and fantasy, empathy is primordial as act, non-primordial in content, with the same three levels.
4. Unlike them, the subject of the content is another I, joined to mine by no continuity of experience.
5. Imitation and contagion yield my own primordial experience, not the other's.
6. Inference by analogy yields at best probable knowledge and presupposes the other already given as an I.
7. **C:** Empathy is an experiencing act *sui generis*: a non-primordial experience in which another's primordial experience announces itself.

**Pressure point: 5 at level 2** (simulation theory; see [three levels of empathy](#three-levels-of-empathy)).

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md)

### Lived body

*Leib* vs *Körper*: the body felt from within, at the zero point of orientation, vs the body as a physical thing.

Stein, 1917, Part III (§4 "I and lived body", §5 the foreign individual). My *Leib* bears fields of sensation, sits at the **zero point of orientation** from which everything is near or far, moves freely, expresses my experiences and carries out my will. Another's hand on a table presses, slack or tensed, and I "see" the pressure as co-given, as hidden sides come with the seen side; my hand "as it were" moves into his place and senses non-primordially: **sensual empathy** (*Einempfindung*). The reversal: from the other's zero point, empathized, my own body appears as one body among others, so empathy is a condition of grasping myself as a psychophysical individual. In [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md) it is the first layer of [body, soul, spirit](#body-soul-spirit). Compare Marcel's "my body" ([being and having](#being-and-having)) and Wojtyła's [integration](#integration) of the soma.

*Lessons:* [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Personal core

*Kern*: an unchangeable core, the personal structure, that survives every imagined variation of a person's circumstances.

Stein 1917, Part IV, "Seele und Person" (pp. 122-125). Variation of character runs into limits: imagine Caesar in a village instead of Rome, or moved into the twentieth century; he would change, and remain Caesar. The psychophysical empirical person can be a more or less complete realization of the spiritual one; **non-unfolding is not non-existence**: the spiritual person exists even when not unfolded. Method: [eidetic variation](#eidetic-variation) run on a person, hitting an invariant that is individual, not general. The 1930s name for it is [individual form](#individual-form). Whether a person is present when no personal capacity is exercised: [metaphysics 6.5](../metaphysics/lessons/06-05-what-a-person-is.md).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Egocentric and theocentric philosophy

Stein's 1929 contrast: Husserl starts from consciousness, Aquinas from being and God; a starting point, not a moral fault.

"Husserl's Phenomenology and the Philosophy of St Thomas Aquinas", in the *Festschrift* for Husserl's seventieth birthday (1929); first drafted as a dialogue between the two men, reworked into prose; paraphrased. **Agreed:** philosophy is rigorous work of reason; truth is objective. **Four differences:** (1) Husserl builds objectivity from within consciousness after the reduction; Aquinas starts from being; (2) egocentric vs theocentric; (3) Aquinas admits faith as a source of truth natural reason may not ignore, Husserl does not; (4) Husserl's intuition of essences is not Aquinas's abstraction, though both say the intellect reaches real structures. **What each lacked, on her reading:** phenomenology could not get from how things are given to what they are, and its transcendental turn made the ego the measure (which Stein, like the Göttingen realists, never accepted); Thomism worked with concepts handed down rather than tested against experience and said little about consciousness from within. Her late project put the descriptions inside the metaphysics. Whether that is synthesis or juxtaposition returns in [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md) ([cosmological and personalist approaches](#cosmological-and-personalist-approaches)).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Finite and eternal being

The momentary, received, held being of the I points to eternal being, pure act.

*Endliches und ewiges Sein* (written in the Cologne Carmel, 1935-37 on the usual dating; reworks *Potency and Act*, 1931; unprintable under the regime; published 1950); subtitle: an attempt at an ascent to the meaning of being. Appendix on Heidegger's philosophy of existence. Paraphrase only. Skeleton: [ascent argument](#ascent-argument).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md). Aquinas on essence and *esse*: [ancient-medieval-philosophy 7.2](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/lessons/07-02-aquinas-essence-and-existence.md).

### Ascent argument

The spine of *Finite and Eternal Being*, reconstructed and paraphrased.

1. In reflection I am given to myself as being ("I am": Augustine, Descartes, Husserl).
2. My being is given as momentary: only the present phase is actual.
3. I do not give myself being; I find myself already existing, carried from moment to moment.
4. Yet I find myself held in being, not dropped into nothing at each instant.
5. What is received and held moment by moment depends on what has being wholly and from itself.
6. **C:** Finite being points to eternal being: being without becoming, pure act.

**Pressure point: 4.** Heidegger finds the facts of 2-3 as [thrownness](#thrownness-and-projection) and finds anxiety, not security; Stein asks where the thrownness comes from. **Heideggerian:** the security is a mood read as evidence, and "held" to "held by someone" smuggles the conclusion. **Stein's defender:** the mood is as much a datum as anxiety; and 5 does not rest on feeling alone, since Aquinas's essence/*esse* argument reaches the same lender, which is why she wanted both traditions. Cosmological arguments as such: [philosophy-of-religion 2.3](../philosophy-of-religion/lessons/02-03-cosmological-arguments-the-per-se-regress.md).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Body, soul, spirit

Stein's person: *Leib* (lived body), *Seele* (psyche; later also form of the body), *Geist* (open to meaning and value).

**1917:** the **lived body** ([lived body](#lived-body)); the **soul** as psyche, the lasting substrate under the stream of experience, with capacities, temperament, vigour that grow and tire, subject to causal laws; the **spirit**, which grasps sense, takes positions, is moved by reasons, under laws of meaning (*Sinngesetze*): a fever *causes* irritability, an insult *motivates* anger. Behind both, the [personal core](#personal-core). **1930s** (*Finite and Eternal Being*, paraphrased): the soul is now also the form of the body in Aquinas's sense ([ancient-medieval-philosophy 3.5](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/lessons/03-05-soul-and-intellect.md)) and an inner centre with depth; spirit names openness to the world, to itself and to God. Reading the 1917 *Seele* as the immortal soul collapses the layers. Wojtyła's soma and psyche: [integration](#integration).

*Lessons:* [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md)

### Individual form

Late Stein: each human being has an individual essence beyond the common nature; individuality lies on the side of form.

Drawing on Jean Hering: being Socrates is more than being human plus this matter. **Not Aquinas**, for whom members of a species share one specific form and are individuated by **signate matter**. **Not Scotus's haecceity**, a contentless individual difference that cannot be grasped in this life: Stein's individual essence has content, *how* this person is human, which empathy and love can catch ([ancient-medieval-philosophy 8.3](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/lessons/08-03-scotus-common-nature-and-haecceity.md)). The 1917 [core](#personal-core) become a metaphysical principle.

### Wojtyla on Scheler

Wojtyła's verdict: Scheler's system is unsuitable for a Christian ethics, because it has value-experience without efficacy or obligation; Scheler's method is usable.

**The text.** Habilitation accepted at Kraków in 1953 (not Lublin), published Lublin 1959 as *An Evaluation of the Possibility of Constructing a Christian Ethics on the Assumptions of Max Scheler's System*. **Two conclusions** (1959 ed., pp. 118 and 122, as Piotr Mazur reports them): (1) the system is, in Tadeusz Biesaga's rendering, "fundamentally unsuitable" for a scientific interpretation of Christian ethics; (2) its method helps study ethical facts from the side of experience. **One root, three shoots** (Biesaga): the root is that Christian ethics holds the person to be the agent of the moral good and evil of his acts, and the system cannot express it. **Emotionalism** (not subjectivism: Scheler is a value realist; the charge is moral life as reception), **no [efficacy](#efficacy)** (an act-center existing only in performing its acts cannot be their real cause; no subject persisting in sleep), **no original obligation** (Scheler derives every ought from value; Wojtyła takes the "I must" of conscience as a datum: [ideal ought and duty ought](#ideal-ought-and-duty-ought)). **The anti-Pharisee thesis** (*Formalismus* Part II, sec. V: whoever wills the good for the joy that clings to good willing no longer wills well): Wojtyła replies that wanting to be good is not vanity but what conscience asks. Not Kant reinstated: he answers both apriorisms.

**The argument in outline** (the standard-form reconstruction is left to you): a Christian ethics must express the person as agent of his acts' moral quality; full description finds efficacy and obligation among its data; Scheler's system confines moral experience to feeling and preferring, founds ought on value, and makes the person a unity of acts; so it cannot express them; but the method is not the source of the failure. **Pressure points:** a Schelerian denies description finds obligation as original (it is ideal ought plus resistance); any phenomenologist adds that "I am the real cause" goes past the given and is bracketed. **Reply:** method necessary but not sufficient, hence the join to Thomist metaphysics ([5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)); the norm absorbs the value and adds something new.

**Later corrections** ([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md)): the person is also an *object* of others' actions, against Scheler's "never an object" ([person as act-center](#person-as-act-center)). [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md) names the same charge from Scheler's side.

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md). Wojtyła's Roman teacher (Garrigou-Lagrange): [thomistic-synthesis 6.3](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/06-03-strict-observance-and-existential-thomism.md).

### Efficacy

*Sprawczość*: the experienced fact that I cause my act; not effectiveness.

In "man acts" I am given to myself as the cause of my action; in "something happens in me" I am the subject but not the cause (*The Acting Person* Part I, ch. 2). A failed rescue has full efficacy. Below action run **dynamisms**, somatic and psychic, occurring in the person; action is the one dynamism of which he is the efficient cause. Missing in Scheler ([Wojtyla on Scheler](#wojtyla-on-scheler)). A claim about who is *given* as cause, not about mechanism: it does not settle the free-will debate; a first-person cousin of agent-causal libertarianism, experienced where agent-causalists posit ([metaphysics 6.4](../metaphysics/lessons/06-04-libertarianism-and-its-critics.md)).

**The challenge.** Hume, *Enquiry* §7: no impression of power in willing to move a limb ([modern-philosophy 4.2](../modern-philosophy/lessons/04-02-causation-and-necessary-connection.md)); Daniel Wegner, *The Illusion of Conscious Will* (2002): the feeling of authorship is inferred from a thought preceding an act and can come apart from causing. **Reply:** Wojtyła's "experience" includes understanding, not a Humean impression; Reid derived active power from our own exertions ([modern-philosophy 4.5](../modern-philosophy/lessons/04-05-reid-common-sense-against-the-way-of-ideas.md)); Wegner's illusions are illusions only against normal cases where feeling and causing coincide.

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)

### Ideal ought and duty ought

Scheler: every ought is founded on value; the ideal ought ("injustice ought not to be") vs the ought of duty ("you ought not to do injustice").

*Formalismus* Part II, sec. IV (1916). The ideal ought is a value considered with respect to its possible real being; the duty-ought adds relation to a will that would realize it. Value does not consist in something's being what ought to be. Every imperative ethics, starting from duty as the most original moral phenomenon, is negative, critical and repressive; an imperative arises only where striving is not already directed at the value, so duty marks resistance. Direction of founding: value, then ideal ought, then duty-ought. Wojtyła reverses it: obligation is an original datum ([Wojtyla on Scheler](#wojtyla-on-scheler)).

*Lessons:* [5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md)

### Man acts and something happens

Wojtyła's contrast between acting and undergoing: the datum from which the person is read.

*The Acting Person* Part I. "Man acts" (*człowiek działa*) vs "something happens in man" (*coś dzieje się w człowieku*): you sneeze on a train; an hour later you decide to call the brother you have not forgiven. Both are yours and conscious; the sneeze occurs in you, the call you author. Scholastic labels: *actus humanus* (the person as author) vs *actus hominis* (the person as subject only). Wojtyła's claim is that the experience, not just the label, carries a philosophy. In [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md) the conformist is one in whom "something happens" rather than "man acts". Agent-causal parallel: [metaphysics 6.4](../metaphysics/lessons/06-04-libertarianism-and-its-critics.md).

### Personalistic norm

The person is a good that does not admit of use; the only adequate attitude toward a person is love.

Wojtyła, *Love and Responsibility* (1960), ch. I ("The Person and the Sexual Urge"), section "The Commandment to Love, and the Personalistic Norm". **Negative form:** in Willetts's rendering the person is a good that "does not admit of use", in either [sense](#two-senses-of-use). **Positive form:** toward a person the only "proper and adequate attitude" (Willetts) is love. Offered as the philosophical justification of the Gospel's commandment of love: the commandment says *love persons*, the norm says *why*. **Kant's Formula of Humanity restated as its floor:** whenever a person is the object of your action, do not treat her as merely a means, and allow that she has or should have ends of her own (SEP: nearly verbatim); not even God uses a person so. 5.3 supplies love's content ([elements of love](#elements-of-love)); participation is its social face ([5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)). Skeleton: [personalistic norm argument](#personalistic-norm-argument). Section titles cited from Willetts (1981); Ignatik (2013) renders terms differently.

**The norm vs the Formula of Humanity** is a named crux (see [the rival accounts at a glance](#the-rival-accounts-at-a-glance)). Not Scheler's "the person is never an object", which is about givenness.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md). The Formula of Humanity: [ethics 2.3](../ethics/lessons/02-03-humanity-autonomy-and-the-lie.md); the norm in the theology of the body: [moral-theology 6.1](../moral-theology/lessons/06-01-the-theology-of-the-body-and-chastity.md).

### Two senses of use

Polish *używać*: (1) use as means, subordinating someone to an end only the user sets; (2) use as enjoyment, making her the occasion of my pleasure.

LR ch. I, "Analysis of the Verb 'to Use'" and following sections. **Sense 1:** fine for a hammer, a violation for someone with ends of her own. **Sense 2:** pleasure normally accompanies action; making it the *end* of how I act toward a person reduces her to its source (central in the sexual sphere, but broader: anyone kept around for how he makes me feel). **The escape from sense 1** ("'Love' as the Opposite of 'Using'"): employer, officer, parent avoid use when both sides know an end, choose it, and subordinate themselves to it *together*; a shared good turns an instrument into a fellow worker. **Critique of utilitarianism:** if the good is pleasure, persons become means to it; two people agreeing to please each other reach only a harmony of egoisms. In [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md), desire unjoined to goodwill slides into sense 2. Not "rely on": the paid tutor, employee, soldier can all avoid use. Sartre's objectification is ontological, not this moral sense ([3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md)).

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md). Classical utilitarianism: [ethics 1.1](../ethics/lessons/01-01-classical-utilitarianism.md); separateness of persons: [ethics 1.3](../ethics/lessons/01-03-justice-and-the-separateness-of-persons.md).

### Incommunicability

The person is non-transferable: no one can will in her place; not "unable to communicate".

Scholastic *incommunicabilis* (5.3: *alteri incommunicabilis*, *sui iuris*). You can carry her bag, not do her choosing. It makes [betrothed love](#betrothed-love) a paradox: what cannot be owned or handed over is given. Belongs to the metaphysics of the person ([metaphysics 6.5](../metaphysics/lessons/06-05-what-a-person-is.md)), not to speech.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Self-determination

In every "I will" the person determines not only an outcome but himself.

**LR ch. I:** the person has an inner life, knows, wills and decides her own ends. **The Acting Person Part II** ("The transcendence of the person in the action", ch. 3 "The personal structure of self-determination"; *samostanowienie*): self-determination has an object, the self; the liar does not only say something false, he makes himself a liar; action is where a person becomes good or bad as a person (fulfilment, ch. 4). Presupposes [self-possession and self-governance](#self-possession-and-self-governance). **Freedom depends on truth:** not indifference; a decision answers to the truth about the good, and conscience binds me to what I have recognized; take truth away and you get a person moved by whatever happens in him ([transcendence of the person](#transcendence-of-the-person)). **Crux with Sartre:** "in choosing I make myself" is Sartre's too, but for Wojtyła within a nature ([existence precedes essence](#existence-precedes-essence)). The anthropology of *Veritatis Splendor*: [moral-theology 4.4](../moral-theology/lessons/04-04-veritatis-splendor.md).

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

### Elements of love

Love taken apart: attraction, desire, goodwill in the subject; reciprocity, sympathy to friendship, betrothed love between persons.

*Love and Responsibility* ch. II ("The Person and Love"), metaphysical analysis (then psychological and ethical analyses). **In the subject:** **attraction** (*upodobanie*, *amor complacentiae*): one person takes another as a good through some value seen in her; engages knowledge, emotion and already the will; danger of untruth (fastening on values she lacks, or on one value instead of the person). **Desire** (*pożądanie*, *amor concupiscentiae*): "you are a good for me"; belongs to love because humans are limited; not **concupiscence**, the sensual craving for an experience; let it rule and the other slides into [use](#two-senses-of-use) as enjoyment. **Goodwill** (*życzliwość*, *benevolentia*): "I want your good" for your sake; love in its most unconditional sense; desire must be joined to it, not abolished. **Between persons:** **reciprocity** (*wzajemność*; Aristotle, *NE* VIII): resting on utility or pleasure it breeds suspicion and jealousy; on goodwill toward a true good, trust. Then [sympathy and friendship](#sympathy-and-friendship) and [betrothed love](#betrothed-love). **Aquinas** (ST I-II q.26 a.4) divides only *amor concupiscentiae* and *amor amicitiae*; Wojtyła adapts *amor concupiscentiae* (in Aquinas, love of the good one wills to someone) to "wanting the person as a good for me". Skeleton: [anatomy of love argument](#anatomy-of-love-argument).

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Betrothed love

*Miłość oblubieńcza*: the gift not of something but of oneself.

From the biblical bride and bridegroom; not "being engaged". The paradox: the person is [incommunicable](#incommunicability), master of himself, cannot be owned or handed over; what the order of nature rules out, the order of love achieves, the free gift of self. **Law of ekstasis:** the giver is not diminished but finds fuller existence by going out of himself. Forms: **marital** (mutual self-gift) and **religious** (gift of self to God; LR ch. IV, "Justice towards the Creator"). Only one who possesses himself can give himself ([self-possession and self-governance](#self-possession-and-self-governance)). **Pressure point** (union accounts of love): Marilyn Friedman (1998) argues merging into a "we" threatens autonomy and proposes love as federation, not fusion; turned on Wojtyła (she does not discuss him): literal self-gift surrenders self-governance; metaphorical self-gift is friendship plus exclusivity. **Reply:** the gift is an act of self-determination, not its abdication, and the receiver gains no ownership. Whether that is a third option is open. The theology of the body: [moral-theology 6.1](../moral-theology/lessons/06-01-the-theology-of-the-body-and-chastity.md); the *Humanae Vitae* argument: [moral-theology 6.2](../moral-theology/lessons/06-02-humanae-vitae-the-argument.md).

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Sympathy and friendship

Wojtyła's *sympatia* is felt mutual affinity that happens to two people; friendship is the will committing to the other's good as one's own. Sympathy must ripen into friendship.

**Sympathy** (*sympatia*, "feeling-with"): emotional closeness, warm, passive, subjective, resting on feeling more than on knowing the person. **Friendship** (*przyjaźń*): the will plays the decisive part; I want your good as my own; you become "another I"; two I's become a "we". Friendship without sympathy's warmth turns cold. **Comradeship** sits beside them: closeness founded on shared work or circumstance. **Not Scheler's *Mitgefühl*:** Scheler's fellow-feeling is a feeling directed at *another's* feeling ([forms of fellow-feeling](#forms-of-fellow-feeling)); Wojtyła's *sympatia* is a mutual affinity preceding friendship; neither is everyday "pity".

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Love as experience and love as virtue

Felt love vs the will's commitment, which affirms the value of the person and accepts responsibility for her and for the love itself.

LR ch. II, ethical analysis: the book's title in miniature. The psychological analysis supplies the raw material: **sensuality** (*zmysłowość*) and **affectivity** (*uczuciowość*), neither love yet; both must be integrated (previewing [integration](#integration)). Love as virtue can be commanded, as any act of will can: Wojtyła's reply to Kant's objection that love as inclination cannot be ([personalistic norm argument](#personalistic-norm-argument)). **Contrast Scheler** ([love as movement toward higher value](#love-as-movement-toward-higher-value)): for Scheler choosing relates only to willing-to-do, never to objects, and love is not an act of will at all. Wojtyła keeps Scheler's value-seeing in attraction and puts love's apex in the will. Nygren (*Agape and Eros*, English 1953) and Frankfurt (love as volitional, 1999) are later neighbours.

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Mirroring and reflexive functions

Consciousness mirrors what the person knows and does, and reflexively lets him live his acts as his own; an aspect of the subject, not the subject.

*The Acting Person* Part I, ch. 1 ("The acting person in the aspect of consciousness"). **Mirroring:** reflects what the person knows and does, himself included, so his actions are lit from within; it does not itself know objects (knowing, self-knowledge included, is the intellect's work). **Reflexive:** turns the light back on the subject, so I *live* my action as mine. Making consciousness the whole person is idealism's error. Contrast Husserl's [transcendental ego](#transcendental-ego) and Sartre's ego-less [for-itself](#in-itself-and-for-itself).

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)

### Self-possession and self-governance

The structures self-determination presupposes: the person belongs to himself, and is both governor and governed.

*The Acting Person* Part II. **Self-possession:** the person belongs to himself; nobody else can do my willing. **Self-governance:** command over oneself. Only a being that possesses and governs itself can determine itself. Integration is their work on the dynamisms; disintegration their failure ([integration](#integration)). Only one who possesses himself can give himself ([betrothed love](#betrothed-love)). Contrast Marcel's *having* ([being and having](#being-and-having)).

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

### Transcendence of the person

Vertical transcendence (self-determination rising above one's dynamisms, bound to truth) vs horizontal transcendence (intentionality toward objects).

*The Acting Person* Part II. In self-determination the person rises above his own dynamisms and above the objects he chooses among: **vertical**. Consciousness reaching out to objects, Husserl's [intentionality](#intentionality), is **horizontal**. Vertical transcendence is bound to truth: freedom as self-determination dependent on truth. Its complement is [integration](#integration).

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)

### Integration

The person takes his somatic and psychic dynamisms *into* the act, so the action belongs to the whole man; the complement of transcendence.

*The Acting Person* Part III (ch. 5 "Integration and the soma"; ch. 6 "Personal integration and the psyche"). **Soma:** *reactivity*, the body responding to stimuli by itself (pulse, reflex, growth, rhythms), plus movement action can use. **Psyche:** *emotivity*, excitations, feelings and emotions, read as responses to values. Neither is the enemy: under self-possession and self-governance they serve the act (trained hands that obey, fear that sharpens attention). **Disintegration** is the failure of self-governance (panic, compulsion, a body out of control), not a defect of the body. Generalizes [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)'s demand that sensuality and affectivity be taken up into love. Whether integration needs a soul as form: [thomistic-synthesis 5.3](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/05-03-the-human-soul-in-the-system.md); embodiment in analytic form: [philosophy-of-mind syllabus](../philosophy-of-mind/syllabus.md).

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

### Participation

*Uczestnictwo*: acting "together with others" while still performing one's own act and fulfilling oneself in it, by choosing the common good as one's own.

*The Acting Person* Part IV, ch. 7 ("Intersubjectivity by participation"). Acting together is a fact about outcomes; participation is a property of the person in that fact. Denied by **individualism** (others a limit, community guarding my good) and **totalism** (individual subordinated, common good imposed). Shows itself in [four attitudes](#authentic-and-inauthentic-attitudes). **Neighbour** (participation in humanity) is deeper than **member** of this firm or nation, and the root of every other form; the commandment of love makes it explicit. The social face of the [personalistic norm](#personalistic-norm). **Not Thomist participation** in *esse* received from God ([thomistic-synthesis 2.3](../thomistic-synthesis/lessons/02-03-participation.md)), and not everyday "taking part" (taking part without conviction is conformism). Contrast Marcel's [availability](#availability) and Sartre's [conflict](#conflict-argument). Solidarity as a social principle: [catholic-social-teaching syllabus](../catholic-social-teaching/syllabus.md) 3.4.

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

### Authentic and inauthentic attitudes

Authentic: solidarity and opposition. Inauthentic: conformism and avoidance.

*The Acting Person* ch. 7.

| Attitude | Kind | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Solidarity | Authentic | Standing readiness to do one's own part for the common good, not taking over others' parts |
| Opposition | Authentic | Disagreeing with how the common good is understood or pursued, *for its sake*; wants more participation; needs dialogue and a community that allows it |
| Conformism | Inauthentic | Outward agreement without conviction, to avoid trouble; in him "something happens" rather than "man acts" |
| Avoidance | Inauthentic | Also rendered non-involvement or evasion: withdrawal from the community and its good; can be deliberate protest, but gives participation up |

The pairing of conformism as counterfeit solidarity and avoidance as counterfeit opposition is a common commentators' reading. **Not Heidegger's authenticity** ([authenticity](#authenticity)). **Conformism vs [das Man](#das-man):** das Man is a structure of everyday existence; conformism is an attitude measured against a common good, for which a person can be faulted.

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

### Cosmological and personalist approaches

Cosmological: the human being as rational animal, defined by genus and difference, one thing in the world. Personalist: subjectivity as lived, the irreducible. Complementary.

"Subjectivity and the Irreducible in Man" (English 1978, in *The Human Being in Action*, ed. A.-T. Tymieniecka; Polish *Podmiotowość i "to, co nieredukowalne" w człowieku*). The **cosmological** approach (Aristotle) explains the human being from what he shares with the world. The **personalist** approach attends to lived experience (*przeżycie*), the **irreducible**, which cannot be derived from cosmological terms and must be described as it shows itself. **Boethius's definition** (an individual substance of a rational nature: [metaphysics 6.5](../metaphysics/lessons/06-05-what-a-person-is.md)) marks out the ground of being on which subjectivity is realized; experience builds on it. Order of knowing: action first; order of being: *operari sequitur esse*. **"The Person: Subject and Community":** the *suppositum* is metaphysical in a "through-the-phenomenal" sense, given *in* the phenomena, not behind them like Kant's thing in itself. Wojtyła keeps the definition and denies only that it is enough.

**Attacked from both sides** (synthesis or juxtaposition? the card states the positions, not the verdict). **Thomist:** Mieczysław Krąpiec (review, *Analecta Cracoviensia*, 1973-74): an "aspect anthropology" for the ethicist's use, not an adequate philosophical anthropology; later commentators: the person's being is presupposed, not established. **Husserlian:** the *suppositum* is the kind of existence-thesis the [epoché](#epoche) suspends, so "through-the-phenomenal" smuggles metaphysics into description. **Defenders:** to the Thomist, Wojtyła never claimed to deduce the *suppositum*, only that experience gives a subject the definition names and misses in part; to the Husserlian, he never performed the transcendental reduction, and his experience of man includes the existence of the one acting, as the Göttingen realists ([Goettingen realist objection](#goettingen-realist-objection)) and Stein ([egocentric and theocentric philosophy](#egocentric-and-theocentric-philosophy)) insisted. *Fides et Ratio* §83 asks philosophy to pass "from phenomenon to foundation"; §59 names those who joined faith with phenomenological method ([fundamental-theology 2.4](../fundamental-theology/lessons/02-04-fides-et-ratio.md)). Skeleton: [bridge argument](#bridge-argument).

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

### Personalistic norm argument

*Love and Responsibility* ch. I, reconstructed in Wojtyła's terms.

1. A person has an inner life: she knows, wills, sets her own ends (self-determination), and no one can will for her (incommunicability).
2. To use (sense 1) is to subordinate a being to an end the user alone sets.
3. A being that sets its own ends cannot be so subordinated without violating what she is.
4. To use (sense 2) is to make a being's capacity to give pleasure the end of my action toward it.
5. **C1 (negative norm):** The person is a good that does not admit of use in either sense.
6. Persons rightly act on and with each other when jointly subordinated to a common good both know and choose.
7. The fitting response to a being is fixed by what it is; to a self-determining someone, it is to affirm her as a good in herself.
8. **C2 (positive norm):** The only adequate attitude toward a person is love.

Steps 1-5 track Kant closely, deliberately; 6-8 are Wojtyła's own (a positive account of cooperation without use; love, not only respect). **Pressure point: 8 and "only".** Kant, *Groundwork* I (Ak 4:399): love as inclination cannot be commanded, so the command to love an enemy means *practical* love (beneficence from duty), not *pathological* love (feeling); so the norm either commands a feeling or collapses into beneficence. A liberal adds: strangers owe respect and fairness, not love; "only" erases justice vs friendship. **Reply:** Wojtyła's love is an act of will, goodwill and affirmation of the person, which can be commanded, and it affirms the *person*, not the good done to her ([love as experience and love as virtue](#love-as-experience-and-love-as-virtue)). Whether "love" then does work "respect" doesn't is the dispute. **Utilitarian reply:** Mill's standard is the general happiness (*Utilitarianism* ch. 2), so "harmony of egoisms" hits only hedonistic egoism. **Rejoinder:** impartiality makes each person a site of pleasure to be summed, use spread across everyone.

*Lessons:* [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md)

### Anatomy of love argument

*Love and Responsibility* ch. II, reconstructed.

1. A person is someone, not something: an interior, self-determining subject.
2. The only adequate response to a person is love.
3. Attraction and desire take the other as a good *for me*; alone, they are compatible with use.
4. Goodwill wills the other's good for her sake: the element that makes love adequate to a person.
5. Reciprocity is stable only when it rests on goodwill toward a true good.
6. Sympathy happens to us; friendship is done by the will, and only what the will does can be answered for.
7. A person is incommunicable: no one can own or transfer him.
8. Yet goodwill can reach the point of giving oneself, which only a free, self-possessing subject can do.
9. **C:** Love's fullest form is betrothed love: a free, mutual gift of self in which the person is fulfilled, not lost.

**Pressure point: 8** (Friedman's federation model; see [betrothed love](#betrothed-love)).

*Lessons:* [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md)

### Action reveals the person argument

*The Acting Person*, Introduction and Parts I-II (paraphrased; cite by part and chapter).

1. The experience of man is a legitimate starting point: what is given in it is evidence about the person.
2. In it, "man acts" is given as different in kind from "something happens in man".
3. What marks acting is efficacy: I am given to myself as the cause of my action.
4. Every action is also self-determination.
5. Only a being that possesses and governs itself can determine itself.
6. **C:** The acting person is a self-possessing, self-governing subject that transcends its dynamisms: a someone, not a place where things happen.

**Pressure point: 3, from given to real** (Hume, Wegner; see [efficacy](#efficacy)). Wojtyła's "reduction" here is explanation, not Husserl's bracketing: the acting person's existence is affirmed from the start.

*Lessons:* [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md)

### Bridge argument

*The Acting Person* Parts III-IV and "Subjectivity and the Irreducible in Man" (paraphrased).

1. The experience of man gives the person as one who acts.
2. In acting, the person both transcends his dynamisms and integrates them.
3. Acting together keeps its personal value only by participation.
4. What is given across all three is a subject reducible neither to its dynamisms nor to the group: the irreducible.
5. That subject is given through the phenomena as the *suppositum*; Boethius's definition marks out its terrain, and *operari sequitur esse* links acting to its being.
6. **C:** Phenomenological description and Thomist metaphysics of the person are complementary.

**Pressure point: 5, from both sides** (see [cosmological and personalist approaches](#cosmological-and-personalist-approaches)). Whether the defenders answer both critics or only stop talking to one is the module's open question.

*Lessons:* [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md)

## Pitfalls

### Terms of art read in their everyday sense

- "Intentional" means directed, not deliberate; "inexistence" means existence-*in*, not non-existence; Husserl's "phenomenon" is the thing as given, not Kant's appearance or Hegel's stage of consciousness. *([1.1](lessons/01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md))*
- An "empty" intention is not vague (its matter is determinate; it lacks fullness); "intuition" is not a hunch; retention is not short-term memory. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md))*
- The lifeworld is not folk opinion or a worldview; idealization is not abstraction. *([1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md))*
- Kierkegaard's "the ethical" is *det Almene*, not morality as such; anxiety has no object and despair is a structure, not a feeling. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md))*
- "Dasein" is not consciousness or the organism; *Existenz* is not being actual ("tables exist" is a category mistake). *([2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md))*
- "The they" includes me; fallenness and idle talk carry no negative evaluation; care is not worry. *([2.3](lessons/02-03-the-they-mood-and-care.md))*
- "Bad faith" is not lying or hypocrisy; anguish is not fear of what will happen; the look is not seeing eyes. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md), [3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md))*
- A mystery is not the unknowable; *disponibilité* is not a free schedule. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md))*
- A Schelerian value is not whatever someone values; feeling is not having an emotion; preferring is not choosing; *ressentiment* is not ordinary resentment. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md))*
- "Non-primordial" is a mode of givenness, not "second-hand" or "less real"; the 1917 *Seele* is the psyche, not the immortal soul. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md), [4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md))*
- "Incommunicable" means non-transferable; "efficacy" means experienced causing, not effectiveness; "experience" in Wojtyła includes understanding; "participation" is neither taking part nor Thomist participation in *esse*. *([5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md), [5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md))*

### Epoche is not doubt

- The epoché is not negation, doubt, conjecture or supposing (§31): the general thesis stays, unaltered and unused; what is closed off is judgment about existence, not existence. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md))*
- Descartes's doubt adds a posited not-being; Husserl calls it really an attempt at universal negation and keeps only the suspension. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md))*
- Husserlian "reduction" is leading back, the opposite of reductionism; Wojtyła's "reduction" in *The Acting Person* is explanation paired with induction, and brackets nothing. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md), [5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md))*
- The epoché does not reject science or demand theory-free research (§32): it changes the theme, not the science. *([1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md))*
- "To the things themselves" is not a claim of access to Kant's things in itself; the Crisis is not relativism about physics. *([1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md), [1.4](lessons/01-04-the-lifeworld-and-the-crisis-of-the-sciences.md))*

### Authenticity is not sincerity

- *Eigentlichkeit* is owning one's existence; there is no inner true self to express, and inauthenticity is not lesser being or a moral verdict (§9). *([2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md))*
- Sartre goes further: sincerity as an ideal of being what one is is itself bad faith; his own "authenticity" is a footnote promise. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md))*
- Being-toward-death is not brooding, expecting or wishing for death; anticipation holds death as possibility. *([2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md))*
- Wojtyła's "authentic attitudes" (solidarity, opposition) are a different notion, measured against a common good; conformism is not das Man. *([5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md))*
- "Existence precedes essence" covers humans only, does not make anything possible (facticity is real), and is not a claim about indeterminism. *([3.1](lessons/03-01-sartre-existence-precedes-essence-and-bad-faith.md))*
- Heidegger did not welcome Sartre's slogan: the Letter on Humanism calls the reversal still metaphysical. *([3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md))*

### The 1933 question

- "The book is from 1927, so it is innocent" and "he joined the party, so the book is Nazi" both skip the premise linking the man's choice to the book's structures. Facts are graded strictly; the connection is argued. *([2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md))*
- Party membership: 1 May 1933 (IEP: 3 May); the teaching ban was lifted between 1949 and 1951 (sources differ): do not state one year as settled. *([2.4](lessons/02-04-being-toward-death-authenticity-and-1933.md))*

### Look, presence and face

- Shame under the look is pre-moral (at being an object at all), not guilt. *([3.2](lessons/03-02-sartre-the-look.md))*
- The face is not the visage: a voice, a hand, a shoulder can present a face; a photograph does not. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md))*
- "Ethics as first philosophy" is about order, not importance, and is not a moral theory. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md))*
- Asymmetry is first-personal: it does not deny that others owe me anything; equality returns with the third. *([3.4](lessons/03-04-levinas-the-face-of-the-other.md))*
- The problem/mystery split does not reject technique: a marriage contains problems inside a mystery. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md))*
- Marcel's presence is not physical nearness, and an attentive listener can still be unavailable: the other given as a thou, not as a case. *([3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md))*

### Empathy is not contagion or projection

- Scheler rejects *Einfühlung* (Lipps's projection) and uses *Nachfühlen*; Stein uses *Einfühlung* in her own sense, glossed as Scheler's *Nachfühlen*: one English "empathy", three technical terms. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md), [4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md))*
- Empathy is not sympathy: I can empathize a rival's glee; sharing (*Mitfühlen*) is a further act. *([4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md))*
- Contagion and identification do not give the other as other; in Scheler's test the question is whether the other's feeling stands before me as an object, not intensity. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md))*
- Scheler distinguishes four forms (feeling-with-one-another, fellow-feeling, contagion, identification) on the base of *Nachfühlen*, not three; the parents at the dead child are *Miteinanderfühlen*, not *Mitgefühl*. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md))*
- Stein's three levels are modes, often stopped at a lower one, and level 2 is not fusion (that is Lipps's error). *([4.3](lessons/04-03-stein-empathy.md))*
- Love is not intense fellow-feeling; it founds fellow-feeling, never the reverse. *([4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md))*

### Values and the person in Scheler

- Four ranks (sensible, vital, spiritual, holy), not five: the useful is a consecutive value of the pleasant; moral good is not a rung. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md))*
- The founding value is the higher (useful founded on pleasant, pleasant on vital), the reverse of the naive reading. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md))*
- Scheler accepts Nietzsche's mechanism of ressentiment; he rejects only the diagnosis of Christian love. Sour grapes is only a precursor. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md))*
- "The person is never an object" is a claim about givenness, not the moral rule "never treat a person as a thing". *([4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md))*
- "Emotionalism" does not mean Scheler made values subjective: he is a value realist. *([5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md))*

### The two senses of use in Wojtyla

- *Używać* covers use as means (subordinating to ends she does not share) and use as enjoyment (making her the source of my pleasure); neither is "any benefit from another". A common good both choose is the escape. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md))*
- The positive norm's "love" is an act of will, not a feeling; read as an emotion, the norm becomes Kant's objection to it. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md))*
- Wojtyła's *sympatia* is not Scheler's *Mitgefühl*; *amor concupiscentiae* is not concupiscence; "betrothed" is not "engaged". *([5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md))*
- Wojtyła rejected Scheler's system, not phenomenology; he holds against Scheler that a person can be an object of action. *([5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md), [5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md))*
- Efficacy is a claim about who is given as cause, not a solution to the free-will debate. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md))*
- Opposition is an authentic attitude; the inauthentic pair is conformism and avoidance. The personalist approach complements the cosmological one; it does not replace Boethius. *([5.5](lessons/05-05-integration-participation-and-the-bridge.md))*

### The synthesis in Stein

- Stein never accepted the transcendental turn and did not drop phenomenology in Carmel; she dropped idealism. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md))*
- Individual form is neither Aquinas's signate matter nor Scotus's contentless haecceity. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md))*
- "Egocentric" names a starting point, not a moral fault. *([4.4](lessons/04-04-stein-the-person-and-aquinas.md))*

### Attribution and dating

- *Love and Responsibility*'s 2013 translation is by Grzegorz Ignatik, not Grabowski; Willetts's is 1981. *([5.2](lessons/05-02-the-personalistic-norm.md), [5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md))*
- Wojtyła's habilitation was at Kraków (1953), published at Lublin (1959): not "a Lublin thesis". *([5.1](lessons/05-01-wojtyla-and-scheler.md))*
- *Osoba i czyn* is 1969 (English 1979); the 1962 date in one SEP section is wrong. *([5.4](lessons/05-04-action-reveals-the-person.md))*
- "Ontological difference" is not a phrase in *Being and Time*; the distinction is (§2). *([2.2](lessons/02-02-heidegger-dasein-and-being-in-the-world.md))*
- "Sachen selbst" is in the 1901 first edition of LU, not a 1913 addition; the *Cartesian Meditations* appeared first in French (1931). *([1.2](lessons/01-02-the-anatomy-of-an-experience.md), [1.3](lessons/01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md))*
- The transparency passage on ressentiment is 1923 text, not 1912; Scheler's sympathy book is quoted from the 1926 printing of the 1923 edition. *([4.1](lessons/04-01-scheler-values-given-in-feeling.md), [4.2](lessons/04-02-scheler-sympathy-love-and-the-person.md))*
- Quote the pseudonym, not "Kierkegaard": de silentio cannot make the movement of faith, and Climacus says he is not a Christian. *([2.1](lessons/02-01-kierkegaard-the-single-individual.md))*
- *Homo Viator* is 1945; Marcel accepted, then rejected, "Christian existentialist", later preferring "neo-Socratic". *([3.3](lessons/03-03-marcel-mystery-presence-and-fidelity.md))*
- Aquinas (ST I-II q.26 a.4) divides *amor concupiscentiae* and *amor amicitiae* only; *complacentiae* and *benevolentiae* are not that article's terms. *([5.3](lessons/05-03-the-anatomy-of-love.md))*
