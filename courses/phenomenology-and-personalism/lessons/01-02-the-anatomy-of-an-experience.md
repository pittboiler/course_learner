# Phenomenology & Personalism · Lesson 1.2: The anatomy of an experience

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 1: Husserl and the phenomenological method · Builds on: [1.1 Intentionality: from Brentano to Husserl](01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md), [modern-philosophy 5.3 Concepts and the categories](../../modern-philosophy/lessons/05-03-concepts-and-the-categories.md), [modern-philosophy 6.1 Transcendental idealism and the thing in itself](../../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-01-transcendental-idealism-and-the-thing-in-itself.md) · Unlocks: [1.3 The natural attitude, the epoché and the reductions](01-03-the-epoche-and-the-reductions.md)

## Why this matters

"Consciousness is always of something" ([1.1](01-01-intentionality-from-brentano-to-husserl.md)) is a slogan. This lesson turns it into an anatomy. What in an act fixes *which* object it reaches, and *as what*? What is the difference between meaning a thing and having it before you? Why is a thing always given from one side? Husserl's answers, in the *Logical Investigations* (1900–01) and *Ideas* I (1913), are the grammar every later thinker in this course writes in. They also yield his reply to Kant: truth is checked inside experience, not against a thing in itself behind it.

## The idea

A friend texts: "I left the blue mug on the kitchen table." Follow what your consciousness does with that mug.

**Quality and matter.** You can *believe* the mug is there, *wonder* whether it is, *wish* it were, or merely *imagine* it. The Fifth Investigation (§20) calls what varies here the act's **quality** (*Qualität*) and what stays fixed its **matter** (*Materie*). Husserl's own series: imagining, asserting, asking and wishing that there are intelligent beings on Mars. The matter is not simply the object. It is

> that peculiarity in the act's phenomenological content which determines *as what* the act apprehends its object, which features, forms and relations it ascribes to it. *(translation ours, from the 1901 German)*

So "the equilateral triangle" and "the equiangular triangle" share an object but differ in matter. Same matter, same object; not conversely ([act quality and matter](../reference.md#act-quality-and-matter)).

**Empty and fulfilled.** Reading the text, you mean the mug in words only: a *signitive*, or **empty**, intention. Walk into the kitchen and see it. The Sixth Investigation (§8) describes what happens as a distinctive experience of **fulfilment** (*Erfüllung*): the very object that was merely thought is now intuitively present, and present *as* what it was meant as. Intuition adds **fullness** (*Fülle*), not a new object. If the mug turns out red, you have **disappointment** (*Enttäuschung*), which §12 insists is a positive experience of conflict, not a mere absence. When what is meant and what is given agree fully, that agreement is lived through as **evidence** (*Evidenz*), and truth is that agreement (§39) ([empty and fulfilled intention](../reference.md#empty-and-fulfilled-intention)).

**Categorial intuition.** Now judge: *the mug is blue*. You see the mug, and you see blue. Do you see the *is*? The Sixth Investigation (§43) says no, and cites Kant's "being is not a real predicate" ([modern-philosophy 6.3](../../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-03-the-critique-of-the-proofs-of-god.md)) as meaning exactly this:

> I can see the colour, not the being-coloured [*Farbig-sein*]. I can feel the smoothness, but not the being-smooth. [...] Being is nothing in the object, no part of it. *(translation ours, from the 1901 German)*

Kant drew the conclusion that form comes from the understanding's own spontaneity ([5.3](../../modern-philosophy/lessons/05-03-concepts-and-the-categories.md)). Husserl draws a different one. The state of affairs *mug-is-blue* is itself given, in an act founded on the sense perception: **categorial intuition** (§45). "Intuition" is widened to cover any act that gives its object itself ([categorial intuition](../reference.md#categorial-intuition)).

**Profiles and horizons.** You see the front of the mug. Yet you perceive a *mug*, not a blue surface. In *Ideas* I §41 Husserl walks round a table: the perception changes continuously, while the table is one and the same. Each side appears in **profiles** (*Abschattungen*). The unseen back, inside and underside are co-intended emptily: the **inner horizon**. The table, kitchen and world around are the **outer horizon**. Every perception of a thing mixes fulfilled and empty intentions, and no perception exhausts it ([profiles and horizons](../reference.md#profiles-and-horizons)). That is not a defect of our senses. *Ideas* I §43 calls it an error of principle to think perception falls short of the thing itself, and absurd to suppose that even God perceives a spatial thing without appearances, or that perception gives a picture or sign in the thing's place. What we see is the thing itself, one-sidedly.

**Noesis and noema.** *Ideas* I §88 names both sides of every act: the **noesis**, the act's real components, and the **noema**, the perceived *as such*, exactly as it is meant (§89):

> The tree plain and simple [*der Baum schlechthin*] can burn down [...]. But the sense [*Sinn*] of this perception [...] cannot burn down; it has no chemical elements, no forces, no real properties. *(translation ours, from the 1913 German)*

What *is* the noema? Dagfinn Føllesdal (1969) read it on the model of Frege's sense: an abstract meaning *through which* the act reaches its object. Robert Sokolowski and John Drummond read it as the object itself considered as intended: one thing seen from two perspectives, not a second entity ([noesis and noema](../reference.md#noesis-and-noema)). The text supports parts of both, and the dispute is live.

**Time, named.** Hear a melody. At the third note the first two are not remembered; they are still held as just-past (**retention**), around a **primal impression** of the present note, with the next note anticipated (**protention**). Husserl's 1905 lectures on this were published in 1928, edited by Heidegger ([inner time-consciousness](../reference.md#inner-time-consciousness)).

**"To the things themselves."** The method's motto comes from the 1901 Introduction:

> We will by no means be content with "mere words", that is, with a merely symbolic understanding of words. [...] We want to go back to the "things themselves" [*Sachen selbst*]. *(translation ours, from the 1901 German)*

Read it against Kant's thing in itself ([6.1](../../modern-philosophy/lessons/06-01-transcendental-idealism-and-the-thing-in-itself.md)). A *Sache* is the matter at issue, as given in fulfilling intuition. The motto demands that empty meanings be cashed in experience; it does not claim access to what lies behind all appearing ([to the things themselves](../reference.md#to-the-things-themselves)). Whether that answers Kant or changes the subject is exactly what Kantians dispute.

## The argument

The load-bearing argument for categorial intuition (Sixth Investigation §§40–45):

1. **A meaning-intention is fulfilled when an intuition gives the same object, as it was meant** (§8). *In words:* knowing is the match between meaning and having.
2. **When I see that this paper is white and say so, the whole meaning is fulfilled, form included** (§40). *In words:* I see not just paper and white but the paper's *being* white.
3. **Sense perception cannot fulfil the form: I see the colour, not the being-coloured** (§43).
4. **Nor does reflection on my own judging supply it: reflection gives the concept *judgment*, not the *is*** (§44, against Locke).
5. **∴ Some non-sensuous act, founded on the sense perception, gives the state of affairs: categorial intuition** (§45). *In words:* intuition is wider than the senses.

**Where the argument is weakest.** Premise 2. A Kantian grants that the judgment is *correct* given what I see, and denies that its form is thereby *given*: the understanding contributes the *is*, and "fulfilment" of form is only correct judging on sensory grounds. Moritz Schlick (*Allgemeine Erkenntnislehre*, 1918) pressed a broader charge against intuition as knowledge: intuiting is acquaintance, and to call it knowing conflates the two. The defender replies that Husserl never equates knowing with acquaintance. Knowledge, for him, is precisely a synthesis between a meaning and what is given: in merely verbal understanding a meaning is enacted but nothing is known (§8). And categorial acts are not a separate faculty but are founded on ordinary seeing, so the claim is modest: the *is* is given only *on the basis of* the senses.

## Worked examples

**Example 1 (clean: a street sign, three times).** (i) *Perception.* Driving past, you see the sign for Elm Street. Quality: perceiving, a believing act. Matter: this sign *as* a green street sign reading "Elm St". Fulfilled: its facing profile. Empty: its back, the post's base, the street beyond (horizons). (ii) *Memory.* Next day you recall it. Quality: remembering. Same object, near-same matter. The sign is intuitively present again, but as past and not in person, so it cannot fulfil "the sign is there now". (iii) *Judgment.* "The sign has been turned to face the wrong street." This is a categorial act; its matter is a state of affairs. It is empty until you go back and *see that* the sign points at the wrong street. That seeing is categorial intuition founded on perception.

**Example 2 (hard: the mannequin).** In a shop you see a woman beckoning from beside the window. Quality: perception. Matter: a woman, beckoning. Empty: she will move, has a back, will speak. She does not move. The intentions are disappointed (§12): a live conflict between the perception of a woman and a perception of a dummy that partly overlaps it. Now you see a mannequin that *depicts* a woman. Husserl's own version is the waxwork lady on the stairs of a waxworks museum (Fifth Investigation §27). He notes that the same matter, *woman*, has moved from a perception into a picture-consciousness, and that no act can be both at once.

Here the account strains. What was the object of the first perception? There was no woman, yet §20 says the act is "directed" at its object whether that object is real or impossible. The Fregean reading handles this easily: the act had a sense (a noema) with no object. The object reading must say that "the woman as intended" was given only as intended. Each reading pays for the case somewhere, and 1.1's puzzle about non-existent objects returns.

## Watch out

- **You might think an "empty" intention is vague, but actually its matter is fully determinate.** The texted mug is meant as blue, on the table, in the kitchen. What it lacks is fullness (*Fülle*), not content. Likewise "intuition" is a term of art: not a hunch, but any act that gives its object itself.
- **You might think "the things themselves" means Kant's things in themselves, but actually it means the matter at issue as given.** In 1901 the motto concerns clarifying logical concepts by fulfilling intuition. *Ideas* I §43 denies there is a hidden thing in itself that perception fails to reach.
- **You might think retention is short-term memory, but actually it is part of the present act.** Remembering is a new act that brings a past back. Retention is the just-past still held in the perceiving, which is why you hear a melody and not one note at a time.

## One-liner

> Every act has a quality and a matter, means its object emptily or has it in person, gives a thing only in profiles against a horizon, and finds truth where what is meant and what is given coincide.

## Problems

**P1 (🟢) *(Exegetical.)*** Apply to a hard case. You are waiting for your friend Mara. (1) The doorbell rings. (2) A voice outside says "It's me", and you recognize it as Mara's. (3) Your phone buzzes with her text: "Here!" For each, give (a) the act quality, (b) the matter (the object, *as what*), and (c) one thing given intuitively and one intended emptily. One line per episode. (d) You open the door to Mara's sister, whose voice is like hers. In Husserl's term, what happens to the intention in (2)? One sentence.

**P2 (🟡) *(Exegetical.)*** Close reading. Husserl, *Ideas* I §41 *(translation ours, from the 1913 German)*:

> Let us start from an example. Continually seeing this table, meanwhile walking round it, changing my position in space in whatever way, I have continuously the consciousness of the bodily presence [*leibhaftiges Dasein*] of this one and the same table, which in itself remains entirely unchanged. But the perception of the table is one that constantly changes; it is a continuity of changing perceptions. [...] The same colour appears "in" continuous manifolds of colour-profiles [*Farbenabschattungen*]. [...] The profile, though it bears the same name, is in principle not of the same genus as what is profiled. The profile is a lived experience [*Erlebnis*]. [...] What is profiled, however, is in principle possible only as something spatial.

(a) What stays the same and what changes? Name the words that decide it. (b) The last three sentences block a specific misreading of *Abschattung*. Which one? Two sentences per part.

**P3 (🔴) *(Exegetical (a) · Evaluative (b).)*** Diagnose. An invented study note:

> "Husserl's noema is the picture of the tree in my head. When I look at a tree, what I actually see is the noema, and I infer the real tree behind it. That is why things come in profiles: the profiles are the sides of the mental picture. Føllesdal's Fregean reading proves the point, since a sense is a mental intermediary."

(a) Name three misreadings and correct each from this lesson. One or two sentences each. (b) Strip out the errors, and a fair worry remains: if the noema is a sense *through which* the act reaches the tree, does the Fregean reading put something between us and the tree after all? Any verdict; 100 words or fewer.

<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Exegetical, strict.)*

**Accept:** any matter that states the object *as what* it is meant; small variations in what you list as empty are fine if the item is genuinely unseen or unheard.

**Must hit, strict:**

- (1) Quality: perceiving (hearing), believing. Matter: the doorbell ringing, *as* a ring at your door. Given: the sound. Empty: who pressed it (you half-expect Mara), the person outside.
- (2) Quality: perceiving with recognition. Matter: the voice *as Mara's*, so Mara as speaking at the door. Given: the voice's sound and timbre. Empty: Mara herself, her face, her presence behind the door. Recognition is a fulfilling identification: what is heard fills "Mara's voice".
- (3) Quality: understanding a sign and believing it, a signitive act carrying a judgment. Matter: the state of affairs *that Mara has arrived*. Given: the marks on the screen. Empty: the arrival itself. The text means it but does not fulfil it.
- (d) Disappointment (*Enttäuschung*): a conflict between what was meant (Mara) and what is given (her sister). It is a positive experience of non-agreement, not a mere absence of fulfilment.

**Wrong turns:** treating (3) as fulfilling "Mara is here". Reading the words gives the sign intuitively, not the arrival. Giving the *sound* as the matter of (2). The matter is the voice as Mara's; the same sound heard as a stranger's would be an act with a different matter.

**Model answer:** (1) Hearing, believing; a ring at my door; the sound is given, the person outside is empty. (2) Hearing as recognizing; the voice as Mara's; the timbre is given, Mara herself is empty. (3) Understanding and believing a message; that Mara has arrived; the marks are given, the arrival is empty. (d) The intention is disappointed: the sister is given in conflict with the meant Mara.

---

**P2** *(Exegetical, strict.)*

**Must hit, strict (a):**

- The same: the table, "this one and the same table", which "in itself remains entirely unchanged".
- What changes: the perception, "a continuity of changing perceptions", and the profiles. The same colour appears "in" ever-changing manifolds of colour-profiles.

**Must hit, strict (b):**

- It blocks treating the profile as a part or surface of the thing: a little spatial picture, sense-datum or side-image that we see instead of the table.
- The profile is a lived experience (*Erlebnis*) belonging to the perceiving. What is profiled is spatial. They are "not of the same genus", so the profile cannot be a spatial thing in or before the mind.

**Wrong turns:** saying the colour itself changes as you walk. The text says the *same* colour appears in changing profiles. Reading "profile" as an aspect of the object, half real and half mental. Husserl's point is that it belongs wholly to the experience.

**Model answer:** (a) The table stays one and the same, "in itself entirely unchanged"; the perception and its colour-profiles change continuously, as a "continuity of changing perceptions". (b) The sentences stop us from taking the profile for a spatial thing, a picture or face of the table that we see in its place. A profile is a lived experience, and only what is profiled is spatial, so the two are "not of the same genus".

---

**P3** *(Exegetical (a) · Evaluative (b).)*

**Must hit, strict (a):** any three of:

- The noema is not a picture in the head. *Ideas* I §43 calls both the picture theory and the sign theory of perception absurd: no picture or sign is given in the thing's place.
- We do not see the noema and infer the tree. What we perceive is the tree itself; the noema is the tree *as perceived*, available in reflection (§88).
- Profiles are not sides of a mental picture. They are lived experiences in which the spatial thing's sides appear (§41); the thing has the sides.
- The noema has no real properties: the tree can burn down, its sense cannot (§89). A mental image would be a real, datable mental item.
- Føllesdal's sense is not "a mental intermediary". A Fregean sense is abstract, not a mental image, and the reading is one side of a live dispute, not a proof.

**Must hit, any verdict (b):**

- State the worry precisely: if the act reaches the tree *via* the noema, the noema sounds like a third thing between act and object.
- Say what the Fregean must hold: a sense is not *perceived*. The act is directed through it, as a word refers through its sense, so it does not block the object.
- Say what the object-reading offers instead (one thing, two perspectives) and what it costs (cases with no object, like the mannequin).

**Wrong turns:** answering (b) by repeating (a). The worry survives once the "mental picture" error is removed. Assuming the object-reading wins because it is "more direct". It must still say what an act with no object is directed at.

**Model answer (b), one of several:** Not necessarily. An intermediary blocks access only if it is what we perceive in place of the object, as a picture would be. A Fregean sense is not perceived at all; it is the way the act is directed, just as "the morning star" reaches Venus through its sense without our first looking at the sense. So the Fregean can keep perception direct. The worry bites only if "via" means a further object of awareness. The object-reading avoids even that appearance, but pays at the mannequin: it must say what was intended when nothing was there.
