# Theology of Debt — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 2 · ~26 lessons · Prereqs: [`old-testament`](../old-testament/syllabus.md), [`new-testament`](../new-testament/syllabus.md), [`moral-theology`](../moral-theology/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `theology-of-debt`

## Goal

This course reads debt as the Catholic tradition has read it, from inside the tradition and with the weight of each teaching marked. Scripture treats debt as law, as judgment and as hope. It later becomes one of the main ways the Church speaks about sin and redemption. And for eighteen centuries the Church condemned lending at interest, then worked out what that condemnation did and did not forbid. You will finish able to read the release laws of Leviticus and Deuteronomy and the prophets' oracles against creditors as theology, and follow the Jubilee from Leviticus through Daniel and Qumran to Jesus's sermon at Nazareth. You will be able to read "forgive us our debts", the debt parables and the bond nailed to the cross in Colossians 2:14 closely enough to name the Greek word each reading turns on. You will be able to explain how sin came to be spoken of as a debt, from the Second Temple period through the Fathers to Anselm's *Cur Deus Homo* and Aquinas on satisfaction, and state exactly what Trent defined about temporal punishment and indulgences and what it left at a lower level. You will be able to follow the usury doctrine from the Fathers and the medieval councils, through ST II-II q.78, the extrinsic titles, the Monti di Pietà and *Vix Pervenit*, to the nineteenth-century responses that allowed moderate interest, and argue whether that doctrine changed or developed. Finally, you will be able to apply modern Catholic teaching to international debt, to credit, and to a personal debt of your own. The course belongs to the **debt thread**. [`history-of-debt`](../history-of-debt/syllabus.md) supplies the episodes and institutions. [`philosophy-of-debt`](../philosophy-of-debt/syllabus.md) makes the secular moral arguments about interest, promising and forgiveness. [`economics-of-debt`](../economics-of-debt/syllabus.md) builds the models. This course supplies the doctrine. It deliberately skips atonement theory as systematic Christology, the sacrament of penance and the practice of indulgences, purgatory as doctrine, Catholic social teaching beyond debt and credit, rabbinic and Islamic law on interest, and the institutional history of lending (see the table).

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md). Every part declares its kind, and every solution is a rubric (must-hit moves, wrong turns) plus a model answer.

- **Close reading (archetype 7), exegetical and strict, is the workhorse.** Passages are 150 words or fewer, from public-domain texts only: scripture in the Douay–Rheims (Challoner), with the lesson's own translation of a Hebrew or Greek word when the word is the point (*neshek*, *deror*, *go'el*, *aphesis*, *opheilēma*, *cheirographon*, *lytron*); Aquinas (English Dominican Province); Anselm's *Cur Deus Homo* in Sidney Norton Deane's translation (1903); the Council of Nicaea's canons, Cyprian, Gregory of Nyssa, Gregory of Nazianzus, Augustine and Leo the Great in Schaff's *Ante-Nicene* and *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*; Luther's Ninety-Five Theses in the Philadelphia edition of his works (1915); the Apology of the Augsburg Confession in the *Triglot Concordia* (1921); Trent in Waterworth's translation (1848); and *Rerum Novarum* (1891). *Vix Pervenit* (1745) is read from its Latin in the lesson's own translation, never from a published modern translation. The medieval conciliar decrees, modern encyclicals, curial documents and modern scholars are paraphrased and cited by name.
- **Find the crux (archetype 5), exegetical.** Anselm's necessity against Aquinas's fittingness; the two readings of Luke 7:47; Noonan's "change" reading of the usury doctrine against the continuity readings; Luther against the treasury of the Church.
- **Apply to a hard case (archetype 3).** The workhorse of the usury modules. Given a contract (a late-payment penalty, a partnership with a merchant, a rent charge, a pawnshop fee, a credit card), say what Aquinas, the titles, Lateran V or *Vix Pervenit* would call it, and where the text stops giving a verdict.
- **Diagnose (archetype 4), exegetical.** Invented sermon paragraphs, op-eds and campaign leaflets that overstate what the Church teaches: "the Church changed its mind about usury", "the Pope has ordered all poor-country debts cancelled", "indulgences were sold forgiveness". Invented quotations are never attributed to real people.
- **Formal model (archetype 8), formal.** Light arithmetic where a text has arithmetic in it: pricing land by the years left to the Jubilee (Lev 25:15–16), a lender's incentive as the seventh year nears (Deut 15:9), the ratio of the two debts in Matthew 18, the rate implied by Nehemiah's "hundredth part", the triple contract shown to replicate a loan at interest, and a modern loan rate split into the parts the titles would allow. Each formal part cites [`economics-of-debt`](../economics-of-debt/syllabus.md) where a model stands behind the arithmetic.

**Authority is graded strictly.** Every doctrinal claim whose weight matters carries its level from [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4–4.5. Overstating a level is an exegetical error: calling the treasury of merit a defined dogma, or a Holy Office response a doctrinal definition. So is understating one, for example calling Trent's anathema on those who deny the Church's power to grant indulgences a pastoral opinion. **Evaluative** parts, verdict-neutral, are reserved for questions genuinely open among Catholics: whether the Jubilee was ever practiced, whether Luke 4 announces a social program, whether Anselm's argument needs its claim of necessity, whether the usury doctrine changed or developed, whether modern consumer interest is usury, and the prudential policy questions of debt relief. One **apologetic** part (Boss 3) states the Lutheran objection to indulgences as the Lutheran confessions state it before replying.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Close-read the lending and release laws (Exodus 22, Leviticus 25, Deuteronomy 15 and 23) and say what each forbids or releases, for whom, with which exceptions, and on what theological ground (the Exodus, the land as God's, the poor brother)
- [ ] Trace the Jubilee from Leviticus through the prophets, Daniel 9 and Qumran to Luke 4, and say what changes when a law of release becomes an eschatological hope
- [ ] Read "forgive us our debts", the debt parables and Colossians 2:14 with the Greek word each reading turns on, and say what the monetary sense adds to the theological one
- [ ] Explain how sin came to be spoken of as a debt in the Second Temple period, and how that language shaped almsgiving, ransom, satisfaction and redemption in the Fathers
- [ ] Reconstruct Anselm's argument from debt to the God-man in *Cur Deus Homo*, and name the crux between Anselm's necessity and Aquinas's fittingness
- [ ] State exactly what Trent defined about temporal punishment and indulgences, what the Church teaches about the treasury of the Church at a lower level, and what Luther objected to, marking each level
- [ ] Reconstruct Aquinas's argument against usury in ST II-II q.78 and apply its distinctions to a contract he never saw
- [ ] Explain the extrinsic titles (*damnum emergens*, *lucrum cessans*, *periculum sortis*, *poena conventionalis*) and the non-loan contracts (partnership, census, the triple contract), and show when a combination of permitted contracts replicates a loan at interest
- [ ] Say what Lateran V decided about the Monti di Pietà, what *Vix Pervenit* teaches, and what the nineteenth-century Roman responses permitted and pointedly did not declare, giving each its authority level
- [ ] Argue whether the usury doctrine changed or developed using Vincent's and Newman's tests, stating Noonan's position and a continuity position each at full strength
- [ ] State what modern papal and curial teaching asks about international debt, and separate its principles from its prudential applications
- [ ] Apply the tradition to a personal debt case (repayment when it hurts, bankruptcy, suretyship, a predatory loan), marking what binds and what is left to prudence

## Modules

### Module 1: Lending, release and judgment in the Old Testament

Israel's law treats a loan to a poor neighbor as charity, not commerce. It schedules release, grounds it in the Exodus and in God's ownership of the land, and the prophets judge the creditors who ignore it.

### Module 3: Sin as debt

### Module 4: Usury: the prohibition

From the Fathers' sermons to the scholastic analysis: what the Church condemned, on what authority and by what argument, and the distinctions that let some compensation on a loan be just.

## Notes

- **The debt thread.** This course is the thread's doctrinal member. [`history-of-debt`](../history-of-debt/syllabus.md) hands over the Jubilee as theology (from its 1.3), the usury doctrine behind the institutions of its 2.1–2.2, and the papal call of 2000 (from its 5.5). [`philosophy-of-debt`](../philosophy-of-debt/syllabus.md) takes Aristotle on money, the justice of interest, moral hazard and odious debt. [`economics-of-debt`](../economics-of-debt/syllabus.md) takes the Jubilee as a scheduled relief mechanism and the models behind the formal parts. The Jubilee read three ways (as law in [`history-of-debt`](../history-of-debt/syllabus.md) 1.3, as theology in 1.3 and 2.1 here, as a credible relief device in [`economics-of-debt`](../economics-of-debt/syllabus.md)) is the thread's showcase; 1.3's lesson should link both siblings. See [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md).
- **Prerequisite hand-offs.** [`old-testament`](../old-testament/syllabus.md) 2.6, 3.4 and 4.1 locate Deuteronomy 15, Nehemiah 5 and Amos's oracles and hand them here; composition and dating questions stay there. [`new-testament`](../new-testament/syllabus.md) 2.2 and 6.1 hand over the Lord's Prayer and Colossians 2:14; the textual and authorship questions stay there. [`moral-theology`](../moral-theology/syllabus.md) supplies commutative justice, cooperation in evil and the manual tradition on restitution, used in 4.3 and 6.3 without being retaught. [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) names this course as the owner of usury as a test case for development (its Scope table and Notes); 5.4 is that case.
- **Authority discipline for this course's hardest claims.** (1) That Christ made satisfaction for us is taught by Trent (Session VI, ch. 7). Anselm's claim that satisfaction was *necessary* is not. Aquinas denied it, and the question is freely disputed. (2) That the Church has the power to grant indulgences and that they are useful is defined (Session XXV, with anathema), as is the survival of temporal punishment after forgiveness (Session VI, canon 30). The treasury of the Church as the explanation is authentic papal teaching (*Unigenitus*, *Indulgentiarum Doctrina*, the Catechism). The manuals graded it variously, and lessons do not call it defined. (3) The Council of Vienne treated the assertion that usury is not sinful as heresy. What "usury" denotes in that decree (profit from a loan by reason of the loan itself) is exactly what 5.4 argues about, so lessons state the decree's weight and do not settle its scope by fiat. (4) *Vix Pervenit* was addressed to the bishops of Italy. How much wider its authority reaches is argued, and lessons present that argument rather than assert an answer. (5) The nineteenth-century responses were pastoral directives to confessors, not doctrinal definitions, and they said so. (6) Modern teaching on international debt states principles at the level of the ordinary magisterium and makes proposals whose application is prudential. Grading holds lessons and answers to these distinctions.
- **Accuracy: verify before writing each lesson; none of these should be asserted from memory.** The canon numbers of Lateran II and Lyons II and the constitution of Vienne on usury (named above without numbers); the exact dates and addressees of the nineteenth-century responses (given only as a range); DH numbers for *Unigenitus*, *Cum postquam*, *Inter multiplices* and *Vix Pervenit*; the years of the census bulls of Martin V and Callixtus III; the section numbers of *Rerum Novarum*, *Centesimus Annus*, *Tertio Millennio Adveniente*, *Caritas in Veritate* and *Laudato Si'* (named here without them); the CCC ranges given above (1471–1479, 1476–1477, 2401–2463, 2838–2845); the chapter numbers cited from *Cur Deus Homo*; whether the Didache's petition uses a debt word; and the Douay verse numbering for Sirach, which differs from modern Bibles. Basil's and Gregory of Nyssa's sermons on usury and Ambrose's *On Tobias* have no public-domain English translation the course relies on, so they are paraphrased and never quoted. No passage of *Vix Pervenit* is quoted from a published English translation.
- **Stance.** Taught from within the tradition. The historically hard parts are taught as hard: indulgence abuses that Trent itself condemned, the medieval canons on Jewish lenders, and the fact that practice on interest shifted while the Church's formal statements stayed guarded. Jewish readings of the release laws and of Daniel 9 are stated as their own tradition states them. Lutheran objections are stated from the Lutheran confessions.
- **Lesson count.** The roadmap estimated ~20. The syllabus runs 26 (+30%, outside the ±25% band) for three reasons. Jacob asked for this thread to be deep. The steering's five-part arc contains two subjects that each need two modules' worth of close reading: sin as debt, which runs from the Second Temple period to Trent (six lessons), and usury, which splits into the prohibition and its development (five lessons plus four). And every lesson here carries its own primary text, so a merger would drop that text rather than compress prose. Merges already made to hold the count: Deuteronomy 15 absorbs Jeremiah 34; the Jubilee's eschatological afterlife (Daniel 9, Qumran) joins the Nazareth lesson; Colossians 2:14 shares a lesson with the ransom vocabulary; the extrinsic titles and the non-loan contracts are the only usury topics split. The roadmap estimate may be updated to match.
