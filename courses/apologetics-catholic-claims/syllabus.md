# Apologetics II: The Catholic Claims — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 2 · ~29 lessons · Prereqs: [`ecclesiology-and-mariology`](../ecclesiology-and-mariology/syllabus.md), [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `apologetics-catholic-claims`

## Goal

This is the intra-Christian half of the apologetics pair, and the half where almost everything is shared ground. Scripture, the creeds of Nicaea and Constantinople, the Trinity, the Incarnation, baptism, the resurrection, the moral law: none of that is in dispute here. What is in dispute is **authority and interpretation** — who may say what the shared texts mean, and by what warrant. You will finish able to state *sola scriptura* as the Westminster Confession, the Book of Concord and Calvin state it, and as its serious modern defenders restate it, and then argue against it on the canon, on sufficiency and on perspicuity without once attacking the version no Protestant holds; to say who fixed the canon and on what authority, and to find the actual crux — not "who made the list" but whether a recognition can be more certain than the body recognizing it; to state justification by faith alone from the Augsburg Confession and its Apology, the Formula of Concord and Westminster, set it against Trent Session VI clause by clause, say exactly what the 1999 Joint Declaration achieved and what it left standing, and explain Catholic merit so that the objection it usually draws misses; to argue the Petrine texts and the patristic record for primacy while conceding what that record will not carry, and to meet the Protestant and Orthodox counter-readings in their authors' terms; to read *Apostolicae Curae* and the Anglican archbishops' reply to it and say what each argument turns on; to state the Orthodox case — the filioque in doctrine and in the lawfulness of its addition, primacy as the East reads it, Vatican I as Orthodox theologians read it, essence and energies, marriage and *oikonomia*, the state of the dead — as Lossky, Meyendorff, Ware and Romanides state it, with real concessions and no pretence that the dialogue documents have settled more than they have; to defend the contested Catholic distinctives — the Marian dogmas, the saints and their images, penance, purgatory and indulgences, the real presence, the sacrifice of the Mass — each against the specific confession that attacks it rather than against "Protestantism" in general; to show that development of doctrine is the question underneath all of them, and to run Newman's notes on a case where they tell against you; and to say what the Catholic case must simply concede about the Inquisition, Galileo, the sale of indulgences and the abuse crisis, and what it can still answer.

It deliberately skips everything that divides Christians from non-Christians — the existence of God, the resurrection as history, Judaism, Islam, pluralism — which is [`apologetics-foundations`](../apologetics-foundations/syllabus.md)'s, and which that course's Scope table reserves to itself as firmly as it hands this course everything intra-Christian. It skips the doctrines themselves: it does not teach ecclesiology, sacramental theology, grace or the Trinity, because four courses on disk teach them properly and cede only the arguing. It skips the events — 1054, the Reformation, Trent, Vatican I, Vatican II — to [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md). It skips development as a method to [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) Module 5, whose notes it uses without re-deriving. And it skips any survey of Protestant or Orthodox theology for its own sake: other traditions appear here only where a Catholic claim is under attack, stated at the strength their own confessions and theologians give them.

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md). This is the second of the two courses where the **apologetic** kind dominates, and it inherits the sibling's mechanics unchanged:

- **Every apologetic problem has two parts, and the first is exegetical.** Part (a) asks for the opposing position — Westminster I on scripture, Apology IV on justification, Calvin on the Supper, Article XXXI on the sacrifices of Masses, Lossky on the filioque, Meyendorff on primacy, the archbishops' reply to *Apostolicae Curae* — stated as its own confession or author states it. It is graded **strictly, as exegesis**, and a wrong statement fails the problem however good the reply that follows. Caricature is the error this course marks hardest, and the commonest caricature here is not malice but blur: attacking *solo scriptura* ("me and my Bible") while calling it *sola scriptura*, or attributing Zwingli's memorialism to Calvin.
- **Part (b) is the reply, graded on its quality, not its conclusion.** A reply that concludes the objection succeeds passes if it is a good reply. Several problems require a **named concession**: the one thing the other side has right, stated flatly, before anything is said against it. The concessions are written into this syllabus, not left to charity.
- **"Protestant" is not a position, and stating it as one is an exegetical error.** Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican and Baptist confessions differ from each other on the Supper, on baptism, on the Church and on the relation of law and gospel, sometimes more than any of them differs from Rome. Every problem names the confession it is arguing with. The same discipline applies eastward: **Orthodoxy has no magisterium that issues binding definitions the way the West does**, so a position may not be stated from the seventeenth-century confessions alone (Dositheus, Mogila), which modern Orthodox theologians themselves treat as of limited authority and partly Latinized. Where Orthodoxy has no defined position — the state of the dead is the clearest case — saying so *is* the right answer.
- **Exegetical parts are strict about weight on both sides.** Overstating a Catholic level (saying Trent defined what its chapters only expound, or that Vatican I defined papal impeccability) is an error; so is overstating a Protestant one (treating the Book of Concord as a magisterium, or a theologian's book as a confession), and so is overstating an ecumenical text (saying the Joint Declaration revoked Trent's canons, or that Ravenna settled primacy). Levels come from [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4–4.5 and nowhere else.
- **Evaluative parts, verdict-neutral, are kept for questions genuinely open**: how much weight the first-millennium record can bear for universal jurisdiction; whether the essence-energies distinction is compatible with divine simplicity as Latin theology states it; whether the Joint Declaration's differentiated consensus is a real agreement or a postponement; whether later facts (Old Catholic co-consecrators after 1931) leave particular Anglican lines outside the 1896 judgment; whether the development of Marian doctrine satisfies Newman's notes. Any verdict passes if the moves are made.
- **Close reading (archetype 7) is the workhorse,** and everything read closely is public domain: the Westminster Confession and the Thirty-Nine Articles in Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*; the Augsburg Confession, its Apology and the Formula of Concord in the 1921 Triglot Concordia; Calvin's *Institutes* in Beveridge; Trent in Waterworth's 1848 translation; *Pastor Aeternus* in Schaff; Nicaea II and the councils in Percival's volume of the *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*; *Apostolicae Curae* (1896) and the Anglican archbishops' *Saepius Officio* (1897); Newman's *Essay on Development* (1845), *Lectures on Justification* (1838, written as an Anglican) and *Letter to Pusey* (1866); Francis de Sales's *The Catholic Controversy* (Mackey, 1886); scripture in the Douay-Rheims (Challoner), with the lesson's own rendering where a Greek word decides a reading.
- **Find the crux (archetype 5) is the second workhorse,** because in this course nearly every dispute has one: the single premise one side must deny. Whether an infallible canon requires an infallible recognizer; whether justifying righteousness can be both imputed and infused; whether a Petrine function can become a Petrine office without a theory of development; whether an addition to a creed can be doctrinally true and canonically unlawful at once; whether the Mass can be a true sacrifice without being another one.
- **Diagnose (archetype 4), exegetical, and pointed in both directions.** Invented tract paragraphs, debate openings, apologetics blog posts and parish talks that get it wrong — a Catholic tract claiming Protestants "made up their own Bible"; a Reformed post claiming Catholics teach that we earn salvation; an Orthodox post claiming Vatican I made the pope a fourth person of the Trinity; a Catholic talk claiming the Joint Declaration ended the Reformation. Invented material is never attributed to a real person.
- **There are no formal parts.** The sibling's odds-form work belongs to a historical case; nothing here is settled by a likelihood ratio, and a probability attached to "the Fathers believed X" would be a decoration. The formal discipline in this course is exegetical precision instead.

**Accuracy discipline.** No invented quotations, ever, on any side. No session number, canon number, Denzinger number, confessional article or document section that has not been checked; where a number is uncertain the document is named without one. Modern Orthodox and Protestant theologians are paraphrased and cited by work. "Most Protestants hold" and "the Orthodox believe" are written only where accurate, and always as reports.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Name what is shared — canon of the creeds, Trinity, Incarnation, scripture as the word of God, baptism — and locate any intra-Christian dispute on the two axes that actually carry it: the seat of authority and the rule of interpretation
- [ ] State *sola scriptura* as Westminster I, the Formula of Concord and Calvin state it, tell it apart from *solo scriptura*, and give its best modern restatement before arguing against it
- [ ] Argue the canon question to its real crux — not who drew up the list, but whether a recognition can carry more certainty than the body that recognizes — stating the self-authentication model and what it explains
- [ ] Distinguish material from formal sufficiency, say why conceding the first costs nothing, argue from the New Testament's own appeals to tradition without proof-texting them, and test perspicuity as the confessions actually state it rather than as caricature states it
- [ ] State justification as Augsburg IV, Apology IV, Formula III and Westminster XI state it — imputation, the alien righteousness, faith as instrument, assurance — and set it against Trent Session VI clause by clause, naming where the two are contradictory and where they are talking past each other
- [ ] Read Paul and James together without flattening either, and say exactly what the New Perspective did and did not do to the sixteenth-century dispute
- [ ] Say precisely what the 1999 Joint Declaration claims, what its Annex adds, what its Lutheran and Catholic critics said, and what Trent's canons still condemn — then explain Catholic merit so that the objection it usually draws misses
- [ ] Argue the Petrine texts, meet Calvin's and Cullmann's readings in their own terms, and say what the patristic record for Roman primacy will and will not carry without a theory of development
- [ ] Read *Apostolicae Curae* and *Saepius Officio* against each other, name what each argument turns on, and state where the question of Anglican orders now stands
- [ ] State the Orthodox case on the filioque in its two separate questions — the doctrine, and the lawfulness of the addition — and say what the 1995 clarification concedes
- [ ] State papal primacy as Meyendorff, Ware, Afanasiev and Romanides read it, say what Ravenna and the later dialogues achieved and what they postponed, and give the Orthodox reading of Vatican I without softening universal ordinary jurisdiction
- [ ] State the essence-energies distinction as Palamas's defenders state it, say what Catholic theologians have said for and against it, and be clear that no magisterial condemnation of it exists
- [ ] Defend the Marian dogmas, the invocation of saints, the veneration of images, penance, purgatory, indulgences, the real presence and the sacrifice of the Mass, each against the specific confession that attacks it and with the abuse conceded first — and keep the Lutheran, Reformed and Zwinglian doctrines of the Supper apart while doing it
- [ ] Run Newman's notes on a Catholic distinctive where they are hard to satisfy, and say what the argument from silence proves and what it cannot
- [ ] Say what the Catholic case must concede about the Inquisition, Galileo, the sale of indulgences and the abuse crisis, and what indefectibility and infallibility actually claim about a Church that did those things

## Modules

### Module 1: Arguing among Christians

Before anything is argued: what is already agreed, what the Church says about the people on the other side of the table, what four centuries of polemic cost, and how to use historical evidence without abusing it.

### Module 2: Against sola scriptura

The formal principle of the Reformation, stated from the confessions and from its best modern defenders, and then tested at its three joints: the canon, sufficiency, and clarity.

| 5.4 | Essence and energies | State the Palamite distinction as its defenders state it, and assess it against Latin simplicity without inventing a condemnation | the distinction: God unknowable in essence and really participable in his uncreated energies, so that deification is real participation in God and not in a created effect; Palamas's argument from the hesychast controversy with Barlaam, and the Constantinopolitan councils of the 1340s and 1351 that received it for the Orthodox; Lossky's use of it as the key to the whole difference between East and West, and Meyendorff's historical account, both stated as they state them; the Latin objection: a **real** distinction in God appears to conflict with divine simplicity as Lateran IV and *Dei Filius* teach it ([`trinity-and-god`](../trinity-and-god/syllabus.md) 1.3), and the corresponding Orthodox charge that Latin simplicity plus created grace makes deification a metaphor; **the concessions, stated plainly**: there is no magisterial Catholic condemnation of Palamas or of the distinction; Catholic assessments range from twentieth-century neo-scholastic rejection to appreciation and retrieval; several Byzantine-rite Catholic churches keep his commemoration; and both sides agree on the datum the distinction was built to protect, which is that deification is real; the crux: what "real distinction" means in each vocabulary, and whether the dispute survives translation |

The practices and doctrines that make Catholicism look, to a Protestant eye, like a different religion. Each is defended against the confession that actually attacks it, and each begins with the abuse conceded. The module ends with the question underneath all of them.

- **Catholic magisterial texts, paraphrased and cited by name:** Trent, Sessions IV, VI, XIII, XIV, XXI, XXII and XXV, in Waterworth's 1848 translation for close reading; *Pastor Aeternus* (1870) in Schaff; Nicaea II in Percival's volume of the *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*; *Apostolicae Curae* (1896) and the Anglican archbishops' *Saepius Officio* (1897), both public domain and both read closely; Dei Verbum, Lumen Gentium, Unitatis Redintegratio, *Ut Unum Sint* (1995), *Dominus Iesus* (2000), the 1995 clarification on the procession of the Holy Spirit, the Ravenna document (2007) and the 1999 Joint Declaration with its Official Common Statement and Annex. Levels of authority come from [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4–4.5 and from nowhere else.
- **Newman, read as a participant and not as a mascot:** the *Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine* (1845), the *Lectures on Justification* (1838, written as an Anglican), the *Letter to Pusey* (dated 7 December 1865, published 1866) and the *Letter to the Duke of Norfolk* (1875) — all public domain. Where Newman's notes tell against a Catholic distinctive, the lesson says so.

## Notes

- **Why this course argues, and how it is kept honest.** Four courses on disk — [`ecclesiology-and-mariology`](../ecclesiology-and-mariology/syllabus.md), [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md), [`creation-grace-last-things`](../creation-grace-last-things/syllabus.md) and [`trinity-and-god`](../trinity-and-god/syllabus.md) — state other traditions' positions carefully and then refuse to reply, and two of them say in their Notes that they are built that way so this course can argue from a base where the Catholic doctrine has been taught properly and the alternatives have been stated without a rebuttal attached. That is the load-bearing arrangement, and it produces the three rules the lessons enforce: the opposing view is stated first and graded first; every module carries at least one concession written into this syllabus; and no rubric anywhere requires Jacob to reach the Catholic conclusion.
- **Ownership collisions found and resolved.** (1) [`ecclesiology-and-mariology`](../ecclesiology-and-mariology/syllabus.md) 4.1–4.5 and Module 6 cover exactly the material of Modules 4 and 6.1 — and its Notes name this course as the place where they are argued, so 4.1–4.2 and 6.1 argue from its statements and re-teach none of them. (2) [`sacraments-and-liturgy`](../sacraments-and-liturgy/syllabus.md)'s Notes list what it hands over, item by item; 4.3, 5.5 and 6.3–6.5 are that list and nothing more. (3) [`creation-grace-last-things`](../creation-grace-last-things/syllabus.md) 5.1–5.3 and 6.3 supply Module 3 and 6.3 with doctrine already stated, including the Joint Declaration read as a text; Module 3 argues and does not re-read it. (4) [`trinity-and-god`](../trinity-and-god/syllabus.md) 5.1–5.3 supplies 5.1 with both doctrines and the history of the addition; 5.1 adds only the argument and the lawfulness question. (5) [`theology-of-debt`](../theology-of-debt/syllabus.md) 3.4–3.6 owns satisfaction, the treasury and Trent on indulgences, and its Boss 3 already runs Luther's Theses 56–62 as an apologetic exercise; 6.3 cites that exercise and does not repeat it. (6) [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md) Boss 1, Boss 2 and Boss 5 already work 1 Clement and Ignatius on church offices, the two recensions of Cyprian ch. 4 with Irenaeus 3.3.2, and the patristic readings of "this rock"; 4.2 argues about what that evidence proves and reads none of those passages again. (7) [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) cedes the Reformation and Orthodox disputes here by name, and its Boss 7 (Bellarmine and the *Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina*) is deliberately not reused in 7.1. (8) [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md) 5.4 already states penal substitution from the Reformed confessions and assesses it; Module 3 cites it and does not re-open the atonement, which is the one Reformation dispute this course leaves entirely to another course. (9) [`apologetics-foundations`](../apologetics-foundations/syllabus.md) 4.1 owns the conflict thesis and the Galileo affair as a test of it; 7.1 uses the same affair for a different argument — institutional error against the claim of divine guidance — and says so. (10) [`new-testament`](../new-testament/syllabus.md), [`old-testament`](../old-testament/syllabus.md) and [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) all name this course in their scope tables for the justification dispute, the canon dispute and *sola scriptura*. (11) Built siblings already treat much of this material (see the Build revision note below); the lessons cite them and start where they stop.
- **The line back to [`apologetics-foundations`](../apologetics-foundations/syllabus.md).** That course argues only what divides Christians from non-Christians; this one argues only what divides Christians from each other. Neither is a prerequisite of the other and neither repeats the other. Where the two would touch — the account of what apologetics is and cannot do, the ethics of persuasion, salvation outside the visible Church — this course cites and moves on. In particular, no argument for the existence of God, for the resurrection, or for the reliability of scripture is made here: all of it is shared ground with every opponent in this course, and an apologist who reaches for it has misread the room.
- **Fidelity discipline, and where it bites hardest.** The single most common failure in intra-Christian apologetics is fighting a position nobody holds. Three specific traps get named in the lessons and graded in the rubrics: *sola scriptura* is not *solo scriptura*, and the confessions themselves say so (2.1); the Reformed doctrine of the Supper is not Zwingli's (6.4); and the Orthodox objection to Vatican I is usually about jurisdiction rather than infallibility (5.3). A fourth, running throughout: Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican and Orthodox positions are four different things, and "they believe" is almost always the beginning of an error.
- **Concessions written into the syllabus, by module.** (1) Blame for the divisions falls on both sides, and Catholic polemic has its own record of forgery and atrocity story. (2) Material sufficiency is a legitimate Catholic option; Jerome and Cajetan doubted the deuterocanon; the Catholic canon was not explicitly closed before 1546. (3) The Reformation doctrine protects something late-medieval preaching genuinely obscured, and the Joint Declaration's Annex concedes more than Catholic apologists usually admit. (4) The papal claims grew, forgeries were used in their service, a pope was condemned by an ecumenical council, and the exegesis of Matthew 16 alone will not reach an office. (5) The manner of the filioque's addition is hard to defend; the 1995 clarification concedes the monarchy of the Father; universal ordinary jurisdiction has cost the local church something real; and no magisterial condemnation of Palamas exists. (6) Marian devotion has gone past its doctrine, relic traffic and indulgence sales were real, private Masses and stipends were abused, and the definition of the real presence is not committed to Aristotle. (7) The Galileo condemnation was an error really made; most of the sixteenth-century disciplinary grievances were justified; and the argument from the abuse crisis is survived rather than answered.
- **Accuracy: verify before writing each lesson, never assert from memory.** Confessional article numbers and their exact wording in Schaff and in the Triglot; chapter numbers in Calvin's *Institutes* beyond I.7, III.11, III.20 and IV.17, which are corroborated here; the session and chapter structure of Trent in Waterworth; the grounds and the operative judgement of *Apostolicae Curae* and the argument of *Saepius Officio*; the dates of the Constantinopolitan councils that received Palamas; the canon of Ephesus that Orthodox writers cite against creedal addition, which is named by content rather than by number; the date, signatories and structure of the 1999 Joint Declaration and which later communions associated themselves with it; the title and issuing body of the 1995 clarification, the Ravenna document and the 2024 study document on the bishop of Rome; the *Consilium de Emendanda Ecclesia* (1537) and its authorship; the Spanish Inquisition execution estimates, which are given as historians' estimates with the range and never as a single number; and every Denzinger and Catechism reference. Where a number cannot be confirmed, the document is named without it.
- **Claims deliberately softened or left out.** Denominational counts are not used as an argument, because there is no agreed counting method and the figure usually quoted does not mean what it is used to mean. The claim that Trent's canons condemn a position Luther never held is presented as a question Catholic scholars raise, not as an established result. Romanides's thesis is stated at full strength and then placed as a minority view among Orthodox historians, which is what it is. Disputed intra-Orthodox eschatological material is left out entirely rather than presented as the Orthodox position. No claim is made about how the Orthodox churches as a whole would receive any dialogue document, since there is no body competent to say. The level of *Apostolicae Curae* is described as differently assessed by Catholic theologians, because it is.
- **Lesson count.** The roadmap estimated ~26; the syllabus runs 29 (+12%, inside the band). Module 5 needs five lessons because the filioque, primacy, Vatican I, the energies and the practical disputes have four separate literatures and one of them (the energies) cannot be stated in Latin categories at all, so a merged lesson would misstate it. Module 6 needs six because each distinctive is attacked by a different confession on different grounds, and merging the real presence with the sacrifice of the Mass — the usual compression — is exactly the merge that produces the standard apologetic failure of 6.5. Compression happened where it could: Module 1 is three lessons because [`philosophical-method`](../philosophical-method/syllabus.md) 4.2 and [`apologetics-foundations`](../apologetics-foundations/syllabus.md) 1.1 already teach the method and the ethics; Module 2 is four because material sufficiency and the New Testament's tradition texts answer one question and belong in one lesson; Module 3 is four because [`creation-grace-last-things`](../creation-grace-last-things/syllabus.md) has already read Trent Session VI and the Joint Declaration as texts; and Module 7 is three, with the Galileo affair, the Inquisition and the conflict thesis compressed into one lesson because two other courses own most of that material. If a lesson overflows when built, split it and merge elsewhere rather than grow past 30.
