# The Thomistic Synthesis — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 2 · ~24 lessons · Prereqs: [`ancient-medieval-philosophy`](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/syllabus.md), [`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `thomistic-synthesis`

## Goal

This course reads Aquinas the way the Church reads him, as its common doctor. It treats his work as one working system in which theology does its thinking, not as a chapter in the history of philosophy. It is the gateway to the dogmatic sequence: `trinity-and-god`, `christology`, `creation-grace-last-things` and `sacraments-and-liturgy` all use its vocabulary without stopping to explain it. You will finish able to read a whole question of the *Summa Theologiae* in the English Dominican translation, following its order of articles and not only the form of one article. You will be able to say why a book about God contains treatises on motion, the soul and human acts, and to run the metaphysics the dogmatic courses assume. That metaphysics covers act and potency extended beyond matter, *esse* and essence, participation, the transcendentals, and the Five Ways read as the opening of a derivation that runs through divine simplicity to the names of God and analogy. It goes on to creation and conservation, providence working through secondary causes, and the human soul's place between spirit and body. The course closes with the Thomist tradition: the commentators, Leo XIII and the manuals, the twentieth-century Thomisms and their quarrels, and exactly what the Church has said about Thomism and at what weight. It deliberately skips several things. Aristotle, Avicenna and Aquinas read historically belong to [`ancient-medieval-philosophy`](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/syllabus.md), and whether act and potency, hylomorphism or the real distinction are true against contemporary rivals belongs to [`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md). Whether cosmological arguments succeed is [`philosophy-of-religion`](../philosophy-of-religion/syllabus.md)'s question. The Trinity and the divine attributes as dogma go to `trinity-and-god`, natural law and the moral part to [`ethics`](../ethics/syllabus.md) and [`moral-theology`](../moral-theology/syllabus.md), and grace, the doctrine of creation and the soul's immortality as doctrine to `creation-grace-last-things`. Aquinas's Christology, sacramental theology, political thought, biblical commentaries, and his angelology beyond one example are also left out.

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md), with the Catholic Theology stance. The course is taught from within the tradition and marks authority on the ladder in the Notes of [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md). The discipline it must add is this: **a Thomist thesis is not a dogma because Aquinas held it or because the Church recommends him.** Some doctrines the Church teaches are stated in Thomist vocabulary, and they keep their own levels. The recommendation of Aquinas as master is a matter of the ordinary magisterium and of Church law. Most school theses (the real distinction, Cajetan on analogy, physical premotion) are freely disputed among Catholics. Collapsing these into one another is an **exegetical error**, graded strictly, in either direction.

- **Close reading is the workhorse**, graded strictly as exegesis: a passage of 150 words or fewer from the *Summa Theologiae* in the English Dominican Province translation (public domain), and occasionally the *Summa contra Gentiles* in the same Province's translation. The questions asked are which reading the words support, and which words decide it. Reading a whole question (prologue, order of articles, how a later article leans on an earlier one) is practised from 1.2 on.
- **Reconstruct** puts an article's *respondeo* in numbered premises and marks which premises come from earlier articles, from the metaphysics of Module 2, or from authority.
- **Apply to a hard case** runs the system on something Aquinas did not discuss: act and potency on an angel, providence on a lottery, the three ways of naming on "God is angry".
- **Evaluative parts** are reserved for questions genuinely disputed *among Thomists and Catholic theologians*. These include whether the real distinction is demonstrated before or after God's existence, Cajetan's proportionality against the attribution reading of analogy, existential against essentialist Thomism, whether non-competitive dual causation is coherent, and whether the twentieth century's many Thomisms were fidelity or dissolution. Any verdict passes if the required moves are made.
- **Apologetic parts** defend the Thomist position against a named alternative stated first at its best: Scotus's univocity, Suárez's denial of the real distinction, occasionalism, the modal-collapse objection to simplicity. The statement of the other side is graded first.
- **Diagnose** uses invented sermons, op-eds and classroom remarks that smuggle in univocal God-talk, a "God of the gaps", or deism. No invented words are attributed to real people.
- There are **no formal parts**.

Aeterni Patris (1879) and other pre-1930 papal documents are read in public-domain translations. Cajetan, John of St Thomas, Suárez, and all twentieth-century authors (Garrigou-Lagrange, Maritain, Gilson, Maréchal, Rahner, Lonergan, de Lubac, Fabro, Wippel) are paraphrased and cited by work. None of them is used for close reading.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Read any question of the *Summa Theologiae* as a unit: say why its articles come in that order, where each objection comes from, whether the *sed contra* is Aquinas's answer, and which distinction in the replies does the work
- [ ] Explain from ST I q.1 and the prologue to q.2 why a science whose subject is God treats motion, the soul and human acts, and lay out the plan of all three parts
- [ ] Run act and potency through every composition Aquinas uses (matter and form, substance and accident, power and operation, essence and *esse*), and say what is left when every potency is removed
- [ ] State the real distinction between essence and *esse* in creatures, give the argument for it, say what Scotists and Suárezians hold instead, and say what is at stake for theology
- [ ] Explain being by participation and being by essence, and show how participation connects the Fourth Way, creation and the transcendentals
- [ ] Say what each of the Five Ways means by its key term inside Aquinas's system, and trace how qq.3-11 identify the five conclusions as one simple, perfect, infinite, unchanging, eternal and unique being
- [ ] Explain divine simplicity as the hinge of the treatise on God, and answer the modal-collapse objection from within the system while stating the objection fairly
- [ ] Say what natural reason can and cannot know about God, given that human knowledge begins in the senses, and apply the ways of causality, negation and eminence to a divine name
- [ ] Tell univocal, equivocal and analogical predication apart, state the attribution and proportionality readings of ST I q.13 a.5, and reply to Scotus's argument for univocity
- [ ] Explain why creation is not a change, why conservation is not a second act, and how God and a creature can each be the whole cause of one effect, without sliding into occasionalism or deism
- [ ] Place any twentieth-century Thomist (Garrigou-Lagrange, Maritain, Gilson, Maréchal, Rahner, Lonergan, Fabro) on the map of schools, and name the premise on which two of them part ways
- [ ] Assign the correct level to a claim about Thomism, separating a doctrine stated in Thomist terms, the recommendation of Aquinas as master, and a freely disputed school thesis, and cite the act or text that fixes each

### Module 1: Reading the Summa

What kind of book the *Summa* is, how to read it a question at a time, and why a science of God has room for everything.

**Boss problem 1:** Take ST I q.1 a.7, "Whether God is the object of this science?", and the prologue to I q.2 (English Dominican Province). (a) Map the article: state each objection's position and the source it relies on, and say whether the *sed contra* settles the question or only points to the answer. *(Exegetical.)* (b) Close-read the *respondeo*: which words separate the *subject* of the science from the many things it treats, and what are the two ways in which something other than God falls under it? *(Exegetical.)* (c) Use (b) and the prologue to I q.2 to explain why the treatise on human acts (I-II) and the treatise on the soul (I qq.75-89) belong in this science, not in philosophy, and what each would lose if moved there. *(Exegetical.)* (d) In 300-500 words, assess whether exitus and reditus is the best key to the plan of the *Summa*, or whether the general prologue's "order of the subject matter" and the needs of beginners explain the plan better. State where each reading strains (the place of Christ in Part III is the usual test), any verdict. *(Evaluative.)*

### Module 4: Knowing and naming God

### Module 5: Creatures and their return

What creating is, how God acts in a world of real causes, where the human being stands, and how the rest of the *Summa* carries the creature back to God.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Creation and conservation | Explain why creation is not a change and why conserving is not a second act | God as efficient, exemplar and final cause of all things (ST I q.44 aa.1, 3-4); creation as the emanation of all being from the universal cause (q.45 a.1); creation is not a change, since there is no subject that changes (q.45 a.2 ad 2); creation in the creature as a relation to its source (q.45 a.3); conservation as the continued giving of *esse* (q.104 a.1) and God present in all things as agent (q.8 a.1); causes of becoming vs causes of being ([`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md) 4.4); a beginning known by faith, not demonstrated (q.46, from [`ancient-medieval-philosophy`](../ancient-medieval-philosophy/syllabus.md) 7.1); why neither the Big Bang nor an eternal universe settles creation ([`cosmology`](../cosmology/syllabus.md); [`philosophy-of-religion`](../philosophy-of-religion/syllabus.md) 2.5); the doctrine of creation ceded to `creation-grace-last-things` |
| 5.2 | Providence and secondary causes | Say how God and a creature can each be the whole cause of one effect | providence extends to all things (ST I q.22 a.2), planned immediately but carried out through intermediaries (a.3), without imposing necessity on everything (a.4; q.19 a.8 on contingent effects); God works in every agent, against those who deny creatures their own action (q.105 a.5; *Summa contra Gentiles* III.69-70); primary and secondary causes on different levels, not competing; occasionalism as the foil ([`modern-philosophy`](../modern-philosophy/syllabus.md) 2.4); chance and evil under providence; *Dei Filius* ch. 1 on providence, with its level; physical premotion vs middle knowledge named and ceded to `creation-grace-last-things` |
| 5.3 | The human soul in the system | Place the rational soul in the hierarchy of being and say what its double status explains | the soul as both form and subsistent, on the boundary of spiritual and bodily being; the soul shares its own *esse* with the body, so there is one *esse* for the whole human being (q.76 a.1, in the replies); one substantial form (q.76 a.4) and the unicity controversy with Franciscan masters after Aquinas's death; the soul is not the human being (q.75 a.4); the separated soul as an incomplete substance, doctrine ceded to `creation-grace-last-things`; the live arguments cited ([`philosophy-of-mind`](../philosophy-of-mind/syllabus.md) 5.1-5.2; corruptionism vs survivalism in [`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md) 6.5) |
| 5.4 | The return: a map of the Second and Third Parts | Say where the moral part and Christ sit in the plan and what each inherits from Part I | the I-II prologue (the human being as image of God and principle of his own acts); the order of I-II and II-II, a map only, with the structure cited from [`moral-theology`](../moral-theology/syllabus.md) 1.1-1.2 and natural law from [`ethics`](../ethics/syllabus.md) 4.4; the last end as the vision of God (I-II q.3 a.8) tied back to 4.2; grace (I-II qq.109-114) ceded to `creation-grace-last-things`; Part III: Christ as the way (the prologue to Part III) and the sacraments, ceded to `christology` and `sacraments-and-liturgy`; why the moral part comes before Christ, and the objection that this makes Christ an afterthought |

**Boss problem 5:** Take ST I q.105 a.5, "Whether God works in every agent?", and I q.22 a.4, "Whether providence imposes any necessity on things foreseen?" (English Dominican Province). (a) Reconstruct the *respondeo* of q.105 a.5 in two parts: first the argument against the view that God acts so that creatures do nothing, then the ways in which Aquinas says God does work in every agent. *(Exegetical.)* (b) Close-read q.22 a.4. ### Module 6: The Thomist tradition and its authority

Seven centuries of reading Aquinas: the commentators who built a school, the Leonine revival that made him the Church's master in the classroom, the twentieth century that split him into many Thomisms, and what the Church has actually said about him.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | The commentators | Say what the great commentators added and why "commentator Thomism" became a charge | canonization (1323) and Doctor of the Church (1567) in brief; Capreolus as "prince of Thomists"; Cajetan's commentary on the *Summa* and his work on analogy (4.4) and on nature; Sylvester of Ferrara on the *Summa contra Gentiles*; Báñez and the Dominican school against the Jesuits (the dispute ceded to `creation-grace-last-things`); John of St Thomas; Suárez's *Disputationes Metaphysicae* as the rival scholasticism (2.2); the later charge (Gilson, de Lubac) that the commentators replaced Aquinas with an essentialist system |
| 6.2 | Leo XIII and the Thomist revival | State the argument and programme of *Aeterni Patris* and what the manuals made of it | nineteenth-century neo-scholasticism before Leo in brief; *Aeterni Patris* (1879): its account of philosophy's service to faith, why Aquinas, and its own limit on what is recommended; the event context cited from [`church-history`](../church-history/syllabus.md) 8.3; the Leonine Commission and the critical edition; Pius X's *Doctoris Angelici* (1914) and the Twenty-Four Thomistic Theses issued the same year by the Congregation of Studies, with their disputed binding force; Pius XI's *Studiorum Ducem* (1923); the 1917 Code on teaching by Aquinas's method, doctrine and principles; the Latin philosophical and dogmatic manuals (the moral manuals cited from [`moral-theology`](../moral-theology/syllabus.md) 4.1); the story that Trent set the *Summa* beside Scripture, which the encyclical repeats and historians have not documented |
| 6.3 | Strict observance and existential Thomism | Place Garrigou-Lagrange, Maritain and Gilson on one map and name what divides them | Garrigou-Lagrange: the Twenty-Four Theses, act and potency as the core, the Angelicum, the anti-modernist frame; Maritain: *The Degrees of Knowledge* (1932), the intuition of being, Thomism engaged with modern science and art (his political thought cited to `catholic-social-teaching`); Gilson: *Being and Some Philosophers* (1949), the primacy of *esse* as Aquinas's discovery, reading Aquinas historically against the commentators; the "Christian philosophy" debate of the 1930s; the crux: is the Thomist system a perennial philosophy or a historically located achievement? |
| 6.4 | Transcendental Thomism and the ressourcement quarrel | Explain what transcendental Thomism took from Kant, and why the natural-desire dispute split Thomists | Maréchal's answer to Kant ([`modern-philosophy`](../modern-philosophy/syllabus.md) 5.1) through the dynamism of the intellect; Rahner's *Spirit in the World* (1939); Lonergan's *Verbum* and *Insight* (1957); de Lubac's *Surnaturel* (1946) and the attack on "pure nature" as a commentators' invention; Garrigou-Lagrange's 1946 polemic against the "new theology"; *Humani Generis* (1950) on the gratuity of the supernatural, paraphrased and with its level marked (the doctrine of grace ceded to `creation-grace-last-things`); after the Council: decline, analytical Thomism (Anscombe, Geach, Kenny, Haldane), the Lublin school and Stein ([`phenomenology-and-personalism`](../phenomenology-and-personalism/syllabus.md) 4.4, 5.1), and the revival |

- **Lesson count.** The roadmap estimated ~22; the syllabus runs 24 (+9%, inside the band). The blurb names four topics; the steering adds eight more, each with its own texts. Merges already made to hold the line: the sources Aquinas fuses go into 2.3 (Dionysius, the *Liber de causis*) and 4.2 (the three ways), not a lesson of their own; creation and conservation share 5.1; human knowing moves into Module 4 (4.1), where it grounds the naming of God, so it does not need its own lesson in Module 5; the Second and Third Parts are one map (5.4). - **Accuracy discipline.** Every ST reference above is to an article whose title and topic are well established. Lessons still re-check each against the English Dominican text before publishing, and give article numbers for replies (*ad 1*, *ad 2*) only after checking. These are to verify at lesson-writing time and are not relied on above: the exact section of *Aeterni Patris* holding the limiting clause and the Trent-altar story; the date and wording of the Congregation of Studies' later clarification of the Twenty-Four Theses; the 1917 Code's canon number on teaching by Aquinas's method (can. 1366 §2 is believed correct); the section numbers of *Fides et Ratio* on Aquinas and on "no philosophy of its own"; whether *Gravissimum Educationis* also names Aquinas; the chapter of *De Ente et Essentia* holding the real-distinction argument (numbering differs between editions); the passage in *De Veritate* on proportion vs proportionality; and the attribution to Capreolus of the title "prince of Thomists". No DH numbers are used. Lateran IV's teaching on dissimilarity and on simplicity, and *Dei Filius* ch. 1 on simplicity and providence, are cited by council and chapter only. Chenu's exitus-reditus thesis is presented as one influential reading, not as settled.
- **Authority discipline.** Three errors are graded as exegetical errors in every module. The first is treating a thesis as dogma because Aquinas held it. The second is treating the Church's recommendation of Aquinas as making every Thomist thesis binding. The third runs the other way: treating a doctrine taught in scholastic vocabulary (the soul as form, God's simplicity, natural knowledge of God) as merely one school's opinion. The levels come from [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4, and where that syllabus and this one differ, that syllabus wins.
