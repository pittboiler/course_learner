# Sacraments & Liturgy · Lesson 3.5: Communion, reservation, adoration

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 3: The Eucharist · Builds on: [3.3](03-03-real-presence-and-transubstantiation.md), [3.4](03-04-the-sacrifice-of-the-mass.md), [`moral-theology` 3.4](../../moral-theology/lessons/03-04-sin-mortal-and-venial.md), [`ecclesiology-and-mariology` 3.2](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md), [5.2](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md) · Unlocks: [4.1](04-01-penance-the-sacrament-and-its-history.md), [5.4](05-04-nullity-dissolution-and-the-pastoral-debates.md), [6.1](06-01-what-the-liturgy-is.md)

## Why this matters

[3.3](03-03-real-presence-and-transubstantiation.md) put Christ on the altar and [3.4](03-04-the-sacrifice-of-the-mass.md) said what is offered there. This lesson follows the sacrament off the altar, into a communicant, a tabernacle, a monstrance: who may receive, prepared how, under which species, and why the sacrament is kept and adored between Masses. Nearly every rule here is discipline resting on a short defined core; telling the two apart is the skill.

## The idea

**What is received is Christ whole.** By concomitance the whole Christ is under each species and every part of it, and remains while the species remain ([3.3](03-03-real-presence-and-transubstantiation.md)). So it is approached with reverence, received whole under one kind, kept for the sick, and adored.

**What is signified is the one body.** The Eucharist's *res tantum*, the reality it finally signifies and effects, is the unity of the mystical body ([1.1](01-01-what-a-sacrament-is.md); ST III q.73 a.3). Aquinas draws the consequence:

> whoever receives this sacrament, expresses thereby that he is made one with Christ, and incorporated in His members; and this is done by living faith, which no one has who is in mortal sin. (ST III q.80 a.4)

Receiving says something with the body; said in mortal sin it is false, "lying to this sacrament". One level up, the same logic governs Christians outside full communion: communion expresses a unity, so as a rule it follows full communion rather than manufacturing it.

Keep [valid, licit and fruitful](../reference.md#valid-licit-fruitful) apart: one communion can be a true reception of Christ, unlawful, and unfruitful at once.

## The doctrine

1. **Preparation.** "Let a man prove himself" (1 Cor 11:28, DR). Trent: ecclesiastical usage declares the proof to be that no one conscious of mortal sin, "how contrite soever he may seem to himself", approaches without prior sacramental confession (Session XIII, ch. 7). Canon 11 anathematizes "faith alone is a sufficient preparation"; an ordinance attached to it requires confession first, under excommunication. Can. 916 allows one exception: a grave reason *and* no opportunity to confess, with an act of perfect contrition that includes the resolve to confess soon. Such contrition reconciles before the sacrament (Session XIV, ch. 4). Grave sin is [`moral-theology` 3.4](../../moral-theology/lessons/03-04-sin-mortal-and-venial.md)'s. *In words:* mortal sin bars communion until confession, and the exception still points to confession.

2. **Three ways of receiving.** Sacramentally only (sinners), spiritually only (by desire), or both (Session XIII, ch. 8; can. 8). *In words:* the sinner truly receives Christ, without fruit; one who cannot receive is joined to him by desire.

3. **The [eucharistic fast](../reference.md#eucharistic-fast).** Can. 919: no food or drink, water and medicine excepted, for an hour before *communion*, not before Mass; the elderly, the infirm and their carers are exempt. The fast from midnight held until Pius XII exempted water (*Christus Dominus*, 6 January 1953), then set three hours from solid food and alcohol, one from other drinks (*Sacram Communionem*, 19 March 1957); Paul VI made it one hour in 1964. *In words:* reverence in the body, wholly the Church's to set.

4. **Frequency.** Lateran IV's canon 21 bound everyone past the age of discretion to yearly confession and Easter communion ([`church-history` 5.1](../../church-history/lessons/05-01-papal-monarchy-innocent-iii-to-boniface-viii.md)). Trent's canon 9 anathematizes denying that the faithful are so bound "in accordance with the precept of holy Mother Church". The precept's terms are law (can. 920). Pius X opened daily communion to all in the state of grace with a right intention (*Sacra Tridentina Synodus*, 20 December 1905), and set first communion at the age of discretion, about seven, with elementary knowledge sufficing (*Quam Singulari*, 8 August 1910; cann. 913–914). *In words:* from a yearly minimum to a daily invitation, by the Church's precept.

**Said plainly.** The Latin Church withheld the cup from the laity for most of seven centuries, made the custom law, defended it by conciliar decree against the Hussites and by anathemas against the Reformers, and from 1963 gave it back. The doctrine did not move. The discipline moved twice.

5. **[Communion under both kinds](../reference.md#communion-under-both-kinds).** Until about the twelfth century Western laity normally received both; the chalice was then withdrawn, chiefly for fear of spilling, and unevenly: Aquinas calls it "a prudent custom in some churches" (ST III q.80 a.12; Fortescue, *The Mass*, 1912, on the Communion, §5). When Jacob of Mies proposed the cup for the laity in Prague (1414), whence the Utraquists (*sub utraque*, "under both"), Constance (Session XIII, 15 June 1415) granted that "in the primitive Church this sacrament was received by the faithful under both kinds", and kept the custom because "the whole body and blood of Christ is truly contained as much under the species of bread as under the species of wine" (lesson's translation). [Trent, Session XXI](../reference.md#trent-session-xxi) (16 July 1562): no divine precept binds the laity to both (can. 1); Christ whole and a true sacrament are received under either (ch. 3, can. 3); the Church had just causes (can. 2). Chapter 2 states the principle:

   > in the dispensation of the sacraments, their substance being untouched, it may ordain, or change, what things soever it may judge most expedient, for the profit of those who receive, or for the veneration of the said sacraments … (Waterworth)

   Trent referred any concession of the cup to the pope (Session XXII). *Sacrosanctum Concilium* 55 (1963) reopened it, Trent's dogmatic principles intact; the GIRM calls both kinds the fuller sign and leaves the norms to the bishop (281–283). It is the usual form in the Eastern rites (CCC 1390). *In words:* the substance is Christ's; one kind or two is the Church's to dispense.

6. **[Admission to communion](../reference.md#admission-to-communion).** Who belongs, and how fully, is [`ecclesiology-and-mariology` 5.2](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/lessons/05-02-subsistit-in-and-the-status-of-other-churches.md)'s, taken whole. UR 8 sets two principles for worship in common, witness to unity, which generally forbids it, and sharing in grace, which sometimes commends it. Eucharistic communion is inseparably linked to full ecclesial communion (Ecumenical Directory, 1993, §129). Under can. 844, members of Eastern Churches not in full communion receive licitly if they ask of their own accord and are properly disposed (§3; OE 27), though their own Churches may be stricter, which others should respect (ED 122). Other Christians receive only in danger of death or a grave necessity judged by the bishop or conference, if they cannot approach their own minister, ask of their own accord, manifest Catholic faith in the sacrament and are properly disposed (§4; ED 131). For the Reformation communities the reason given is UR 22's defect of Orders (CCC 1400). Concelebration without full communion is never lawful (*Ecclesia de Eucharistia* 44). Can. 915 is [5.4](05-04-nullity-dissolution-and-the-pastoral-debates.md)'s. *In words:* the rule follows full communion; the exceptions serve a person's need, not intercommunion.

7. **Reservation and [viaticum](../reference.md#viaticum).** Nicaea's canon 13 will not deprive the dying of "the last and most indispensable Viaticum". Trent anathematizes denying that it is lawful to reserve the Eucharist or carry it to the sick (Session XIII, ch. 6, can. 7). Viaticum is reservation's primary and original purpose; communion outside Mass and adoration are secondary (*Eucharisticum Mysterium* 49, 1967, quoting a 1949 instruction). The dying are to receive it while fully conscious (cann. 921–922); when it is withheld is [4.3](04-03-anointing-of-the-sick.md)'s. One tabernacle per church, prominent, immovable, locked, a lamp before it (cann. 938, 940). *In words:* kept first for the dying, and adored because kept.

8. **[Adoration](../reference.md#reservation-and-adoration).** Latria and its christological ground are [3.3](03-03-real-presence-and-transubstantiation.md)'s; canon 6 also defends the feast, processions and public exposition, and denies that adorers are idolaters. The forms are second-millennium: the elevation after the words of institution (France, about 1200, per Fortescue), Corpus Christi ([3.2](03-02-the-controversies-that-forced-precision.md)), later exposition and benediction. *Eucharisticum Mysterium* ties them to the altar: the Mass is the origin and consummation of worship outside it (§3e), and Mass is not celebrated in the same space before the exposed sacrament (§61; can. 941 §2). *In words:* the cult points back to the altar.

9. **The Eucharist builds the Church.** "For we, being many, are one bread, one body: all that partake of one bread" (1 Cor 10:17, DR; *koinōnia* is glossed at [`ecclesiology-and-mariology` 1.4](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/lessons/01-04-people-of-god-communion-sacrament.md)). [E&M 3.2](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/lessons/03-02-the-local-and-the-universal-church.md) gave LG 26 and the Orthodox [eucharistic ecclesiology](../reference.md#eucharistic-ecclesiology). Henri de Lubac (*Corpus Mysticum*, 1944) argued that the early medieval West called the Eucharist the "mystical body" and the Church the "true body" until, after Berengar, the terms changed places. John Paul II: "the Eucharist builds the Church and the Church makes the Eucharist" (*Ecclesia de Eucharistia* 26). *In words:* the Church receives her unity at the altar, so who approaches it is an ecclesial question.

**Mark the levels** ([`fundamental-theology` 4.4](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md), [4.5](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-05-reading-a-documents-weight.md); [levels](../reference.md#levels-of-authority)). **Rung 1**, as anathematized canons: Session XIII cans. 6–9 and 11, Session XXI cans. 1–3. Two are narrower than they look: canon 9 defines that the Church's precept binds, not its terms. Canon 11's anathema strikes "faith alone"; confession first is the attached ordinance, constant teaching (CCC 1385, **at least rung 3**) and law (can. 916). Session XXI's chapter 2, the premise of canon 2 and restated in GIRM 282, is **at least rung 3**. Constance's doctrinal core was later defined at Trent; its own standing in 1415 is [`church-history` 5.4](../../church-history/lessons/05-04-avignon-the-great-schism-and-the-councils.md)'s question. UR 8 and 15, and *Ecclesia de Eucharistia* where it teaches, are **rung 3**. The fast, the Easter dates, the age, the cup's distribution, can. 844, Lateran IV's canon, Pius X's decrees and the norms of the Directory and *Eucharisticum Mysterium* are discipline, **no rung**. Aquinas and de Lubac have **no magisterial level**.

## Worked examples

**Ex 1 (clean): the clock, and three verdicts.** Invented. Florin, in the state of grace, has coffee and a biscuit at 9:30, takes his blood-pressure pill with water at 10:00, and receives at about 10:40 at the 10:00 Mass. The hour runs to communion, not to the start of Mass: 70 minutes, and pill and water are excepted anyway (can. 919 §1). *Valid:* he truly receives Christ. *Licit:* yes. *Fruitful:* yes, by his disposition. Had he eaten at 9:50, the reception would be illicit, yet still Christ received, and a misread clock does not by itself make it fruitless. Under the 1957 rule even 9:30 was too late.

**Ex 2 (strain): the Lutheran groom.** Invented. At a nuptial Mass the groom, a Lutheran who sincerely confesses Christ's true presence in the Supper, asks to receive with his Catholic bride. Can. 844 §4's conditions are cumulative: there is no danger of death, a wedding is a grave necessity only if the bishop's or conference's norms make it one (ED 130), and his own pastor is reachable. So, under the general law, no, and not as a verdict on his sincerity: communion expresses a full communion not yet there (ED 129), and the exception meets an individual's grave need, not intercommunion (*Ecclesia de Eucharistia* 45). The strain is in what the canon leaves open: "grave necessity" is for local norms, and *Ecclesia de Eucharistia* 46 glosses "Catholic faith" in the sacrament only as the faith the Church professes about it. Whether that must include the Mass as sacrifice is where readings part.

## Watch out

- **You might think a communion in mortal sin "doesn't count".** It is a true reception, sacramentally only (Session XIII, ch. 8): Christ received, no fruit, a sacrilege.
- **You might think the hour, the Easter dates or the age of seven are doctrine.** They are law; canon 9 defines only that the precept binds. Understating is as wrong: that nothing necessary is lost under one kind is **rung 1**, not pastoral reassurance.
- **You might think can. 844 §4 admits any Christian who believes in the real presence.** Every one of its conditions must hold at once.

## One-liner

> Defined: Christ whole under either kind, received worthily, rightly kept, rightly adored. The hour, the cup, the age and who is admitted are the Church's dispensation around it.

**P1** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:**

- **1.** "Christ whole under the host" is right: Session XXI ch. 3, can. 3, **rung 1**. "Adds nothing" is wrong: both kinds are the fuller sign (GIRM 281). "Concession to Protestants" is wrong: SC 55 restored it with Trent's dogmatic principles intact, and it is the usual form in the Eastern rites (CCC 1390). Discipline.

- **3b.** Mass is not celebrated in the same space before the exposed sacrament (EM 61; can. 941 §2). Discipline.
- **4.** The age is the age of discretion, about seven, with elementary knowledge sufficing (*Quam Singulari*, 1910); can. 913 asks understanding according to capacity, and can. 914 asks communion soon after the use of reason. Discipline.

**Wrong turns:** calling item 1 wholly wrong, when its first clause is rung-1 doctrine; calling the restored cup a change of doctrine; saying can. 6 obliges every parish to hold exposition; turning item 4 into the open question of the order of initiation ([2.3](02-03-confirmation.md)), when the error is the criterion of understanding transubstantiation.

**Model answer:** (1) Christ is wholly received under the host (Trent XXI can. 3, rung 1), but both kinds are the fuller sign (GIRM 281), and SC 55 restored the cup with Trent's doctrine intact: discipline. (2) Trent defined only that no divine precept requires both kinds and that the Church had just causes (cans. 1–2, rung 1); the bishop now sets the norms (GIRM 283): discipline. (3a) *Eucharisticum Mysterium* 50 urges adoration and can. 942 recommends annual exposition, whose legitimacy is rung 1 (XIII can. 6): ending it is lawful, the reason false. (3b) Mass may not be said before the exposed sacrament in the same space (can. 941 §2): discipline. (4) *Quam Singulari* fixed the age of discretion, about seven, with elementary knowledge; cann. 913–914: discipline.

**P2** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:**

- **(a) Lawful.** Can. 844 §3 (OE 27) asks only that he request it of his own accord and be properly disposed; danger of death is not a condition. His own Church may have a stricter rule, which the Directory asks Catholics to respect (ED 122), and that is his to weigh.

**Model answer:** (a) Lawful: can. 844 §3 asks only a request of his own accord and right disposition, with no danger of death required. The priest should respect any stricter rule of Stavros's own Church (ED 122). (b) Not lawful: can. 844 §4's conditions are cumulative. Danger of death, no access to her pastor and a free request are met, but she does not manifest Catholic faith in the sacrament. (c) Not lawful: can. 916 excuses only with a grave reason and no opportunity to confess, and he can confess today. He should make a spiritual communion and go to confession at five. (d) Lawful: the infirm are exempt from the fast (can. 919 §3).

**P3** *(Exegetical (a) · Evaluative (b))*

**Must hit, strict (a):**

- The two points: **on the part of the sacrament**, both body and blood should be received, because its perfection lies in both (in the full article this binds the consecrating priest); **on the part of the recipient**, reverence and caution, which the danger of spilling among a large and mixed people threatens, ground the withholding of the blood from the people.
- The words: "a prudent custom in **some churches**".
- A judgment of prudence for reverence's sake, not a claim that the sacrament is lessened (reply 2 in the full article puts its perfection in the consecration). The power is Session XXI, **ch. 2**: changes in the dispensation, the substance untouched, "for the veneration of the said sacraments".

**Must hit, any verdict (b):**

- Fix what is not open: no divine precept either way (XXI can. 1) and Christ whole and a true sacrament under either species (can. 3), both **rung 1**; one kind or two is the Church's to dispense (ch. 2), now under the bishop's norms (GIRM 283).
- Weigh the fuller sign (GIRM 281) against reverence and practice (Aquinas's spilling, numbers, ministers, hygiene).
- Concede the strongest point against your verdict.

**Wrong turns:** reading "the perfection of the sacrament lies in both" as making one-kind communion a lesser sacrament; treating the custom as a universal law in Aquinas's day; in (b), making either practice doctrinally required, or saying those who receive one kind lose grace; appealing to John 6 as a command of both kinds, which Trent denied (ch. 1).

**Model answer (a):** Aquinas distinguishes the sacrament, whose perfection lies in both species, so that the consecrating priest receives both, from the recipient, who owes a reverence and caution that the risk of spilling among a growing, mixed people makes hard to keep. "A prudent custom in some churches" marks the withholding as local practice, not law. His reason is prudence for reverence's sake, exactly the kind of change Session XXI ch. 2 claims the power to make, "their substance being untouched".

**Model answer (b), one of several:** Yes, where it can be done reverently. Nothing doctrinal is at stake: the laity are bound to neither practice (XXI can. 1), and Christ whole and a true sacrament are received under either (can. 3). So the question is the sign. Both kinds show the meal and the covenant in the blood more fully (GIRM 281), and the Church has restored them under the bishop's norms (GIRM 283). Aquinas's worry is real: with large crowds, too few ministers and the risk of spilling, reverence can suffer. That concedes the strongest point against: where those conditions cannot be met, one kind honours the sacrament better. So the default should be the cup, dropped where reverence cannot be secured.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [3.3](03-03-real-presence-and-transubstantiation.md) (Real presence and transubstantiation):** *(Exegetical.)* Three sentences from the Corpus Christi worship aid of a parish in Gorsemoor, **invented**, written for this problem:

> (1) "Trent defined that Christ remains present in the communicant for fifteen minutes after Communion, which is why we kneel in thanksgiving afterwards."
>
> (2) "We call his presence in the host 'real' because his presence in the word proclaimed and in the gathered assembly is only a figure of speech."
>
> (3) "Our Orthodox neighbours, by contrast, hold that the bread on their altars only symbolizes Christ's body."

For each sentence, write one sentence that corrects it and gives the weight of what corrects it: a rung, "no magisterial level", or, for another Church's teaching, fidelity to that Church's own texts.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Accept:** in (1), any wording that keeps the defined permanence and the explanatory test apart; in (3), either the Synod of Jerusalem or the later Orthodox writers, so long as a change is stated and nothing is added in reply.

**Must hit, strict:**

- **(1)** Trent defined that the presence is not confined to the moment of use and remains in hosts kept after communion (Session XIII can. 4, **rung 1**), but it sets no duration. The presence lasts while the species subsist (CCC 1377); when they cease is judged by Aquinas's test, whether bread would by then have been corrupted (ST III q.77 a.4), which has **no magisterial level**. The fifteen minutes is defined nowhere, and the thanksgiving needs no such warrant.
- **(2)** The presence is called "real" to mark presence par excellence, because substantial, not to deny that Christ's other presences are real (*Mysterium Fidei* 39, **rung 3**; CCC 1374). The substantial presence itself is **rung 1** (can. 1).
- **(3)** Unfaithful. The Synod of Jerusalem (1672; the Confession of Dositheus, Decree 17) says the bread is changed, transubstantiated (*metousiousthai*), made over and refashioned into the Lord's true body, truly, really and substantially, while denying that the word explains the manner. Later Orthodox writers differ over the word, some preferring *metaballō*, "to change", and declining any account of how; both affirm a change, not a symbol. Graded on fidelity: no Catholic rung applies.

**Wrong turns:** in (1), swapping the fifteen minutes for another figure, calling Aquinas's corruption test defined, or reading canon 4 as fixing how long the presence lasts in the communicant; in (2), correcting by calling the other presences "spiritual only", or giving *Mysterium Fidei* rung 1; in (3), volunteering a rebuttal, making Jerusalem 1672 the voice of every Orthodox theologian, or giving an Orthodox synod a Catholic rung.

**Model answer:** (1) Trent defined that the presence remains beyond the moment of use (Session XIII can. 4, rung 1) but fixed no duration: it lasts while the species subsist (CCC 1377), and when they cease is judged by Aquinas's test of whether bread would have been corrupted (ST III q.77 a.4), which has no magisterial level. (2) "Real" marks the substantial presence (can. 1, rung 1) as presence par excellence, without denying that Christ's other presences are real (*Mysterium Fidei* 39, rung 3). (3) Unfaithful to the Orthodox texts: the Synod of Jerusalem (1672) says the bread is changed and transubstantiated into the Lord's true body, truly, really and substantially, while denying that the word explains the manner, and later writers who prefer *metaballō*, "to change", still speak of a change.
