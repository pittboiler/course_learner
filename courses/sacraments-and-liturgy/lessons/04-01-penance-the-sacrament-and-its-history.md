# Sacraments & Liturgy · Lesson 4.1: Penance: the sacrament and its history

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 4: The sacraments of healing · Builds on: [1.2 Matter, form, minister, intention](01-02-matter-form-minister-intention.md), [1.3 Ex opere operato, character, and fruitfulness](01-03-ex-opere-operato-character-and-fruitfulness.md), [3.5 Communion, reservation, adoration](03-05-communion-reservation-adoration.md), [`theology-of-debt` 3.4](../../theology-of-debt/lessons/03-04-aquinas-satisfaction-and-the-debt-of-punishment.md), [`moral-theology` 3.4](../../moral-theology/lessons/03-04-sin-mortal-and-venial.md) · Unlocks: [4.2 Indulgences as a practice](04-02-indulgences-as-a-practice.md), [4.3 Anointing of the sick](04-03-anointing-of-the-sick.md)

## Why this matters

Baptism forgives everything, once, and cannot be repeated. What of a baptized Christian who then commits apostasy or murder? The answer is a sacrament whose outward shape changed more than any other's: public and once in a lifetime, then private and repeatable, then fixed by Trent. This lesson states its matter, form, minister and effects at their levels, and tells the history without hiding changes.

## The idea

**The problem is sin after baptism.** Had the baptized kept their justice, Trent says, no second sacrament of forgiveness would be needed (Session XIV, ch. 1). Hebrews 6:4–6 calls it "impossible" for the fallen "to be renewed again to penance"; the early fight over that verse is [`new-testament` 6.2](../../new-testament/lessons/06-02-hebrews.md)'s. Hermas (*Shepherd*, Mandate 4.3) allowed one repentance after baptism. Tertullian, still a Catholic, gave the image (*On Repentance* 7, ANF 3):

> In the vestibule He has stationed the second repentance for opening to such as knock: but now once for all, because now for the second time; but never more...

Novatian denied that the Church could reconcile apostates at all; Carthage in 251 graded their penance instead ([`patristics` 2.4](../../patristics/lessons/02-04-cyprian-of-carthage.md)).

**Three shapes of one power.** In [canonical penance](../reference.md#canonical-penance) the grave sinner did public penance for years in an order of penitents, barred from communion until the bishop reconciled him, in some regions once in a lifetime (CCC 1447), so many waited for their deathbed. [Tariff penance](../reference.md#tariff-penance), carried onto the continent by Irish missionaries from the seventh century (CCC 1447), was private and repeatable, with penitential books pricing each sin ([`theology-of-debt` 3.5](../../theology-of-debt/lessons/03-05-the-christian-jubilee-and-the-treasury.md)); in time absolution came at the confession, satisfaction after. Then the scholastic settlement: three acts of the penitent, absolution as form, and the indicative "I absolve thee" where formulas had been prayers into the twelfth century (defended in ST III q.84 a.3). Lateran IV, canon 21 (1215; [`church-history` 5.1](../../church-history/lessons/05-01-papal-monarchy-innocent-iii-to-boniface-viii.md)), bound everyone past the age of discretion to confess to his own priest yearly, and forbade the priest to betray the sinner by word, sign or any other means.

## Source

Leo the Great, Letter 168 (*Magna indignatione*), to the bishops of Campania, Samnium and Picenum, 6 March 459; the lesson's translation of the Latin in Denzinger (NPNF prints only a summary).

> That presumption too, against the apostolic rule, which I have learned some commit by unlawful usurpation, I decree be removed by every means: namely, as to the penance the faithful ask for, that no confession written in a booklet, giving the kind of each sin, be read out in public. For it suffices that the guilt of consciences be made known to priests alone by secret confession. Faith that does not shrink from blushing before men for fear of God may seem praiseworthy; yet not everyone's sins are such that those asking for penance would not fear to publish them. Let so objectionable a custom be removed. ... For that confession suffices which is offered first to God, then to the priest, who comes as intercessor for the penitents' offences. Only so can more be drawn to penance: if the confessing person's conscience is not published to the people.

Read the shape of the act. It is a disciplinary decree (*constituo*), not a definition, from the heart of the canonical era. What was public was the *penance*, not the list of sins: secret confession to priests "suffices". The priest is *precator*, intercessor; absolution is still prayer. Trent agrees: secret confession was in use from the beginning, and no divine precept commands public confession (XIV ch. 5). The letter does not show a repeatable private penance, or an indicative form.

## The doctrine

Defining text: Trent, Session XIV (25 November 1551), chs. 1–9 and canons 1–15 on penance (Waterworth).

1. **Penance is its own sacrament.** A true sacrament, instituted by Christ for reconciling the faithful "as often as they fall into sin after baptism" (can. 1), distinct from baptism (can. 2). Christ instituted it when, risen, he breathed on the apostles: "Whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them: and whose sins you shall retain, they are retained" (John 20:23, DR; can. 3). The verbs are *aphēte*, "you release", and *kratēte*, "you hold fast" (lesson's gloss). Releasing or holding is a judgement, Trent argues, and no one judges a cause he does not know, so grave sins must be told (ch. 5). Matthew 18:18, the plural "whatsoever you shall bind", serves for the minister (ch. 6, can. 10), while 16:19 is only alluded to in "the keys" (chs. 5, 8; both verses as primacy texts, [`ecclesiology-and-mariology` 4.1](../../ecclesiology-and-mariology/lessons/04-01-the-petrine-texts.md)). Trent does not cite James 5:16's confession "one to another". *In words:* baptism forgives once; this sacrament forgives by judgement, as often as needed.
2. **Matter and form.** The penitent's [three acts](../reference.md#acts-of-the-penitent), contrition, confession and satisfaction, are "as it were the matter" (can. 4): real matter, the Roman Catechism explains, but not applied from outside like water. (Scotus had made the absolution alone the sacrament.) The form is in "I absolve thee" (ch. 3). In the Latin rite, after a prayer for pardon and peace (CCC 1449):

   > *Ego te absolvo a peccatis tuis in nomine Patris, et Filii, et Spiritus Sancti.* (I absolve you from your sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit; lesson's translation.)

   The Byzantine formulas are invocations (CCC 1481). *In words:* the penitent supplies the sign's stuff by what he does; the priest's sentence makes it a sacrament.
3. **[Contrition and attrition](../reference.md#contrition-and-attrition).** Contrition is sorrow for sin with the purpose of sinning no more (ch. 4). Perfected by charity, it reconciles before the sacrament, though not without the desire of it. Attrition, sorrow from sin's ugliness or fear of hell, is a gift of God if it excludes the will to sin and hopes for pardon: it cannot justify without the sacrament, but disposes for grace within it (ch. 4; can. 5). Whether it needs some beginning of love of God divided contritionists from attritionists until the Holy Office (5 May 1667, under Alexander VII) forbade either side to censure the other pending a decision. None has come. *In words:* fear of hell is a graced start; whether it needs love is open.
4. **Confession** is of divine right (can. 6): every mortal sin remembered after diligent examination, by kind and number, with the circumstances that change the kind (can. 7; can. 988 §1). Lateran IV fixed only the yearly minimum (ch. 5; can. 8; can. 989). *In words:* the duty is Christ's; "yearly" is the Church's floor.
5. **The minister.** Priests alone absolve, even priests in mortal sin (can. 10). Absolution is "after the manner of a judicial act" (ch. 6; can. 9), a tribunal of mercy and a healing (*Reconciliatio et Paenitentia*, 1984, §31). Orders are not enough: absolution without jurisdiction is void (ch. 7), and the Code requires the faculty for validity (can. 966 §1), save in danger of death, when any priest absolves from every sin and censure (can. 976). Bishops may reserve cases with effect before God (can. 11). The Code now reserves censures, not sins, and a confessor may lift an undeclared automatic excommunication when waiting would be hard, imposing recourse within a month (can. 1357). *In words:* ordination gives the power; the Church gives the right to use it on this penitent.
6. **The [seal](../reference.md#seal-of-confession)** is "inviolable": the confessor may not betray a penitent in any way or for any reason (can. 983 §1), nor use the knowledge to his detriment even without revelation (can. 984 §1; the Holy Office condemned the contrary in 1682). Direct violation incurs automatic excommunication reserved to the Apostolic See (can. 1386 §1, as revised in 2021). The secret "admits of no exceptions" (CCC 1467); the Apostolic Penitentiary's note of 2019 grounds it in divine law and denies any human power jurisdiction over it. Aquinas: the priest knows it "not as man, but as God knows it" (Supp. q.11 a.1 ad 1). *In words:* no case lets a confessor speak.
7. **[General absolution](../reference.md#general-absolution).** Individual, integral confession is the only ordinary way (can. 960; RP §33). Absolving many at once is allowed only in imminent danger of death, or in grave necessity as the diocesan bishop judges it: too few confessors, leaving penitents long without grace or communion through no fault of their own; a feast-day crowd does not count (can. 961). It is received validly only by one disposed and intending to confess each grave sin individually (can. 962 §1), before any further general absolution (can. 963). *Misericordia Dei* (2002) tightened the practice. *In words:* an emergency exit that cancels no confession owed.
8. **Effects.** Reconciliation with God (ch. 3) and with the Church (can. 959). Guilt and eternal punishment are remitted; temporal punishment can remain (can. 15), and satisfaction through Christ's merits answers to it (can. 13; [`theology-of-debt` 3.4](../../theology-of-debt/lessons/03-04-aquinas-satisfaction-and-the-debt-of-punishment.md); indulgences, [4.2](04-02-indulgences-as-a-practice.md)). *In words:* friendship restored, a remainder to work off.

**Mark the levels** ([`fundamental-theology` 4.4](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-04-the-ladder-of-doctrinal-authority.md); [levels](../reference.md#levels-of-authority)). **Rung 1**, Session XIV's canons: a true, repeatable sacrament (cans. 1–2); John 20 (can. 3); the three acts (can. 4); sorrow from fear (can. 5); confession of divine right and integral (cans. 6–7, as [`moral-theology` 1.1](../../moral-theology/lessons/01-01-what-moral-theology-is.md) grades can. 7); absolution judicial, by priests alone (cans. 9–10); reservation (can. 11); satisfaction and remaining punishment (cans. 13, 15). **Taught in the chapters, not defined:** the form (ch. 3); perfect contrition reconciling with the desire of the sacrament (ch. 4); jurisdiction (ch. 7). The seal's admitting no exception: **at least rung 3** (CCC 1467; the 2019 note), never defined. **Law, no rung:** the yearly precept (which canon 8 protects), faculties, penalties, general absolution, Leo's decree. **Rung 5, by decree:** whether attrition needs love (1667). Thomist and Scotist accounts of the matter are school theology; the once-only rule was a discipline, later dropped.

## The timeline

```mermaid
graph LR
  A["2nd c.: one repentance<br/>after baptism"] --> B["3rd c. on: canonical penance<br/>public, once in a life"]
  B --> C["459 Leo: sins told<br/>to priests alone"]
  C --> D["7th c. on: tariff penance<br/>private, repeatable"]
  D --> E["absolution moves<br/>before satisfaction"]
  E --> F["12th-13th c.: three acts,<br/>'I absolve thee'"]
  F --> G["1215 Lateran IV c. 21:<br/>yearly confession, seal"]
  G --> H["1551 Trent XIV defines"]
  H --> I["1983 Code; 2002<br/>Misericordia Dei"]
```

Publicity, frequency, the timing of absolution and the form's grammar changed; the Church holds that the power to bind and loose, and the telling of grave sin to its minister, did not.

## Worked examples

**Ex 1 (clean): an ordinary confession.** Invented: Leif Sandvik, conscious of two grave sins, goes to his pastor in Lent, sorry chiefly from fear of hell and resolved not to sin again. He names both by kind and number, accepts a penance, and is absolved with the Latin form. Quasi-matter: attrition with a purpose of amendment (can. 987), integral confession, satisfaction accepted. Form: said. Minister: a priest with the faculty by office (can. 968 §1). [Verdicts](../reference.md#valid-licit-fruitful): **valid**, **licit**, and **fruitful**, since attrition disposes within the sacrament and he placed no obstacle ([1.3](01-03-ex-opere-operato-character-and-fruitfulness.md)).

**Ex 2 (strain): the seal when an innocent man may pay.** Invented: a penitent confesses a fatal hit-and-run for which his neighbour has been charged. The priest may not tell the police, testify, hint, or treat the penitent differently (cann. 983–984). Inside the confession he can urge the penitent to prevent the injustice, and judge whether a man who will let his neighbour be convicted has the purpose of amendment absolution requires (can. 987). The cost is real, and the answer does not move (CCC 1467). The priest knew the sin only as God's minister, and secrecy draws penitents to confess (Supp. q.11 a.1). Civil compulsion meets the same answer (the 2019 note).

## Watch out

- **You might think the Lateran precept makes confession obligatory.** Confessing grave sin is of divine right (can. 6); Lateran fixed the yearly minimum (ch. 5).
- **You might think Trent merely tolerates attrition.** Canon 5 defines sorrow from fear as true and preparing for grace (rung 1).
- **Levels: the seal is neither a defined dogma nor a rule that bends in hard cases.** It is law, taught as exceptionless and grounded in divine law: at least rung 3.

## One-liner

> One power to bind and loose under changing disciplines: the penitent brings sorrow, confession and satisfaction, the priest gives sentence, and the seal admits no exception.

## Problems

**P1 (🟢)** *(Exegetical)* An **invented** hospital chaplaincy leaflet, written for this problem, for the invented Aldermoor General Hospital:

> (1) "If no priest is free, our deacon chaplain can hear your confession and give you absolution."
>
> (2) "If you are dying, please ask for the hospital chaplain: a visiting priest from outside the diocese has no faculty here and cannot absolve you."
>
> (3) "What you say in confession is confidential, but the chaplain may share anything relevant to your care with the medical team."

For each sentence, say what is wrong, give the correct statement with the canon or Trent text that fixes it, and give the level where a level is involved. Two sentences per item.

**P2 (🟡)** *(Exegetical)* **Invented**, written for this problem. On the Saturday before Christmas, 300 people come to a penance service at Holy Family parish in the invented Diocese of Hartsmere. Two priests are present; hearing everyone would take five hours. The bishop has determined no case of grave necessity for the diocese. After a communal examination of conscience and an act of contrition, the pastor gives general absolution, adding: "No need to come back for individual confession; this covers everything." Two penitents are each conscious of one grave sin and sorry for it. Ottilie Brandt intends to confess hers individually in January. Noemi Castellanos came because the service offered a way never to tell a priest, and she intends never to.

(a) Was the general absolution lawfully given? Name the canon and the condition that decides.

(b) For each penitent give three verdicts, valid, licit and fruitful, naming the element that decides each. Two sentences per penitent.

**P3 (🔴)** *(Exegetical)* Trent, Session XIV, ch. 7, "On the Reservation of Cases" (Waterworth, abridged):

> ...it has ever been firmly held in the Church of God, and this Synod ratifies it as a thing most true, that the absolution, which a priest pronounces upon one over whom he has not either an ordinary or a delegated jurisdiction, ought to be of no weight whatever. And it hath seemed to our most holy Fathers to be of great importance to the discipline of the Christian people, that certain more atrocious and more heinous crimes should be absolved, not by all priests, but only by the highest priests... But it is consonant to the divine authority, that this reservation of cases have effect, not merely in external polity, but also in God's sight. Nevertheless, for fear lest any may perish on this account, it has always been very piously observed in the said Church of God, that there be no reservation at the point of death...

(a) Name the chapter's two claims about the priest's power and its one exception, and say what the first claim implies for a priest who has orders but no faculty.

(b) Give the level of the claim that reservation holds "in God's sight", citing the canon. Then say how the current Code handles reservation, and how an ordinary confessor can help a penitent bound by an undeclared automatic excommunication outside danger of death. Two sentences per part.

<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:**

- **(1) The deacon.** Priests alone are ministers of absolution: Trent, Session XIV, can. 10, **rung 1**; can. 965. A deacon's "absolution" is no sacrament at all; he can pray with the patient and help him make an act of contrition, which, perfected by charity and joined to the desire of the sacrament, reconciles before it is received (ch. 4, taught).
- **(2) The dying.** In danger of death any priest, even one lacking the faculty, absolves **validly and licitly** from all censures and sins, even if an approved priest is present (can. 976; law). Trent's ch. 7 records the same practice: no reservation at the point of death.
- **(3) The seal.** The seal is inviolable: the confessor may not betray the penitent in any manner or for any reason (can. 983 §1), nor use the knowledge to his detriment (can. 984 §1). That it admits no exception is **at least rung 3** (CCC 1467; the Apostolic Penitentiary's 2019 note), and direct violation incurs automatic excommunication reserved to the Apostolic See (can. 1386 §1, law).

**Wrong turns:** calling the deacon's act valid but illicit (a deacon is not a minister of this sacrament, so nothing is conferred); granting the visiting priest validity but not liceity, when can. 976 says both; treating the seal like medical confidentiality, which has exceptions (the 2019 note says the seal is not comparable to professional secrecy); calling the seal a defined dogma.

**Model answer:** (1) Only priests absolve (Trent XIV can. 10, rung 1; can. 965), so a deacon cannot. He can help the patient make an act of contrition, which with the desire of the sacrament reconciles before it is received. (2) Wrong: in danger of death any priest, with or without a faculty, absolves validly and licitly from every sin and censure (can. 976). Trent records the same rule: no reservation at the point of death. (3) Wrong: the seal forbids betraying a penitent in any way and for any reason, and forbids using the knowledge against him (cann. 983–984). It admits no exception (CCC 1467, at least rung 3), and the patient's care team is not one.

---

**P2** *(Exegetical)*

**Accept:** for Ottilie's validity, any answer that rests it on can. 962 §1 and reads can. 961 as a prohibition rather than an invalidating law; noting the pastor's own fault is extra.

**Must hit, strict (a):**

- **Unlawful.** Can. 961 §1 2° says grave necessity does not exist when confessors are lacking "only" because of the large number of penitents at a great feast or pilgrimage, and §2 reserves the judgement to the diocesan bishop, who has made none. The pastor's added sentence also contradicts can. 962 §1 and can. 963, and the duty to instruct in can. 962 §2.

**Must hit, strict (b):**

- **Ottilie.** **Valid**: the Code puts the conditions for valid reception on the penitent's side, proper disposition and the intention to confess each grave sin individually (can. 962 §1), and she has both; can. 961 forbids but contains no invalidating clause (can. 10). **Illicit** as celebrated, the fault being the pastor's (can. 961), not hers. **Fruitful**: she is reconciled. She must still confess the sin individually as soon as she can and before any further general absolution (can. 963).
- **Noemi.** **Invalid** for her: the deciding element is the recipient's intention, which can. 962 §1 requires for validity and she lacks; *Misericordia Dei* §7 repeats it. **Illicit** as celebrated. **Not fruitful**: no sacramental forgiveness was received, and her grave sin still has to be confessed.

**Wrong turns:** collapsing the verdicts, either "unlawful, therefore invalid for everyone" or "absolved, so Ottilie need not confess"; reading can. 961 as an invalidating law; making Noemi's absolution valid because the priest acted validly, when validity here turns on the recipient; treating a Christmas crowd as grave necessity.

**Model answer:** (a) Unlawful. Can. 961 §1 2° excludes necessity that arises only from a feast-day crowd, and the bishop, who alone judges it (§2), has determined none. (b) Ottilie received it validly, since she is disposed and intends individual confession (can. 962 §1), and fruitfully. The celebration was illicit through the pastor's fault, and she must still confess the sin before another general absolution (can. 963). Noemi lacks the intention can. 962 §1 requires, so the absolution is invalid for her and bears no fruit. The celebration was illicit as well, and her sin remains to be confessed.

---

**P3** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict (a):**

- **Claim 1:** absolution given by a priest without ordinary or delegated jurisdiction over the penitent is "of no weight whatever", that is, void. **Claim 2:** popes, and bishops in their dioceses, may reserve graver cases to themselves, and the reservation holds before God, not only in external government. **Exception:** none at the point of death.
- **Implication:** orders alone do not make a valid absolution. The power must be usable over this penitent, which the Code now expresses as the faculty required for validity (can. 966 §1).

**Must hit, strict (b):**

- **Level:** **rung 1**. Can. 11 anathematizes anyone who says bishops may reserve cases only as regards external polity. Which cases are reserved at any time is law, with no rung.
- **Today:** the Code reserves certain censures, not sins as such; examples are direct violation of the seal (can. 1386 §1) and absolving an accomplice (can. 1384), both reserved to the Apostolic See. An ordinary confessor may remit, in the sacramental forum, an undeclared automatic excommunication or interdict when it is hard for the penitent to remain in grave sin meanwhile, imposing recourse within a month (can. 1357); in danger of death, can. 976 applies.

**Wrong turns:** reading "of no weight" as merely unlawful; grading reservation as mere discipline (can. 11 defines its force before God) or, the other way, treating today's list of reserved censures as defined; saying the current Code still reserves sins; sending the penitent to Rome when can. 1357 lets the confessor act now.

**Model answer:** (a) The chapter holds that absolution without ordinary or delegated jurisdiction is void, and that the highest priests may reserve graver cases with effect before God; at the point of death there is no reservation. So a priest with orders but no faculty absolves invalidly outside danger of death, which can. 966 §1 now states. (b) That reservation binds before God is defined, rung 1 (can. 11); the particular reservations are law. The current Code reserves censures rather than sins, and can. 1357 lets any confessor lift an undeclared automatic excommunication in confession when waiting would be hard, on condition of recourse within a month.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [3.4](03-04-the-sacrifice-of-the-mass.md) (The sacrifice of the Mass):** *(Exegetical.)* Aquinas asks whether the Eucharist benefits anyone besides those who receive it (*Summa Theologiae* III q.79 a.7, English Dominican Province, abridged):

> ...this sacrament is not only a sacrament, but also a sacrifice. For, it has the nature of a sacrifice inasmuch as in this sacrament Christ's Passion is represented ... and it has the nature of a sacrament inasmuch as invisible grace is bestowed in this sacrament under a visible species. So, then, this sacrament benefits recipients by way both of sacrament and of sacrifice, because it is offered for all who partake of it. ... But to others who do not receive it, it is beneficial by way of sacrifice, inasmuch as it is offered for their salvation.

(a) **Invented**, written for this problem: Mbali Dlamini has a Mass offered for her son, who is working at sea and will not even know when it is said. She means to receive Communion at it, she says, "so that he gets the sacrament's grace through me". By the passage, how does the Mass benefit her son, and what does her Communion pass to him as a sacrament? **Two sentences.**

(b) Trent condemns anyone who says the Mass "profits him only who receives" (Session XXII, can. 3). Give the level of that canon and the level of Aquinas's account, in this passage, of *how* the benefit reaches each group; then name the error on each side. **Two sentences.**

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Accept:** in (a), a note that she may pray for him at Communion, so long as the sacrament's own grace stays with her; in (b), a note that the Eucharist's being both sacrament and sacrifice is itself Trent's teaching (Sessions XIII and XXII), so long as Aquinas's account of how each benefits stays on the explanation side.

**Must hit, strict (a):**

- **By way of sacrifice.** The Mass is offered for his salvation, and that is the one route the passage opens to those who do not receive.
- **Nothing as a sacrament.** The sacrament's grace is "bestowed ... under a visible species" on the one who receives it. Her Communion profits her, as sacrament and as sacrifice; it passes no sacramental grace to him.

**Must hit, strict (b):**

- **Can. 3: rung 1.** That the Mass profits others besides the one who receives is defined.
- **Aquinas's how: a school's explanation, no magisterial level.** The two "natures" with their grounds, the sacrament profiting by reception and the sacrifice by being offered for someone, belong in the right-hand column of 3.4's table, like his q.79 a.5 answer on how much a Mass avails.
- **The two errors.** Overstatement: reading the article's mechanism as what Trent defined. Understatement: treating the benefit to those absent as Aquinas's opinion, open to denial, because his explanation is.

**Wrong turns:** in (a), saying her Communion "counts for" him as a sacrament; concluding that since he receives nothing the Mass does nothing for him, which the passage and can. 3 both deny; treating "by way of sacrifice" as a lesser, merely symbolic benefit, when the sacrifice is true and propitiatory. In (b), giving Aquinas's account the canon's rung because Trent and the Catechism also speak of the sacrifice as offered for others; demoting can. 3 to theology because the *how* is open.

**Model answer:** (a) The Mass benefits him by way of sacrifice, because it is offered for his salvation, which is the route the passage opens to those who do not receive. Her Communion profits her as sacrament and as sacrifice, but as a sacrament it passes nothing to him, since its grace is given under the visible species to the one who receives. (b) That the Mass profits more than the one who receives is defined (Session XXII, can. 3, rung 1), while Aquinas's account of how it reaches each group, the sacrament by reception and the sacrifice by being offered for someone, is a school's explanation with no magisterial level. Giving his account the canon's weight overstates it; treating the benefit to those absent as merely his opinion, because his explanation is open, understates the canon.
