# General Biology — Syllabus

> Life Sciences · Tier 0 · ~17 lessons · Prereqs: none · Roadmap id: `general-biology`

## Goal

Build the one working model of life every later course quietly assumes: cells running on chemistry, storing information in DNA, copying and dividing, inheriting traits by rules, and being reshaped over deep time by selection. You'll finish able to reason from molecule to organism to population — the through-line, not the memorized parts. It deliberately stays shallow on biochemical mechanism (that's `molecular-cell-biology` and `biochemistry`) and skips organismal-anatomy detail; here we learn the field's spine so its branches make sense.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Explain why water, carbon, and the four biomolecule families make cellular life possible
- [ ] Distinguish prokaryotic from eukaryotic cells and name what each organelle is *for*
- [ ] Trace an enzyme's job and predict how it bends a reaction's rate and equilibrium
- [ ] Sketch how respiration and photosynthesis move energy and carbon in opposite directions
- [ ] Walk a cell through the cell cycle and say what checkpoints guard against
- [ ] Contrast mitosis and meiosis and explain why meiosis generates variation
- [ ] Solve monohybrid and dihybrid crosses and read the ratios back as biology
- [ ] Follow the central dogma from gene to protein and locate where mutations act
- [ ] State natural selection's logic and predict how allele frequencies shift under it
- [ ] Give evidence that common descent, not separate creation, explains life's pattern
- [ ] Place any organism in the tree of life and read an ecological role from its niche

## Modules

### Module 1: The cell & the molecules of life

From the chemistry that makes life possible up to the cell — life's smallest unit — and the parts inside it.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | The chemistry of life | Explain why water and carbon are the backbone of biology | polar covalent bonds, hydrogen bonding, water's properties, carbon's versatility |
| 1.2 | The four biomolecules | Identify each macromolecule family by structure and job | carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids, monomer→polymer, dehydration synthesis |
| 1.3 | Cell theory & two kinds of cell | Contrast prokaryotic and eukaryotic organization | cell theory, prokaryote vs eukaryote, surface-area-to-volume, membranes |
| 1.4 | A tour of the organelles | Name each organelle and the task it performs | nucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast, ER, Golgi, ribosome, endosymbiosis |

**Boss problem 1:** A biologist finds a newly discovered single-celled organism: it has a nucleus, mitochondria, and a cell wall of cellulose, but no chloroplasts. Classify it (prokaryote/eukaryote; likely kingdom), predict how it gets energy, and estimate whether it is larger or smaller than a typical bacterium — justifying each call from cell theory and organelle function.

### Module 2: Energy, metabolism & cell division

How cells pay for everything they do, and how they make more of themselves.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Energy, ATP & enzymes | Explain how cells store energy and speed reactions | free energy, ATP cycle, activation energy, enzymes, active site, catalysis |
| 2.2 | Cellular respiration | Trace how a glucose molecule becomes usable energy | glycolysis, citric acid cycle, electron transport, ATP yield, aerobic vs anaerobic |
| 2.3 | Photosynthesis | Trace how light and CO₂ become sugar | light reactions, Calvin cycle, chlorophyll, carbon fixation, respiration mirror |
| 2.4 | The cell cycle | Walk a cell through growth, replication, and division | interphase, S phase, checkpoints, cancer as loss of control |
| 2.5 | Mitosis & meiosis | Contrast the two divisions and explain why meiosis makes variation | mitosis phases, meiosis I/II, homologous pairs, crossing over, haploid vs diploid |

**Boss problem 2:** A cell with 8 chromosomes (2n = 8) enters division. (a) After mitosis, how many cells, and how many chromosomes each? (b) After meiosis? (c) Explain, in energy terms, why the dividing cell must have run respiration first. (d) A drug freezes the spindle at metaphase — name one process it halts in *each* division and the consequence for the daughter cells.

### Module 3: Genetics & the central dogma

The rules by which traits pass on, and the molecular machinery underneath those rules.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Mendel & the monohybrid cross | Predict offspring ratios from one gene | alleles, dominant/recessive, Punnett square, segregation, genotype vs phenotype |
| 3.2 | Dihybrid crosses & beyond | Handle two genes and the exceptions to Mendel | independent assortment, 9:3:3:1, incomplete dominance, codominance, linkage |
| 3.3 | DNA structure & replication | Explain how DNA stores and copies information | double helix, base pairing, semiconservative replication, the genetic alphabet |
| 3.4 | The central dogma | Follow a gene from DNA to protein | transcription, translation, codons, the genetic code, mutation types |

**Boss problem 3:** Two heterozygous pea plants (round-yellow, RrYy) are crossed. (a) Give the phenotype ratio of the offspring. (b) A single base is deleted early in the gene for round shape — predict the effect on that protein and explain via the reading frame. (c) One offspring is round but yellow-green, intermediate between the parents' yellows — which non-Mendelian pattern explains this, and what does it imply about the two yellow alleles?

### Module 4: Evolution & the diversity of life

The single idea that unifies all of biology, and the living world it produced.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Natural selection | State the logic of selection and what it requires | variation, heritability, differential fitness, adaptation, artificial selection |
| 4.2 | Evolution in populations | Predict how allele frequencies change over time | gene pool, Hardy-Weinberg intuition, drift, gene flow, speciation, common descent |
| 4.3 | The tree of life | Place any organism in life's classification | domains, kingdoms, phylogeny, homology vs analogy, reading a tree |
| 4.4 | A taste of ecology | Read an organism's role from its environment | ecosystems, trophic levels, energy flow, niches, population dynamics |

**Boss problem 4:** On an island, a beetle population is 84% dark (allele *B*, dominant) and 16% light (*b*). A new predator hunts by sight on the now-pale volcanic soil. (a) Using selection logic, predict which allele's frequency rises and why. (b) Sketch how the phenotype ratio shifts over several generations. (c) The island later splits in two by a lava flow — explain how this could, over deep time, produce two beetle species, and what tree-of-life evidence would later reveal their shared ancestry.

## Sources of truth

- Campbell *Biology* — for scope, sequencing, and the standard undergrad framing.
- Alberts *Molecular Biology of the Cell* — for the cell- and molecular-level treatments (kept light here).
- Freeman *Biological Science* — for the "reason it through, don't memorize" pedagogy this course follows.
