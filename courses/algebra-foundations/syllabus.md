# Algebra I & II — Syllabus

> Foundations · Tier F · ~12 lessons · Prereqs: none · Roadmap id: `algebra-foundations`

## Goal

Build the symbol-pushing fluency everything else stands on: manipulate expressions, solve equations and inequalities, read and graph functions, and command the exponent/log machinery — fast, correct, and without a reference sheet. This is the spine that feeds `precalculus` and gets used constantly by `calc-refresher` and `micro-refresher`. Deliberately skips trigonometry (its own course), conics and sequences (`precalculus`), and anything involving limits or calculus.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Simplify any algebraic expression with confidence — like terms, distribution, order of operations
- [ ] Solve linear equations and inequalities, and flip the sign at the right moment
- [ ] Say whether a relation is a function, and state its domain and range
- [ ] Graph a line from any form and read slope as a rate of change
- [ ] Solve a system of linear equations by substitution, elimination, or graph — and know when there's no solution
- [ ] Apply the exponent laws in both directions without hesitation
- [ ] Multiply and factor polynomials, including the tricky $ax^2+bx+c$ case
- [ ] Solve a quadratic three ways — factoring, completing the square, the quadratic formula — and pick the fastest
- [ ] Simplify, add, and divide rational expressions, and rationalize a radical
- [ ] Read exponential growth/decay, and solve for the exponent with logarithms

## Modules

### Module 1: Expressions & linear equations

The grammar of algebra: what a symbol means, and how to isolate the one you want.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Variables & expressions | Evaluate, combine like terms, and distribute without slips | variables, like terms, distributive property, order of operations |
| 1.2 | Linear equations & inequalities | Solve for the unknown and translate a word problem into one equation | isolating a variable, inverse operations, inequality sign-flip, interval notation |

**Boss problem 1:** Plan A charges a 20-dollar base fee plus 0.10 per minute; Plan B is 35 dollars flat. Write each cost as an expression in minutes, set up the inequality for when A is cheaper, solve it, and state the break-even minute in plain English.

### Module 2: Functions, lines & systems

From "an expression" to "a machine with inputs and outputs" — then two machines at once.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The function concept | Decide if a rule is a function and state its domain and range | function notation $f(x)$, domain/range, vertical line test |
| 2.2 | The coordinate plane, slope & lines | Graph a line from any form and read slope as a rate | coordinate plane, slope, slope-intercept & point-slope form, intercepts |
| 2.3 | Systems of linear equations | Solve two equations three ways and diagnose no/infinite solutions | substitution, elimination, graphical intersection, consistency |

**Boss problem 2:** Demand is $p = 100 - 2q$ and supply is $p = 10 + q$. Find the equilibrium $(q,p)$ by solving the system, graph both lines, and explain what the intersection means — the same equilibrium you'll compute again in `micro-refresher`.

### Module 3: Polynomials & factoring

Exponent rules first, then multiplying and un-multiplying whole expressions.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Exponents & polynomial operations | Apply every exponent law and add/multiply polynomials | product/quotient/power rules, negative & zero exponents, FOIL, degree |
| 3.2 | Factoring | Factor by inspection, grouping, and special patterns | GCF, difference of squares, trinomial factoring, factor by grouping |

**Boss problem 3:** Expand $(2x-3)(x+4)$, then — going the other way — factor $6x^2 + x - 12$ completely and verify by re-multiplying. Explain why factoring is "harder" than expanding.

### Module 4: Quadratics, rationals & radicals

The three hardest expression types in Tier F, each tamed.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Quadratic equations | Solve any quadratic and know which method is fastest | factoring, completing the square, quadratic formula, discriminant, vertex |
| 4.2 | Rational expressions | Simplify, multiply, and add fractions of polynomials | common denominators, cancellation, excluded values, complex fractions |
| 4.3 | Radicals & rational exponents | Simplify roots, rationalize, and convert to fractional powers | simplifying radicals, rationalizing denominators, $x^{m/n}$ notation |

**Boss problem 4:** A ball's height is $h(t) = -16t^2 + 32t + 48$ feet. Find when it lands by factoring, confirm with the quadratic formula, then complete the square to get the maximum height and the time it occurs. State the discriminant's sign and what it told you in advance.

### Module 5: Exponentials & logarithms

The inverse pair that runs growth, decay, and half of finance.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Exponential functions | Read and graph growth vs decay and set up an exponential model | $b^x$, growth/decay rate, base $e$, exponential vs linear |
| 5.2 | Logarithms | Define a log as an inverse and use the laws to solve for an exponent | $\log_b$, product/quotient/power laws, change of base, solving $b^x = c$ |

**Boss problem 5:** An investment grows by 5% a year: $A = P(1.05)^t$. Using logarithms, find how long it takes to double, then explain why the doubling time doesn't depend on $P$ — the reasoning you'll reuse for continuous growth in `calc-refresher`.
