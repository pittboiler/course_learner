# Empirical Political Economy — Syllabus

> Politics & Society · Tier 2 · ~22 lessons · Prereqs: [`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md), [`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `empirical-political-economy`

## Goal

[`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md) derives what a set of political rules should produce; [`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) builds the designs that turn observational data into causal claims. This course is the join: how political questions actually get identified and answered with data, and how much of the resulting literature survives contact with replication. You will finish able to state the estimand behind a political claim before choosing a design, read a close-election regression discontinuity and say what population its estimate is for, run a manipulation test and say what a failed one proves, set up a difference-in-differences test of an electoral reform and account for staggered adoption, compute intent-to-treat and complier effects and a minimum detectable effect for a turnout experiment, sign the bias in a cross-country institutions regression, build and criticize a shift-share exposure measure, turn a corpus into a measure of political position, and read a specification curve and a funnel plot for what publication filtering has done to a published mean. The running discipline is that every result is reported as *the estimates in this literature*, with the design that produced them and the assumption they rest on named — several of the field's most cited findings have failed replication, and the course says so where that is the case rather than after the fact. It deliberately skips every estimator's theory ([`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md)), the formal models being tested ([`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md)), institutional theory and the political economy of autocracy (`institutions-and-development`), case-based and comparative-historical explanation ([`comparative-politics`](../comparative-politics/syllabus.md)), and every normative question about whether the outcomes measured here are good ([`political-philosophy`](../political-philosophy/syllabus.md)).

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md). This is an econometrics-style course, so most parts are **formal** (archetype 8) and graded as in any math course, with one evaluative part per problem set carrying the judgment:

- **Formal: the identification argument.** State the estimand, write the design's identifying assumption for *this* application, and name the one observable pattern that would falsify it. Graded strictly — a design is right or wrong for a question.
- **Formal: estimator algebra.** Sign an omitted-variable bias, compute a local linear RD jump, a 2x2 or staggered DiD, an intent-to-treat and a complier average effect, a Wald IV ratio, a design effect, a shift-share exposure, a minimum detectable effect. Every number in a lesson or boss problem is recomputed by script before it ships.
- **Formal: counterexample.** Build the data-generating process that breaks the design — sorting at the threshold, a differential pre-trend, an instrument with a direct path, a shift-share whose shares are the confounder.
- **Formal: read the estimate.** Given a table or a figure (bandwidths, polynomial orders, event-study leads, a specification curve, a funnel plot), say what it shows and what it hides.
- **Diagnose** (exegetical, strict) — an invented abstract, press release or campaign memo makes a causal claim; name the design it is implicitly running, the assumption doing the work, and the check that is missing. Invented material is never attributed to a real person or organization.
- **Steelman and reply / find the crux** (evaluative, verdict-neutral) — are close elections as good as random? Does the audit evidence show voters punish corruption? Did the China shock move votes, or did the places it hit already differ? Any verdict passes if the moves are made, and the must-hit moves always include naming the identifying assumption the verdict turns on and the evidence that would move it.

Two standing rules. First, **report findings as the estimates in this literature**, with the design attached: a lesson says "the close-election estimates put the incumbency advantage at ...", never "incumbency is worth ...". Second, **contested is contested** — where a famous result has failed replication, been overturned by a better design, or shrunk under a corrected standard error, the lesson says so and states both sides. Modern scholarship is copyrighted and is paraphrased with citations, never quoted at length.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Turn a prediction from [`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md) into an estimand — whose effect, of what, at what value — and name the design that could identify it
- [ ] Sign the bias in a cross-country institutions-and-growth regression and say which of the three endogeneity sources is doing the work
- [ ] Say what an index of corruption, ideology or democracy measures, and compute what classical error in it does to a coefficient
- [ ] Read a close-election RD figure and table, compute the local linear jump, and state the population the estimate is for
- [ ] Run a manipulation test on the density at a threshold, and say what a failed test does and does not prove
- [ ] State the case for and against treating close elections as as-good-as-random, and name the evidence each side rests on
- [ ] Set up a difference-in-differences test of an electoral or franchise reform, defend parallel trends with an event study, and say what staggered adoption does to the two-way fixed-effects coefficient
- [ ] Compute an intent-to-treat effect, a complier average causal effect, a design effect for cluster randomization, and a minimum detectable effect for a turnout experiment
- [ ] Say what a persuasion-rate estimate from a media natural experiment assumes, and what pattern would break it
- [ ] Build a shift-share exposure measure, compute it for two regions, and say what must be exogenous for it to identify anything
- [ ] Turn a corpus into a measure of political position or polarization, and say what the measure is validated against
- [ ] Read a specification curve and a funnel plot, and compute what filtering on significance does to the mean published estimate

## Modules

### Module 1: From a model to an estimate

Before any design: what exactly is being estimated, why political treatments are never assigned, and whether the variable in the dataset is the variable in the theory.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What an empirical political-economy paper claims | Turn a model's comparative static into an estimand, and separate testing a prediction from testing a mechanism | the estimand first (population, treatment, contrast, target parameter); potential outcomes recalled from [`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 3.1; comparative statics from [`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md) as testable claims; reduced-form vs structural tests of the same model; SUTVA in politics (campaigns spill over, districts are adjacent, a rule change hits everyone at once); the credibility revolution and what it traded away |
| 1.2 | Why political treatments are never assigned | Name and sign the selection problem behind the standard political regression | who wins, who reforms, who gets audited, who is targeted — selection on the outcome's own causes; OVB sign recalled from [`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 3.4; reverse causation and simultaneity ([`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 3.5); the cross-country regression (institutions on growth, inequality on redistribution) as the canonical failure; why "control for GDP" is usually a bad control ([`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 3.3) |
| 1.3 | Measurement before identification | Choose a measure of a political concept and say what its error does to the estimate | construct validity: measuring turnout (voter files vs self-report and its overreport bias), ideology (roll-call scaling and ideal points), corruption, democracy (indices cited to [`comparative-politics`](../comparative-politics/syllabus.md) 3.2); classical error and attenuation ([`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 3.5); non-classical error when the measure is a perception; error in the *treatment* vs error in the outcome |

**Boss problem 1:** A working paper reports that municipalities governed by a left-wing party spend 0.8 units more per capita, and concludes that "party control causes spending." (a) In the long regression the coefficient on median income is -0.4, and the regression of median income on the left-control indicator has slope -2. Taking the true effect of party control to be zero, compute the coefficient the short regression reports, and state the general omitted-variable-bias expression you used. *Formal.* (b) The paper's corruption control is an index with classical measurement error: the signal variance is 1 and the error variance is 3. Compute the reliability ratio and the coefficient a true value of 2 would show up as, and say what that does to the argument that corruption has been controlled for. *Formal.* (c) Write the estimand the paper should have stated — population, treatment, contrast, target parameter — and name one design from [`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) Module 4 that could identify it. *Formal.* (d) An invented press release from a think tank summarizes the paper as "left governments spend more, so electing them raises services." Name the design the release implicitly runs, the assumption doing the work, and the check that is missing. *Exegetical.* (e) In 300-500 words: can any cross-country regression of growth on an institutions index support a causal claim? State the strongest version of the case for, the strongest against, and the single assumption your verdict depends on. Any verdict. *Evaluative.*

### Module 2: Close elections and the regression-discontinuity design

The field's signature design, the results it produced, and the argument about whether it works at all.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The close-election RD | Read vote margin as a running variable and compute an incumbency-advantage estimate | margin of victory as the running variable, the zero threshold, sharp RD recalled in one paragraph from [`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 4.7; the continuity assumption stated for elections; incumbency advantage as the founding application; personal vs party incumbency; what the estimate is local *to* |
| 2.2 | Does the design hold? Sorting and the manipulation debate | Test for manipulation at the threshold and state both sides of the close-elections argument | density/manipulation tests (McCrary and its successors) and the binomial test on counts in a window; covariate balance at the cutoff; the challenge to close US House races on imbalance in incumbent-party covariates; later multi-country evidence reporting balance in most settings; recounts, certification and who controls them; the donut RD; why a failed density test is evidence about sorting, not proof of bias |
| 2.3 | Do parties matter? RD beyond incumbency | Apply the design to a party or candidate characteristic and say what changed the estimand | the "does the winner's party change policy?" design at the mayoral and legislative level; close-election RD for candidate characteristics (gender, occupation) and what a candidate-level contrast identifies that a party-level one does not; multi-party and multi-candidate running variables; regression-discontinuity-in-differences and dynastic or dynamic incumbency effects; convergence predictions from [`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md) 2.1 as the null being tested |
| 2.4 | Reading an RD study | Read a table of estimates across bandwidths and polynomial orders and say what it supports | local linear vs high-order global polynomials and why the second misleads; bandwidth choice, bias correction and robust confidence intervals; placebo cutoffs and placebo outcomes; the specification curve as a reading device; external validity: an estimate at a margin of zero is not an estimate for a landslide |

**Boss problem 2:** (a) In a window around the threshold, a local linear fit of an incumbent party's next-election win rate on its previous margin $m$ gives $\hat y = 0.42 + 0.30\,m$ for $m < 0$ and $\hat y = 0.58 + 0.20\,m$ for $m \ge 0$. Compute the RD estimate and state, in one sentence, the population it is for. *Formal.* (b) In the same window there are 120 races, 72 of them barely won by the incumbent party and 48 barely lost. Under the null that a race in the window falls on either side with probability one half, compute the $z$ statistic and the two-sided normal p-value. Say what the result is evidence *of*, and why it is not by itself proof that the RD estimate is biased. *Formal.* (c) Construct a data-generating process in which incumbents who barely lose can force a recount and win it with some probability, and show that the RD estimate is biased even though the density test could pass. *Formal (counterexample).* (d) The paper reports estimates of 0.16 (local linear, optimal bandwidth), 0.15 (half bandwidth), 0.17 (double bandwidth) and 0.31 (global fourth-order polynomial). Say which number you would report and why, and what a reader should conclude from the fourth entry. *Exegetical.* (e) In 300-500 words: are close elections as good as random? State the strongest case on each side, name the specific evidence each rests on, and say what would change your mind. Any verdict. *Evaluative.*

**Boss problem 3:** (a) A reform is adopted by one group of states and not another. Mean spending is 10.2 (treated, before), 12.6 (treated, after), 9.8 (control, before) and 10.9 (control, after). Compute the difference-in-differences estimate and write the two-way fixed-effects regression that would produce it. *Formal.* (b) Now suppose two units adopt at different dates. Unit A is treated from period 2, unit B from period 3; untreated potential outcomes are zero for both in all three periods; the effect of treatment is 1 in a unit's first treated period and 3 in its second. Write out the six observations, compute the average effect over all treated unit-periods, then estimate the two-way fixed-effects coefficient on the treatment indicator. Explain the gap by identifying the 2x2 comparison that uses the already-treated unit as a control, and name an estimator that avoids it ([`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 4.5). *Formal.* (c) Three governors are each observed in a first term (re-election eligible) and a second (term-limited), with tax rates (5.0, 5.6), (6.2, 6.5) and (4.4, 5.3). State what the model predicts precisely, what the estimates show, which of the model's assumptions the shortfall most plausibly indicts, and reply to the strongest objection to your reading. Any verdict. *Evaluative.*

### Module 5: Corruption, institutions and economic shocks

**Boss problem 5:** (a) An instrument has a reduced-form coefficient of 0.8 on the outcome and a first-stage coefficient of 0.4 on the endogenous regressor. Compute the just-identified IV estimate, and write it as a ratio of two reduced forms. *Formal.* (b) Suppose the instrument also has a direct effect of 0.1 on the outcome, violating exclusion. Compute the resulting bias in the IV estimate and express it as a fraction of the estimate in (a). Say what happens to that fraction as the first stage weakens, and connect it to why a weak instrument is dangerous for reasons beyond its standard error ([`econometrics`](../econometrics/syllabus.md) 3.8). *Formal.* (c) Three industries have national import-shock values 10, 2 and 0 per worker. Region 1's initial employment shares are 0.5, 0.3, 0.2; region 2's are 0.1, 0.4, 0.5. Compute each region's exposure and the difference; if the second-stage coefficient is 0.4 percentage points of vote share per unit of exposure, compute the predicted difference in vote-share movement. Then state which of the two identification stories (exogenous shocks or exogenous shares) your calculation is relying on, and name a confounder that survives the other one. *Formal.* (d) An invented ministry press release announces that the country "rose eleven places in the corruption rankings, showing the reforms are working." Name what the index measures, two reasons the movement may contain no information about corruption, and one measure that would. *Exegetical.* (e) In 300-500 words: does the settler-mortality evidence establish that institutions cause development? State the exclusion restriction precisely, give the strongest channel that would violate it, say what the data dispute does and does not settle, and name the evidence that would move you. Any verdict. *Evaluative.*

**Boss problem 6:** (a) A naive-Bayes classifier is trained on two parties' manifestos. Under party L the word probabilities are 0.02 for "jobs", 0.03 for "workers" and 0.01 for "tax"; under party R they are 0.01, 0.005 and 0.04. A new document contains "jobs" twice, "workers" once and "tax" once. Compute the log-likelihood ratio and the posterior odds under equal priors, classify the document, and name one property of real political text that the independence assumption gets badly wrong. *Formal.* (b) Legislature X has party A at ideal points $\{-1,-1,-1\}$ and party B at $\{1,1,1\}$; legislature Y has party A at $\{-3,-1,1\}$ and party B at $\{-1,1,3\}$. Compute each legislature's difference in party means, then compute the share of each party's members lying at or beyond the other party's median. Say which legislature is more polarized and on which definition, and which measure a claim about legislative gridlock should use. *Formal.* (c) A literature reports 12 estimates of the same effect. Suppose the true effect is zero, every study's estimate is normal with standard error 1, and only estimates significant at the 5 percent level are published. Compute the expected published estimate conditional on publication and a positive sign, using $E[Z \mid Z > 1.96] = \varphi(1.96)/(1-\Phi(1.96))$, and say what a funnel plot of this literature would look like. *Formal.* (d) An invented journal abstract reports that "a text-based measure of populist rhetoric predicts subsequent welfare spending across 40 countries." Name the measurement step, the identification step, and the validation the abstract does not report. *Exegetical.* (e) In 300-500 words: after this course, how much should a reader believe a single well-identified political-economy estimate published in a top journal? Use at least two specific findings from Modules 2-5, state what pre-registration does and does not fix, and reply to the strongest objection to your position. Any verdict. *Evaluative.*

The forbidden 2x2 — unit B against already-treated unit A from period 2 to 3 — equals $(1-0) - (3-1) = -1$, which is what cancels the good comparisons. - **Claims kept soft on purpose, and what the lesson author must check.** No magnitude, date, sample size or author-year is asserted anywhere above, because too many of this literature's headline numbers have moved. Before a lesson states any of these precisely, verify against the source: the size of the incumbency advantage in the close-election estimates and how it differs by country and office; the exact form of the covariate-imbalance challenge to close US House races and the scope of the later multi-country evidence reporting balance elsewhere; the social-pressure mailing effect (on the order of several percentage points, the largest in the mail literature — confirm the figure and the treatment arm it belongs to); the pooled effect and the number of studies in the coordinated information-and-accountability replication; the persuasion-rate estimates from the cable-news entry and channel-position designs, which are disputed in magnitude; the audit-program vote-loss estimates and the media-presence heterogeneity; the settler-mortality first-stage strength and the specific data objections; the China-shock vote-share coefficients and the unit of analysis they are estimated at. Where a lesson reports a number, it reports the design, the sample and the standard error with it.
- **Lesson count.** The roadmap estimated ~22 and this syllabus runs exactly 22. Module 2 takes four lessons because the close-election design is both the field's signature result and its most serious methodological dispute, and reading an RD table (2.4) is a distinct skill from building one. Module 6 takes three rather than four: text as data is scoped as a measurement step, not an NLP course, and the replication turn is one lesson because the theory of the problem is already owned by [`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md) 3.3.
- **Where it goes next.** Nothing in the roadmap has this course as a prerequisite, so it is a terminus: it is where a reader of [`political-economy`](../political-economy/syllabus.md), [`comparative-politics`](../comparative-politics/syllabus.md) or `institutions-and-development` goes to ask whether any of it is true. The natural pairings are [`philosophy-of-economics`](../philosophy-of-economics/syllabus.md) Module 6 (what a model with false assumptions can explain, once you have seen what testing one costs) and [`philosophy-of-science`](../philosophy-of-science/syllabus.md) Module 3 (the statistics wars behind Module 6's arithmetic).
