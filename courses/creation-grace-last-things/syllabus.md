# Creation, Grace & the Last Things — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 2 · ~28 lessons · Prereqs: [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `creation-grace-last-things`

## Goal

This is the arc of the whole Christian story below God and Christ: where the world and the human creature came from, what went wrong, what God does about it in us, and where it all ends. The manuals split it into four treatises — *De Deo Creante*, *De Deo Elevante*, *De Gratia*, *De Novissimis* — and this course runs them as one, because each is unintelligible without the others: original sin is defined by what it lost, grace by what it restores and exceeds, and the last things by what grace was for. You will finish able to state what the Church defines about creation out of nothing and the goodness of what God made, and to say exactly what has and has not been taught about evolution and about the origin of the human race; to state the doctrine of the human person — image of God, one substance of body and soul, a soul immediately created and immortal — and cite the councils that fixed each clause; to state original sin as Trent defines it and to judge whether a modern reinterpretation stays inside that definition; to run the theology of grace as *Summa Theologiae* I-II qq.109-114 gives it, distinguishing actual from sanctifying grace and saying what each does; to state the Catholic doctrine of justification from Trent Session VI and read the 1999 Joint Declaration for what it claims to have achieved; to say what merit is and what it is not; to lay out the *de auxiliis* controversy with Báñez and Molina each at full strength, and to say what Rome decided and what it pointedly did not; and to teach the last things — death, the particular judgment, heaven and the beatific vision, hell, purgatory, the resurrection of the body, the last judgment and the new creation — with the weight of every claim marked and the genuinely open questions, including the hope that all may be saved and the fate of infants who die unbaptised, kept open.

It deliberately skips a great deal that neighbours own. Genesis 1-11 as text — genre, composition, Near Eastern parallels — is [`old-testament`](../old-testament/syllabus.md)'s; Paul on justification as exegesis, including the New Perspective, is [`new-testament`](../new-testament/syllabus.md)'s; Augustine's anti-Pelagian works read as texts are [`patristics`](../patristics/syllabus.md)'s; the metaphysics of freedom is [`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md)'s and the metaphysics of creation and of the soul is [`thomistic-synthesis`](../thomistic-synthesis/syllabus.md)'s; the moral theology of sin is [`moral-theology`](../moral-theology/syllabus.md)'s; the problem of evil as philosophy is [`philosophy-of-religion`](../philosophy-of-religion/syllabus.md)'s; and the argued dispute with Protestants over justification is `apologetics-catholic-claims`'s. It also skips the Trinity in itself, the Incarnation and the atonement models, baptism and penance as sacraments, the Church as the place where grace is dispensed, and Marian doctrine.

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md), with the Catholic Theology stance: taught from within the tradition, with every claim's level marked on the ladder in [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.4. This course's characteristic discipline is that **this is the treatise where the gap between what is defined and what a school teaches is widest, and where the temptation to close it is strongest.** That the human race contracted a sin transmitted by propagation is defined at Trent; *how* it is transmitted is not. That grace is necessary for every salutary act is defined; that it works by physical premotion is a Dominican opinion. That God predestines is taught; that he does so after foreseeing merits, or before, is the question Rome refused to settle. That the damned exist in hell eternally is defined; that any particular person is there is not taught at all. Collapsing a level in either direction is graded strictly.

- **Close reading (archetype 7), exegetical and strict,** on public-domain texts only: Trent in Waterworth's translation (1848), especially the decree on original sin (Session V) and the decree on justification with its canons (Session VI); *Dei Filius* in Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*; Augustine's anti-Pelagian and anti-Manichean works in the *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, first series; Aquinas in the English Dominican Province translation, above all I-II qq.109-114; scripture in the Douay-Rheims (Challoner), with the lesson's own translation where a Greek or Latin word decides a reading (*eph' hō* / *in quo*, *charis*, *dikaioō*, *gratia gratum faciens*, *meritum de condigno*, *apokatastasis*). Lateran IV, Vienne, Lateran V, Orange II, Lyons II, Florence and *Benedictus Deus* have no public-domain English version that can be assumed, so they are given in the lesson's own translation of the Latin, with the Latin printed where a word decides a reading. Vatican II, the Catechism, the Joint Declaration, encyclicals and the International Theological Commission's studies are **paraphrased and cited by name**, never quoted from a copyrighted translation.
- **Assign the level (a strict exegetical form used in every module).** A list of claims, each to be placed on the ladder with the act or source that fixes it named: that God created all things out of nothing; that the world had a beginning in time; that the rational soul is of itself the form of the body; that each soul is immediately created by God; that Adam's sin is transmitted by propagation and not by imitation; that concupiscence in the baptised is not properly sin; that grace can be resisted; that the justified truly merit eternal life; that the souls of the fully purified see God before the resurrection; that the punishments of hell are eternal; that purgatory exists; that unbaptised infants go to limbo.
- **Find the crux (archetype 5), exegetical.** Augustine against Pelagius; Orange II against the Massilians; Trent against both Luther's *simul iustus et peccator* and the Pelagian reading of the same canons; Báñez against Molina; de Lubac against Garrigou-Lagrange; the Balthasar dispute over the hope for all.
- **Reconstruct (archetype 1), exegetical.** Aquinas from the last end to the necessity of grace (I-II q.109); Trent's chain of causes in justification; the argument of *Benedictus Deus* against John XXII's opinion; Molina's route from the freedom of the will to middle knowledge; the ITC's route from the universal salvific will and the necessity of baptism to a hope rather than a doctrine.
- **Diagnose (archetype 4), exegetical.** Invented homilies, catechist's handouts, funeral remarks, RCIA talks and book reviews that get the grammar wrong: "original sin just means we all make mistakes"; "grace is God helping those who help themselves"; "we know she is in heaven now"; "purgatory is a second chance"; "hell is empty"; "the soul is the real you and the body is a shell". Invented words are never attributed to a real person.
- **Evaluative parts are verdict-neutral and reserved for questions genuinely open among Catholics**: whether a modern reinterpretation of original sin (Rahner, Schoonenberg) stays inside Trent's definition; whether monogenism is still required and what *Humani Generis* actually binds; whether the natural desire to see God is de Lubac's or the commentators'; whether the Joint Declaration's differentiated consensus really reaches the sixteenth-century condemnations; Báñez against Molina on efficacious grace; whether "hell as definitive self-exclusion" is a development or a softening; whether one may hope that all are saved; whether limbo remains a tenable hypothesis. Any verdict passes if the required moves are made.
- **One apologetic part only**, in Boss 3: the moral objection that inherited guilt is unjust, as its strongest secular critics press it. The statement of the objection is graded first and strictly.
- There are **no formal parts**, and **no apologetic parts aimed at Protestants** — the justification dispute belongs to `apologetics-catholic-claims`, and this course's job is to state the Catholic doctrine accurately enough that the later argument is worth having.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] State what Lateran IV and Vatican I define about creation — out of nothing, freely, in time, both orders of creatures, for God's glory — and name the error each clause excludes
- [ ] Say what the goodness of creation commits a Catholic to, and why every form of metaphysical dualism has been condemned rather than tolerated as an option
- [ ] Say exactly what the Church has taught about evolution, in which document, at what weight, and what it has left entirely to the sciences
- [ ] State the doctrine of the human person — image of God, one substance, a soul that is the form of the body, immediately created and immortal — and cite the council that fixed each clause
- [ ] State original sin as Trent defines it: what was lost, what is transmitted, how it is transmitted and how it is taken away, and what Trent says about concupiscence in the baptised
- [ ] Judge whether a modern reinterpretation of original sin stays inside that definition, and say precisely which clause it strains
- [ ] Lay out the polygenism question: what *Humani Generis* said, at what level, and where Catholic theologians now stand, without overstating any of the three
- [ ] Distinguish actual from sanctifying grace, and operating from cooperating and prevenient from subsequent grace, and say what work each distinction does
- [ ] State the doctrine of the gratuity of the supernatural order, and lay out the natural-desire quarrel with the settled and the disputed kept apart
- [ ] Reconstruct Trent Session VI's account of justification — its causes, its increase, its loss and its recovery — and say what it denies without naming Luther
- [ ] Say what Catholic doctrine means by merit, what it excludes, and why "God crowns his own gifts" is not a concession but the whole doctrine
- [ ] State predestination as the Church teaches it, and lay out Báñez and Molina each in words that school would sign, saying what Rome decided and what it refused to decide
- [ ] Tell the five condemned Jansenist propositions from what Jansenius's defenders said he actually held, and say what the question of *droit* and *fait* was about
- [ ] Teach the four last things with their levels marked: what *Benedictus Deus* settled, what the Church defines about hell and about purgatory, and what she has never taught about any named person
- [ ] State what the resurrection of the body claims and what a glorified body is, and answer the sameness objection
- [ ] Lay out the hope for universal salvation and the limbo question as freely disputed questions, stating the strongest case on each side without pretending either is settled

## Modules

### Module 1: Creation

What the Church confesses about the world's origin: that it is made out of nothing by a free act, that everything in it is good, that it includes creatures we never see, and what all of this does and does not say about biology.

|---|---|---|---|

| 1.4 | Creation and evolution in Church teaching | Say exactly what the Church has taught about evolution, where, and at what weight | the two-questions frame: the sciences ask how, the doctrine asks from whom and why, and a creator is not a rival cause inside the world ([`thomistic-synthesis`](../thomistic-synthesis/syllabus.md) 5.2); *Providentissimus Deus* (1893) and the principle that scripture does not teach natural science; *Humani Generis* (1950): research and discussion of the human body's origin from pre-existing living matter permitted to competent inquirers, while the Catholic faith holds that souls are immediately created by God — paraphrased, with its genre and level read off ([`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) 4.5); John Paul II's 1996 message to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences and the argument over how one French phrase should be translated; Benedict XVI on creation and evolution as answers to different questions; the International Theological Commission's *Communion and Stewardship* (2004) on the image of God and on evolutionary science; what has never been taught: any timetable, any mechanism, any design inference (all ceded to [`apologetics-foundations`](../apologetics-foundations/syllabus.md) 4.2); the polygenism question named and deferred to 3.4, because it is a question about original sin |

### Module 3: Original sin

### Module 4: Grace

**Boss problem 7:** (a) Close-read 1 Corinthians 15:35-49 (Douay-Rheims, with the Greek of *sōma psychikon* and *sōma pneumatikon* supplied). Set out the objection in numbered premises, state Lateran IV's claim precisely, and give the reply the hylomorphism of 2.2 makes available, citing [`metaphysics`](../metaphysics/syllabus.md) 6.5-6.6 for what the reply borrows. *(Exegetical, then a small evaluative step.)* (c) Assign a level, and name the act or source that fixes it, to each of: that the dead will rise with their own bodies; that a glorified body is impassible, subtle, agile and clear; that Christ will come again to judge the living and the dead; that the world will be renewed rather than annihilated; that the date of the parousia is unknowable; that unbaptised infants enjoy natural happiness in limbo; that we may hope all are saved. *(Exegetical.)* (d) In 400-500 words, any verdict, on **one** of the two disputed questions of 7.3. For limbo: state what the necessity of baptism and the reality of original sin require, reconstruct the 2007 study's route to hope in numbered premises, and say what would have to change for the question to be settled. In either case, mark clearly which of your premises are defined doctrine, which are common teaching, and which are theological opinion. *(Evaluative.)*

- **What this course assumes.** From [`christology`](../christology/syllabus.md), exactly as its Notes hand them over: the Paschal Mystery and the threefold office (5.1), the models of redemption (5.2-5.4) and Christ's own merit (3.4) as what Module 5 applies to the individual, and the risen and ascended body (6.2) as what 7.1 takes as the pattern. From [`thomistic-synthesis`](../thomistic-synthesis/syllabus.md): creation, conservation and providence (5.1-5.2), the vision of God as the created intellect's possible end (4.2), the rational soul (5.3) and the natural-desire episode (6.4). [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) and [`philosophical-method`](../philosophical-method/syllabus.md) are not formal prerequisites but are used in every module; a lesson that needs either reloads the point in a sentence and links.
- **Stance.** Taught from within the tradition, with authority marked, and historically honest. The hard parts are taught as hard: that the mechanism of original sin's transmission has never been defined and that the reason *Humani Generis* gave against polygenism was stated conditionally; that Orange II, the charter of the Church's doctrine of grace, was a small local council that was effectively lost for a thousand years; that a pope taught something about the beatific vision that his successor's definition excluded; that Rome heard the *de auxiliis* case for nine years and then declined to decide it; that Jansenism was an attempt to be more Augustinian than the Church and that the Church's own rigorist century did real pastoral damage; and that the fate of unbaptised infants was answered for centuries with a hypothesis that has never been taught. None of these is softened before it is stated.
- **Authority discipline for this course's hardest claims.** (1) Creation out of nothing, in time, freely, of both orders of creatures, and the goodness of everything created, are defined (Lateran IV, Vatican I). (2) The soul as the form of the body (Vienne) and the soul's immortality and individual multiplication (Lateran V) are defined; the manuals' further psychology is not. (3) That original sin is transmitted by propagation and not by imitation is defined at Trent; *how* propagation transmits it is left open by the decree itself, and traducianism is excluded by the immediate creation of each soul. (4) *Humani Generis* is an encyclical, so authentic ordinary magisterium; its polygenism passage gives a conditional reason, and lessons never present it as a definition, nor as having been rescinded. (5) That grace is necessary, prevenient, and gratuitous is defined; physical premotion and middle knowledge are permitted school opinions, and Paul V's 1607 silence is a decision not to define, not a decision in either school's favour. (6) That the justified truly merit is defined; the *condigno* / *congruo* apparatus is scholastic vocabulary. (7) *Benedictus Deus* defines the immediacy of the vision and of hell; purgatory is defined at Lyons II, Florence and Trent, while fire, duration and every other detail are undefined and Trent expressly told preachers to leave them alone. (8) The eternity of hell's punishments is defined; that any particular person is damned has never been taught. (9) Limbo is a theological hypothesis and has never been taught, and the 2007 study is a study of the International Theological Commission approved for publication, not an act of the Magisterium — reading its weight correctly is itself a graded exercise.
- **Accuracy: verify before writing each lesson, never assert from memory.** **No Denzinger-Hünermann numbers appear in this syllabus, and none should be added to a lesson without checking it against Denzinger-Hünermann directly.** The following are used above on strong but unverified recall and must each be checked against a printed text before the lesson that carries it is published: the exact wording and clause order of *Firmiter* on the two orders of creatures and on the demons; the canons of *Dei Filius* chapter 1 on creation and their numbering; the wording and decree title of the Council of Vienne's teaching on the soul as form of the body; the text of Lateran V's *Apostolici regiminis*; the canon numbering of the Council of Carthage (418) and of Orange II, and the exact wording of Orange II's conclusion on predestination to evil; the chapter and canon numbering of Trent Session V and Session VI, in particular which chapter sets out the causes of justification and which treats merit, and the canons on free will, on merit and on the certainty of grace; the session number and content of Trent's decree on purgatory and the sentence instructing bishops to exclude difficult questions and uncertain imagery from popular preaching; the full text and clause order of *Benedictus Deus*, and the account of John XXII's sermons and his retraction; the precise wording of the *Humani Generis* passages on the origin of the body, on immediate creation of souls, on polygenism and on the gratuity of the supernatural; the date, signatories and exact claims of the *Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification*, its Annex, the 1998 CDF response, and the later associations by the Methodist and Reformed bodies; the dates of the Congregation *de Auxiliis* and the terms of Paul V's closure; the five propositions of *Cum Occasione* (1653) as the bull states them; the date and title of *Unigenitus* (1713); the title and date of the 1979 CDF letter on eschatology; the section numbers of *Gaudium et Spes* and *Lumen Gentium* cited by subject above (the lessons name them by subject unless the numbers have been checked); and every Catechism paragraph. Where a number cannot be confirmed, the document is named without it.
