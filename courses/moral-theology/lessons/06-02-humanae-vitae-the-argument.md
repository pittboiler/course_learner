# Moral Theology · Lesson 6.2: Humanae Vitae: the argument

> ⏱ ~15 min · Module 6: Sexuality and truthfulness · Builds on: [6.1 The theology of the body and chastity](06-01-the-theology-of-the-body-and-chastity.md), [4.3 Proportionalism and the fundamental option](04-03-proportionalism-and-the-fundamental-option.md), [4.4 Veritatis Splendor](04-04-veritatis-splendor.md) · Unlocks: [6.3 Humanae Vitae: its authority](06-03-humanae-vitae-its-authority.md)

## Why this matters

No modern moral teaching has been argued over longer. *Humanae Vitae* (Paul VI, 25 July 1968) reaffirmed that contraception is excluded. It did so after most members of a papal commission had advised change. The story of 1968 is told in [`church-history` 9.3](../../church-history/lessons/09-03-after-the-council.md). This lesson is about the arguments. What exactly did the majority argue, and what does the encyclical answer? Which argument do its best defenders use today? You need all three at full strength before you can judge [6.3](06-03-humanae-vitae-its-authority.md)'s question, how much authority the teaching carries.

## The idea

Both sides agree on a great deal. Married love is good. Couples may have serious reasons to space or limit births. Intercourse in the infertile times is legitimate: HV 11 says so, as Pius XII had in his 1951 address to midwives (cited in GS 51, note 14). Parenthood should be responsible.

The dispute is narrow. **What is the unit that must be "open to life"?** The majority answered: *the marriage as a whole*. A marriage generously open to children may make particular acts infertile, just as a surgeon may sacrifice a part for the whole body. The encyclical answered: *each and every act* (HV 11). The act's meaning of union and its meaning of procreation may not be separated by the couple's own initiative (HV 12). A third answer, from Germain Grisez and John Finnis, moves the question from the act to the will. Contraception, on their account, is a choice *against* a possible new life.

## Source

The tradition's condemnation long predates 1968, and its earliest reasons were not HV's. Augustine, *On Marriage and Concupiscence* I.15.17 (Holmes and Wallis, rev. Warfield; *NPNF*, first series, vol. 5, ch. 17):

> For although propagation of offspring is not the motive of the intercourse, there is still no attempt to prevent such propagation, either by wrong desire or evil appliance. They who resort to these, although called by the name of spouses, are really not such … Sometimes, indeed, this lustful cruelty, or, if you please, cruel lust, resorts to such extravagant methods as to use poisonous drugs to secure barrenness.

Read it closely. **"Wrong desire or evil appliance"** puts the wrong in two places at once, the will and the means. That is exactly the fault line of this lesson. **"Lustful cruelty"** sets sterilizing drugs in one series with exposure and abortion, which the next sentences name. Augustine judges the whole practice as a hostility to the child. But the first clause shows his frame. Intercourse *not* sought for offspring is, for him, a venial sin tolerated in marriage (the same chapter says so). He condemned the Manichees for advising couples to watch for the woman's fertile time and abstain then, "lest the soul should be entangled in flesh" (*On the Morals of the Manichaeans* 18.65, Stothert, *NPNF* first series, vol. 4). So Augustine condemned periodic continence too, and on his premises consistently.

## The argument

**The majority's case, at full strength** (paraphrased; the commission's papers leaked in 1967 and are commonly called the majority report). HV 3 itself states its core question fairly.

1. **Procreation is a good of the marriage.** *Gaudium et Spes* 50 orders marriage and conjugal love to children. GS 51 asks that methods be judged by objective standards drawn from the nature of the person and his acts. Neither text says that each act must be procreative. GS 51, note 14, expressly left the question to the pope's commission.
2. **The tradition already separates intercourse from procreation.** Once the Church approved intercourse in known infertile times with the aim of avoiding conception, it granted that couples may rightly will intercourse and will that no child result. Augustine's premise, that only procreation fully justifies intercourse, had been quietly dropped.
3. **So the rest is a question of means, and means are judged by the whole.** This is the [principle of totality](../reference.md#the-principle-of-totality) extended from the body to the marriage. Pius XII allowed a part of the body to be sacrificed for the good of the whole. So particular acts may be made infertile for the good of a marriage that is fruitful as a whole.
4. **Reading morality off biological structure is [physicalism](../reference.md#physicalism).** Man rightly directs nature by intelligence; medicine does it daily. HV 16 reports this objection itself. Why should the rhythm of the cycle be sacrosanct when the rhythm of the heart is not?
5. **Therefore contraception within a responsibly generous marriage can be licit**, and the teaching of *Casti Connubii* (31 December 1930), which was not a definition, may develop, as its defenders argued the Church's judgments on usury had developed.

*In words:* keep the good (openness to children), drop the unit (each act), since the tradition dropped its old reason for insisting on the unit.

**The encyclical's reply** (paraphrased; Boss 6 asks you to reconstruct it in full).

6. **[Responsible parenthood](../reference.md#responsible-parenthood)** is real, but it means discerning God's order, not simply deciding (HV 10).
7. **Each and every marital act must retain its intrinsic relationship to procreation** (HV 11). Infertile acts keep it, because their unitive adaptation is not suppressed by the couple.
8. **[The inseparability principle](../reference.md#the-inseparability-principle):** God has joined the unitive and procreative meanings of the act, and man may not break the connection on his own initiative (HV 12). To use the gift while depriving it of its meaning contradicts the Creator's design, and man is not master of the sources of life (HV 13).
9. **So contraception is excluded** whether before, during or after intercourse (HV 14), along with direct sterilization and abortion. HV 14 rejects step 3 by name. A contraceptive act cannot borrow its goodness from the fertile acts of past and future as if they formed one whole. Tolerating a lesser evil is sometimes lawful; *doing* evil that good may come is not. HV 17 ends by insisting on a *correct* understanding of Pius XII's totality.
10. **Step 2 is answered by HV 16.** It concedes that the intention to avoid a child may be the same in both cases. The difference is the act. The couple using infertile periods use a faculty as nature provides it, abstaining when there is reason. The contracepting couple obstruct the generative process.

**The Grisez–Finnis argument** (paraphrased; Grisez, Boyle, Finnis and May, *The Thomist* 52, 1988). It builds on the basic goods of [`ethics` 4.5](../../ethics/lessons/04-05-the-new-natural-law-theory.md).

11. Human life is a basic good, and one may never choose to act directly against a basic good.
12. Contraception is a distinct act, chosen because one foresees that a new life might begin and wants it not to. It is a [contralife](../reference.md#the-contralife-argument) choice.
13. Abstaining is not an act against anything, so periodic continence need not involve that will.

*In words:* the wrong lies in the will against the possible child, the heir of Augustine's "wrong desire". It is not a wrong against the faculty's design.

**Mark the levels.** See [the levels](../reference.md#the-levels-in-moral-theology). **The teaching** that contraception is excluded and each act must stay open to life (HV 11, 14) is **at least authentic ordinary magisterium, requiring religious submission** ([`fundamental-theology` 4.3](../../fundamental-theology/lessons/04-03-religious-submission.md)). Whether it is also infallibly taught is **a live dispute** ([6.3](06-03-humanae-vitae-its-authority.md)). It was never solemnly defined. *Veritatis Splendor* 80 lists contraceptive practices among its examples of [intrinsically evil acts](../reference.md#intrinsically-evil-acts), quoting HV 14. The licitness of periodic continence for well-grounded reasons (HV 16) is taught in the same document, at the same rung. **The majority report has no magisterial weight.** It was advice, and HV 6 says the pope could not treat it as definitive. **The arguments are another matter.** The Church teaches the norm and gives reasons (HV 12). The contralife argument and the older faculty-frustration argument are **theological arguments, freely disputed** as arguments. Rejecting one of them does not touch the norm. HV 17's warnings about infidelity and state coercion are offered as confirmation. They are not the ground of the teaching.

## Map of positions

| Question | Majority (totality) | *Humanae Vitae* (inseparability) | Grisez–Finnis (contralife) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What must be open to life? | The marriage as a whole | Each marital act (HV 11) | The will: never choose against a possible life |
| Where is the wrong, if any? | Only in a selfish, closed marriage | Breaking the act's two meanings (HV 12) | A choice against the good of life |
| Why do infertile periods differ? | They do not, in principle | Abstaining and using nature versus obstructing it (HV 16) | Abstaining is no act against life |
| Wrong outside marriage too? | Not addressed | The argument is framed for the marital act | Yes: any contraceptive choice is contralife |
| Weight | None magisterial | Norm: at least authentic magisterium | A theologians' argument |

## Worked examples

**Ex 1 (clean): a therapeutic drug.** A married woman is prescribed a hormonal drug to treat a disease of the uterus, and foresees that it will suppress ovulation. HV 15 settles it. Therapeutic means necessary to cure a disease are not illicit, even when an impediment to procreation is foreseen, provided that impediment is not intended. This is [double effect](../reference.md#double-effect) ([4.2](04-02-double-effect-and-cooperation-as-catholic-method.md)). The object is healing, and infertility is a side effect. The test is not "artificial against natural". It is what the act is chosen to do. Whether a given prescription is truly therapeutic is a prudential and medical judgment, not a doctrinal one.

**Ex 2 (where it strains): same intention, different act.** A couple with a serious reason to avoid pregnancy uses the infertile periods. Another couple, with the same reason, uses a barrier. HV 16 concedes that both intend that no child result. The majority presses step 2. If the will is identical, the difference is only the route, and a route through biology is physicalism. The inseparability reply is that the moral object is not the goal alone. It is the chosen act: intercourse left whole, or intercourse deliberately deprived of its procreative meaning ([2.2](02-02-the-moral-object.md)). This is where the argument has to earn its keep. The critic says "the act as nature made it" is biology dressed as morality. The defender says the body is the person, not an instrument the person uses ([6.1](06-01-the-theology-of-the-body-and-chastity.md)). The contralife argument avoids this ground by looking at the will. But it pays elsewhere: a couple may use periodic continence with a will against the child, and its authors grant that such a choice would share the wrong. Note too that HV 16 does not make periodic continence automatically licit. It requires well-grounded reasons.

## Watch out

- **Understating the level.** "The commission voted for change, so the teaching is the pope's private opinion." An encyclical is at least authentic magisterium, and the commission was advisory (HV 5–6).
- **Overstating the level.** "*Humanae Vitae* was defined *ex cathedra*." It was not. Whether it is infallible by the ordinary universal magisterium is disputed ([6.3](06-03-humanae-vitae-its-authority.md)).
- **Tying the norm to one argument.** If the contralife argument fails, the teaching does not fall with it. If the teaching is binding, the argument is not thereby proven.
- **"Artificial" is not the criterion.** HV 16 praises intelligence directing nature, and HV 15 permits drugs with contraceptive effects. The criterion is an act specifically intended to prevent procreation (HV 14).
- **Caricaturing the majority** as indifferent to children. It required marriages to be generously open to life. Its dispute was over the unit.

## One-liner

> All sides want marriage open to life; the majority made the marriage the unit, *Humanae Vitae* made each act the unit because its two meanings are inseparable, and Grisez and Finnis made the will the unit.

## Problems

**P1 (🟢)** *(Exegetical)* Reconstruct the Grisez–Finnis contralife argument in four or five numbered premises, ending with the conclusion that contraception is always wrong. Then answer: (a) which premise explains why periodic continence can differ from contraception, and (b) one conclusion the argument yields that HV 11–14 does not state. **One sentence each for (a) and (b).**

**P2 (🟡)** *(Exegetical)* Two positions, paraphrased for the problem:

> **A.** "A couple generous to children over their marriage may make some acts infertile. Their marriage is fruitful as a whole, as the body is healthy as a whole when a diseased part is removed."
>
> **B.** "Each act carries both meanings, union and procreation. To use it while stripping it of one meaning is to act as master of the sources of life, not as minister."

(a) Name each position and its source. (b) State the crux in one sentence. (c) Say which section of *Humanae Vitae* rejects A's analogy by name, and give its two reasons. (d) Give the strongest reply a defender of A makes to B, stated as that defender would put it. **Two sentences per part.**

**P3 (🔴)** *(Exegetical)* An **invented** marriage-preparation handout, not the words of any real person:

> "The Church forbids contraception because it is artificial, and anything natural is fine. That is why NFP is Catholic birth control: couples may use it whenever they prefer a smaller family. Of course, the Pope's own commission voted for change, so *Humanae Vitae* is really his personal opinion. Still, it is infallible, which is why its predictions in section 17 all came true."

Name four distinct errors. For each, give the text or principle that corrects it, and say which errors misstate a level and in which direction. **200 words or fewer.**

<summary>Solutions</summary>

**P1** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:** premises close to these. (1) Human life is a basic good. (2) It is always wrong to choose to act directly against a basic good. (3) To contracept is to choose an act, distinct from intercourse, whose point is that a foreseen possible new life not begin. (4) Such a choice is a will against the good of human life, a contralife will. (5) So contraception is always wrong. **(a)** Premise 3, with its implication that abstaining chooses no act against life, so periodic continence need not involve a contralife will. **(b)** Contraception is wrong outside marriage too, as a further wrong added to fornication; or, periodic continence chosen with a will against the child shares the wrong. Either passes.

**Wrong turns:** grounding the argument in the inseparability of HV 12 or in the frustration of a faculty. It deliberately avoids both. Making the possible child an existing person who is wronged; the argument concerns the will, not a victim. Calling the argument magisterial teaching; it is a theologians' argument.

**Model answer:** 1. Life is a basic good. 2. One may never choose against a basic good. 3. Contraception is a choice that a possible new life not begin. 4. That choice is contralife. 5. So contraception is always wrong. (a) Premise 3: abstaining chooses nothing against life. (b) It makes contraception wrong outside marriage as well.

**P2** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:** **(a)** A is the majority report's **totality** argument (1966, paraphrased in HV 3). B is *Humanae Vitae*'s **inseparability** principle (HV 12–13). **(b)** The crux: **is the unit that must stay open to life the single act or the marriage as a whole?** Equivalently, can an act take its moral species from the whole of which it is part? **(c)** **HV 14.** It denies that a deliberately contraceptive act merges with the fertile acts of past and future to form one whole. It also says one may not do evil that good may come, even for the gravest reasons. Credit for HV 17's appeal to a *correct* understanding of Pius XII's totality: part for the whole body, not act for the marriage. **(d)** The Church already allows a couple to will intercourse together with the absence of a child (HV 16 concedes that the intention is the same). So B's "meaning of the act" reduces to its biological structure, which is physicalism.

**Wrong turns:** stating the crux as "whether children matter"; both sides say they do. Answering (c) with HV 12, which states B but does not answer A's analogy. Weakening A in (d), for example "sex is private".

**Model answer:** (a) A: majority report, totality. B: HV 12–13, inseparability. (b) Whether openness to life belongs to each act or to the marriage as a whole. (c) HV 14 denies that contraceptive acts merge with fertile ones into one whole, and forbids doing evil for good. (d) Since the Church approves avoiding children by periodic continence, B's "meaning" is only biology.

**P3** *(Exegetical)*

**Must hit, strict:** four errors.

1. **"Artificial" as the criterion.** HV 15 allows therapeutic drugs that impede procreation when that is not intended. HV 16 praises intelligence ordering nature. The criterion is an act intended to prevent procreation (HV 14).
2. **NFP "whenever they prefer".** HV 16 requires well-grounded reasons. It concedes the intention may equal a contracepting couple's; the difference lies in the act.
3. **"Personal opinion": understates.** The commission was advisory (HV 5–6), and an encyclical is at least authentic ordinary magisterium owed religious submission.
4. **"Infallible", and so the predictions are guaranteed: overstates twice.** HV was not defined, and infallibility by the ordinary universal magisterium is disputed (6.3). In any case HV 17's predictions are confirmation, not the teaching, and no doctrinal level attaches to their empirical truth.

**Wrong turns:** correcting 3 by asserting infallibility, or 4 by calling the teaching optional. Missing that 3 and 4 fail in opposite directions. Treating 2 as correct because NFP is licit.

**Model answer:** The handout makes "artificial" the test, but HV 15 permits therapeutic drugs and HV 14 forbids acts intended to prevent procreation. It frees NFP from any reason, but HV 16 requires well-grounded ones. It understates the level: the commission only advised (HV 5–6), and an encyclical is at least authentic magisterium owed religious submission. Then it overstates: HV was never defined, and whether it is infallibly taught is disputed. Nor are the predictions of HV 17 the teaching, so their truth carries no doctrinal guarantee.

## Flashback

**From Lesson [5.5](05-05-assisted-reproduction-and-the-embryo.md) (Assisted reproduction and the embryo):** *(Exegetical.)* An **invented** case. Dr. Lena Moreau, a university biologist, proposes to derive stem-cell lines from frozen embryos that a fertility clinic has scheduled for thawing and disposal next month. She offers two arguments. "First, these embryos will be discarded whatever I do, so my research ends no life that would otherwise go on, and it may yield cures. Second, the Church itself admits it has never settled when the embryo receives a spiritual soul, so it cannot treat destroying one as killing a person." (a) Answer each argument, naming the text. (b) Give the level of (i) the judgment that her research is gravely wrong and (ii) the claim that the embryo is ensouled from fertilization. Name the act that fixes each level, or say that none does. **Two sentences per part.**

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Must hit, strict (a):**
- **First argument.** The embryo is to be respected and treated as a person from conception (DV I.1). EV 63 applies the evaluation of abortion expressly to killing embryos in research, and DP 19 rejects using abandoned frozen embryos for research. That others have decided to discard them does not change *her* act: she chooses the embryos' destruction as the means to her results, and a good end cannot redeem that object ([2.3](02-03-intention-and-circumstances.md)).
- **Second argument.** The teaching concerns how the embryo must be *treated*, and both DV I.1 and EV 60 decline on purpose to rest it on a thesis about ensoulment. EV 60 adds that the mere probability that a person is present suffices to forbid killing the embryo.

**Must hit, strict (b):**
- **(i)** The norm behind it, against directly killing the innocent, is **rung 1** (EV 57). Its application to the embryo, research included, is taught expressly and repeatedly (EV 63, DV I.5, DP 19): **at least rung 3.**
- **(ii)** **No magisterial act.** The Magisterium expressly declines to commit itself (DV I.1; EV 60), and the question is freely disputed.

**Wrong turns:** accepting the first argument as double effect, when the embryos' death is the means chosen, not a side effect. Answering the second by claiming that the Church teaches ensoulment at fertilization, which overstates. Promoting (i) to rung 1 because its norm is rung 1. Reading DP 19's abstention on prenatal adoption as openness to research, which DP 19 rejects outright. Invoking Ground B: nothing here is procreative, and Ground A alone decides the case.

**Model answer:** (a) The embryo must be treated as a person from conception (DV I.1), EV 63 extends the judgment on abortion to killing embryos in research, and DP 19 rejects research on abandoned frozen embryos; that they would be discarded anyway does not change the fact that she chooses their destruction as the means to a good end. Her second argument misreads the teaching, which concerns treatment, not ensoulment, and EV 60 holds that even the probability of a person present forbids killing. (b) The norm against killing the innocent is rung 1 (EV 57), and its application to embryos, taught expressly in EV 63, DV I.5 and DP 19, is at least rung 3. Ensoulment at fertilization is not taught by any act: DV I.1 and EV 60 deliberately leave it open.

</details>
