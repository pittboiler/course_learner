# Moral Theology — Syllabus

> Catholic Theology · Tier 2 · ~28 lessons · Prereqs: [`ethics`](../ethics/syllabus.md), [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) · Roadmap id: `moral-theology`

## Goal

This course teaches Catholic moral theology from within the tradition: the moral life as the road to beatitude, walked by human acts, shaped by virtue, measured by law and conscience, wounded by sin, and healed and raised by grace. It follows the architecture of the *Summa Theologiae* I-II, then tests that architecture where modern moral theology fought hardest: the manuals and their casuistry, proportionalism and the fundamental option, *Veritatis Splendor*, and the teaching on life, sexuality and truthfulness. You will finish able to analyse any act by its object, end and circumstances, say what an erroneous conscience binds and excuses, tell mortal from venial sin, run double effect and the principles of cooperation as the tradition actually uses them, and assign an exact authority level to a moral teaching, including the teachings whose level Catholic theologians themselves dispute, such as *Humanae Vitae* and the 2018 revision of CCC 2267. It deliberately skips natural law and double effect as philosophy (owned by [`ethics`](../ethics/syllabus.md), cited here), the theory of the ladder and of development ([`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md)), grace and justification as doctrine (`creation-grace-last-things`), the sacrament of penance and marriage as a sacrament (`sacraments-and-liturgy`), the Church's teaching on the economy, property, labor and war (`catholic-social-teaching`), Wojtyła's philosophical personalism ([`phenomenology-and-personalism`](../phenomenology-and-personalism/syllabus.md) Module 5), and usury (`theology-of-debt`).

## How practice works in this course

Problems follow [HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md](../../HUMANITIES-BUILD-BRIEF.md), with the Catholic Theology stance: taught from within the tradition, authority marked on every claim whose weight matters, using the ladder in the Notes of [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md).

- **Exegetical parts are strict.** What Aquinas says makes an erring conscience bind, the three conditions for mortal sin, what *Veritatis Splendor* rejects in proportionalism and what it leaves standing, what *Evangelium Vitae* 57 confirms and in what form. Overstating a level is an exegetical error. So is understating one: calling the teaching on abortion "a prudential judgment" is wrong in the same way that calling the 2018 death-penalty text "infallible" is.
- **Evaluative parts are reserved for questions genuinely disputed among Catholic moral theologians.** These include probabilism against probabiliorism, how to describe the object in hard cases (salpingectomy against methotrexate for ectopic pregnancy), embryo adoption, whether every false assertion to an aggressor is a lie, whether the 2018 revision is a development or a contradiction, and what level *Humanae Vitae*'s central teaching carries. Either school's answer passes if the required moves are made. The rubric never rewards the grader's side.
- **Apologetic parts** ask for the Catholic position against a named alternative stated at its best: a secular consequentialist, a proportionalist Catholic moralist, a Protestant critic of natural law. Fidelity to the other side is graded first.
- **Archetypes.** Apply-to-a-hard-case is the workhorse, because the three fonts, double effect and cooperation are tools for cases. Every module also uses at least two of reconstruct, close reading, find the crux, diagnose and steelman-and-reply.
- **Texts.** Close reading uses public-domain texts only: Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* (English Dominican Province); Augustine, *On Lying* and *Against Lying* (*Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, first series); the *Didache* (*Ante-Nicene Fathers*); Newman, *A Letter Addressed to His Grace the Duke of Norfolk* (1875); scripture in the Douay–Rheims. Trent is read in Waterworth's translation. *Veritatis Splendor*, *Evangelium Vitae*, *Humanae Vitae*, the Catechism and all modern moralists (Pinckaers, Grisez, Finnis, McCormick, Fuchs, Rhonheimer, Brugger, Feser) are paraphrased and cited by name and section.

## Dangerous Checklist

When you finish, you can:

- [ ] Explain why Catholic moral theology begins with beatitude rather than obligation, and place any question on the map of ST I-II (end, acts, passions, habits, law, grace)
- [ ] Say what the New Law is, and how natural law, the Decalogue and the Gospel relate, marking which claims are doctrine and which are theological synthesis
- [ ] Decide whether an act is voluntary and imputable, sorting out violence, fear, concupiscence and the three kinds of ignorance
- [ ] Analyse any act by its three fonts, and show why a good intention cannot redeem an evil object while a bad intention corrupts a good one
- [ ] State what a certain but erroneous conscience binds and when it excuses, and explain the "primacy of conscience" as Newman and *Veritatis Splendor* each understand it
- [ ] Explain how infused virtue differs from acquired virtue, why charity is the form of the virtues, and what the gifts of the Holy Spirit add
- [ ] Classify a sin as mortal or venial from the three conditions, and say in what analogical sense a sin can be "social"
- [ ] Run double effect and the principles of formal and material cooperation on a new case, and say where they stop giving a verdict
- [ ] Reconstruct the proportionalist method and the fundamental option theory at their best, then state exactly what *Veritatis Splendor* rejects and why
- [ ] **Assign the authority level of a moral teaching (the killing of the innocent, abortion, euthanasia, contraception, the death penalty, lying) and state the live dispute where there is one, with both readings at full strength**
- [ ] Apply the Church's teaching on end-of-life care and assisted reproduction to a clinical case, separating what is taught from what is freely disputed
- [ ] Decide whether a deception is a lie on Augustine's and Aquinas's account, and state the modern Catholic dispute over deceiving an aggressor

## Modules

### Module 1: Beatitude, law and grace

The frame everything else hangs on: the moral life is the pursuit of happiness in God, measured by law and made possible by grace.

| # | Lesson | Goal (one line) | Key concepts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What moral theology is | Say what makes moral theology theology, and why it was renewed after the manuals | the rich young man (Mt 19:16–22) as *Veritatis Splendor*'s opening, the manual tradition and its focus on sin and confession, Vatican II's call for renewal (*Optatam Totius* 16), freedom of indifference vs freedom for excellence (Pinckaers), the plan of ST I-II |
| 1.2 | Beatitude, the last end | Explain why there is one last end and why only God satisfies it | ST I-II q.1 (the last end), q.2 (wealth, honor, pleasure and the rest ruled out), q.3 a.8 (the vision of the divine essence), perfect vs imperfect happiness, the Beatitudes (Mt 5:3–12), the natural desire to see God named as disputed and ceded to `creation-grace-last-things` |
| 1.3 | Natural law in light of revelation | Give the theological setting of natural law on top of the philosophical account in ethics 4.4–4.5 | law as participation in eternal law (ST I-II q.91 a.2), Romans 2:14–15, the Decalogue's moral precepts as natural law (q.100 a.1), sin's darkening of practical reason, the moral necessity of revelation (*Dei Filius* ch. 2, from fundamental-theology 1.2), the Magisterium's claimed competence to interpret natural law (*Humanae Vitae* 4) and its level |
| 1.4 | The New Law | State what the law of the Gospel is and why it is not a new code | ST I-II q.106 a.1 (chiefly the grace of the Holy Spirit, secondarily written), q.108 (external acts, precepts and counsels), the Sermon on the Mount as the New Law (exegesis cited from new-testament 2.2), Trent Session VI on the commandments not being impossible for the justified, the universal call to holiness (*Lumen Gentium* ch. 5), legalism and antinomianism as the twin errors |

**Boss problem 1:** Read ST I-II q.106 a.1, "Whether the New Law is a written law?", and q.91 a.2, "Whether there is in us a natural law?" (English Dominican Province), with Romans 2:14–15 (Douay–Rheims). (a) Close reading: state what Aquinas says the New Law is chiefly and secondarily, and which words make the written elements secondary without making them optional. *(Exegetical.)* (b) Reconstruct in numbered premises the account on which natural law is a participation in eternal law, and say what this adds to the philosophical account of ethics 4.4. *(Exegetical.)* (c) Assign a level on the fundamental-theology ladder to each claim, citing the source: (i) the commandments are not impossible to keep for one who is justified; (ii) the Magisterium is competent to interpret the natural moral law; (iii) happiness consists in the vision of God; (iv) there is a natural desire to see God that nature alone cannot fulfil. Where the level is disputed, say so. *(Exegetical.)* (d) A Protestant critic argues that a "law of grace" which still commands specific acts is legalism by another name. State the objection as its best defenders would, then reply from q.106–108 in 150 words. *(Apologetic.)*

### Module 2: Human acts and conscience

What makes an act *mine*, what makes it good or evil, and the judgment by which I apply the law to it.

|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | The voluntary | Decide whether an act is voluntary and how far it is imputable | the human act vs the act of a human (ST I-II q.1 a.1), q.6 on violence, fear (mixed voluntary), concupiscence and ignorance, antecedent, concomitant and consequent ignorance, vincible vs invincible, diminished imputability (CCC 1735) |

**Boss problem 2:** Read ST I-II q.19 a.5 and a.6 (English Dominican Province) and the section on conscience in Newman's *Letter to the Duke of Norfolk* (public domain). Name the crux, then argue one side in 300–400 words, using *Veritatis Splendor* and *Donum Veritatis* (paraphrased) and marking the level of the teaching at issue. *(Evaluative.)*

**Boss problem 3:** (a) Read ST I-II q.63 a.4, "Whether virtue acquired by habituation is of the same species as infused virtue?" (English Dominican Province). ## Notes

- **Authority discipline.** Every lesson uses the ladder in [`fundamental-theology`](../fundamental-theology/syllabus.md) Notes and marks levels conservatively. Four teachings are the course's standing test cases, and lessons must present each exactly, neither collapsing it upward nor downward:
  - *Evangelium Vitae* 57, 62 and 65 use solemn formulas that invoke Petrine authority and the teaching of the bishops. They are commonly read as confirming teaching of the ordinary universal magisterium rather than as ex cathedra definitions. The CDF's 1998 doctrinal commentary uses some of these teachings among its examples, and lessons state which paragraph of the Profession of Faith it assigns each only after checking the text.
  - The existence of intrinsically evil acts (*Veritatis Splendor*) is at least authentic ordinary magisterium, and is presented there as the constant teaching of the tradition. Whether it is also definitive is argued; lessons say so.
  - *Humanae Vitae*'s central teaching is at least authentic ordinary magisterium requiring religious submission. Whether it is infallibly taught is a live dispute (6.3), and both readings get full strength.
  - The 2018 text of CCC 2267 is an act of the authentic ordinary magisterium, promulgated by papal rescript into the Catechism. Whether it contradicts or develops earlier teaching, and whether its "inadmissible" states a judgment about principle or about present conditions, are disputed (5.4). Neither the earlier acceptance of the death penalty nor the revision is a solemn definition. Lessons say what follows from that and do not decide the dispute in the lesson's voice.
- **Accuracy discipline.** Section numbers are given in this syllabus only where they are well established. Lessons re-verify every CCC, encyclical and *Summa* reference against the text before publishing, and cite by document name alone where a number is uncertain. No DH numbers are used unless checked against Denzinger–Hünermann. Canon numbers from the 1983 Code's penal law are avoided: Book VI was revised in 2021 and some canons were renumbered. Modern moralists are paraphrased, and no quotation is attributed to them.
- **Where this goes next.** `catholic-social-teaching` takes social sin and structures of sin (3.5), cooperation (4.2) and double effect in war (4.2, 5.1) into the economy, the state and just war. `theology-of-debt` uses the development lens (4.1, 5.4) for usury. `sacraments-and-liturgy` takes sin (3.4) into penance, and 6.1 into marriage as a sacrament. `apologetics-catholic-claims` can argue the life and sexual ethics teachings against their critics from the positions stated here.
- **Cross-subject bridges.** Double effect as a Catholic method (4.2) and as a secular principle ([`ethics`](../ethics/syllabus.md) 3.2) is one principle in two settings, and the lessons should say so. Natural law (1.3) runs through [`ethics`](../ethics/syllabus.md) 4.4, [`philosophy-of-law`](../philosophy-of-law/syllabus.md) 2.1 and [`history-of-political-thought`](../history-of-political-thought/syllabus.md) 2.3. Probabilism (4.1) is an early theory of acting under moral uncertainty, the question [`decision-theory`](../decision-theory/syllabus.md) and contemporary "moral uncertainty" work pose formally.
